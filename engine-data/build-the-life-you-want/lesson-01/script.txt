=== Title ===
[runner] What if you never arrived at happiness, and that was perfectly fine?
Today we learn why the real goal is simply getting happier.
=== The Finish Line Myth ===
[finish_line] Many of us picture happiness as a finish line.
[trophy] Land the job, find the partner, buy the house, and you win the prize.
Then you expect to keep that feeling forever.
Sound familiar?
=== Why It Fails ===
[calendar] But the glow of each win fades, often faster than we expect.
[new_line] Soon a new finish line appears further down the road.
Chasing a permanent destination leaves us feeling like we always fall short.
=== A Direction ===
[compass] Arthur Brooks and Oprah Winfrey offer a different picture.
Happiness is not a place you reach.
[arrow] It is a direction you can keep moving in, one small step at a time.
So the goal is not happy, but happier.
=== Smell Not Dinner ===
[turkey] The authors use a Thanksgiving dinner to explain this.
[steam] The delicious smell tells you dinner is there, but the smell is not the meal.
Happy feelings work the same way.
They are evidence of happiness, not happiness itself.
=== Feelings Will Pass ===
[sun] That means good feelings will come and go, and that is normal.
[rain_cloud] You can be moving in a happier direction on a gloomy afternoon.
[empty_plate] Chasing constant good feelings is like trying to live on the smell of dinner.
=== Unhappiness Stays ===
[shield] Unhappiness will never vanish completely either, and the book says that is not a failure.
[alarm] Bad feelings often act like alarms, warning us of a threat or a problem to fix.
Fear can keep you safe, and sadness can show you what matters.
Removing them entirely would leave us unprotected.
=== Daily Choices ===
[footsteps] Getting happier comes from small choices repeated every day.
[checklist] A phone call to a friend, a moment of gratitude, a decision to help.
Each one nudges you a little further along the path.
Small steps matter because they are steps you actually control.
=== Your Next Step ===
[signpost] Tonight, ask yourself one question.
What is one small choice tomorrow that would make me a bit happier than today?
