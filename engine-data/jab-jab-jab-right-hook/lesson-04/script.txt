=== Title ===
[phone_feed] Your fans liked your page, so they see your posts, right?
On Facebook, that is not how it works.
=== The Gatekeeper ===
[gatekeeper] Between your brand and your fans stands a gatekeeper, the Facebook algorithm.
[post_stack] Every day, each person has far more posts waiting than they could ever scroll through.
So Facebook picks only a few to show, and hides the rest.
=== EdgeRank ===
[edgerank] When Gary wrote the book, Facebook called its filter EdgeRank.
[affinity] It weighed affinity, meaning how often someone interacts with you.
[weight] It weighed the type of interaction, since a comment or share counts more than a quick like.
[clock] And it weighed time, so fresh posts beat stale ones.
=== Engagement Snowballs ===
[snowball] Here is the twist, each post affects the next one.
When people like, comment and share, Facebook shows your future posts to more of them.
[shrinking] When they ignore you, your reach shrinks, and your next post starts from behind.
=== The Ad Trap ===
[billboard] Many brands treat the feed like a billboard, posting nonstop sales pitches.
[scroll_past] Fans scroll right past, the algorithm notices, and those brands slowly vanish from view.
A bad post is not neutral.
It costs you tomorrow's audience.
=== Earn It ===
[friend_post] Gary's test is simple, would this post feel at home next to a friend's photo?
Ask what your fans actually care about, not what you want to sell.
[laugh_face] Make it funny, useful or surprising enough that someone wants to react.
[short_text] Keep the words short and let a strong image do the work.
=== Pay to Amplify ===
[coins] Gary also argues that paying to promote posts on Facebook is worth the money.
But money is a megaphone, not a fix.
[megaphone] Promote the posts your fans already love, so the boost multiplies something good instead of something boring.
=== Act Today ===
[your_posts] Look back at your last 10 posts.
[ranked_list] Rank them by comments and shares, not by how much you liked them.
Make more of what topped the list.
[repeat_loop] Then watch whether your reach grows week after week.
=== Your Challenge ===
Before you post next, ask yourself, would I stop scrolling for this?
If not, rewrite it until you would.
