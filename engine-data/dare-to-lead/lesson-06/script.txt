=== Braving Trust ===
[cracked_jar] Telling a colleague you don't trust them can feel like dropping a bomb.
What if you could name the exact problem instead?
=== Trust Is Too Big ===
[big_word] Trust is a huge, heavy word.
Saying it out loud can feel like an attack on someone's whole character.
[two_people] When someone hears they aren't trusted, they usually get defensive, and the conversation ends.
Brown's answer is to break trust into smaller pieces people can actually discuss.
=== The Marble Jar ===
[marble_jar] Brown pictures trust as a jar of marbles.
[one_marble] Each small moment of care, like remembering a detail or keeping a promise, adds a marble.
Trust is built slowly, in ordinary moments, not grand gestures.
=== Boundaries, Reliability ===
[letter_b] B is for boundaries: you respect mine, and you ask when you're unsure.
[letter_r] R is for reliability: you do what you say, again and again.
That means knowing your limits, so you don't overpromise.
=== Accountability, Vault ===
[letter_a] A is for accountability: you own your mistakes, apologize, and make amends.
[letter_v] V is for the vault: you keep what I share in confidence.
[whisper] It also means you don't share other people's secrets with me just to feel close.
=== Integrity, Judgment ===
[letter_i] I is for integrity: choosing courage over comfort, and practicing your values, not just stating them.
Integrity also means doing what's right, not what's fun, fast or easy.
[letter_n] N is for non-judgment: we can both ask for help without being judged.
=== Generosity ===
[letter_g] G is for generosity: you assume the most generous explanation for my words and actions.
[braving_word] Put the seven letters together, and they spell braving.
Brown uses it as a checklist for teams, and for checking how much we trust ourselves.
=== Name the Behavior ===
[manager] Imagine a manager whose teammate keeps missing deadlines.
Instead of saying she doesn't trust him, she says she's struggling with reliability on this project.
[desk_talk] Now they can talk about one specific, fixable behavior.
=== Your Turn ===
[checklist] Pick one relationship at work and run through the seven behaviors.
Which marble could you add this week?
