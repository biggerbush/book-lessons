=== Keep Score ===
[pickup_game] Picture a casual game in the park where nobody keeps score.
[chalk_tally] Now someone starts counting points, and suddenly everyone plays harder.
=== Scoring Changes Play ===
[authors] The authors build Discipline 3 on a simple truth about people.
[quote_card] As they put it, "People play differently when they're keeping score."
=== The Coach's Report ===
[spreadsheet] Most workplaces do track numbers, but in dense spreadsheets built for managers.
[charts_pile] They are crowded with columns and trends that only a leader has time to study.
The book calls this a coach's scoreboard, useful to the boss but rarely motivating to the team.
=== The Players' Board ===
[stadium] Now picture the scoreboard above a sports field.
[fan] Any fan can glance up and instantly know who is winning.
Discipline 3 says every team needs that kind of players' scoreboard for its wildly important goal.
=== Simple and Visible ===
[checklist] The authors offer a few tests for a good one.
[single_line] First, it is simple, showing only the data needed to play.
[wall_board] Second, it is visible, posted where the team sees it daily, not buried in a shared folder.
=== Lead, Lag, at a Glance ===
[two_lines] Third, it shows both lead and lag measures, so people see their actions move the result.
[timer] Fourth, anyone can tell within seconds whether the team is winning.
If people have to study it, it is a report, not a scoreboard.
=== Built by the Team ===
[team_marker] The book encourages teams to design their own scoreboard, even on a plain whiteboard.
[smiling_team] When people build it, they own it, and they care more about the score.
[green_tick] Every time a line climbs or a mark turns green, the team gets a small win.
=== Why It Works ===
[dark_field] Without a clear score, even hard workers cannot tell if their effort matters.
[climbing_line] A visible score turns abstract strategy into a game the team can see itself winning.
That feeling of winning, the book argues, is what drives real engagement.
=== Your Turn ===
[blank_board] This week, sketch one simple scoreboard for your top goal and post it where your team will see it.
Then ask: can everyone tell in five seconds whether you are winning?
