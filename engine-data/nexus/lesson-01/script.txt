=== The Wise Human ===
[sapiens] We named our species Homo sapiens, which means wise human.
[puzzle] So why are we so good at gathering knowledge, and so bad at using it?
=== A Huge Library ===
[library] Over thousands of years, humans have piled up astonishing amounts of information.
[galaxy] We have mapped distant galaxies and peered inside tiny cells.
[warning] Yet Harari points out that we now face ecological collapse, nuclear danger, and technologies we may not control.
=== The Naive View ===
[chain] Harari calls a popular belief the naive view of information.
[arrow_truth] It says more information leads to more truth.
[arrow_wisdom] And more truth leads to wisdom and power.
Under this view, every problem is just a lack of data.
=== A Tempting Promise ===
[tech_campus] Many technology optimists hold this belief today.
[open_pipes] They imagine that if information simply flows freely, lies will fade and the truth will win.
It sounds hopeful and simple.
Harari says history tells a different story.
=== The Printing Press ===
[press] Take the printing press, which spread across Europe in the fifteenth century.
[telescope] The naive view says printing sparked the scientific revolution.
[pamphlets] But Harari notes that presses also churned out sensational tales and conspiracy theories.
[witch_book] One popular printed manual taught readers how to spot and punish witches.
Wild fantasies spread as fast as discoveries.
=== Truth Is Costly ===
[scale] Why does this happen?
Harari explains that truth is usually expensive, complicated, and sometimes painful.
[cheap_story] Fiction can be cheap, simple, and flattering.
Checking facts takes time and money, while inventing a tale takes almost nothing.
In an open flood of information, the truth is often outnumbered.
=== Not Just Bad Apples ===
[sorcerer] Old stories like the sorcerer's apprentice blame disaster on foolish individuals.
Harari disagrees.
[network] He argues the deeper problem lies in how large groups share information.
[crowd] Good people inside a badly built network can still drift toward disaster.
=== What to Do ===
[raw_material] So treat more information as raw material, not as wisdom.
[magnifier] When you read something, ask not only how much you know, but how well it has been checked.
[filter] Volume is not the same as accuracy.
=== Your Challenge ===
[challenge] Next time someone says we just need more data, ask yourself what would actually make that data trustworthy.
