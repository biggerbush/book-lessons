=== Your Brain Decides First ===
[title_brain] You think you win a negotiation with better arguments.
Chris Voss learned the opposite while negotiating with kidnappers for the FBI.
=== The Old Rulebook ===
[harvard_book] For decades, negotiation was taught as a rational problem to solve.
[getting_to_yes] The classic Harvard approach, in the book Getting to Yes, urged people to separate the people from the problem.
[calculator] It pictured both sides as calm calculators, weighing offers logically.
Voss says this misses how humans actually work.
=== Two Systems ===
[kahneman] He points to psychologist Daniel Kahneman, who described two ways our minds work.
[fast_animal] System 1 is fast, instinctive and emotional.
[slow_thinker] System 2 is slow, careful and logical.
System 1 reacts first, and System 2 often just explains the choice afterward.
=== No Emotion, No Choice ===
[neuro_scan] Voss also cites neuroscientist Antonio Damasio.
[stuck_person] Damasio studied people whose brain damage left them unable to feel emotions.
They could still reason, yet they struggled to make even simple decisions.
[scale_heart] Feelings, it turns out, are what tip the scales.
=== Harvard Showdown ===
[classroom] Voss once joined a negotiation course at Harvard Law School.
[student_group] Sharp students arrived armed with clever logic and tactics.
[fbi_agent] Voss relied instead on his crisis training, staying curious about what the other side felt and wanted.
He outperformed the trained students again and again.
=== Listen, Don't Argue ===
[arrow_wall] When you fire facts at someone, their emotional brain often digs in.
[open_ear] Listening lowers their guard instead.
[thought_bubble] Once you understand what someone fears and hopes, you know what will actually move them.
=== Tactical Empathy ===
[empathy_link] Voss calls this tactical empathy.
[two_chairs] It means understanding the other side's feelings, and showing them you understand.
It is not agreeing, and it is not being soft.
[key_lock] It is a tool for influence, because people say yes to those who make them feel understood.
=== Try It This Week ===
[calendar] Before your next tough conversation, set aside your arguments for a moment.
[note_card] Write down what the other person might be feeling, and what they might be afraid of losing.
Start there.
=== Your Challenge ===
[final_question] In your next disagreement, will you try to win the argument, or understand the person?
