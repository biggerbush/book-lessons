=== Admit It First ===
[title_card] What if the fastest way to win a dispute is to admit you were wrong?
=== The Bulldog in the Park ===
[bulldog] Carnegie often walked his little Boston bulldog, Rex, in a park near his home.
[no_leash] He let Rex run free, without a leash or a muzzle.
[policeman] One day a policeman on horseback stopped him and gave him a stern warning.
Carnegie promised to obey, then slowly slipped back into his old habit.
=== Caught Again ===
[hilltop] Weeks later, Carnegie and Rex came over a hill and met the very same officer.
This time, Carnegie didn't wait for the lecture.
[confession] He spoke first, saying he was caught, he was guilty, and he had no excuse.
=== The Officer Softens ===
[soft_face] The policeman's whole manner changed.
He said a small dog like that was hardly dangerous.
[out_of_sight] He even suggested Carnegie take Rex over the hill, out of sight.
The honest confession had left him nothing to fight against.
=== Why It Works ===
[raised_shield] When someone accuses us, our instinct is to defend ourselves.
[open_hands] But when we criticise ourselves first, the other person often feels generous and wants to be kind.
[stopwatch] Carnegie's advice is to admit errors quickly and emphatically, before anyone else can raise them.
=== Saving Face ===
[flip_arrow] The same idea works in reverse, when someone else is the one who slipped.
[crowd] Correcting people in front of others wounds their pride, so they dig in.
[crown] Letting them keep their dignity makes change much easier to accept.
=== The New Title ===
[engineer] General Electric once faced a delicate problem with Charles Steinmetz, a brilliant engineer.
[dept_desk] He was a genius with electricity, but a poor fit as a department head.
[name_plate] So the company gave him a new title, consulting engineer, and let someone else run the department.
Steinmetz was pleased, and the company kept its star.
=== Try It Today ===
[speech_bubble] Next time you get something wrong, say so plainly, before anyone else does.
[exit_door] When someone else gets it wrong, offer them a graceful way out, ideally in private.
=== Your Turn ===
[mirror] What small mistake could you own today, before anyone else points it out?
