=== Who Holds the Wheel ===
[steering_wheel] Picture your workday as a car.
Who is actually holding the steering wheel, you or someone else?
=== Autonomy Is Choice ===
[curious_kid] Pink argues that people are naturally curious and self-directed.
[lone_desk] Autonomy is not the same as working alone.
[choice_paths] It means acting with real choice, even while you depend on others.
Yet much of traditional management assumes people must be watched to do anything.
=== The FedEx Day ===
[atlassian] At the Australian software company Atlassian, the founders tried an unusual experiment.
[clock_24] Once a quarter, engineers got 24 hours to work on anything they wanted.
[package] The only rule was to deliver something overnight, like a FedEx package.
=== What Came Out ===
[lightbulbs] Those days produced a burst of software fixes and fresh product ideas.
[team_demo] At the end, teams showed off their work to the whole company.
[pie_twenty] It worked so well that Atlassian later tried giving engineers 20 percent of their time for their own projects.
=== The Four Ts ===
Pink breaks autonomy into four Ts.
[task_card] Task is what you work on.
[time_clock] Time is when you do it.
[toolbox] Technique is how you do it.
[team_circle] Team is who you do it with.
The more of these you control, the more you bring your best.
=== Results Only ===
[rowe_sign] Some companies go even further with what is called a results-only work environment.
[empty_desk] There are no fixed schedules, and nobody counts hours spent at a desk.
[finish_flag] People come and go as they like, as long as the work gets done.
=== Control or Engage ===
Pink puts the trade-off simply.
[leash] Tight control can get you compliance, which works for following instructions.
[spark] But engagement, the kind that sparks great work, comes from a sense of ownership.
=== Start Small ===
[calendar] You can try this even without permission from above.
[mini_project] Block out a few hours to pick your own task and your own technique.
[share_table] Then share what you made with someone, just like the Atlassian engineers did.
=== Your Four Ts ===
[question_mark] Which of the four Ts do you control least right now?
What one small step could win back a little of it this week?
