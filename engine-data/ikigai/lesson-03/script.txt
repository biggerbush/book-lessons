=== A Simple Rule ===
[empty_bowl] What if one secret to a long life fit into a short phrase you could say before every meal?
Okinawans have exactly that, and many of them say it out loud.
=== Hara Hachi Bu ===
[elder_table] On Okinawa, older people often say hara hachi bu before they eat.
[stomach_meter] It means, roughly, fill your belly to 80 percent.
The idea: stop eating while you could still eat a little more.
=== Why Stop Early ===
[full_plate] Most of us keep eating until we feel completely full, sometimes even past it.
[light_body] Okinawans stop earlier, leaving a little room in the stomach.
The authors link this habit of eating a little less to staying healthy for longer.
=== Small Plates ===
[small_plates] The book suggests a practical trick: serve your food on many small plates.
[tray] A typical Japanese meal arrives as a tray of little dishes, rice, soup, vegetables, a bit of fish.
Smaller portions make it easier to stop at 80 percent without feeling deprived.
=== Eat the Rainbow ===
[rainbow] What Okinawans eat matters as much as how much.
[veg_basket] Their diet is rich in vegetables and fruits, in many different colours.
[count_206] The authors report the local diet includes 206 different foods, spices included.
=== Okinawan Staples ===
[sweet_potato] Some staples come up again and again.
Sweet potatoes, tofu, seaweed, and plenty of leafy greens.
[green_tea] Green tea is a daily habit too.
[sugar_salt] Sugar and salt play a much smaller role than in many Western kitchens.
=== One Pillar ===
[pillar] In this book, food is one pillar of a long life, not the whole story.
[scale] Eating a bit less and a bit more variety is easy to start and easy to keep.
[daily_meal] It turns each ordinary meal into a small act of care for your future self.
=== Try It Tonight ===
[fork_down] At your next meal, eat slowly and pause halfway.
[check_in] Ask yourself whether you are still hungry, or just eating because food is there.
[last_bite] When you feel comfortably satisfied, put your fork down, even if a few bites remain.
