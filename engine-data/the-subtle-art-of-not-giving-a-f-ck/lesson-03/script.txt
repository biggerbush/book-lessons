=== You Are Not Special ===
[title_star] What if believing you are special is what holds you back?
=== Meet Jimmy ===
[jimmy] Mark Manson describes a friend he calls Jimmy, who always had a big new business idea.
[pitch_deck] Jimmy pitched his plans to anyone who would listen.
[empty_office] But he rarely did the work to make any of them real.
He felt great about himself anyway, sure he was destined for success.
=== Feeling Good Is Cheap ===
[trophy] Manson criticises the self-esteem movement, which taught that simply feeling good about yourself leads to success.
[mirror] But feeling good without earning it creates people like Jimmy, confident and stuck.
=== Two Faces ===
[crown] Entitlement has an obvious face, the person who thinks they are better than everyone.
[raincloud] It also has a hidden face, the person who thinks they are uniquely broken.
[seesaw] One says I am awesome and you all suck, the other says I suck and you are all awesome.
Both put themselves at the center, as an exception to the rules.
=== Why Broken Feels Safe ===
[victim_badge] Believing your pain is unlike anyone else's can feel strangely comforting.
It means nobody can understand you, so nobody can expect you to change.
[crowd] Yet millions of others have faced almost every struggle you face.
=== The Highlight Reel ===
[phone_screen] Manson says media constantly shows us extremes, the richest, the funniest, the most beautiful.
[ruler] Against that measuring stick, ordinary feels like failure.
[spotlight] So we feel pressure to be exceptional, or to believe we secretly already are.
He calls this the tyranny of exceptionalism.
=== Average Is Freeing ===
[bell_curve] The truth is that most of us are pretty average at most things.
[weight_lifted] Accepting it removes the pressure to prove your greatness every day.
[workbench] And people who do become exceptional usually got there by admitting they were not good enough yet.
=== Real Self-Worth ===
[scale] Manson suggests a better test of self-worth: how you feel about your weaknesses, not your strengths.
[notebook] Someone healthy can say, I am bad at this, and then work on it.
[open_door] That honest admission is where growth begins.
=== Your Turn ===
[question] Pick one area where you think you are too gifted or too broken to need effort.
What would change if you treated yourself as simply ordinary, and started practicing?
