=== Title ===
[title_card] What if everything you wanted to say had to fit into one short sentence?
An army and an airline both learned to do exactly that.
=== When Plans Break ===
[soldiers] Picture an army unit heading out with a detailed plan.
[battle_map] Every move is drawn on the map, step by step.
[explosion] Then the fighting starts, the weather turns, and the plan falls apart.
The Heaths point out that military planners have long expected plans to break on contact.
=== Commander's Intent ===
[order_sheet] So the Army adds a short statement to the top of its orders.
It is called Commander's Intent.
[flag_hill] It plainly states the goal, the result the mission must achieve no matter what.
[soldier_think] When the plan collapses, soldiers still know what matters, so they can improvise toward it.
=== Two Questions ===
[question_one] To find that intent, an officer can ask a simple question.
If we do nothing else tomorrow, what must we do?
[question_two] Or another one: what is the single most important thing?
[red_pencil] Answering means crossing out ideas that are good but not essential.
=== The Airline ===
[plane] Southwest Airlines had its own version of Commander's Intent.
[kelleher] Herb Kelleher, its longtime leader, boiled the strategy down to one line.
[price_tag] "We are the low-fare airline," said Herb Kelleher.
Every decision could be measured against those six words.
=== The Salad Test ===
[marketer] Imagine someone in marketing says passengers would love a chicken salad on one route.
[salad] It sounds like a lovely idea.
[scale] But does it help Southwest stay the low-fare airline?
If not, the answer is no, and any employee can make that call.
=== Saying Less ===
[podium] In the 1992 Clinton campaign, adviser James Carville kept the whole team focused on the economy.
[three_balls] His point was that juggling three messages means landing none of them.
[one_arrow] Every extra priority weakens the one that truly counts.
=== Simple Is Not Dumb ===
[proverb_book] Simple does not mean dumbed down.
It means the core, packed tightly, the way a proverb holds deep wisdom in a few words.
[note_card] Before your next email or meeting, write your core message in one sentence.
[trash_can] Then cut anything that does not serve it.
=== Your Challenge ===
[challenge] What is the one thing your team must get right, even if every plan falls apart?
Write that sentence down today, and share it.
