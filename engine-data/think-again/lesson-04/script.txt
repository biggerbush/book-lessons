=== Title ===
[two_brothers] Two brothers argued loudly with each other for weeks.
That shouting match helped get the first airplane off the ground.
=== Two Kinds of Conflict ===
[team_table] Picture a team arguing around a table.
[label_task] Psychologist Karen Jehn found that one kind of fight is about the work itself, the task.
[label_person] The other kind is personal, when people clash over who they are.
That's relationship conflict, and it feels like an attack.
=== Why It Matters ===
[sad_team] Personal conflict tends to drag teams down.
People get defensive and stop sharing what they know.
[bright_bulb] But disagreeing about ideas can spark better decisions.
It pushes everyone to test assumptions instead of nodding along.
=== The Wright Brothers ===
[wright_shop] Wilbur and Orville Wright ran a bicycle shop and dreamed of flight.
[dad_books] Their father encouraged them to argue, and they grew up debating at home.
[propeller] When designing their propeller, they argued loudly for weeks.
=== Swapping Sides ===
[swap_arrows] Sometimes they would switch positions, each arguing the other's view.
That trick forced them to understand both sides fully.
They were not trying to win.
They were trying to get it right.
[blueprint] Eventually they realized they were both wrong.
=== Twin Propellers ===
[twin_props] The answer was two propellers spinning in opposite directions.
Neither brother would have found it alone.
[heart_link] And through all the shouting, their bond stayed strong.
They fought about the design, never about each other.
=== Keep It Healthy ===
[thermometer] The trick is keeping the heat on the idea, not the person.
[speech_bubble] Say you disagree with this plan, not that someone always gets things wrong.
Criticism of a plan stays useful, while criticism of a person just stings.
[puzzle_piece] Treat the argument as a puzzle you are solving together.
=== Avoid Fake Harmony ===
[smile_mask] Agreeing just to keep the peace can be dangerous too.
Silence lets bad ideas slip through unchallenged.
Grant warns that teams avoiding all conflict often miss problems hiding in plain sight.
[open_door] Invite disagreement, and make it clear that dissent is welcome.
=== Your Challenge ===
Next time you disagree, ask yourself whether it is about the idea or the person.
If it's the idea, lean in, and if it's personal, step back and reset.
