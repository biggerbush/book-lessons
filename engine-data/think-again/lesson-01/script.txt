=== Think Again ===
[brain_gears] How often do you change your mind about something that really matters?
Adam Grant thinks most of us do it far too rarely.
=== The Preacher ===
[preacher] Grant builds on an idea from psychologist Phil Tetlock, who noticed that we slip into familiar mental roles.
The first role is the preacher.
[pulpit] When our sacred beliefs feel threatened, we deliver sermons to protect them.
We stop listening and start lecturing.
=== The Prosecutor ===
[prosecutor] The second role is the prosecutor.
[evidence_box] Here we line up arguments to prove someone else wrong.
Our goal is to win the case, not to find the truth.
=== The Politician ===
[politician] The third role is the politician.
[crowd] We try to win over an audience and earn their approval.
[thumbs_up] So we tell people what they want to hear, even when we are not convinced ourselves.
=== The Hidden Problem ===
[mirror] In all three roles, we are busy being right.
[locked_door] Our own views stay locked away, safe from any questioning.
We may try to change other minds, but we never change our own.
=== Enter the Scientist ===
[scientist] Grant offers a fourth role, the scientist.
[hypothesis] A scientist treats a belief as a hypothesis, a best guess waiting to be tested.
[flask] They run experiments and update their views when the evidence surprises them.
Being wrong is not a disaster.
It is simply new information.
=== The Startup Study ===
[italy_map] Grant describes an experiment with early stage entrepreneurs in Italy.
[startup_desk] All of them attended a training programme on building a business.
[two_groups] Half were also taught to treat their strategy as a theory and their plans as tests to run.
=== What Happened ===
[revenue_bars] The scientific thinkers went on to bring in far more revenue than the others.
[pivot_arrow] They were also more willing to change course when their tests showed an idea was not working.
Treating plans as experiments made them better at rethinking, and better at business.
=== Your Experiment ===
[question_mark] This week, pick one opinion you hold strongly.
Then ask yourself what evidence would change your mind, and go looking for it.
