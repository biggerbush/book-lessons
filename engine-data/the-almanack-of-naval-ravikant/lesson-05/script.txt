=== The quiet payoff ===
[hourglass] What if the best things in your life are slowly growing in the background?
Naval says they are, but only if you stay put long enough.
=== Compounding everywhere ===
[curve] Picture a curve that starts almost flat, then bends sharply upward.
That is compound interest, where gains build on earlier gains.
[three_jars] Naval argues it applies far beyond money, to relationships and knowledge too.
Most of the payoff arrives late, after years of quiet growth.
=== One deal or many ===
[two_players] Imagine two people who will do business together once, and never again.
Each is tempted to grab as much as possible and walk away.
[calendar] Now imagine they expect to work together for decades.
Suddenly honesty pays, because every deal shapes the next one.
=== The short-term trap ===
[handshake] Naval sees these repeated, long-term relationships as the place where real returns live.
[broken_chain] Cutting corners for a quick win resets your compounding back to zero.
You start over with new people who have no reason to trust you.
=== Trust saves time ===
[old_friends] When you have worked with someone for years, you stop checking every detail.
[fast_arrow] Decisions move faster, and you can take bigger swings together.
That saved friction is itself a kind of compounding wealth.
=== Picking partners ===
[magnifier] So who should you play with?
[three_traits] Naval looks for intelligence, energy and above all integrity.
[cracked_coin] Without integrity, the other two can work against you.
Integrity is what makes the long game possible.
=== A lifetime filter ===
[lifetime_rule] Naval offers a simple filter.
[day_vs_life] If you cannot picture working with someone for life, do not work with them for even a day.
That sounds strict, but it saves you from investing years in the wrong people.
=== Your next move ===
[contact_list] Look at the people you work and spend time with right now.
[seedling] Choose the ones you trust, and keep showing up for them, year after year.
In Naval's words, "Play long-term games with long-term people."
=== Your challenge ===
[mirror] Who in your life is worth a ten year game?
And are you the kind of person someone would choose for theirs?
