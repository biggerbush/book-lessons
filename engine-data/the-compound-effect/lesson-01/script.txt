=== The Magic Penny ===
[cash_offer] Would you take 3 million dollars in cash today, or a single penny that doubles every day for a month?
Most people grab the cash.
=== A Slow Start ===
[penny] Darren Hardy opens his book with exactly this choice.
[day_five] On day 5, your penny has grown to just 16 cents.
[day_twenty] By day 20, it is worth only about 5 thousand dollars.
The cash still looks like the smart pick.
=== The Final Surge ===
[rising_curve] Then the doubling starts to roar.
[day_thirty_one] By day 31, that humble penny is worth more than 10 million dollars.
Most of the growth arrives in the final few days.
The people who quit early never see it.
=== Three Friends ===
[three_friends] Hardy then describes three friends with similar lives, incomes and weight.
Larry keeps doing exactly what he has always done.
[scott] Scott makes tiny upgrades, like reading ten pages a day and cutting 125 calories.
[brad] Brad makes tiny downgrades, like buying a big screen television and adding a few snacks.
=== Nothing Seems to Happen ===
[five_months] After five months, the three friends look exactly the same.
Scott's good choices seem pointless, and Brad's bad ones seem harmless.
[quit_sign] This is where most people give up, because small choices show no reward yet.
=== Then It Shows ===
[month_31] By month 31, Scott has lost 33 pounds.
[scale] Brad has gained 33 pounds.
Larry is right where he started.
[gap_arrow] That is a 66 pound gap, built from choices too small to notice.
=== The Formula ===
[formula] Hardy sums it up as a simple formula.
Small, smart choices, plus consistency, plus time, equal a radical difference.
[reverse_arrow] The same math works in reverse for bad habits.
One extra snack or skipped walk compounds just as surely as a good one.
=== Why We Miss It ===
[single_moment] In any single moment, a choice feels too small to matter.
So we skip the good one and indulge the bad one without noticing.
[future_self] But your future self lives with the total of every one of them.
=== Your Penny ===
[your_penny] What is one small, smart choice you could repeat every day, starting today?
Make it tiny, then keep it going long enough to see the curve rise.
