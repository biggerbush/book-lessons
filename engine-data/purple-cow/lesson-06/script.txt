=== Who Spreads the Word ===
[title_cow] Your product does not need everyone to love it.
It needs the right few people to start sneezing.
=== The Bell Curve ===
[bell_curve] Godin borrows a familiar curve that shows how new ideas spread through a market.
[left_edge] On the far left sit innovators and early adopters, a small group hungry for anything new.
[big_middle] In the big middle sit the majority, who want what already works and ignore what is new.
=== The Common Mistake ===
Most companies aim straight at that big middle, because that is where the money seems to be.
[wall] But the majority is busy, satisfied, and very good at ignoring you.
[ad_bounce] So messages aimed at them simply bounce off.
=== Meet the Sneezers ===
[sneezer] Godin calls the people who spread ideas sneezers.
[sneeze_cloud] When they find something they love, they tell friends, coworkers and followers without being asked.
Their excitement carries an idea across the market like a cold.
=== The Otaku ===
[otaku_badge] Godin also uses a Japanese word, otaku, for a deep obsession with a category.
[shelf] Picture someone who owns a whole shelf of hot sauces and travels to try new ones.
Those obsessed customers are where a remarkable product finds its first fans.
=== Build for Them ===
[blueprint] Godin's advice is to design the product itself for these eager few, not for the average buyer.
[magnifier] Ask what would make a passionate fan stop, grin, and grab a friend's arm.
[edge_feature] That might be a feature the majority would never request.
=== Let Them Carry It ===
[arrow_flow] Once sneezers adopt it, their stories travel toward the middle of the curve.
[crowd] The majority trusts a friend's recommendation far more than any ad.
So you reach the masses by ignoring them at first.
That is how a purple cow goes from niche to normal.
=== Find Your Few ===
[map_pin] Every market has its own sneezers, from software to cycling to cooking.
[name_list] Your job is to know exactly who they are and where they gather.
[gift_box] Then make something so remarkable for them that keeping quiet feels impossible.
=== Your Challenge ===
Who are the ten people in your field most likely to talk about something new?
Write their names down, then design your next idea for them.
