1. The core idea: when people do things that annoy or hurt you (don't text back, leave you out, run late, criticise you, hold opinions you dislike), the usual reaction is to try to control them. The Let Them Theory says: let them. Stop spending energy on what you can't control.
2. Let Them is paired with Let Me. Let them do what they do, then ask what you will do about it. The power is in your response, not their behaviour.
3. Letting go of control: we exhaust ourselves trying to manage other people's opinions, choices and moods. Accepting that they are free to act as they choose gives you your time and peace back.
4. People show you who they are. If someone doesn't make an effort, let them. Their behaviour is information about the relationship, so you can adjust your investment.
5. Friendships and relationships: let people drift or leave, and put your energy into those who show up. Don't chase, convince or beg.
6. Other people's opinions: let them think what they think of you. You don't need approval to live your life.
7. Comparison and your own life: let others succeed and move at their own pace, and use that as a cue to focus on your own goals.
8. Emotions: you can't control how others feel or react, but you can manage your own emotions and choose a calm, deliberate response.
9. Practical use: pause, say "let them" to yourself, name what is in your control, then decide your next move ("let me").
