=== Turn It Off ===
[remote] Think about the last ad you sat through on purpose.
You probably can't remember one, and that is exactly the problem.
=== A Simple Old Recipe ===
[factory] For decades, companies followed one dependable recipe.
[average_box] Make a safe, ordinary product that appeals to as many people as possible.
[tv_set] Then buy lots of television ads to tell everyone about it.
=== The Money Machine ===
Seth Godin calls this system the TV-industrial complex.
[store_shelf] Ads brought shoppers, and shoppers pushed stores to stock the product.
[cash_stack] Sales brought profits, which paid for even more ads.
[loop_arrow] Round and round it went, and for a long time it worked beautifully.
=== Why It Worked Then ===
[family_couch] Back then, families gathered around a few channels each evening.
[few_products] Stores carried fewer choices, so a familiar brand stood out easily.
People actually needed help deciding, and ads gave them that help.
Getting noticed was mostly a matter of paying for it.
=== Then Choices Exploded ===
[crowded_aisle] Today, nearly every aisle is packed with options that all look pretty good.
[clock] Meanwhile, people have less time and attention than ever.
Most of what we already own works fine, so we don't urgently need anything new.
Shoppers are not looking for help anymore, they are looking for ways to filter things out.
=== Everyone Tunes Out ===
[ad_pile] Ads now arrive from every direction, all day long.
[earmuffs] So people protect themselves by ignoring nearly all of them.
They skip, scroll, mute, and walk away.
Ignoring ads is no longer rude, it is how people survive the flood.
=== Shouting Gets Pricey ===
[price_tag] Reaching people through ads keeps getting more expensive.
[tiny_crowd] And each dollar buys less real attention than it used to.
Godin's point is that you can no longer simply buy your way into people's minds.
=== The Broken Loop ===
[snapped_loop] So the old money machine has snapped.
An average product plus a big ad budget now usually means an average product nobody notices.
[lightbulb] Godin argues the attention has to come from the product itself, not from the noise around it.
=== Your Turn ===
Picture your product with no advertising at all, and ask whether anyone would notice it.
If the honest answer is no, fix the product before you buy another ad.
