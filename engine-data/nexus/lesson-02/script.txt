=== Stars and Strangers ===
[night_sky] Why would billions of people care what the stars said on the day they were born?
The answer reveals what information is really for.
=== The Usual Picture ===
[mirror] Most of us picture information as a mirror held up to the world.
[map] Good information, we assume, is like an accurate map of reality.
Harari says this picture is mostly wrong.
=== What It Really Does ===
[network] Information's main job is to connect things, linking separate points into a network.
[thread] Think of it as a thread that ties people together, not a camera that records them.
Some information represents reality well, but that is the exception, not the rule.
=== The Astrologer ===
[astrologer] For centuries, astrologers read the planets as messages about kings, wars and marriages.
[horoscope] The planets did not actually predict those things.
Yet horoscopes connected rulers, advisers and ordinary people through shared decisions and rituals.
False as a picture of reality, astrology was powerful as a connector.
=== The Holy Book ===
[holy_book] Harari points to the Bible as another example.
[crowd] Whatever its historical accuracy, it linked enormous numbers of people across continents and centuries.
Its power came less from accuracy than from the communities it bound together.
A story can be powerful without being true.
=== Why Fiction Wins ===
[scales] Truth is often complicated, painful and costly to find.
[simple_tale] A fiction can be simple, flattering and easy to share.
So a false story can spread further and hold people tighter than a messy fact.
=== Power Is Not Proof ===
[megaphone] Here is the uncomfortable lesson.
When a message moves crowds, that tells you it connects people well.
[question] It tells you nothing about whether it is true.
Both questions matter, and we often confuse them.
=== Two Questions ===
[checklist] Next time a message moves you, ask two separate questions.
First, who does this connect me to?
Second, how do I know it matches reality?
Keeping those questions apart is the first step to thinking clearly.
