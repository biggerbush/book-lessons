=== Title ===
[title_card] Who decided your society's rules, and why do millions of strangers follow them?
Harari's answer is surprising: the rules exist because we all believe in them.
=== Two Famous Codes ===
[clay_stele] Picture an ancient stone carved with laws, from Babylon nearly 4,000 years ago.
[parchment] Now picture a paper document from America in 1776.
Harari puts these two side by side to reveal something surprising.
=== Hammurabi's Order ===
[king] The Babylonian code was issued by King Hammurabi, who claimed the gods had appointed him.
[pyramid] It split people into superiors, commoners and slaves.
[scales] Punishments and compensation changed depending on which class you belonged to.
=== The American Order ===
[founders] The American Declaration of Independence claimed instead that all men are created equal.
[rights_list] It said people have rights to life, liberty and the pursuit of happiness.
Both documents insisted their principles were natural and eternal.
=== Imagined, Not Fake ===
[dna_helix] Harari points out that biology contains neither ranks of nobles nor equal rights.
[crowd] Both orders exist only because large numbers of people believe in them, and act accordingly.
[lone_doubter] If one person stops believing, the order barely notices, because everyone else around them still does.
That is why imagined orders are so sturdy.
=== The Memory Problem ===
[village] A small band can keep its customs in everyone's heads.
[big_kingdom] But a kingdom needs to track taxes, debts, property and laws for huge numbers of people.
[brain] Human brains simply were not built to store that much data.
=== Sumer Invents Writing ===
[clay_tablet] Around 5,000 years ago, Sumerians in Mesopotamia began pressing marks into clay tablets.
[barley] Early tablets mostly recorded numbers, like amounts of barley owed or delivered.
[name_kushim] One of the first names in history may be Kushim, who signed such a record.
=== Archives Build Empires ===
[shelves] Writing let rulers store information outside human heads.
[scribes] Clerks, archives and filing systems grew, letting imagined orders stretch across entire empires.
[census] Strangers who would never meet could now be taxed, judged and ranked by the same rules.
=== Your Turn ===
[mirror] Pick one rule you follow today, at work or in law, and ask what keeps it real.
Is it nature itself, or millions of people, including you, choosing to believe together?
