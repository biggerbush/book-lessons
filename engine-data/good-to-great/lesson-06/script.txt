=== Title ===
[title_card] What if rules and red tape are a sign something is already broken?
Great companies need fewer rules, not more.
=== The bureaucracy trap ===
[startup] Picture a young company bursting with energy and ideas.
[crowd] As it grows, more people join, and some of them are the wrong fit.
[rulebook] So leaders add rules, forms, and approvals to keep everyone in line.
Collins argues bureaucracy mostly exists to make up for incompetence and a lack of discipline.
=== Freedom in a frame ===
[frame] The great companies built a clear framework, and gave people real freedom inside it.
[pilot] Collins compares this to an airline pilot.
The pilot follows a strict system, yet still holds final responsibility for the plane.
When you have self-disciplined people, you need far fewer controls.
=== Rinse the cottage cheese ===
[athlete] Collins tells of Dave Scott, a six-time Ironman triathlon champion.
[cheese] Scott rinsed his cottage cheese to wash off extra fat.
[bowl] It shows a mindset: doing every small thing, every day, to be the best.
Great companies had that same fanatical consistency.
=== The stop doing list ===
[todo] Most of us keep a long to-do list.
[stop_list] Collins says great companies also kept a stop doing list.
Discipline meant saying no to attractive opportunities that did not fit their focus.
[scissors] They cut projects, products, and habits that pulled them off course.
=== Not a tyrant ===
[boss] Discipline is not one harsh boss barking orders.
When a tyrant enforces discipline, it often collapses after they leave.
[team] Lasting discipline lives inside the people and the culture.
=== Tech as accelerator ===
[gadget] What about technology?
Collins found it was never the main cause of a company's leap.
[network] Walgreens invested heavily in a computer network linking its pharmacies.
It worked because it served their focus on convenient drugstores.
[rocket] Technology sped up momentum that was already there.
=== Crawl, walk, run ===
[web] During the internet frenzy, many rivals rushed online.
[steps] Walgreens chose a slower path, which its leaders called crawl, walk, run.
They tested first, then moved fast once they understood how the web fit.
=== Your challenge ===
[pen] Write your own stop doing list today.
Name one thing you will quit, so your best effort goes where it matters.
