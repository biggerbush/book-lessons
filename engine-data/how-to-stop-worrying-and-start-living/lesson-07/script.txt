[title_card] What if the fastest cure for your worries was hidden inside someone else's problem?
Today we explore the final habit, and maybe the most surprising one.
=== The Inward Spiral ===
[worried_mind] Worry grows best in a closed room.
The longer we stare at our own troubles, the bigger and darker they seem to get.
[spotlight] Carnegie noticed that anxious people usually keep a bright spotlight pointed squarely at themselves.
His remedy was simple: swing that spotlight around and point it at other people.
=== Adler's Prescription ===
[adler] To make his case, Carnegie turns to Alfred Adler, a famous Austrian psychiatrist.
[prescription] Adler told gloomy patients they could feel better in fourteen days by following one daily rule.
Each morning, think about how you could bring some pleasure to someone else.
It sounds almost too easy, yet Adler believed it worked.
=== Margaret's Bed ===
[margaret] Carnegie shares the story of Margaret Tayler Yates, the wife of a naval officer stationed in Hawaii.
[bed] Because of heart trouble, she spent most of every day lying in bed.
Her days shrank down to her own aches and fears.
=== The Phone Rings ===
[explosions] Then came the attack on Pearl Harbor.
[telephone] A phone was set beside her bed, and she became a contact point for frightened military families.
She took their calls and passed along news about where their loved ones were.
=== Out of Bed ===
[comfort] Some callers had just lost their husbands, so she comforted them as best she could.
She became so busy caring for strangers that she simply forgot about herself.
[sunrise] Soon she was spending far more of her time out of bed than in it.
=== Why It Works ===
[gears] Helping others quietly pulls together everything Carnegie teaches.
[clock] A kind act keeps you focused on today, the only day you can actually live.
[busy_hands] It fills your hands and your thoughts, leaving little room for brooding.
[scale] And seeing other people's struggles makes your own blessings easier to count.
=== Small Is Enough ===
You do not need a crisis to try this.
[thank_you] Picture a short thank you note, a sincere compliment, or a real question about someone's day.
[ripple] Each small kindness sends ripples outward, and some of that calm flows straight back to you.
=== Your Challenge ===
[calendar] For the next fourteen days, start each morning by asking who you could make a little happier.
Then go do it, and watch what happens to your worries.

[worried_head] What if the quickest way to quiet your worries is to stop thinking about yourself for a while?
=== The Inward Spiral ===
[spiral] Worry loves an audience of one.
When we brood, our attention circles back to our own fears, again and again.
[magnifier] Under that constant inward gaze, every small problem starts to look enormous.
Carnegie's answer is surprisingly simple: turn your attention outward, toward other people.
=== A Doctor's Advice ===
[adler] Carnegie draws on the work of the psychiatrist Alfred Adler.
Adler believed that people who care only about themselves run into the most trouble in life.
[prescription] He told gloomy patients they could feel better within fourteen days by following one habit.
His advice, as Carnegie reports it: "Try to think every day how you can please someone."
=== A Morning in Hawaii ===
[bed] Carnegie tells the story of Margaret Tayler Yates, who spent most of her days in bed with heart trouble.
[bombs] Then came the morning of the Pearl Harbor attack, with bombs falling near her home.
[telephone] She had a phone placed beside her bed and began sharing news with frightened military families.
=== Forgetting Herself ===
[clock] Hour after hour, she answered calls, passed on information, and calmed anxious wives.
[standing] She became so absorbed in helping others that she forgot about her own illness.
After that day, she spent far less time in bed and far more time living.
=== Kindness Every Day ===
You do not need a crisis to use this idea.
[mail_carrier] Carnegie suggests taking a real interest in the people you meet each day, like the mail carrier or a shop clerk.
[smile] Ask about their day, their family, or their work, and actually listen.
Each small kindness gently pulls your mind away from your own troubles.
=== Why It Works ===
[scale] Your mind can only hold so much at one time.
When you fill it with someone else's needs, there is simply less room left for worry.
[thank_card] And kindness tends to come back to you as warmth, gratitude, and connection.
=== Tying It Together ===
[puzzle] This habit quietly connects many of Carnegie's other ideas.
Helping someone keeps you busy, puts your own problems in proportion, and reminds you how much you have.
[sunrise] Worry shrinks when your life starts pointing outward instead of inward.
=== Your Challenge ===
