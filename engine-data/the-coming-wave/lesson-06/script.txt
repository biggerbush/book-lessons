=== Title ===
[title_fork] Picture a road that splits in two, and both directions lead somewhere frightening.
This is the dilemma at the heart of The Coming Wave.
=== Two Bad Roads ===
[left_road] Down one road, powerful technology spreads with no real controls.
[right_road] Down the other, governments grab total control to stop it.
[traveler] Suleyman argues we are drifting toward one or the other, and neither is safe.
=== The Catastrophe Road ===
[dna_printer] Start with the first road, where tools like DNA synthesis become cheap and everywhere.
[small_group] A tiny group, or even one person, could one day design a dangerous pathogen.
[world_map] Suleyman warns such an outbreak could spread worldwide, far faster than anyone could respond.
=== Accidents Happen Too ===
[lab_building] It is not only villains we should worry about.
The book points out that leaks have happened even from high security labs.
[warning_sign] With more labs and more powerful tools, the odds of a serious mistake keep rising.
=== The Urge to Clamp Down ===
[frightened] Faced with risks like these, a frightened public will demand protection.
[control_room] The obvious answer for a state is to watch everything, everywhere, all the time.
[camera_wall] Every lab, every message and every purchase could be monitored by AI.
=== The Dystopia Road ===
[china_city] Suleyman points to China as an early glimpse of how this could look.
[face_scanner] There, facial recognition and data tracking already help the state watch and control its citizens.
[prison_bars] Taken further, the price of safety would be our freedom.
=== Why Not Just Stop ===
[pause_button] So why not simply halt the technology?
[empty_shelf] Suleyman says that brings its own disaster, which he calls stagnation.
[globe_heat] Without new tools, problems like climate change, disease and ageing populations grow harder to solve.
=== Holding Both Fears ===
[balance_scale] The point is not to pick a side, but to keep both dangers in view.
[two_flags] Whenever you hear a call to unleash technology, ask what could go wrong.
Whenever you hear a call to lock it down, ask what freedoms it would cost.
=== Your Turn ===
Think of one new technology you use every day.
What would too little control look like, and what would too much look like?
