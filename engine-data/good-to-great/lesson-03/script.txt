=== Who Before What ===
[title_bus] Most leaders pick a destination first, then look for people to get them there.
The great companies did it the other way around.
=== The Bus ===
[bus] Picture your organization as a bus, and you as the driver.
[right_seats] Before choosing a route, great leaders filled the seats with the right people.
[exit_door] They also helped the wrong people step off.
Only then did they decide where to drive.
=== Why People First ===
[road_fork] Markets shift, and the road you planned today may close tomorrow.
[passengers] If people joined only for the destination, they will balk when you change direction.
[self_starter] If they joined for who else is aboard, they adapt and push themselves without heavy supervision.
=== Wells Fargo Bets ===
[wells_bank] In the 1970s, Wells Fargo leader Dick Cooley sensed that banking would soon change dramatically.
[crystal_ball] He could not predict exactly how.
[talent_pool] So he hired outstanding people whenever he found them, often without a specific job in mind.
=== The Payoff ===
[storm_cloud] When banking deregulation arrived in the 1980s, many banks struggled to adjust.
[ready_team] Wells Fargo already had a deep bench of capable people ready to respond.
[trend_arrow] That team helped it adapt faster than rivals like Bank of America.
=== Rigorous Not Ruthless ===
[scales] This is not about firing people casually.
Collins calls it being rigorous, not ruthless.
[high_bar] Rigor means holding a high standard for every seat, starting with the top team.
[clear_choice] It also means not letting a poor fit linger for years, which is unfair to everyone involved.
=== Hire With Care ===
[rule_book] The book offers practical rules for this.
[magnify] First, when in doubt, do not hire.
Keep looking until you find the right person.
[clock] Second, when you know a people change is needed, act instead of waiting and hoping.
=== Best on Best ===
[spotlight] Third, put your strongest people on your biggest opportunities, not your biggest problems.
[problem_box] Fixing problems can only make you good.
[rocket] Building opportunities is what makes you great.
=== Your Turn ===
[mirror] Look at your team, and ask the question Collins suggests.
If this were a hiring decision today, would you hire each person again?
