=== Passion Is Developed ===
[lightning] Many of us wait for a lightning bolt to reveal our one true passion.
Angela Duckworth says that bolt almost never strikes.
=== The Spark Myth ===
[graduate] Graduates often hear one big piece of advice: follow your passion.
[question_mark] But that assumes your passion is already out there, fully formed, just waiting to be found.
[seedling] Duckworth's research suggests interest works more like a seedling that needs time and care.
=== A Late Bloomer ===
[julia_child] Consider the famous cook Julia Child, as described in the book.
[french_meal] She did not discover her love of French cooking until her late thirties, while living in France.
[cookbook] Years of learning and hard work then turned that first delight into a lifelong calling.
=== Discovery Comes First ===
[playground] Interest usually begins with relaxed exploration, trying things simply because they seem fun.
[sampler] At this stage, you are sampling, not committing.
[magnifier] Nobody can tell you in advance what will grab you, so curiosity has to lead.
=== Then Development ===
[calendar] Once something catches your attention, it needs to be sparked again and again.
[mentor] Encouraging people around you, like teachers, coaches or friends, help keep that early interest alive.
[growth_chart] Slowly, a passing fancy grows into a genuine, durable interest.
=== Novelty Versus Nuance ===
[shiny_toy] Beginners are drawn to novelty, the thrill of something brand new.
[layers] Experts stay interested by noticing nuance, the fine details that beginners miss.
[puzzle] It is the same field, but they keep finding fresh puzzles hidden inside it.
=== Deepening for Life ===
[tree] Over the years, interest deepens like roots spreading underground.
[books_stack] Gritty people keep asking new questions about the same subject.
That is why their passion lasts while other people drift away out of boredom.
=== Start Exploring ===
[notebook] So try asking yourself a few simple questions.
[thought_bubble] What do you enjoy thinking about?
Where does your mind wander when it is free?
[sign_up] Then pick one interest and take a small, playful step to explore it this week.
=== Your Turn ===
What curiosity have you dismissed because it did not feel like a grand passion yet?
What might happen if you gave it time to grow?
