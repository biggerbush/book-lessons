=== Your Motivation Software ===
[laptop] What if the way we motivate people is running on software that's decades out of date?
Daniel Pink thinks it is, and he wants us to upgrade.
=== Motivation 1.0 ===
[os_screen] Pink imagines society as a computer, running an operating system for motivation.
[campfire] The first version, Motivation 1.0, was simple: find food, stay safe, and reproduce.
=== Motivation 2.0 ===
[village] As people built complex societies, survival alone wasn't enough to keep things running.
[carrot_stick] So we upgraded to Motivation 2.0: reward the behavior you want, punish the behavior you don't.
[factory] In the early 1900s, Frederick Winslow Taylor brought this thinking into factories, paying workers to follow precise steps.
=== Why It Worked ===
[assembly_line] For simple, repetitive work, this system did its job remarkably well.
[widgets] Pay more for more widgets, and you got more widgets.
[time_clock] For most of the last century, workplaces were built entirely around it.
=== Two Encyclopedias ===
Pink offers a thought experiment.
[calendar_1995] Imagine it's the mid 1990s, and two new encyclopedias are launching.
[paid_team] One is backed by Microsoft, with paid writers, editors, and managers.
[volunteers] The other is written for free by volunteers, who contribute simply because they enjoy it.
Which one would you bet on?
=== The Surprise ===
[wiki_globe] Yet the volunteer project, Wikipedia, grew into one of the largest and most popular encyclopedias in the world.
[closed_sign] In 2009, Microsoft shut down its encyclopedia, Encarta.
Motivation 2.0 simply can't explain that outcome.
=== Bugs in the System ===
[open_code] Pink sees the same pattern in open source software, much of it built by programmers who aren't paid for it.
[warning_icon] He calls these compatibility problems.
People aren't behaving the way the old system predicts.
[heart_badge] They're driven by challenge, enjoyment, and the desire to contribute.
=== Motivation 3.0 ===
[upgrade_bar] So Pink argues it's time for Motivation 3.0.
It assumes people want to direct their own work and find it meaningful.
[inner_spark] Rewards still matter, but the deepest fuel comes from inside us.
And as more work becomes creative and complex, that inner fuel matters more than ever.
=== Your Upgrade ===
This week, notice one task you do only for the reward.
Ask yourself what would change if you did it because it mattered to you.
