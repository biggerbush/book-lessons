=== People First ===
[profit_stack] Most companies chase profits first, and treat people as an afterthought.
Ben Horowitz says that gets the order exactly backwards.
=== The Order ===
[three_blocks] He offers a simple rule for running a company.
In his words, "Take care of the people, the products, and the profits, in that order."
[domino_chain] Good people build good products.
Good products bring in the profits.
[wobbly_tower] Skip the first step, and everything after it wobbles.
=== What Good Looks Like ===
[office_desk] So what makes a company a good place to work?
For Horowitz, it is a place where people can simply focus on their work.
[green_arrow] They trust that if they do a great job, good things happen for the company and for them.
=== What Bad Looks Like ===
[tangled_maze] In a bad company, energy leaks away in every direction.
[turf_fence] People fight over turf, play politics, and wrestle with broken processes.
[blank_target] Worse, nobody is quite sure what good work even looks like.
=== Why It Matters ===
[sunny_sky] When things go well, being a good company hardly seems to matter.
People stay for the growth, the money and the excitement.
[storm_cloud] But every company eventually hits hard times.
[lone_worker] Then the excitement fades, and the job itself is the only reason left to stay.
=== Train Your People ===
[classroom] That is why Horowitz pushes hard for real training.
[busy_calendar] Busy startups often skip it, claiming they have no time.
[stopwatch] But untrained people guess, stumble, and quietly waste far more time.
=== A Real Example ===
[guide_booklet] At Netscape, Horowitz co-wrote a short guide called Good Product Manager, Bad Product Manager.
[two_columns] It contrasted, point by point, what strong and weak product managers actually do.
Suddenly everyone shared one clear picture of the job.
[fair_scales] And with a clear standard, managers could judge performance fairly and coach people toward it.
=== Two Kinds of Training ===
[two_paths] Horowitz suggests two kinds of training.
[skill_tools] One teaches the specific skills each job requires.
[manager_coach] The other teaches managers how to lead their teams well.
=== Your Move ===
[notepad] Pick one role on your team and write down what great looks like.
[teach_board] Then turn it into a short lesson, and teach it this week.
