=== The Coin Flip ===
[coin] I'll flip a coin.
Tails, you lose 100 dollars.
Heads, you win 150.
Do you take the bet?
=== Most People Refuse ===
[crowd] Most people refuse, even though the odds favor them.
[scale] The fear of losing 100 outweighs the hope of winning 150.
[question_mark] So Kahneman asks: how much would you need to win to accept the risk?
For many people, the answer is about 200 dollars, roughly double the loss.
=== Loss Aversion ===
[two_faces] This is loss aversion.
A loss feels about twice as painful as an equal gain feels good.
[quote_card] In Kahneman's words, "losses loom larger than gains."
=== Jack and Jill ===
[jack] Now meet two people from the book, Jack and Jill.
[jill] Today, each of them has 5 million dollars.
[yesterday] But yesterday, Jack had 1 million and Jill had 9 million.
Are they equally happy?
Not at all.
Jack feels lucky, while Jill feels crushed.
=== The Reference Point ===
[ref_line] This is the heart of prospect theory, developed by Kahneman and Amos Tversky.
We don't judge outcomes by where we end up.
[arrows] We judge them as gains or losses from a reference point, usually where we started.
Same 5 million, opposite feelings.
=== Feelings Flatten Out ===
[small_step] The theory adds one more twist.
Going from 100 to 200 dollars feels like a big jump.
[big_step] Going from 900 to 1000 feels smaller, though it's the same 100 dollars.
[curve] Our feelings flatten as amounts grow, for gains and losses alike.
=== Why It Holds Us Back ===
[shield] Loss aversion makes evolutionary sense, since creatures that treated threats as urgent survived better.
[stuck] But today it can keep us stuck.
We turn down good bets, cling to what we have, and avoid helpful changes.
=== Think in Bundles ===
[stack] Kahneman's remedy is to see each risk as one of many.
[many_coins] Accept a hundred bets like that coin flip, and you will almost surely come out ahead.
[mantra] He suggests a simple reminder: you win a few, you lose a few.
=== Your Turn ===
[your_risk] Think of one small, favorable risk you avoided because losing felt too big.
Would you take it if it were one of a hundred?
