=== Title ===
[megaphone] Picture a brand shouting into a crowded room where everyone is busy chatting.
Nobody turns around, and on Twitter, that is exactly what broadcasting looks like.
=== A Big Party ===
[party_room] Gary Vaynerchuk treats Twitter like a giant, noisy party happening in real time.
[small_groups] People gather in little circles, talking about news, games, shows and everyday frustrations.
[loud_guest] A guest who only talks about himself gets ignored, no matter how loudly he speaks.
=== Stop Broadcasting ===
[notice_board] Many brands use Twitter like a notice board, pinning up sales and press releases.
[silent_feed] Those posts sink quickly and collect silence, because nobody asked for them.
Gary argues the real power of Twitter is not posting more, it is listening and replying.
=== Listen First ===
[search_bar] Twitter search lets you hear what people are saying about almost anything, right now.
[keyword_tags] Type in your product, your industry or your town, and live conversations appear.
[speech_bubble] Each one is an open door to say something helpful, funny or kind.
=== Replies Are Jabs ===
[reply_arrow] A thoughtful reply is a jab, a small gift of attention that asks for nothing back.
[smiling_user] When a brand answers a real person, that person feels noticed and remembers the moment.
[thread_chain] Repeat this often, and a relationship slowly forms, one short exchange at a time.
=== Ride the Moment ===
[trend_list] Gary also encourages joining trending topics when your brand genuinely belongs in the conversation.
[hashtag_sign] A hashtag shows you where the crowd is already gathered and what it cares about.
The trick is adding something fresh, not just slapping your logo on someone else's moment.
=== The Oreo Blackout ===
[dark_stadium] During the 2013 Super Bowl, the stadium lights suddenly went out mid-game.
[oreo_tweet] Oreo quickly tweeted a cookie in shadow with a playful line about dunking in the dark.
[retweet_wave] It spread far because it joined a moment millions of people were already talking about.
=== Fast and Fitting ===
[stopwatch] On Twitter, speed matters, because a conversation that is hot now is cold tomorrow.
[thumbs_check] But fit matters too, so skip topics that are tragic, divisive or simply not yours.
Being quick, relevant and human beats being polished and late.
=== Your Turn ===
[phone_search] Today, search Twitter for one word tied to your work and reply helpfully to three strangers.
What did you learn just by listening first?
