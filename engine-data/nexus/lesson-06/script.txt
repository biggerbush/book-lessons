=== Two Countries ===
[stage] Imagine two countries with the same people, the same land, and the same problems.
The only difference is how news flows inside each one.
=== Two Networks ===
[two_maps] Harari says democracies and dictatorships are best understood as two different shapes of information network.
[hub] A dictatorship is a hub, where every message flows to one center.
[web] A democracy is a web, with many centers talking to each other.
=== The Hub ===
[palace] In a dictatorship, reports travel up to the ruler, and orders travel back down.
[gate] The center decides what is true and who gets to hear it.
This can move fast, but it rests on one risky assumption: the center is never wrong.
=== The Web ===
[newspaper] In a democracy, much information never passes through the government at all.
[courthouse] Newspapers, courts, universities, businesses and citizens gather and judge facts on their own.
It is slower and noisier, but no single point controls the whole picture.
=== The Applause ===
[hall] Harari retells a story about a Soviet party meeting under Stalin.
[clapping] When Stalin's name was mentioned, the whole hall stood and clapped, and nobody dared to stop first.
[director] Finally a factory director sat down, and the others followed.
That night he was arrested.
=== Silence at the Top ===
[warning] The lesson is not really about clapping.
It is about what people learn to hide.
[ruler_alone] When honesty is dangerous, bad news stops travelling to the center.
[famine] Harari shows how Stalin's farm collectivization went disastrously wrong, yet nobody could safely say so.
The errors grew into a famine that killed millions.
=== More Than Voting ===
[ballot] Harari warns that democracy is not simply majority rule.
[strongman] An elected leader who silences courts and journalists can turn a web back into a hub.
[shield] The independent channels are what let a society notice mistakes, including the majority's own.
=== Noise Is a Feature ===
[argument] All that public arguing can look like chaos.
[repair] But in Harari's view, the noise is the self-correcting system at work, finding errors before they spread.
