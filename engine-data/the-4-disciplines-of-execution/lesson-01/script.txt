=== Quiet Endings ===
[title_storm] Most great strategies are not killed by a bad idea.
They simply fade away while everyone is busy.
=== The Big Launch ===
[leader] Picture a leader announcing an exciting new goal to her team.
[slides] There are slides, a kickoff meeting, and real enthusiasm in the room.
[calendar] Then Monday arrives, and the team goes back to work.
=== Then Silence ===
[inbox] Emails pile up, customers call, and small problems need fixing right now.
Everyone stays busy, yet the new goal goes nowhere.
Nobody decides to abandon it.
[fading_goal] It just gets a little quieter each week, until no one mentions it at all.
=== Meet the Whirlwind ===
The authors have a name for what happened here.
[whirlwind] They call it the whirlwind.
[gears] It is all the urgent work needed just to keep the day running.
It eats the time and energy you meant to spend on something new.
=== Urgent Beats Important ===
[urgent_bell] Urgent tasks shout for attention and demand action immediately.
[seedling] Your important new goal is quiet, so it can always wait until tomorrow.
Urgency acts on you, while importance waits for you to act.
In a head to head fight, the authors say, the whirlwind wins almost every time.
=== Not the Enemy ===
[shop_front] The whirlwind is not bad work, though.
It keeps customers served and the lights on.
[balance] You cannot ignore it, so the real challenge is executing your goal in the middle of it.
=== Two Kinds of Goals ===
[pen] The book separates strategies you can complete with the stroke of a pen, like approving a purchase.
Those get done, because money or authority makes them happen.
[crowd] Harder strategies need many people to change their daily behaviour.
Those are exactly the ones the whirlwind swallows.
=== Wrong Diagnosis ===
[blame_finger] When a goal stalls, leaders often blame lazy people or a weak plan.
The authors argue the real culprit is usually the whirlwind.
Usually, people are simply drowning in urgent work.
[magnifier] Once you can see it, you can start protecting your goal from it.
=== Your Challenge ===
[stopwatch] This week, notice how much of each day the whirlwind takes.
Then ask yourself which important goal it is quietly crushing.
