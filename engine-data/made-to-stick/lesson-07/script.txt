=== Stories ===
[campfire] Facts tell people what to do.
Stories let them rehearse doing it.
=== Why Stories Work ===
[listener] When you hear a good story, you do not just collect information.
[mind_movie] You run it in your head, like a movie you are inside.
You feel the choices and the stakes as if they were yours.
=== Flight Simulator ===
[pilot] Pilots train in flight simulators before they ever fly a real plane.
[cockpit] The Heaths argue that stories do the same job for the mind.
They let us practise a situation before we face it, so we know how to act.
=== The Study Test ===
[students] The book describes a study where students prepared for an exam by imagining different things.
[grade_a] Some pictured the result, a high grade on their paper.
[desk_study] Others pictured the process, sitting down and studying step by step.
The process group studied more and did better.
Rehearsing the path beats dreaming of the finish line.
=== A Springboard Story ===
[manager] At the World Bank, a manager named Stephen Denning struggled to sell colleagues on sharing knowledge.
[charts] Charts and arguments got him nowhere.
[health_worker] So he told a short story about a health worker in a small town in Zambia.
[laptop] In 1995, this health worker went online and found an answer about treating malaria.
=== Why It Landed ===
[light_bulb] Suddenly his listeners could picture what the bank might become.
They began imagining their own versions of the story.
Denning called these springboard stories, because they launch people toward new ideas.
=== All Six at Once ===
[checklist] Now hold that story up against the SUCCESs checklist.
The core is simple: sharing knowledge can save lives.
[surprise] It is unexpected, a remote health worker finding help online.
It is concrete, credible and emotional, with a real person and real patients.
And because it is a story, all of it arrives together.
=== Spot, Don't Invent ===
[notebook] The Heaths say you rarely need to invent a great story.
[magnifier] The best ones are often already happening around you.
Your job is to notice them, collect them, and retell them.
=== Your Challenge ===
[story_card] Think of one real moment that shows your idea working.
This week, tell that story instead of explaining the idea.
