=== Grit Has Helpers ===
[title_kid] What if grit isn't only something you dig up inside yourself?
What if the people and places around you can grow it too?
=== Warm and Firm ===
[parent] Duckworth says the parents who build the most grit do two things at once.
[heart] They are warm and supportive.
[bar_high] And they also set high expectations and clear limits.
Psychologists call this authoritative parenting, and Duckworth calls it wise parenting.
=== Four Kinds of Parents ===
[grid] Picture a simple grid with support on one side and demands on the other.
[low_corner] Low on both is neglectful.
Demanding without warmth is authoritarian, and warmth without demands is permissive.
[wise_corner] Only the corner with plenty of both reliably helps kids grow persistent.
=== Playing Fields ===
[ball] Duckworth points to extracurricular activities as training grounds for grit.
[coach] Sports, music, debate and the like come with a demanding coach and a clear goal.
They also come with encouragement from teammates and adults.
=== Sticking With It ===
[clipboard] Duckworth describes a study of students that tracked what they did outside class.
[calendar] Students who stuck with an activity for more than one year, and kept advancing, did especially well later.
Researchers called this quality follow-through.
=== The Hard Thing Rule ===
[family] In Duckworth's own home, there is a family rule called the Hard Thing Rule.
[violin] First, everyone, parents included, must do something hard that takes daily deliberate practice.
[stop_sign] Second, you can quit, but only at a natural stopping point, like the end of a season.
=== Your Choice ===
[menu] Third, you choose your own hard thing, because nobody picks it for you.
[hourglass] Later, a fourth rule kicks in for high school.
Each child must commit to one activity for at least two years.
Choice keeps interest alive, while commitment builds the habit of finishing.
=== Join Gritty Groups ===
[team] Culture works the same way on adults.
[flag] When you join a group where everyone works hard and keeps going, you tend to adopt its standards.
Persisting becomes simply what people like us do.
[mirror] Over time, the group's grit becomes your own.
=== Your Hard Thing ===
[pencil] This week, pick one hard thing for yourself and decide when your natural stopping point will be.
Then invite someone you love to choose one too.
