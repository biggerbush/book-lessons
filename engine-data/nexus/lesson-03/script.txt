=== Paper Realities ===
[title_card] Your nation and the money in your wallet both rest on something surprising.
They exist because millions of strangers share the same stories and paperwork.
=== Strangers Together ===
[chimp_band] Chimpanzees can cooperate, but only with a few dozen animals they know personally.
[crowd_city] Humans cooperate with millions of people they will never meet.
Harari says the secret is shared information that links us all together.
=== Three Kinds of Real ===
[rock] Some things are objectively real, like a rock that exists whether anyone notices it or not.
[headache] Some are subjectively real, like a headache that only you can feel.
[banknote] And some are intersubjective, real because many minds share the same story about them.
=== Nations and Money ===
[flag] A nation has no physical body you can touch.
[treaty] Yet it can sign treaties, wage wars and collect taxes.
A banknote is just paper, yet it buys bread because everyone believes it will.
If the shared story fades, these powerful realities fade with it.
=== The Power of a Story ===
[herzl] Harari points to Theodor Herzl, who imagined a Jewish state in a book.
[book_open] His story inspired people across many countries to work toward one goal.
A vivid tale can rally strangers faster than any order.
=== Enter the Documents ===
But a story alone cannot run a state.
Herzl's dream also needed bureaucrats, records and offices to become a real country.
[clay_tablet] In ancient Mesopotamia, people pressed records of loans and property into clay tablets.
[deed_owner] Ownership came to mean what the tablet said, not what neighbours remembered.
The document did not just describe reality, it created it.
=== Why Paper Feels Cold ===
[filing_cab] Harari notes we evolved to love stories, not filing cabinets.
That is why bureaucracy often feels confusing, cold and even sinister.
[sewer_pipe] Yet records quietly keep cities running, from tax systems to sewage pipes.
=== Your Move ===
[notebook] This week, notice one shared story you rely on, like a currency, a company or a country.
[form_sheet] Then spot the paperwork holding it up, such as contracts, passports or receipts.
Seeing both layers helps you understand how power really works around you.
=== Your Challenge ===
[question] If everyone suddenly stopped believing in money, what would your wallet be worth tomorrow?
