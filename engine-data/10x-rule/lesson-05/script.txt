=== Title ===
[race_track] Picture a race where you stop glancing sideways at the other runners.
What if your goal was to get so far ahead that there is no race at all?
=== The trap of competing ===
[two_shops] Most businesses watch their rivals very closely.
They match prices, copy ideas, and try to be a little better.
[mirror] Grant Cardone says this habit keeps you thinking small.
When you measure yourself against others, their limits quietly become your limits.
=== A blunt warning ===
[book_title] Cardone puts it bluntly, titling one chapter "Competition Is for Sissies."
His point is not to ignore the market.
[crown] It is to change your goal from keeping up to taking over.
Competing means sharing the pie.
Dominating means setting the pace everyone else must follow.
=== Do what others skip ===
[phone] Domination starts with doing the things your rivals skip.
Return every call.
Follow up after the sale.
Show up again when others give up.
[stack_tasks] Cardone argues these extra actions, repeated daily, create a gap competitors cannot close.
=== Expand in a storm ===
[storm_cloud] One example Cardone returns to is the economic downturn.
When times get hard, most companies cut back, stop marketing, and wait.
[open_door] Cardone describes doing the opposite in his own businesses.
He pushed harder while rivals retreated, and won customers they had left behind.
=== The tables turn ===
[spotlight] Once you dominate, the whole dynamic flips.
Customers come to you first because yours is the name they keep seeing.
[followers] Competitors start studying you, copying you, and reacting to your moves.
You stop chasing.
You become the one being chased.
=== Pick one arena ===
[target_map] Domination does not mean beating everyone at everything.
Choose the market, territory, or skill where you want to own the space.
[magnifier] Then study it for one thing only, which is what your rivals refuse to do.
=== Your move ===
[notepad] Write down three actions your competitors routinely skip.
Maybe it is a second follow up call, a faster reply, or a handwritten thank you note.
[calendar] Commit to doing all three every week, without waiting to see what anyone else does.
=== Your challenge ===
[question] Where in your work are you trying to keep up, when you could be pulling away?
[finish_line] Choose that place today, and start building a lead no one can close.
