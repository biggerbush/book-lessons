=== Eat and Sleep ===
[plate_and_bed] What if your dinner plate and your pillow mattered as much as your workouts?
Let's look at both, with purpose.
=== No Perfect Diet ===
[diet_signs] Attia is wary of anyone selling one perfect diet for everyone.
[two_people] Two people can eat the same meal and respond very differently.
No single plan wins for everyone.
So he treats food like an experiment, tested on your own body.
=== Three Levers ===
[lever_one] He sees three main levers for changing how you eat.
You can eat less overall, which is calorie restriction.
[lever_two] You can cut out certain foods, which is dietary restriction.
[lever_three] Or you can limit when you eat, which is time restriction.
The best mix depends on your body and your goals.
=== Measure, Not Guess ===
[glucose_patch] Attia has worn a continuous glucose monitor, a small sensor on the arm.
[spike_chart] It showed how different meals sent his blood sugar up or kept it steady.
That kind of feedback turns vague rules into personal choices.
=== Protein Protects ===
[muscle_arm] As we age, muscle quietly melts away unless we fight for it.
[scale_icon] Attia argues most older adults eat too little protein.
[protein_dose] He suggests about 1.6 grams per kilogram of body weight each day, as a starting point.
That is roughly double the standard official recommendation.
[meal_row] Spread across several meals, it helps your body build and repair muscle.
=== A Lesson Learned ===
[fasting_clock] Attia once relied heavily on long fasts.
[shrinking_arm] Over time, he concluded that fasting could cost him too much precious muscle.
So he changed course, putting protein and muscle first.
=== Sleep Is a Pillar ===
[surgeon] As a young surgeon in training, Attia treated sleep as optional.
He later saw how wrong that was.
[brain_clean] Poor sleep is linked to worse blood sugar control, weaker thinking, and higher Alzheimer's risk.
[sleeper] Good sleep supports nearly everything else on your health list.
=== Protect Your Night ===
[dark_room] Attia's advice is practical: a dark, cool bedroom.
[clock_steady] Keep a steady sleep and wake time.
[wine_glass] Limit alcohol, especially near bedtime, since it disrupts sleep.
[phone_off] Put screens away well before lights out.
Small habits like these add up night after night.
