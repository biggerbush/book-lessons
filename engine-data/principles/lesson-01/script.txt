=== The Bet ===
[title_card] In 1982, Ray Dalio staked his career on a prediction.
He was certain he was right, and he was completely wrong.
=== A Young Star ===
[young_dalio] Back then, Dalio was a young investor running a small investment firm called Bridgewater.
[debt_chart] He saw countries borrowing far more than they could ever pay back.
In 1982, Mexico defaulted on its debts, just as he had expected.
[check_mark] It felt like proof that his thinking was razor sharp.
=== Going Public ===
[tv_studio] So he went public, predicting on television that a depression was coming.
[capitol] He even testified before Congress, warning of an economic collapse.
[bet_chips] Then he placed his bets to match his confidence.
=== The Opposite ===
[fed_building] Instead, the Federal Reserve made money easier to borrow.
[rising_arrow] The stock market began one of its greatest rises in history.
His calls were not just wrong, they were the opposite of what happened.
=== Losing It All ===
[empty_desks] Bridgewater lost so much money that Dalio had to let every employee go.
[dad_loan] He was so broke he borrowed 4,000 dollars from his father to pay family bills.
His company was down to one person, himself.
=== The Real Mistake ===
[mirror] Looking back, Dalio saw that the problem was not a lack of intelligence.
It was arrogance.
[blinders] He had trusted his own view so completely that he never seriously tested it.
=== A New Question ===
[question_one] Before, he had asked himself a simple question, am I right?
[question_two] Now he asked a better one, how do I know I am right?
That small shift changed how he made every big decision afterward.
=== Testing Your View ===
[thinkers] He began seeking out the smartest people he could find who disagreed with him.
The goal was not to win the argument, but to understand their reasoning.
[shield] He also learned to place bets that would not ruin him if he turned out wrong.
=== Your Turn ===
[your_belief] Think of one belief you hold with total confidence right now.
Ask yourself, how do I know I am right, and who could show me I am wrong?
