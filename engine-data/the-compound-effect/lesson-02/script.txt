=== Who Is in Charge ===
[mirror] Who is really in charge of how your life turns out?
Darren Hardy says the answer is staring back at you in the mirror.
=== The Blame Game ===
[traffic_jam] It is easy to blame the traffic, the economy, or a difficult boss.
[shrug] Blame feels comfortable because it means nothing is our fault.
But it also quietly means nothing is in our power.
=== Sum of Your Choices ===
[fork_road] Hardy argues that your life today is the total of every choice you have made.
[calendar] What you ate, what you said, and how you spent each evening all count.
[stack] Those small decisions stacked up into the results you are living with right now.
=== One Hundred Percent ===
[scale] Many of us think responsibility is shared, fifty-fifty, like a fair deal.
[full_bar] Hardy says that is not enough.
You must take 100 percent responsibility for your results.
Even when someone else is partly to blame, your response is still yours.
=== A Marriage Experiment ===
[couple] Hardy applied this idea to his own marriage.
[journal] Rather than waiting for his wife to change, he started a journal about her.
[pen] Every day for a year, he wrote down something she did that he appreciated.
=== What Changed ===
[magnifier] Searching for her good qualities trained him to notice them everywhere.
[heart] His attitude softened, and he began treating her with more warmth.
[gift_box] When he finally gave her the finished journal, she was deeply moved.
He changed the relationship by changing only his own part.
=== Making Your Own Luck ===
[clover] Hardy also rejects the idea that successful people are simply lucky.
[equation] He sees luck as preparation, plus attitude, plus opportunity, plus action.
[gears] Only opportunity comes from outside you.
The other three pieces are your choices.
=== Take It Back ===
[notepad] Pick one result in your life that you are unhappy with.
[pointing] Ask yourself honestly which of your choices helped create it.
[key] Then name one choice you can make differently today.
=== Your Challenge ===
[sunrise] For the next week, catch every excuse before it leaves your mouth.
Replace it with one simple question: what can I do about this?
