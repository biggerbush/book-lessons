=== Loud Equals Good? ===
[title_card] Somewhere along the way, being loud started to look like being good.
This lesson asks how that happened, and whether it should have.
=== The Extrovert Ideal ===
[bold_figure] Picture the person our culture admires most.
They speak up fast, love a crowd, and look at ease in the spotlight.
[quiet_figure] Susan Cain calls this the Extrovert Ideal, and the quiet person beside them often gets overlooked.
It feels timeless, but it is surprisingly new.
=== A Farm Boy Dazzled ===
[farm_boy] Cain starts with Dale Carnegie, growing up poor on a Missouri farm around 1900.
[stage_speaker] He watched a travelling lecturer describe rising from nothing, and he was dazzled by the power of speech.
[debate_medal] He practised speaking, won debates in college, and later built a career teaching others to sell themselves.
=== Culture of Character ===
[old_town] Carnegie's story mirrors a bigger shift that the historian Warren Susman described.
In the 1800s, America mostly lived in what he called a Culture of Character.
[virtue_words] The ideal person was serious, disciplined and honourable, and what mattered was how you behaved in private.
[lincoln] Cain notes that Abraham Lincoln was admired for his modesty, not his showmanship.
=== A World of Strangers ===
[city_skyline] Then people poured from farms and small towns into growing cities and big companies.
[handshake] Suddenly you had to impress strangers quickly, in interviews, sales calls and first meetings.
Neighbours no longer knew your reputation, so your first impression had to do the work.
=== Personality Takes Over ===
[advice_book] Susman saw self-help books change their language to match.
[new_words] Old words like duty and honour gave way to words like magnetic, forceful and fascinating.
[mirror] The goal was no longer to be good, but to be impressive.
=== Still With Us ===
[school_desk] Cain argues this ideal still shapes how we judge people today.
[spotlight] We often mistake the fastest talker for the smartest person in the room.
That bias is cultural, a habit we learned, not a law of nature.
=== Try This ===
[notebook] Next time someone impresses you, ask whether it was their ideas or just their confidence.
[scale] And give the quiet voice a moment before you decide.
=== Your Challenge ===
[question_mark] Whose quiet strengths might you have overlooked this week, including your own?
