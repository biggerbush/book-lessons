=== Start With Why ===
[title_circles] Three circles, one inside the other, can change how you explain anything.
Most of us read them in the wrong order.
=== Three Rings ===
[outer_ring] Picture a target with three rings.
[middle_ring] The outer ring is labeled what, and the middle ring is labeled how.
[center_dot] At the very center sits a small dot labeled why.
Sinek calls this the Golden Circle.
=== What and How ===
[product_box] What is the easy part, the products you sell or the job you do.
Nearly every organization can describe its what in seconds.
[gears] How is the way you do it, the process or values that make you different.
Some organizations know their how.
=== The Missing Why ===
[question_mark] Why is your purpose, the belief that gets you out of bed.
[coin_stack] Sinek is firm that making money is not a why.
Profit is a result, not a reason.
Very few organizations can clearly say why they exist.
=== Outside In ===
[arrow_inward] Most companies talk from the outside in, starting at the outer ring.
[sales_pitch] Their pitch sounds like this: we make great computers, they look good and work easily, want one?
Notice it begins with the product and ends by asking for the sale.
It is accurate, but it leaves people cold.
=== Inside Out ===
[arrow_outward] Sinek shows Apple flipping the order.
[belief_banner] Apple starts with a belief: challenging the status quo and thinking differently.
[laptop] Then the how, products that are beautifully designed and simple, and only then the what, which happens to be computers.
=== Same Facts ===
[two_cards] Both messages hold the same facts.
Only the order changed.
[heart] Yet the second one gives people something to believe in, not just something to buy.
As Sinek puts it, "People don't buy what you do, they buy why you do it."
=== Draw Your Circle ===
[notebook] Try sketching your own three rings on paper.
[pen] Fill in what and how first, because those are easy.
[center_star] Then ask why you started, and what you believe, until the center feels true.
Say it out loud in one simple sentence.
=== Your Challenge ===
Next time you pitch an idea, open with your why.
Watch whether people lean in.
