=== The Daily Grind ===
[tired_runner] Practice can feel like a treadmill that never ends.
What if the secret to growing faster is having more fun, and sometimes going backward?
=== Grind Wears Us Out ===
[practice_room] Adam Grant points out that endless repetition often drains our motivation.
[empty_battery] When practice becomes pure duty, we burn out or simply get bored.
Either way, we stop improving long before our potential runs out.
=== Deliberate Play ===
[game_dice] His answer is something he calls deliberate play.
It means designing practice as a game, so building skills feels like fun.
[smiling_kid] You still work on specific skills, but the challenge itself pulls you forward.
=== The Futsal Example ===
[brazil_flag] Grant points to soccer in Brazil, where many young players grow up playing futsal.
[futsal_ball] Futsal uses a smaller, heavier ball on a tight court with fewer players.
[quick_feet] Every child touches the ball constantly and must invent clever moves in tiny spaces.
It feels like play, yet it sharpens skills that carry over to the full-size game.
=== Make Your Own Game ===
[score_card] You can do the same with almost any skill.
Add a playful rule, a time limit, or a friendly score.
[puzzle_piece] Turn a dull drill into a puzzle you actually want to solve.
=== When You Hit a Wall ===
[stone_wall] Even with play, progress eventually stalls.
Grant says the way forward is rarely a straight line.
[zigzag_path] Sometimes you need to zigzag, taking a detour that looks like a step back.
=== Tiger's Detour ===
[golf_club] Grant describes how Tiger Woods rebuilt his golf swing even after winning the Masters.
[dip_chart] For a while his results got worse, as he unlearned old habits.
[trophy] Then the new swing helped carry him to even greater heights.
=== Back to Go Forward ===
[mountain] Picture a hiker on a small peak who must climb down to reach a taller one.
[guide_figure] Grant also suggests asking someone who has traveled the route before you.
Their view of the map helps you see which detours are worth taking.
=== Your Turn ===
[question_mark] What skill could you turn into a game this week?
And where might one step back set you up for a bigger leap forward?
