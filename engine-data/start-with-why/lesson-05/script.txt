=== Title ===
[title_card] Knowing your why is only the start.
Here is what makes people actually believe it.
=== Three Jobs ===
[three_rings] Picture the Golden Circle as three rings, each with its own job.
[magnifier] Sinek says the why needs clarity, the how needs discipline, and the what needs consistency.
[handshake] When all three line up, people start to trust you.
=== Clarity of Why ===
[foggy_sign] A fuzzy purpose is like a sign lost in fog.
Nobody can follow it, including you.
[plane] Sinek points to Southwest Airlines, whose founder Herb Kelleher saw the airline as a champion of the common man.
That one clear idea told everyone what the company stood for.
=== Discipline of How ===
[three_icons] Southwest's hows followed directly: keep it cheap, keep it fun, keep it simple.
Those were not slogans on a wall.
[checklist] They guided real choices, and the company held to them even when other options looked tempting.
=== Verbs, Not Nouns ===
[sticky_note] Sinek suggests writing your values as verbs, not nouns.
[crossed_word] Integrity on a poster is hard to act on.
[action_arrow] Always do the right thing tells you exactly what to do on Monday morning.
=== Consistency of What ===
[product_shelf] Your what is everything people can see and touch.
[apple_logo] Sinek argues Apple's products, ads and stores all point back to the same belief about challenging the status quo.
Each one acts as proof of the why.
=== The Celery Test ===
[shopping_cart] Sinek offers a simple check he calls the celery test.
[advice_crowd] Imagine friends urging you to buy cookies, candy, rice milk and celery.
[celery] If your why is health, you buy only rice milk and celery.
Anyone glancing at your cart instantly knows what you believe.
=== Trust Builds ===
[bridge] Trust grows when what you say and what you do keep matching.
[broken_link] One choice that contradicts your why weakens that link.
Over time, that steady match is what turns customers into believers.
[calendar] So check one decision this week against your why, not just your budget.
=== Your Turn ===
[mirror] If someone looked at your shopping cart of daily choices, what would they say you believe?
Which one item does not belong?
