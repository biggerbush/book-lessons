=== Good Is the Enemy ===
[comfy_couch] What if the thing holding you back is not failure, but being pretty good?
That comfort might be the hardest trap of all to escape.
=== The Comfort Trap ===
[opening_line] Jim Collins opens his book with a famous line: "Good is the enemy of great."
[school_sign] He notes that we rarely have great schools, mainly because we already have good ones.
[ladder] When things feel fine, there is little pressure to climb any higher.
So most companies, and most people, simply settle.
=== The Big Question ===
[question_mark] Collins wanted to know whether a merely good company could ever become truly great.
[research_team] He and his research team spent years digging into that question.
[file_stack] They screened more than 1,400 large American companies.
=== A Strict Test ===
[stock_chart] To qualify, a company first needed 15 years of fairly average results.
Then came a turning point.
[triple_line] After it, returns had to beat the market at least three times over, for another 15 years.
[eleven_stars] Only 11 companies passed the test.
=== Matching Pairs ===
[twin_figures] For each winner, the team found a similar company in the same industry that failed to leap.
[magnifier] Comparing each pair closely revealed what truly made the difference.
=== The Drugstore Story ===
[drugstore] One of the 11 was Walgreens, a familiar and rather unglamorous drugstore chain.
For decades, its stock had roughly tracked the market.
Then, from 1975 to 2000, it beat the market more than 15 times over.
[trophy] That outpaced famous giants like General Electric and Coca-Cola.
[rival_store] Its comparison company, the rival drugstore chain Eckerd, never made the same leap.
=== Ordinary Beginnings ===
[steel_beam] Others were just as ordinary, like Nucor, a company in the tough, unexciting steel business.
[no_spotlight] None of them started out as a dazzling star.
If a drugstore and a steel maker could do it, the leap is open to ordinary organisations too.
=== A Matter of Choice ===
[crossroads] Collins concluded that greatness is not mainly a product of circumstance.
It is largely a matter of conscious choice.
[map] The coming lessons explore the patterns these companies shared.
=== Your Challenge ===
[mirror] Where in your life or work have you settled for good enough?
Pick one area this week, and write down what great would actually look like.
