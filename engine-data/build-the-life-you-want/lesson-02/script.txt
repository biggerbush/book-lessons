=== A Diet for Joy ===
[plate] What if happiness worked like a healthy meal?
Skip one ingredient, and you will feel hungry no matter how much you eat.
=== Three Macronutrients ===
[three_bowls] Arthur Brooks and Oprah Winfrey say happiness has three macronutrients.
[labels] They are enjoyment, satisfaction, and purpose.
Like protein, carbs, and fat, you need all three in balance.
=== Enjoyment Is Shared ===
[lone_snack] Enjoyment is not the same as pleasure.
Pleasure is a quick animal reward, like munching chips alone in front of a screen.
[shared_meal] Enjoyment takes that pleasure and adds people and memory.
Picture the same food, shared at a table with friends, then remembered fondly for years.
=== Satisfaction Takes Work ===
[mountain] Satisfaction is the glow you feel after struggling toward something and getting it.
[climber] Think of finishing a hard project, or reaching the top after a long climb.
No effort, no satisfaction.
=== Why It Fades ===
[treadmill] The catch is that satisfaction does not last.
You hit the goal, feel great, and soon the feeling slips away.
[new_goal] So you chase the next goal, and the next, like running on a treadmill.
=== The Happiness Fraction ===
[fraction] The authors offer a simple formula.
Satisfaction equals what you have, divided by what you want.
[haves_pile] Most of us try to raise the top number by piling up more stuff, money, and praise.
But the wants keep growing just as fast.
=== Shrink the Wants ===
[scissors] The smarter move is to shrink the bottom number.
When you want less, the same life suddenly feels like more.
[wants_list] That means noticing your cravings and deliberately letting some of them go.
Managing wants beats endlessly adding haves.
=== Purpose Holds It ===
[compass] Purpose is the third nutrient.
It is the feeling that your life makes sense and matters.
[hard_days] Purpose helps you carry pain and setbacks, because they fit into a larger story.
[balanced_plate] A happy life mixes all three, rather than gorging on just one.
=== Your Challenge ===
[pen_paper] This week, write down one thing you want that you could simply stop wanting.
Then watch what happens to your satisfaction.
