=== Rewire Your Habits ===
[cookie] What if a stubborn habit was just a puzzle you could solve in a few weeks?
Charles Duhigg tried it on his own afternoon cookie.
=== The Cookie Problem ===
[writer_desk] Duhigg noticed that almost every afternoon, he left his desk and headed to the cafeteria.
[choc_cookie] There he bought a chocolate chip cookie and ate it while chatting with colleagues.
[scale] The habit was making him gain weight, and his willpower kept losing.
=== Step 1: The Routine ===
[magnifier] So he treated the habit like a detective case.
[routine_path] First, he named the routine: get up, walk to the cafeteria, buy a cookie, chat.
[question_mark] The hard part was figuring out what started it and what he really wanted.
=== Step 2: Test Rewards ===
[reward_menu] Next, he ran little experiments with different rewards.
[apple] One day he went outside for a walk.
Another day he ate an apple instead.
[notepad] After each, he wrote down 3 words about how he felt.
[alarm_clock] Then he set an alarm for 15 minutes later and checked if he still wanted the cookie.
=== The Real Craving ===
[friends_chat] One day, he simply walked to a friend's desk and talked for a while.
[cookie_fade] Fifteen minutes later, the urge for a cookie was gone.
The reward he craved was not sugar.
It was a break and some company.
=== Step 3: Find the Cue ===
[five_icons] To find the trigger, he checked five things whenever the urge hit.
Where was he?
[clock_face] What time was it?
How did he feel?
Who was nearby?
What had he just done?
[time_330] The urge always struck at roughly the same time, around 3:30 in the afternoon.
=== Step 4: Make a Plan ===
[plan_card] Finally, he wrote a simple plan.
[daily_alarm] At 3:30 every day, he would walk to a friend's desk and talk for 10 minutes.
[new_loop] Within weeks, it became automatic, and he rarely thought about the cookie.
=== Your Toolkit ===
[toolbox] The method works on almost any habit.
Name the routine.
Test rewards to learn your real craving.
Spot the cue.
Then plan your new response.
Every habit you understand is a habit you can change.
