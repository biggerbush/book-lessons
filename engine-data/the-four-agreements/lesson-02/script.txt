=== The Courtroom Inside ===
[courtroom] What if there were a courtroom inside your head, open every hour of every day?
And what if you were always the one on trial?
=== The Book of Law ===
[law_book] Ruiz says everything we learned growing up gets written into an inner Book of Law.
[rule_lines] It holds the rules for how we should look, act and feel.
We never voted on these rules, but we obey them anyway.
=== The Judge ===
[judge] Ruiz says one part of the mind acts as a Judge.
[gavel] It checks everything we do and think against that Book of Law.
When we break a rule, the Judge finds us guilty.
=== The Victim ===
[victim] Another part of the mind plays the Victim.
It receives the verdict and carries the guilt, the blame and the shame.
[heavy_sack] Its quiet refrain is poor me, I am not good enough.
=== One Small Mistake ===
[friend] Picture yourself snapping at a friend after a long day.
[guilty_sign] The Judge declares you guilty, and the Victim feels the sting.
That could be the end of it.
=== Animals Pay Once ===
[dog] Ruiz points out that an animal makes a mistake and pays for it once.
Then it simply moves on.
[replay] Humans, he says, can pay a thousand times for the same mistake.
Every time we remember, the Judge reopens the case.
=== Paying Again ===
[partner] Ruiz notes that a partner or family member may remind us of the old mistake.
[coins] And we pay again, with fresh guilt, as if it just happened.
Ruiz calls this injustice, because true justice means paying once for each mistake.
=== The Harshest Critic ===
[mirror] Ruiz says no one abuses us more than we abuse ourselves.
[fence] Our self-abuse even sets the limit on how much mistreatment we accept from others.
If we treat ourselves harshly, we tolerate harshness from others too.
So quieting the Judge changes more than our inner mood.
=== Your Challenge ===
[closed_file] Next time an old mistake replays in your mind, ask yourself whether you have already paid for it.
If you have, close the case and let the Victim set down the weight.
