=== Two Words to Lead By ===
[two_words] What if two words could guide your hardest decisions at work?
Brené Brown argues they can, if you choose them carefully.
=== Too Many Values ===
[wall_poster] Most of us could list a dozen things we value.
[tangled_map] But a long list is no help when a tough choice arrives.
Brown's point is blunt: when everything is a core value, nothing really is.
=== Start With a List ===
[value_list] Brown offers a long list of values, from honesty and humor to faith and fairness.
[circled_words] First, you circle the ones that speak to you.
[two_circles] Then comes the hard part: you narrow them down to just two.
=== Three Test Questions ===
[question_one] To narrow down, ask of each value: does this define me?
[question_two] Is this who I am at my best?
[filter_icon] Is this a filter I use to make hard decisions?
Many of your other values usually turn out to fit under your top two.
=== Brown's Own Two ===
[brene_figure] Brown did this exercise herself.
[faith_courage] Her two core values are faith and courage.
[compass] She treats them as a compass, a quick check on whether a choice fits who she wants to be.
Two words are easy to remember when stress makes everything else blurry.
=== Values Become Actions ===
[action_cards] A value only matters when you can see it in behavior.
So for each value, Brown asks you to write down a few specific behaviors that support it.
[slippery_slope] Then name the slippery behaviors, the habits that pull you outside that value.
[memory_frame] Finally, recall a time when you fully lived into that value.
=== Use Them Under Pressure ===
[tense_meeting] Picture a meeting where you are tempted to stay quiet.
[scale] If courage is your value, the choice gets simpler: say the hard thing kindly.
[team_circle] Brown also encourages teams to share their two values, so colleagues know how to support each other in living them.
=== Your Challenge ===
[pen_paper] Today, circle ten values, then cut them down to two.
For each one, write a behavior you can practice tomorrow, and share them with someone who will remind you.
