=== Kind Worlds, Wicked ===
[game_board] Some places reward practice like a fair game.
[storm_cloud] Others quietly punish it, and they look exactly the same from the inside.
=== Naming the Two Worlds ===
[hogarth] Psychologist Robin Hogarth gave these places names.
[sun_sign] He called some learning environments kind.
[thorn_sign] He called others wicked.
The difference is all about rules and feedback.
=== Kind Worlds ===
[golf_course] Golf is a kind world.
The rules stay the same, and the course does not change its mind.
[golf_ball] You swing, the ball flies, and you see right away what happened.
[chess_board] Chess works the same way, with clear rules and quick, honest feedback.
In worlds like these, early practice and endless repetition really pay off.
=== Patterns That Repeat ===
[grandmaster] Chess masters seem to see a whole board in a glance.
[real_game] Show them a position from a real game, and they can recall it remarkably well.
[random_board] Scatter the pieces randomly, and their memory advantage mostly disappears.
Their skill is recognizing patterns that repeat, and kind worlds repeat constantly.
=== Wicked Worlds ===
[maze] A wicked world is different.
[shifting_rule] The rules are unclear or keep changing.
[delayed_clock] Feedback arrives late, arrives wrong, or never arrives at all.
Most of work and life, from medicine to business, looks more like this.
=== The Tongue Doctor ===
[doctor] Hogarth described an early twentieth century New York doctor famous for spotting typhoid.
[tongue] He felt each patient's tongue with his hands, then predicted illness.
[sick_patients] Again and again, his patients got typhoid, so he felt sure his method worked.
[germs] In truth, his unwashed hands were spreading the disease from patient to patient.
=== When Practice Misleads ===
That doctor got plenty of practice and plenty of feedback.
[wrong_arrow] But the feedback taught him exactly the wrong lesson.
[confident_face] In wicked worlds, repetition can build confidence without building skill.
Narrow experience makes the old patterns feel trustworthy, even when the situation has changed.
=== Know Your World ===
[question_mark] So before you double down on drills, ask which world you are in.
[checklist] Are the rules stable, and do you get fast, accurate feedback?
[wide_lens] If not, slow down, test your assumptions, and look beyond your usual cases.
=== Your Challenge ===
[mirror] Think of one decision you make often at work.
Is it golf, or is it a maze?
