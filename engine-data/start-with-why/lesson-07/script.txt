=== When Purpose Fades ===
[shrinking_tag] Success can quietly erase the very reason a company succeeded.
What happens when an organization forgets why it started?
=== A Small Store ===
[small_store] Sam Walton opened his first store in small town Arkansas.
[walton] He believed deeply in serving people, meaning his customers, his workers, and his communities.
[low_prices] Low prices were how he served them, not the point in themselves.
=== Growth Arrives ===
[store_map] Walmart spread across the country and became one of the largest companies in the world.
[walton_close] Walton stayed close to the business, so his purpose stayed visible to everyone.
His belief in people guided decisions, even as the company kept growing.
=== The Founder Leaves ===
[empty_chair] Then, in 1992, Sam Walton died.
[successors] The leaders who followed kept the stores, the systems, and the famous low prices.
But according to Sinek, the reason behind them began to fade.
=== Cheap Becomes Everything ===
[price_tag] Price slowly turned from a way of serving people into the whole goal.
[scale] The company chased cheaper and cheaper, often at the expense of the people Walton cared about.
[headlines] Sinek describes years of bad press and lawsuits over how employees were treated.
Some communities even fought to keep new stores out.
=== The Split ===
[split_line] Sinek calls this moment the split.
[what_arrow] What the company does keeps growing louder and bigger.
[why_dot] Meanwhile, why it does it grows quieter, until people inside barely remember.
Results become the purpose, and trust starts to crack.
=== Why It Happens ===
Growth itself is not the problem.
[founder_icon] In small organizations, the founder's purpose is felt every day.
[org_chart] As layers of managers appear, the founder gets farther from the front line.
[spreadsheet] Leaders start measuring what is easy to count, like sales and costs, and lose sight of why.
=== Keep It Clear ===
[keeper] The fix is to make keeping the why someone's real job.
[sign_post] Say your purpose out loud, often, at every level.
[successor_pick] Choose future leaders who truly share the original belief, not just the skills.
[checklist] Before big decisions, ask whether this choice still serves the reason you started.
=== Your Challenge ===
Think of your team, your business, or even your family.
Is your why still loud, or has it gone quiet?
