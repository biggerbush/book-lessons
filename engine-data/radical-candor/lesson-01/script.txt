=== Feedback That Stuck ===
[title_kim] What if the most caring thing a boss ever did for you felt a little like a punch?
Kim Scott got exactly that, and it changed her career.
=== A Big Presentation ===
[google_office] Early in her time at Google, Scott gave an update to the company's founders and chief executive.
[nerves] Presenting to the top leaders of a company is nerve-racking for anyone.
[growth_chart] Her business was growing fast, and the meeting went really well.
She walked out feeling great.
=== The Walk Back ===
[sheryl] Afterward, her boss, Sheryl Sandberg, walked with her.
[thumbs_up] Sandberg started by praising the specific things that had gone well.
[speech_bubble] Then she mentioned, almost casually, that Scott had said um a lot.
=== Brushed Off ===
[shrug] Scott waved it away as a minor verbal habit.
It seemed too small to matter.
[coach] Sandberg offered to have Google pay for a speech coach.
Scott brushed that off too, the way most of us dodge awkward news.
=== Getting Direct ===
[serious_face] So Sandberg changed tactics and got blunt.
[smart_bulb] She said Scott was one of the smartest people she knew, but all those ums made her sound far less smart.
[lightning] This time, the message landed.
=== Why It Worked ===
[heart] Scott could hear it because Sandberg clearly cared about her as a person.
[gift_box] The bluntness came wrapped in obvious respect, not contempt.
Scott realised the gentle hints had been too soft to get through.
=== Fixing the Habit ===
[coach_session] Scott finally went to the speech coach.
[mirror] Working with the coach, she began to break a habit she had barely noticed.
[stage] It was a small fix with a big payoff for every talk ahead.
=== The Real Lesson ===
[scale] Scott saw that staying quiet to spare someone's feelings is not kindness.
[open_door] Telling people what they need to hear, clearly enough to land, is how you show you care.
[you_icon] The next time you notice a problem, say it plainly, and say it because you want them to succeed.
