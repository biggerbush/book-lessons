=== No Recipe ===
[cookbook] Imagine reaching for a cookbook in a crisis, and finding every page blank.
That is what leadership feels like when things get truly hard.
=== The Easy Advice ===
[shelf] Business shelves are full of advice for problems that are already fairly clear.
[big_goal] Set a bold goal, hire great people, build a strong culture.
Ben Horowitz says that part is the easy part.
=== The Hard Part ===
[missed_goal] The hard part is what you do when you miss that bold goal.
[entitled] Or when the great people you hired start acting entitled.
No book gives a tidy formula for those moments.
These are the moments that actually decide whether a company survives.
=== Loudcloud Rises ===
[startup] Horowitz co-founded a cloud services company called Loudcloud during the dot-com boom.
[investors] Money was flowing, customers were signing up, and the future looked bright.
=== The Bubble Bursts ===
[burst] Then the dot-com bubble burst.
[customers] Many of Loudcloud's customers were young internet companies, and they started disappearing.
[cash_gauge] The company's money was running out fast.
Almost overnight, the plan that made sense a year earlier stopped making sense.
=== Choices With No Map ===
[crossroads] Horowitz faced decisions no textbook covered.
[ipo_bell] He took Loudcloud public in a brutal market, when many people thought it was a terrible idea.
Later he sold the original services business and rebuilt what remained as a software company called Opsware.
[hp_logo] Opsware was eventually sold to Hewlett-Packard.
=== Why No Formula ===
[moving_parts] Hard problems involve people, timing, and fear, all shifting at once.
Every situation is a little different, so a rule that worked once can fail the next time.
[compass] So instead of a recipe, Horowitz offers his own hard-won stories, like a compass rather than a map.
=== Act Anyway ===
[fact_sheet] When a hard problem lands, stop hunting for the perfect formula.
Gather the facts you can, then make the best call available.
[own_it] Own that decision, and adjust as you learn more.
Moving forward imperfectly usually beats freezing while you wait for certainty.
