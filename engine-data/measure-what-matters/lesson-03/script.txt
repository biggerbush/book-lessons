=== Too Many Goals ===
[juggler] Imagine trying to juggle twenty balls at once.
Sooner or later, every single one hits the floor.
=== Fewer Is Stronger ===
[goal_list] Many teams write long lists of goals, hoping to cover everything.
[spotlight] John Doerr argues the opposite: real focus means choosing only a handful.
[five_fingers] He suggests about three to five objectives for each cycle.
=== Saying No ===
[crossed_out] Picking a few means crossing many good ideas off the list.
That hurts, but it is the point.
[scale] Every yes to one priority is a quiet no to something else.
=== Intel Under Threat ===
[calendar] In late 1979, Intel faced a real threat.
[rival_chip] Rival Motorola had a microprocessor that many saw as faster and easier to use than Intel's 8086.
[warning_note] A worried salesperson sent an urgent message warning that Intel was losing customers.
=== One Clear Mission ===
[meeting] Intel's leaders met, and within about a week they launched a campaign called Operation Crush.
[target] The objective was bold and simple: secure 2,000 design wins in one year.
[new_gadget] A design win means a customer chooses your chip for a new product.
=== Everyone Pulls ===
[sales_team] Engineers, marketers and salespeople all pointed their work at that one target.
[one_team] Departments that rarely worked closely together suddenly moved like a single team.
[shield] Instead of fighting only on speed, Intel stressed its support, reliability and long-term plans for customers.
=== The Payoff ===
[counter] By the end of 1980, Intel had beaten its target, with roughly 2,500 design wins.
[ibm_pc] One of them was IBM, which picked an Intel chip for its first personal computer.
[rocket] That single win helped shape Intel's future for decades.
=== Why It Worked ===
[magnifier] Operation Crush worked because Intel chose one fight and put everything behind it.
[few_blocks] A few clear priorities make it obvious where time, money and energy should go.
[young_doerr] Doerr saw it firsthand as a young Intel salesperson, and he never forgot the lesson.
=== Your Turn ===
Look at your goals for this quarter and circle no more than three.
Then ask yourself what you will say no to, so those three can truly win.
