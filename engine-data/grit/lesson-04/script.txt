=== Practice Smarter ===
[calendar] You can practice something for years and still not get any better.
Here is what the best performers do differently.
=== More Hours Isn't Enough ===
[driver] Think about driving a car.
Most of us drive almost every day, yet we stopped improving long ago.
[clock] Simply piling up hours does not make us experts.
What matters is how those hours are spent.
=== The Expert Puzzle ===
[researcher] Duckworth draws on the work of psychologist Anders Ericsson, who studied world-class performers.
[violinist] He found experts practice in a particular way.
He called it deliberate practice, and it has four parts.
=== Pick a Stretch Goal ===
[target] First, experts set a clear, specific goal.
They choose one small weakness, not their whole performance.
[piano_keys] For a pianist, that might be one tricky passage, not the whole piece.
[ladder] The goal sits just beyond what they can do right now.
=== Full Focus ===
[headphones] Second, they work on that goal with total concentration.
No phones, no daydreaming, no autopilot.
[hourglass] This is so tiring that experts often practice in sessions of about an hour, then rest.
=== Get Feedback ===
[coach] Third, they seek fast, honest feedback.
A coach, a recording, or a score shows exactly what went wrong.
[red_marks] Experts lean toward their mistakes, rather than away from them.
=== Repeat and Refine ===
[loop_arrow] Fourth, they repeat, adjusting each time until the weakness is fixed.
[mirror] They also reflect on what changed, so each attempt teaches them something.
[checkmark] Then they pick a new weakness and begin again.
=== The Spelling Bee ===
[trophy] Duckworth studied finalists in the National Spelling Bee.
[flashcards] The kids who spent more time studying words alone advanced further than those who mostly quizzed or read for fun.
Grittier spellers did more of this hard practice.
[frown_face] Yet the spellers rated that solitary study as the most effortful and least enjoyable.
Practice that works often does not feel good in the moment.
=== Your Turn ===
[notebook] This week, choose one small weakness in a skill you care about.
Give it twenty focused minutes, find some feedback, and try again tomorrow.
