=== No Bad Teams ===
[title_crew] Imagine two teams with the same people, the same boats, and the same orders.
One keeps winning, the other keeps losing, and the reason might surprise you.
=== Hell Week ===
[beach] Picture SEAL training during Hell Week, on a cold beach.
[boat_crews] Trainees are split into boat crews of about seven men each.
[heavy_boat] Each crew races carrying a heavy rubber boat on their heads, then paddles it through crashing surf.
[ranking] Every race is ranked, from first place to last.
=== Best and Worst ===
[crew_two] One crew, Boat Crew Two, won almost every race.
[crew_six] Another, Boat Crew Six, kept finishing at or near the back.
[reward] Winning crews earned a short rest, so losing hurt even more.
=== Two Leaders ===
[leader_two] Crew Two's leader kept his men working together and pushing toward the goal.
[leader_six] Crew Six's leader was frustrated and blamed his men for every loss.
He believed he had simply been given a bad crew.
=== The Swap ===
[instructors] So the instructors tried an experiment.
[swap_arrows] They swapped the two boat crew leaders and changed nothing else.
Same men, same boats, same brutal races.
Would the losing crew stay losers?
=== The Result ===
[trophy] In the very next race, Boat Crew Six, the losing crew, came in first.
[second_place] Boat Crew Two came a close second, because the habits its old leader built stayed with the team.
[spotlight] The men of Crew Six were exactly the same people as before.
The only real difference had been the leader.
=== Why It Works ===
[standard_bar] The authors argue that a leader sets the standard the whole team lives up to.
[tolerate_sign] As the book puts it, "it's not what you preach, it's what you tolerate."
[low_bar] If a leader accepts poor performance, that becomes the team's new normal.
[team_pull] A strong leader refuses to accept losing and gets the team pulling together.
=== Raise the Bar ===
[mirror] So when your team struggles, look first at the leader, and that may mean you.
[clipboard] Ask what standard you have quietly been accepting.
[steps_up] Then set a higher one, explain why, and hold to it every day.
=== Your Challenge ===
[question_mark] Think of one area where your team keeps falling short.
What are you tolerating there, and what will you stop accepting this week?
