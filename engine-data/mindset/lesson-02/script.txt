=== Red Marks ===
[failed_test] You get a test back covered in red marks.
Is that a judgement of who you are, or a map of what to learn next?
=== Same Setback ===
[two_students] Picture two students holding the same disappointing grade.
[split_screen] Yet in their heads, they are living through completely different events.
Dweck argues the difference comes down to what each one believes about ability.
=== Failure as Verdict ===
[gavel] With a fixed mindset, a setback lands like a judge's ruling.
If ability is set, then failing means you simply do not have it.
[label_tag] So one bad result grows into a label: I am a failure.
=== Failure as Data ===
[compass] With a growth mindset, the same setback works more like a compass reading.
It shows where you are now, not where you must stay.
[toolbox] The natural next question becomes: what strategy, effort or help do I need?
=== The Brain Study ===
[brain_scan] In a study Dweck describes at Columbia, researchers tracked brain activity as people answered hard questions.
[feedback_card] Each person learned if they were right, then saw the correct answer.
Fixed-mindset participants perked up mainly at hearing right or wrong.
[right_answer] They tuned out the information that could help them improve.
Growth-mindset participants paid close attention to it.
=== Avoiding Help ===
[university] At the University of Hong Kong, classes are taught in English.
[english_book] Students arriving with weak English were asked if they would take a course to improve it.
Those with a growth mindset said yes.
Those with a fixed mindset showed little interest, even though their studies depended on it.
=== Effort Too ===
[steep_hill] The same split shows up with effort.
In a fixed mindset, needing to try hard feels like evidence you are not naturally good.
[engine] In a growth mindset, effort is the engine that actually builds ability.
=== Read the Data ===
[notebook] Next time something goes wrong, write down what happened, without adjectives about yourself.
Then ask what that result is telling you to change.
[arrow_forward] Choose one specific next step, and take it this week.
=== Your Turn ===
[mirror] Think of one recent setback you treated as a verdict.
What would it teach you if you read it as information instead?
