=== The Mountain Is You ===
[hiker] Imagine a hiker stuck at the foot of a mountain, blaming the weather, the trail, and the map.
[mirror] Now imagine the mountain has her face.
=== The Same Wall Again ===
[calendar] Maybe you have noticed the same problem showing up again and again.
[new_job] A new job, but the same burnout.
[new_partner] A new relationship, but the same arguments.
The scenery changes, yet the wall looks familiar.
=== Wiest's Big Claim ===
[book] Brianna Wiest argues that the obstacle in our way is rarely our circumstances.
[pattern_loop] It is usually our own habits, fears, and reactions, repeating in a loop we have never looked at closely.
That is the mountain.
=== Wanting and Blocking ===
[two_arrows] Her key example is the gap between what we want and what we do.
[savings_jar] Someone wants financial security, then spends impulsively every time they feel stressed.
[phone_heart] Someone longs for closeness, then pulls away the moment a relationship gets serious.
Part of them wants to move forward, and part of them keeps pulling back.
=== Why It Stays Hidden ===
[fog] These patterns feel normal, so they hide in plain sight.
[finger_point] It is easier to point at a boss, a partner, or bad luck.
But blaming the outside world keeps us circling the base of the mountain forever.
=== Good News in Disguise ===
[key] Here is the hopeful twist.
If the mountain is you, then the power to climb it is also yours.
[small_step] You cannot control the economy or other people, but you can examine your own choices.
=== The Climb Changes You ===
[trail] Wiest frames the climb itself as the point.
[backpack] Each step teaches you something about how you work.
[summit_flag] By the time you reach the top, you are no longer the person who started at the bottom.
=== How to Start ===
[notebook] Start by writing down one frustration that keeps returning in your life.
[magnifier] Then ask a simple question: what part do I play in this?
Ask with curiosity, not blame.
[footprint] Seeing your pattern clearly is the first step up the mountain.
=== Your First Step ===
[question_mark] What problem keeps following you from place to place?
This week, notice it once, and simply name it as yours.
