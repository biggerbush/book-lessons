=== A Scraped Knee ===
[scraped_knee] What if a scraped knee is not a parenting failure, but part of the plan?
Jonathan Haidt thinks small bumps and scares are exactly how kids grow strong.
=== Antifragile Kids ===
[teacup] Some things break when you knock them, like a teacup.
[muscle] Other things get stronger when stressed, like a muscle after a workout.
Haidt borrows a word for this second kind from the writer Nassim Taleb: antifragile.
Children, he argues, are antifragile by design.
=== The Immune System ===
[immune_shield] A child's immune system shows how this works.
[germs] It needs to meet everyday germs to learn how to fight them.
[clean_bubble] Raise a child in a perfectly clean bubble, and that system stays weak.
Haidt says the mind works the same way with fear and conflict.
=== What Free Play Is ===
[kids_running] Free play is play that kids choose and run themselves, with no adult in charge.
Haidt draws on psychologist Peter Gray, who studies how children learn through play.
[tag_game] Picture a game of tag in the park.
The kids invent the rules, argue about fairness, and settle it themselves.
That squabble is practice for friendship and teamwork.
=== A Little Bit Scary ===
[climbing_tree] The best play often carries a small thrill of danger.
Norwegian researcher Ellen Sandseter observed that children seek out risky play on purpose.
[fast_swing] They climb high, go fast, wrestle, and wander out of sight of grown-ups.
Each time, they take just the dose of fear they can handle.
=== Fear Shrinks ===
[fear_meter] When a child faces a little fear and comes through fine, that fear gets smaller.
[confident_kid] The child learns a lasting lesson: I can handle hard things.
Haidt sees this as natural protection against anxiety.
=== Discover Mode ===
Haidt describes two basic modes children can be in.
[compass] In discover mode, kids are curious, open, and eager to explore.
[shield_mode] In defend mode, they watch for threats and pull back.
Free play keeps kids in discover mode, where growth happens.
=== Step Back ===
For adults, this means stepping back on purpose.
[park_bench] Sit on the bench instead of hovering by the slide.
[wobbly_fort] Let small conflicts and wobbly forts play out without rescue.
=== Your Challenge ===
[clock] This week, give a child an hour of play where you set no rules and solve no problems.
Then watch what they figure out on their own.
