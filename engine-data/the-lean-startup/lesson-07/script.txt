=== Faster Loops Win ===
[stopwatch] What if the winning startup is not the one with the best idea, but the one that learns fastest?
=== The Loop ===
[loop_circle] Eric Ries describes a startup as a loop with three steps: build, measure, learn.
[build_step] You build something, see how customers respond, and learn what to try next.
[timer] The goal is not to perfect any single step, but to shrink the total time around the whole circle.
=== Stuffing Envelopes ===
[envelopes] Ries shares a simple example about stuffing newsletters into envelopes.
[two_kids] Picture two children helping their dad with a big mailing.
[stack_folded] Their instinct is to fold every letter first, then stuff every envelope, then seal and stamp them all.
=== One at a Time ===
[single_letter] The alternative is to fold, stuff, seal and stamp one envelope completely before starting the next.
It sounds slower, because you keep switching tasks.
[finish_flag] But as reported in the book, the one at a time approach tends to finish faster.
=== Problems Show Up Early ===
[magnifier] The real bonus is what small batches reveal.
[wrong_size] Imagine the letters don't fit the envelopes.
With one at a time, you discover it on the very first envelope.
[big_pile] With big batches, you find out after folding hundreds.
=== Ship Small Batches ===
[product_box] The same logic applies to products.
[big_launch] A team that releases one giant update every few months learns very slowly.
[tiny_updates] A team that releases small changes often gets feedback within days.
=== IMVU Ships Daily ===
[imvu_office] At IMVU, Ries's company pushed new code to customers around fifty times a day.
[shield] Automated tests and alerts acted like an immune system, catching problems and rolling back bad changes quickly.
[dial] Each tiny release became a fast, cheap experiment.
=== Plan in Reverse ===
[arrow_back] Ries adds a twist: plan the loop backwards.
[question_mark] First decide what you need to learn.
[ruler] Then decide what you would measure to learn it.
[hammer] Only then build the smallest thing that produces that measurement.
=== Your Challenge ===
[calendar] How long does one full loop take on your current project?
[scissors] Pick one change you could cut into a smaller batch and ship this week.
