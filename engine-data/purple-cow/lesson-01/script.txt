=== Purple Cow ===
[title_cow] Picture a cow so strange you would pull over just to stare at it.
Why does it grab you, when a thousand normal cows never do?
=== A Drive Through France ===
[car] Seth Godin opens his book with a family road trip through the French countryside.
[cows] Out the window, herds of cows grazed in sunny green fields.
At first, everyone was charmed.
The cows looked like they had stepped out of a picture book.
=== Twenty Minutes Later ===
[clock] But within about twenty minutes, the charm was gone.
Nobody was looking out the window anymore.
[cow_copies] Each new cow looked just like the last one.
Even a beautiful, healthy cow had become boring.
=== Then a Purple Cow ===
[purple_cow] Now imagine one cow in that field is bright purple.
[faces_glass] Suddenly everyone in the car is pressed against the glass.
[phone_photo] You would point, take a photo, and tell your friends about it later.
Same field, same fence, but one cow changes everything.
=== Good Becomes Invisible ===
Here is the core idea.
[filter] Our attention quietly filters out anything that looks like what we have already seen.
[cow_row] A good cow standing beside other good cows simply disappears.
Being good does not mean being noticed.
=== Most Products Are Brown ===
Godin argues that most products today are brown cows.
[shelf] Picture a supermarket shelf lined with boxes that all blur together.
[fine_box] Each one is perfectly fine.
But your eyes slide right past them, the way they slid past the French cows.
=== Different Grabs Attention ===
[spotlight] A purple cow does not win by being louder.
It wins because its difference is part of the thing itself.
[contrast] You cannot miss it, because nothing around it looks the same.
Different stops people.
Familiar lets them keep walking.
=== Spot Your Brown Cows ===
So here is something to try today.
[notebook] Pick one thing you make, like a product, a service, or a presentation.
[stranger] Imagine a stranger seeing it after twenty minutes of similar options.
Would they still look up, or would it blend into the field?
=== Your Challenge ===
[question] What is one change that would make your work look purple in a field of brown?
