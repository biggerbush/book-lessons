=== Quick Wins ===
[shopping_cart] Coupons, flash sales and scary warnings all get people to buy.
So why do so many companies that use them still struggle?
=== Two Ways to Influence ===
[two_paths] Sinek says there are really only two ways to change what people do.
[puppet] One path is manipulation, nudging people with outside pressure.
[lightbulb] The other path is inspiration, drawing people in with a shared belief.
Manipulation is everywhere, because it honestly works, at least for a while.
=== The Price Trap ===
[price_tag] The most common lever is price.
[drug_bottle] But Sinek compares heavy discounting to a drug habit.
The sales high fades, so you need another cut, and your margins shrink each time.
=== Promotions and Rebates ===
[gift_box] Promotions add a free gift or a buy one, get one deal.
[rebate_form] Mail-in rebates go further.
Sinek notes companies often count on many buyers never sending the form in.
That may boost profits, but it builds no real relationship.
=== The Fear Lever ===
[storm_cloud] Fear is a powerful motivator too.
[ibm_badge] Sinek recalls an old saying in tech buying: nobody ever got fired for buying IBM.
Buyers picked the safe brand to protect their jobs, not because they loved it.
Fear can push a decision, but nobody feels loyal to what scared them.
=== Chasing Novelty ===
[flip_phone] Novelty is the shiny new feature.
Sinek describes the Motorola Razr, a slim phone that became a huge hit.
[sales_chart] Sales soared, then the excitement faded and the company struggled to follow it up.
A gimmick grabs attention, but rivals copy it and buyers move on.
=== Deals, Not Loyalty ===
[cash_register] Every lever buys a single transaction.
The customer is loyal to the deal, not to you.
[rising_cost] So you must keep paying for each sale, again and again.
Sinek points to carmakers whose cash-back offers trained buyers to wait for the next deal.
=== Check Your Levers ===
[magnifier] Look back at your last few big pushes.
Ask which ones relied on a discount, a freebie, a scare or a gimmick.
[handshake] Then ask what would make someone choose you even without that push.
=== Your Challenge ===
[calendar] This week, try one pitch with no discount and no pressure.
Would people still say yes because they believe what you believe?
