=== Two Twins ===
[twins] Luck and risk are twins, born at the same moment.
You rarely see one without the other quietly standing nearby.
=== A Rare Computer ===
[school] In the late 1960s, a high school near Seattle called Lakeside did something remarkable.
[computer] It got a computer, at a time when almost no high schools in the world had one.
[young_gates] A teenage student named Bill Gates fell in love with it.
=== The Lucky Break ===
Gates spent nights there, learning to program long before most people had touched a computer.
[microsoft] That early head start helped him build Microsoft.
[lottery] Being at that one school was a stroke of luck few students in the world shared.
=== The Other Friend ===
[kent] Gates had a best friend there named Kent Evans.
Housel says Kent was just as brilliant, and just as ambitious.
[mountain] But Kent died in a mountaineering accident before he finished high school.
=== Same Start, Different End ===
[split_path] Two boys, same school, same talent, same dreams.
One became one of the richest people alive.
The other never got the chance, through no fault of his own.
That is risk, the twin of luck, at work.
=== Forces Beyond Us ===
[scale] Every outcome sits on a scale, with our choices on one side.
[storm_cloud] On the other side are forces we cannot control or even see.
[billions] With billions of people making choices, chance shapes results more than we like to admit.
=== Careful With Praise ===
[trophy] So when someone succeeds, it is not all genius.
[fallen_man] And when someone fails, it is not all foolishness.
[mirror] The same goes for you, whether things go well or badly.
Housel suggests being careful whom you admire, and careful whom you look down on.
=== Look for Patterns ===
[magnifier] Instead of copying one dazzling success story, look for broad patterns that show up again and again.
[many_people] Lessons that work for many people are more trustworthy than the extreme case of one.
[forgive] And when you stumble, forgive yourself, and give others that same grace.
=== Your Turn ===
[question] Think of one success you are proud of and one failure you regret.
How much of each was luck, and how much was risk?
