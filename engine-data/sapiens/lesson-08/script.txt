=== The Power of Not Knowing ===
[blank_map] What if the most powerful sentence in history was just three words long?
Those words are, we do not know.
=== Answers Already Written ===
[old_scroll] For most of history, people assumed everything important was already known.
[wise_sage] That knowledge lived in sacred texts and the teachings of ancient sages.
If those sources were silent on a question, the question was probably not important.
[villager] A puzzled villager would consult a priest or scholar rather than run an experiment.
=== Three Small Words ===
[lab_notebook] Modern science starts from the opposite assumption.
It admits that we do not know the most important things.
[question_mark] Even its favourite theories stay open to challenge if new evidence turns up.
That humility turned ignorance from an embarrassment into an invitation.
=== Monsters at the Edge ===
[old_map] Harari shows this shift through maps.
Earlier maps rarely left anything empty.
[sea_monster] Where knowledge ran out, mapmakers filled the edges with imagined lands and strange creatures.
=== The Blank Space ===
[empty_region] From the fifteenth century, European mapmakers began leaving large areas blank.
Those empty spaces quietly confessed, we have not been there yet.
[sailing_ship] And an empty space on a map practically begs someone to go and fill it.
=== Columbus Got It Wrong ===
[columbus] When Christopher Columbus crossed the Atlantic in 1492, he believed he had reached the edge of Asia.
He held onto that belief for the rest of his life.
His mental map had no room for a continent nobody had predicted.
=== Named for a Doubter ===
[vespucci] The Italian explorer Amerigo Vespucci drew a bolder conclusion.
He suggested these lands might be an entirely unknown continent.
[world_map] In 1507, a respected mapmaker labelled that continent America, after him.
Harari notes how fitting it is that a whole continent honours a man willing to admit, we did not know.
=== Paying to Find Out ===
[telescope] Admitting ignorance was only the first step.
[money_bag] Rulers, merchants and scholars began funding voyages, observations and experiments, hoping new knowledge would bring new power.
[toolbox] Science stopped asking only what is true and started asking what new abilities discovery could unlock.
=== Your Blank Space ===
[your_map] Where in your own life are you filling the blank edges with confident monsters?
This week, say I do not know out loud once, then go and find out.
