=== Title ===
[title] Want to feel close to a stranger in under an hour?
The secret is not what you say, but what you ask.
=== Small Talk Trap ===
[two_strangers] Picture two people meeting at a party.
[fact_bubble] One asks where the other works, and gets a job title back.
[shrug] It is polite, but nothing much happens.
Facts tell us what someone does, not who they are.
Many of us stay there out of habit.
=== What Makes It Deep ===
[deep_bubble] Duhigg says a deep question asks about values, beliefs or experiences.
[why_arrow] Instead of where you work, try asking what you love about your work.
[heart_icon] That small shift invites a person to share how they feel.
=== The Lab Experiment ===
[aron] Duhigg describes research led by psychologist Arthur Aron.
[pairs] Aron put strangers into pairs and gave them a list of questions to ask each other.
[ladder] The questions started light and slowly became more personal.
One early question asked what a perfect day would look like.
=== Closeness Fast ===
[control_pair] Other pairs spent the same time making small talk.
[meter] The pairs asking deep questions felt much closer at the end.
[clock] In under an hour, strangers built something that usually takes much longer.
=== Why It Works ===
[open_door] A deep question signals that you care about the answer.
[feelings] It gives people permission to talk about emotions, not just details.
[reply_arrow] And when someone opens up, we tend to open up in return.
Deep questions are not prying, they are invitations.
=== Share Back ===
[mirror] That is the second half of the skill.
Duhigg stresses that closeness grows when vulnerability goes both ways.
[two_hearts] If you ask a deep question, be ready to offer a real answer yourself.
[interview] Otherwise it feels like an interview, not a conversation.
=== Try It Today ===
[swap_card] Swap one fact question for a feeling question.
[example_q] Ask a colleague what made them choose their field.
[follow_up] Then ask what surprised them about it.
Listen for the values hiding inside their answer.
Notice how the conversation changes.
=== Your Challenge ===
[challenge] This week, ask one person a question you genuinely want answered about their life.
What might you learn?
