=== The Roseto Mystery ===
[roseto_town] What if the secret to a long, healthy life had little to do with diet or exercise?
A tiny Pennsylvania town once held the answer.
=== A Doctor's Hunch ===
[doctor_wolf] In the late 1950s, a physician named Stewart Wolf heard something strange from a local doctor.
[heart_icon] Heart disease, then a leading killer in America, was rare among the people of Roseto.
[italy_map] Roseto was settled by immigrants from a village of the same name in southern Italy.
=== Breaking the Rules ===
Wolf and his team studied the town closely.
[chart_deaths] Almost nobody under 55 died of a heart attack or showed signs of heart disease.
[frying_pan] Yet the Rosetans cooked with lard, and many smoked and carried extra weight.
By every textbook rule, they should have been sick.
=== Not the Genes ===
[family_tree] Maybe it was good genes, Wolf thought.
[relatives] So he tracked down Rosetans' relatives living elsewhere in America.
Those relatives did not share Roseto's remarkable health.
The secret was not in their blood.
=== Not the Place ===
Maybe it was the land itself, then.
[nearby_towns] But two neighboring towns had far higher rates of death from heart disease.
[same_hills] They shared the same countryside and the same medical care.
So it was not geography either.
=== A Walk Through Town ===
Then Wolf walked the streets and saw it.
[street_chat] People stopped to chat, and neighbors cooked for one another in their backyards.
[three_gens] Grandparents, parents and children often lived together under one roof.
[church] A single church brought the whole town together.
=== A Web of Belonging ===
[civic_clubs] In a town of under 2,000 people, there were 22 civic organizations.
[equal_houses] Wealthier families avoided showing off, so no one felt left behind.
This dense web of belonging protected people from the stresses of modern life.
=== Zooming Out ===
[lone_figure] We usually explain health, and success, by looking only at one person's choices.
[zoom_out] Roseto shows we also have to zoom out and see the community around them.
That is the lens this whole book uses.
Your surroundings shape you more than you think.
