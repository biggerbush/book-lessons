=== Your Last Decade ===
[old_self] Picture yourself at 90, kneeling to lift a laughing grandchild off the floor.
Will your body still say yes?
=== A Decathlon for Life ===
[athlete] A decathlete trains for ten different events, not just one.
Peter Attia borrows this idea and calls it the Centenarian Decathlon.
[event_list] You pick roughly ten physical tasks you want to still manage in the last decade of your life.
=== Pick Your Events ===
[stairs] His examples include climbing four flights of stairs in three minutes.
[groceries] Carrying two bags of groceries for five blocks.
[suitcase] Lifting a suitcase into an airplane's overhead bin.
[floor_rise] Getting up off the floor using no more than one arm for support.
Your list should be personal, like gardening, hiking, or playing with kids.
=== Strength Fades ===
Here is the catch.
[decline_line] Strength and muscle tend to decline steadily as we age.
So whatever you can lift today, you will likely lift less in thirty years.
[heavy_box] That means you need to train well beyond your target now, building a buffer for the decline.
=== Work Backwards ===
[toddler] Say you want to lift a thirty pound grandchild at 85.
Then at 50, you should be comfortably handling far more than thirty pounds.
[arrow_back] This is the heart of the method: start from the future you want and work backwards.
=== Grip and Carry ===
[grip_hand] Attia puts special weight on grip strength, which studies link to longer life.
[dead_hang] He favors simple tests like hanging from a bar for as long as you can.
[farmer_carry] Carrying heavy weights in each hand, called farmer's carries, builds grip and a strong core.
=== Stability Matters ===
Strength alone is not enough.
[balance] You also need stability, the control that keeps joints safe and your body steady.
[falling] Falls are a leading cause of accidental death in older adults, so balance really matters.
=== Train with Purpose ===
[trainee] Think of yourself as an athlete in training, with your event decades away.
[barbell] Lift regularly, with movements like carrying, pulling, and rising from a squat.
Each session is a deposit into your future body.
=== Your Challenge ===
[notepad] Write down three things you want to do at 90.
Then ask yourself what training this week keeps that possible.
