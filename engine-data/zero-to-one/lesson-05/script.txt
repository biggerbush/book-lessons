=== Owning the Market ===
[empty_map] Everyone dreams of owning a market, but almost nobody knows how a monopoly is actually built.
Peter Thiel says it starts far smaller than you think.
=== Far Better Tech ===
[gear_icon] The first ingredient is proprietary technology, something hard for others to copy.
[ten_x_badge] Thiel says it should be roughly 10 times better than the closest alternative.
[search_bar] Google's search was so much better that rivals struggled to catch up.
A small improvement is not enough, because customers will not switch for it.
=== Network Effects ===
[phone_web] The second ingredient is network effects, where a product grows more useful as more people join.
[harvard_gate] Facebook began with students at just one university, Harvard.
[small_circle] Thiel notes that networks must start small, because a nearly empty network is worthless to its first users.
=== Economies of Scale ===
[code_screen] The third ingredient is economies of scale.
[cost_curve] Software costs almost nothing to copy, so every new customer makes the business stronger.
Big fixed costs get spread across more and more people.
=== Branding ===
[apple_logo] The fourth ingredient is a strong brand, like Apple.
[iceberg] But Thiel warns that a brand only lasts when real substance sits underneath it.
=== Start Tiny ===
[paypal_logo] Now comes the key move: dominate a tiny market first.
[ebay_sellers] PayPal focused on a few thousand power sellers on eBay.
These sellers badly needed easy online payments, so PayPal could win them over fast.
[crowded_field] Chasing a huge market first means fighting everyone at once, often with nothing special to offer.
=== Then Expand ===
[bookshelf] Amazon followed the same path, starting with books before selling almost everything.
[ripple_rings] Each victory becomes a launchpad into a slightly bigger, related market.
[last_runner] Thiel says the real prize is being the last mover, not the first.
That means still dominating your market many years from now.
=== Your Tiny Market ===
[notebook] Write down the smallest group of people who urgently need what you offer.
[target] Then ask how you could serve nearly every one of them better than anyone else.
=== Your Challenge ===
Which tiny market could you own completely this year?
And which of the four ingredients could make that lead last?
