=== Natural Pace ===
What if the smartest people in history worked slower than you do?
Their secret was not speed, but patience.
=== A Long Look Back ===
[copernicus] Consider the astronomer Nicolaus Copernicus.
[sun_diagram] He developed the idea that the Earth circles the Sun.
[calendar_page] Yet he spent decades refining it, and published only near the end of his life.
=== Great Minds, Slow Clocks ===
Newport points to many great thinkers of the past with a similar rhythm.
[scientists] Scientists like Galileo, Newton and Marie Curie had stretches of intense work.
[hammock] They also had long periods of slower thinking in between.
Their big ideas unfolded over years, not weeks.
=== The Modern Sprint ===
[office_desk] Compare that to a typical office today.
[stopwatch] Every task is due now, and every day feels like a race.
[tired_worker] Newport argues this pace is unnatural, and it leaves us drained without producing better work.
Busy days pile up, but the meaningful work keeps slipping.
=== Hamilton Took Years ===
[miranda] Newport also points to Lin-Manuel Miranda, creator of the musical Hamilton.
[book_stack] Miranda began with a biography of Alexander Hamilton and slowly shaped it into songs.
[stage] The show took him years to complete before it became a hit.
=== Stretch the Horizon ===
The lesson is to judge your work on longer timelines.
[timeline] Instead of asking what you finished today, ask what you built this year.
[growing_tree] Progress measured over years looks steady, even when individual days feel slow.
=== Plan in Years ===
[five_year] Newport suggests thinking about your goals on a horizon of around five years.
That is long enough for something meaningful to grow.
[blueprint] Ask which big projects you want to complete in that window.
Suddenly, a few months of extra care on each one feels reasonable.
=== Ease the Daily Pace ===
[weekly_plan] Then look at your week and remove the false urgency.
[deadline_tag] Give projects more realistic deadlines than your first anxious guess.
[walking_path] Aim for a pace you could keep up for years, not just this month.
=== Your Challenge ===
[question_mark] Pick one project you are rushing right now.
What would change if you gave it twice the time, and judged it five years from today?
