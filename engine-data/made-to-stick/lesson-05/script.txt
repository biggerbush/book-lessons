=== Can You Prove It? ===
[question_mark] Why do we believe some ideas at once, and doubt others backed by the same facts?
The answer is not who says it, but how it lets us check.
=== Borrowed Trust ===
[expert] Usually we borrow belief from experts, celebrities or official seals.
[empty_chair] But most of us have no famous expert standing beside us.
[glowing_idea] The Heaths argue an idea can carry its own credibility inside it.
=== A Custody Case ===
[jury_box] In one study reported in the book, mock jurors heard a custody case about a mother and her young son.
[two_scales] The arguments stayed the same, but one side's version included small, vivid details.
[toothbrush] One detail said the boy brushed his teeth with a Darth Vader toothbrush.
=== Details Persuade ===
[tilted_scale] Jurors leaned toward whichever side got the vivid details, even though those details proved nothing.
[magnifier] A sharp, specific detail signals that the speaker really knows the situation.
Vague claims feel thin, while precise ones feel witnessed.
=== Numbers Need Scale ===
[spreadsheet] Statistics are powerful evidence, but huge numbers slide right past our feelings.
[ruler] The fix is to translate them into something human-sized, something we can see or hear.
=== The Bucket of BBs ===
[metal_bucket] Activists warning about nuclear weapons used a simple metal bucket.
[single_bb] First they dropped one BB pellet inside, standing for the Hiroshima bomb.
[bb_avalanche] Then they poured in thousands more, representing the world's nuclear arsenal, rattling for long seconds.
The audience did not just learn the number, they heard it.
=== Test It Yourself ===
[debate_stage] In a 1980 debate, Ronald Reagan skipped reciting economic statistics.
[mirror] He asked voters, "Are you better off than you were four years ago?"
[nodding_crowd] Listeners checked their own lives, so the answer felt like their discovery, not his claim.
=== Your Toolkit ===
[checklist] Before your next pitch, add one vivid, true detail.
[small_object] Shrink your biggest number into something people can picture or hear.
[invitation] Then hand them a test they can run themselves.
=== Your Turn ===
[open_hand] What claim are you making this week that people simply have to take on faith?
How could you let them check it for themselves?
