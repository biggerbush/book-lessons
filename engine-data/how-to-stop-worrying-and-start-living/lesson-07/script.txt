=== Title ===
[title_card] What if the fastest cure for your worries was hidden inside someone else's problem?
Today we explore the final habit, and maybe the most surprising one.
=== The Inward Spiral ===
[worried_mind] Worry grows best in a closed room.
The longer we stare at our own troubles, the bigger and darker they seem to get.
[spotlight] Carnegie noticed that anxious people usually keep a bright spotlight pointed squarely at themselves.
His remedy was simple: swing that spotlight around and point it at other people.
=== Adler's Prescription ===
[adler] To make his case, Carnegie turns to Alfred Adler, a famous Austrian psychiatrist.
[prescription] Adler told gloomy patients they could feel better in fourteen days by following one daily rule.
Each morning, think about how you could bring some pleasure to someone else.
It sounds almost too easy, yet Adler believed it worked.
=== Margaret's Bed ===
[margaret] Carnegie shares the story of Margaret Tayler Yates, the wife of a naval officer stationed in Hawaii.
[bed] Because of heart trouble, she spent most of every day lying in bed.
Her days shrank down to her own aches and fears.
=== The Phone Rings ===
[explosions] Then came the attack on Pearl Harbor.
[telephone] A phone was set beside her bed, and she became a contact point for frightened military families.
She took their calls and passed along news about where their loved ones were.
=== Out of Bed ===
[comfort] Some callers had just lost their husbands, so she comforted them as best she could.
She became so busy caring for strangers that she simply forgot about herself.
[sunrise] Soon she was spending far more of her time out of bed than in it.
=== Why It Works ===
[gears] Helping others quietly pulls together everything Carnegie teaches.
[clock] A kind act keeps you focused on today, the only day you can actually live.
[busy_hands] It fills your hands and your thoughts, leaving little room for brooding.
[scale] And seeing other people's struggles makes your own blessings easier to count.
=== Small Is Enough ===
You do not need a crisis to try this.
[thank_you] Picture a short thank you note, a sincere compliment, or a real question about someone's day.
[ripple] Each small kindness sends ripples outward, and some of that calm flows straight back to you.
=== Your Challenge ===
[calendar] For the next fourteen days, start each morning by asking who you could make a little happier.
Then go do it, and watch what happens to your worries.
