=== Quiet Power ===
[quiet_ceo] Picture the leader of a great company.
Now forget the celebrity on the magazine cover, because Jim Collins found someone very different.
=== The Five Levels ===
[ladder] Collins describes a ladder of leadership with five levels.
[level_four] Many effective leaders reach level 4, where they rally people around a clear vision.
[level_five] At the top sits level 5, a rare blend of personal humility and fierce professional will.
=== An Unlikely Boss ===
[darwin_smith] In 1971, Kimberly-Clark picked Darwin Smith as its chief executive.
[law_book] He was a mild-mannered in-house lawyer, not a star.
[old_mill] The company was an old paper maker, steadily falling behind the stock market.
=== Will Under Pressure ===
[doctor_chart] Two months into the job, doctors told Smith he had cancer and less than a year to live.
[calendar] He kept working anyway, traveling for treatment while still running the company.
He lived another 25 years, and that same stubborn will shaped his biggest decision.
=== The Big Bet ===
[paper_mills] Smith decided to sell the company's traditional paper mills.
[kimberly_sign] He even sold the mill in Kimberly, Wisconsin, the town that gave the company its name.
[diaper_box] He poured the money into consumer brands like Huggies and Kleenex.
[rival_giant] That meant battling the giant Procter and Gamble head to head.
=== The Payoff ===
[stock_chart] Over the next 20 years, Kimberly-Clark's cumulative stock returns reached 4.1 times the general market.
[trophy] It became the world's leading paper-based consumer products company.
=== Humble at Heart ===
[spotlight] Yet Smith never chased the spotlight.
Looking back, he said, "I never stopped trying to become qualified for the job."
[window_mirror] Collins calls this habit the window and the mirror.
Level 5 leaders look out the window to credit others, and into the mirror when things go wrong.
=== Ambition for Others ===
[successor] They also set up their successors to win, because their ambition is for the company, not themselves.
[ego_boss] Many comparison companies had larger-than-life bosses whose firms stumbled after they left.
