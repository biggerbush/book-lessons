=== The Curse ===
[tapper_desk] Tap out a famous song on a table, and surely someone across from you will guess it.
They almost certainly won't, and that explains why smart people explain things so badly.
=== A Simple Game ===
[stanford] In 1990, a Stanford graduate student named Elizabeth Newton ran a small experiment.
[two_roles] She split people into two roles, tappers and listeners.
[song_list] Tappers picked well known songs, like Happy Birthday, and knocked out the rhythm on a table.
Listeners had to name the song.
=== The Big Gap ===
[prediction] Beforehand, tappers predicted listeners would guess right about half the time.
[real_result] In reality, listeners named just 2.5 songs out of 120.
That is roughly 1 song in 40.
[gap_arrow] The tappers expected to succeed about twenty times more often than they did.
=== Inside Your Head ===
[thought_cloud] Here is why. While tapping, the tapper hears the melody playing in their head.
[knock_marks] The listener hears none of it, only a string of disconnected knocks.
[puzzled_face] So the tapper is baffled. How could anyone miss a tune this obvious?
=== The Curse Named ===
[curse_label] The Heaths call this the Curse of Knowledge.
[lock_key] Once we know something, we find it hard to imagine not knowing it.
We can no longer recreate our listener's state of mind.
=== Experts Everywhere ===
[ceo_podium] Picture a leader telling staff to maximize shareholder value.
The leader hears a rich strategy playing in their head.
[staff_faces] The staff hear only vague words, like listeners hearing knocks.
[jargon_cloud] Doctors, teachers and engineers fall into the same trap, slipping into abstract expert language.
=== The Way Out ===
[two_doors] There are only two ways to beat the curse.
One is never to learn anything, which helps nobody.
[toolbox] The other is to transform your ideas so outsiders can grasp them.
[success_word] That is the job of six principles: simple, unexpected, concrete, credible, emotional, and stories, spelling SUCCESs.
=== Hear the Knocks ===
[mirror] Start by catching yourself mid tap.
[listener_view] Before explaining, ask what your audience actually hears without the music in your head.
[test_friend] Then try it on someone new, and watch their face instead of trusting your own certainty.
