=== The Missing Half ===
[report_card] A scorecard tells you where you stand, but it never tells you how to get better.
That missing half is what this lesson is about.
=== The Dreaded Review ===
[annual_review] For decades, most companies judged people once a year, in a single annual review.
[old_folder] Feedback arrived months late, tied to goals everyone had already forgotten.
[stressed_pair] Managers dreaded writing them, and employees dreaded reading them.
=== Meet CFRs ===
[cfr_letters] Doerr's answer is three letters: C, F and R.
They stand for conversations, feedback and recognition.
[two_gears] He sees them as the partner to OKRs, turning goals into continuous coaching rather than a yearly verdict.
=== Conversations ===
[one_on_one] The first piece is regular one on one conversations between a manager and each team member.
[chat_bubbles] They talk about goals, progress, obstacles, and where the person wants to grow.
The manager acts less like a judge and more like a coach.
=== Feedback ===
[two_arrows] The second piece is feedback that flows both ways, up and down.
[clock] It arrives close to the moment, while there is still time to change course.
Specific, timely feedback helps people fix small problems before they grow.
=== Recognition ===
[trophy] The third piece is recognition, given often and for real contributions.
[peer_thanks] Doerr wants it coming from peers, not just bosses, and tied to the goals that matter.
People who feel seen tend to keep pushing.
=== Adobe's Check-in ===
[adobe_office] The book tells how Adobe scrapped its annual reviews, a change led by its people chief, Donna Morris.
[check_in] In their place came frequent check-ins between managers and employees about expectations, feedback and growth.
[happy_team] Instead of one big judgment, people got honest conversations all year round.
=== Goals, Not Paychecks ===
[pay_envelope] Doerr also urges keeping OKR scores separate from pay and bonuses.
[stretch_line] If money rides on every number, people set safe goals and stop stretching.
OKRs say what to aim for, while CFRs help people get there.
=== Your Next Step ===
[calendar] This week, book a short check-in with someone on your team, or with your own manager.
Ask what is going well, what is stuck, and what help would make the difference.
