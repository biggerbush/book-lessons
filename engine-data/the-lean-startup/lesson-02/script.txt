=== Busy Is Not Progress ===
[busy_team] Your team worked hard all month and shipped lots of features.
But did you actually learn anything about your customers?
=== A Different Yardstick ===
[ruler] Eric Ries says startups need a different way to measure progress.
[factory] In a factory, progress means more products rolling off the line.
[question] In a startup, nobody yet knows what customers want, so building more can simply mean wasting more.
=== The IMVU Bet ===
[ries] Ries cofounded IMVU, a company making three D avatars for online chat.
[chat_window] The team bet that people would want an add-on for the instant messaging programs they already used.
[code_pile] So they spent months writing code to make it work with many existing chat networks.
=== Nobody Came ===
[launch] They launched, and almost nobody downloaded it.
[interview] When the team sat down with real customers, people kept refusing to install the add-on.
[friends] Many did not want to bring their existing friends into something they had not tried yet.
=== A Painful Lesson ===
[trash_can] Much of that carefully built code ended up thrown away.
Ries was crushed, and asked himself what all that work had really been for.
[lightbulb] The honest answer was the learning: customers wanted a standalone network where they could meet new people.
=== Could It Be Cheaper? ===
[hourglass] Then came a harder thought.
Could they have learned the same thing with far less effort?
[sketch] Yes, by testing the idea with real customers before building most of it.
Those months of code could have gone somewhere better.
=== What Validated Means ===
[label] Ries calls this validated learning.
[chart] It means proving something true about customers, using real evidence from what they actually do.
[excuse] It is different from shrugging after a failure and saying we learned a lot, with nothing to show.
=== The New Scorecard ===
[scorecard] So the scorecard changes.
Instead of asking how much did we build, ask what did we prove.
[checkmark] Any effort that teaches you nothing about customers is waste, however polished it looks.
=== Your Challenge ===
Look back at your last month of work.
Write down one thing you proved about customers, and if the page is blank, plan your next experiment.
