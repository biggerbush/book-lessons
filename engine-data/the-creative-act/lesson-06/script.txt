=== Who Is It For ===
[title_card] Who are you really making your work for?
Rick Rubin's answer is simple, and it might surprise you.
=== The Guessing Game ===
[creator] Picture someone at a desk, about to make something new.
[crowd] Behind them floats an imaginary crowd of future fans and critics.
[question_mark] Every few minutes, the creator turns around and asks, what will they like?
Rubin says this habit quietly drains the life from the work.
=== Nobody Can Predict ===
[shifting_wave] Tastes rise and fall like waves, and no one knows where the next one breaks.
[target] So aiming at an audience means aiming at something that keeps moving.
You end up chasing a guess instead of making something real.
=== The Audience Last ===
Rubin's rule is direct: the audience comes last.
[scale] That does not mean other people are unimportant.
It means their opinions belong after the work is made, not during.
While you are creating, there is only you and the work.
=== Your Inner Compass ===
[compass] Instead of guessing, Rubin asks you to trust an inner sense of what feels right.
[heartbeat] Notice the moment you lean in, feel a spark, or get excited.
That physical reaction is more reliable than any forecast.
=== Rubin in the Studio ===
[mixing_desk] Rubin describes his value in the studio as his honest reactions, not technical expertise.
[thumbs_up] He listens closely and notices what genuinely moves him.
His taste is the tool, and he trusts it.
=== Why It Connects ===
[thread] Here is the paradox: work made for yourself often reaches others more deeply.
[mirror] When you follow what is true for you, people sense that honesty.
Sincerity travels further than calculation.
=== Not for Everyone ===
[volume_dial] Trying to please everyone usually turns the dial down to something bland.
[split_crowd] Strong work may divide people, with some loving it and some walking away.
Rubin sees that as a sign of something alive, not a failure.
=== Your Challenge ===
[sticky_note] On your next project, write one question where you can see it: do I love this?
What would you make if you knew no one else would ever see it?
