=== Day-Tight Living ===
[worried_man] What if most of your stress comes from days you are not even living in?
One simple idea can shrink that weight.
=== A Worried Student ===
[osler_young] In 1871, a young medical student named William Osler was anxious about his exams and his future.
[book_page] Then he read a line by the writer Thomas Carlyle.
[small_task] Its message was that our job is not to peer at distant things but to do the work right in front of us.
=== What It Changed ===
[hospital] Osler went on to help found the Johns Hopkins School of Medicine.
[oxford_gown] He became a professor of medicine at Oxford and was knighted.
He credited much of his success to that one habit of mind.
=== The Ocean Liner ===
[yale_stage] Years later, Osler spoke to students at Yale.
[ship] He described a great ocean liner crossing the sea.
[button] With the press of a button, the captain could seal off sections of the ship so water could not spread.
=== Your Own Doors ===
[mind_ship] Osler told the students they were each a far more marvellous vessel than that ship.
[door_past] Press a button and shut out yesterday, he said.
[door_future] Then press another and shut out tomorrow, so you can sail safely through today.
=== Today Is Enough ===
[two_loads] Carnegie's point is that no one can carry yesterday and tomorrow on top of today.
That combined weight makes even strong people stumble.
[sunset] As Robert Louis Stevenson put it, "Anyone can carry his burden, however hard, until nightfall."
=== Planning Is Not Worry ===
[calendar] This does not mean ignoring the future.
Carnegie says careful thought and planning for tomorrow is wise.
[desk_work] What hurts us is anxious brooding, not planning.
Osler believed the best way to prepare for tomorrow is to do today's work superbly well.
=== Try It Tonight ===
[notebook] Here is how to try it.
Each morning, write down what today actually asks of you.
[closed_doors] When a worry about yesterday or next month shows up, picture shutting a heavy door on it.
Then return to the task in front of you.
=== Your Challenge ===
[question] Which door will you close today, yesterday or tomorrow?
For the next 24 hours, live only inside today's compartment.
