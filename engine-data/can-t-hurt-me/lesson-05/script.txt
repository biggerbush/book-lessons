=== Title ===
[runner] Your mind taps out long before your body truly has to.
That gap is where your hidden strength lives.
=== The Quitting Voice ===
[tired_person] Picture yourself deep in a hard workout, lungs burning, legs shaking.
[speech_bubble] A voice in your head says you are finished and have nothing left.
[goggins] David Goggins says that voice is usually lying to you.
=== The 40 Percent Rule ===
[fuel_gauge] Imagine a fuel gauge that measures your effort.
[forty_mark] Goggins says that when you first feel done, you have usually used only about 40 percent.
[sixty_left] The other 60 percent is still in the tank, waiting to be used.
=== The Governor ===
[car] He compares this to a governor, the device that caps how fast a car can go.
[brain] Your brain has a governor too, built to keep you comfortable and safe.
[brake_pedal] It hits the brakes early, long before you are in real danger.
=== An Untrained 100 ===
[race_clock] Goggins learned this in a 24 hour race where he needed to cover 100 miles.
[dumbbells] He was a big, muscular guy who lifted weights and had barely been running.
[calendar] He signed up with only days to prepare.
=== Body Breaking Down ===
[aching_feet] Partway through, his legs and feet were failing, and he was urinating blood.
Every part of him begged to stop.
[finish_line] He kept moving anyway, one painful step at a time, and reached 100 miles.
=== What He Learned ===
[light_bulb] That race showed him that the limit he felt was not his real limit.
[signal_flag] Since then, he treats the first urge to quit as a signal, not an order.
[rising_arrow] Each time he pushes past that point, his sense of what is possible grows.
=== Moving Your Limit ===
[stopwatch] You can practice this on a small scale, in a workout or a tough task at work.
[plus_step] When you reach your usual stopping point, push a little further than feels comfortable.
[growing_bar] Over time, your governor resets, and your normal gets bigger.
=== Your Challenge ===
[question_mark] Next time your mind says you are done, pause and ask one question.
Is this really my limit, or just my first 40 percent?
