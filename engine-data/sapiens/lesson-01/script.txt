=== Not Alone ===
[lone_figure] Picture a world where you could meet another kind of human on a walk.
For most of our history, that was normal.
=== What Human Means ===
[family_tree] To a biologist, a human is any animal in the genus Homo, not just us.
[sapiens_tag] Our species is called Homo sapiens, meaning wise man.
[sibling_icons] Harari argues we once had many human siblings.
=== A Crowded Planet ===
[world_map] From about 2 million years ago until roughly 10,000 years ago, several human species lived at the same time.
[neanderthal] In Europe and western Asia lived the Neanderthals, bulkier and more muscular than us, built for the Ice Age cold.
[erectus] In eastern Asia lived Homo erectus, who survived for close to 2 million years.
=== Island Dwarfs ===
[flores_island] On the Indonesian island of Flores lived a tiny human species, Homo floresiensis.
[tape_measure] Adults reached only about one metre tall.
Harari suggests scarce food on the island favoured smaller bodies over time.
=== A Finger Bone ===
[finger_bone] In 2010, scientists studied a fossil finger bone from a Siberian cave.
[dna_helix] Its DNA revealed a human species nobody knew about, now called Denisovans.
Who knows how many more lost cousins are waiting to be found.
=== Then There Was One ===
[sapiens_walk] Around 70,000 years ago, Sapiens spread out of East Africa across the world.
[fading_figures] Over the following tens of thousands of years, the other humans disappeared.
[question_mark] Did we breed with them, or replace them?
[quote_card] Harari notes a grim possibility, writing that tolerance is not a Sapiens trademark.
=== Mixed Ancestry ===
[dna_chart] In 2010, DNA studies found that modern Europeans and Middle Easterners carry a few percent Neanderthal DNA.
[melanesia_map] Some Melanesians and Aboriginal Australians carry Denisovan DNA too.
[balance_scale] Harari thinks the truth lies in between: some mixing, but mostly replacement.
=== Why It Matters ===
[mirror] Being the only humans makes us feel special and separate from other animals.
[tree_branch] Harari reminds us we are just one surviving branch of a once bushy family tree.
[hourglass] Homo erectus lasted almost 2 million years, and Harari doubts we will last that long.
=== Your Turn ===
[side_by_side] Next time you feel humans stand apart from nature, picture a Neanderthal by your side.
What changes if you see yourself as one survivor among many cousins?
