=== Who Hits the Brakes? ===
[racing_car] Many experts warn that the coming wave is risky, so why is nobody slowing down?
The answer is a set of incentives that are almost impossible to resist.
=== The Incentive Engine ===
[engine] Suleyman describes technology as pushed forward by a powerful engine of human motivations.
[gear_rivalry] National rivalry, profit and curiosity are among its biggest gears.
[gear_lock] Each gear turns the others, so stopping one barely slows the machine.
=== The AlphaGo Moment ===
[go_board] In 2016, DeepMind's AlphaGo beat Lee Sedol, one of the world's best players of the ancient game Go.
[china_screen] Huge audiences in China watched a Western company's machine master a game they cherished.
[plan_2030] Soon after, China announced a plan to lead the world in artificial intelligence by 2030.
=== The Race Logic ===
[two_runners] Once rivals see each other sprinting, every nation reasons the same way.
If we pause, they will not, and we will fall behind.
[stopwatch] So caution starts to look like surrender, and the race speeds up.
=== Follow the Money ===
[coin_stack] Companies chase the vast economic value these tools promise.
[investor] Investors reward whoever moves fastest, and punish whoever hesitates.
[ladder] For a business, slowing down can simply mean being overtaken by a competitor.
=== Curiosity and Openness ===
[scientist] Then there is the pure thrill of discovery that drives researchers.
[journal] Science runs on sharing, so breakthroughs are published and copied around the world.
[problem_list] And humanity needs new tools for problems like climate change and disease, which adds moral pressure to keep going.
=== Pessimism Aversion ===
[audience] Suleyman recalls raising these dangers with experts and leaders.
[shrug] Again and again, the conversation quickly drifted somewhere more comfortable.
[blindfold] He calls this pessimism aversion, our instinct to look away from dark possibilities.
It feels like optimism, but it leaves us unprepared.
=== Facing the Risk ===
[mirror] The first step is simply noticing when you, or your team, avoid an uncomfortable scenario.
[notebook] Name the incentive pushing you forward, then ask what could go wrong if it wins.
Clear eyes are not doom. They are the start of control.
