=== Title ===
[brave_leader] What if the thing you hide to look strong is the very thing that makes you brave?
=== What It Really Is ===
[three_clouds] Brené Brown defines vulnerability as what we feel in moments of uncertainty, risk and emotional exposure.
[speech_bubble] Think of asking for a raise, giving hard news, or admitting you were wrong.
In each moment, you cannot control the outcome.
That shaky feeling is vulnerability.
=== The Soldier Test ===
[soldiers] Brown once put a question to a group of special forces soldiers.
[question_card] Could anyone name an act of courage that did not involve vulnerability?
The room went quiet.
[raised_hand] Finally, one soldier said he could not think of a single one.
Brown says that across years of asking audiences, no one has offered an example.
=== Myth One: Weakness ===
[scale] So the first myth falls apart.
If courage always requires vulnerability, then vulnerability cannot be weakness.
[mountain] It is the price of entry for every brave act.
=== Myth: Going Alone ===
[shield_person] Some people say they simply do not do vulnerability.
But uncertainty comes for everyone, so the only choice is whether you face it on purpose.
[lone_climber] Others believe they can go it alone.
Brown argues we are wired for connection, and leaders need support too.
=== Myth: No Discomfort ===
[blueprint] Another myth says you can engineer the discomfort out.
You cannot.
Feeling exposed is part of the deal.
[two_hands] A related myth says you need trust before you can be vulnerable.
Brown says the two grow together, a little at a time.
=== Myth: Oversharing ===
[diary] The last myth confuses vulnerability with disclosure.
Spilling every feeling at work is not courage.
[fence] Brown is clear that vulnerability needs boundaries.
Sharing is about purpose and relationship, not dumping.
=== At Work ===
[meeting_table] So what does it look like for a leader?
[i_dont_know] Saying I do not know yet, but I will find out.
Asking a teammate for help.
[open_door] Inviting honest feedback on an idea you love.
Each one is small, risky, and real.
=== Your Challenge ===
[calendar] This week, pick one moment where you would normally play it safe.
Name the uncertainty to yourself, then show up anyway.
