=== Title ===
[title_phone] Your phone is full of apps that each seem a little bit useful.
Cal Newport says a little bit useful is not nearly good enough.
=== The Any-Benefit Trap ===
[open_door] Most of us adopt a tool if we can imagine any benefit at all.
[sign_up] A social network might help you find an old friend or one useful article.
[scale_tipped] That sounds reasonable, but it ignores everything the tool quietly takes from you.
Newport calls this the any-benefit approach.
=== Think Like a Craftsman ===
[toolbox] He suggests a different mindset, the craftsman approach to tool selection.
[woodworker] A skilled craftsman does not keep every tool that could possibly help.
[workbench] She keeps only tools whose benefits clearly outweigh their costs for her most important work.
=== The Farmer's Hay ===
[farmer] Newport describes Forrest Pritchard, a farmer whose family had always made their own hay.
[hay_baler] Making hay needed expensive machinery, and it seemed obviously useful.
[calculator] But when Pritchard added up the full costs, buying hay turned out to be cheaper.
[for_sale] So he sold the equipment, even though it offered some benefit.
=== Start With Your Goals ===
[goal_flags] To apply this yourself, first name your main goals in work and in life.
[activity_list] Under each goal, write the two or three activities that matter most for reaching it.
[magnifier] Then hold each tool up against those few key activities.
=== Weigh It Honestly ===
[scale_balance] Ask whether the tool substantially helps those activities, and whether it substantially hurts them.
[clock_drain] Remember that every app competes for the same limited attention you need for deep work.
[check_cross] Keep it only if the positives clearly win.
=== The 30-Day Test ===
[calendar_30] For social media, Newport suggests a simple experiment.
[logout] Stop using each service for 30 days, without deactivating it or announcing it.
=== Two Questions ===
[question_one] After 30 days, ask whether those weeks would have been notably better with the service.
[question_two] Then ask whether people actually cared that you were gone.
[trash_bin] If both answers are no, Newport says quit for good.
If the answer is unclear, it is your call, though he leans toward quitting.
=== Your Challenge ===
Pick one app you use daily, and name your top goal.
Does that app clearly earn its place, or does it just offer some benefit?
