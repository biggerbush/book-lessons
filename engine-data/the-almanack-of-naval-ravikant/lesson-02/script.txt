=== Your Hidden Edge ===
[spark] What if your biggest career advantage is something no school could ever teach you?
Naval Ravikant has a name for it: specific knowledge.
=== Trainable Is Replaceable ===
[classroom] Picture a classroom full of students learning the exact same skill.
If society can train you for a job, it can train someone else to replace you.
[robot_arm] Or build a machine to do it.
Naval argues real value lies in knowledge that cannot be packaged into a course.
=== What It Looks Like ===
[toolbox] Specific knowledge is often technical or creative, and it is hard to put into words.
[apprentice] It is learned by doing, through apprenticeship and practice, rather than lectures.
That is exactly why it is so hard to copy.
=== Play Versus Work ===
[two_people] Imagine two people doing the same task side by side.
[smile_face] One finds it fun and loses track of time.
[sweat_drop] The other finds it draining and counts the minutes.
Naval says your edge is work that feels like play to you but looks like work to others.
=== Follow Your Curiosity ===
[trend_arrow] Most people chase whatever career seems hottest right now.
Naval suggests the opposite.
[compass] Follow your genuine curiosity, your natural talents and the topics you obsess over.
That path leads where crowds cannot easily follow.
=== Look Back To Childhood ===
[kid_reading] Clues often show up in what you did naturally as a kid or teenager.
Maybe you were always explaining, arguing, building or tinkering.
[book_stack] Naval himself grew up reading constantly and loving science and technology.
That lifelong curiosity shaped the work he became known for.
=== Blend Your Skills ===
[venn] Your edge rarely comes from one skill alone.
It comes from your unique blend of talents, experiences and interests.
[crown] Naval's advice is to keep redefining what you do until you are the best in the world at it.
=== Be Authentically You ===
[mirror] Nobody can compete with you at being you.
Naval puts it simply: "Escape competition through authenticity."
[fingerprint] When your work flows from who you are, it becomes nearly impossible to copy.
=== Your Challenge ===
[notebook] This week, write down three things you do for fun that friends would call work.
Then ask which one the world might happily pay you for.
