=== Title ===
[scale] Why do you keep doing the thing you swore you would stop?
Tony Robbins says the answer is hiding in two simple forces.
=== Two Forces ===
[pain_cloud] Robbins argues that almost everything you do is driven by one of two needs.
[sun] You act to avoid pain, or you act to gain pleasure.
[person] Every habit, good or bad, exists because your brain links it to one of these.
=== Pain Usually Wins ===
[tug_of_war] When the two pull against each other, the book says pain tends to win.
Most people will do far more to avoid pain than to chase pleasure.
[shield] That is why the fear of failing can stop a good plan before it starts.
=== It Is Your Links ===
[brain] Here is the key point.
It is not actual pain that drives you, but the pain you link to something in your mind.
[chain_links] Robbins calls these neuro-associations, connections your nervous system has learned between actions and feelings.
=== Short Versus Long ===
[cake] A second trap is timing.
Many habits bring quick pleasure and slow pain, like an extra slice of cake.
[calendar] Your brain feels the pleasure today, while the cost arrives months later.
So the habit keeps winning.
=== Why We Procrastinate ===
[desk] Robbins uses procrastination as his example.
You put off an important task because starting it feels more painful than waiting.
[clock] Then the deadline gets close, and suddenly waiting hurts more than working.
[pencil] The links flip, and you finally act.
=== Get Leverage ===
[mirror] Robbins says you do not have to wait for a deadline to flip the links.
You can do it on purpose.
[weights] Ask what the old habit is costing you, in your health, money, relationships and pride.
Picture that cost piling up until you truly feel enough is enough.
=== Link New Pleasure ===
[path] Then attach real pleasure to the new behaviour.
[trophy] Imagine, in vivid detail, how it will feel to have made the change.
[balance] When the old habit means pain and the new one means pleasure, change stops being a fight.
=== Your Challenge ===
[notebook] Pick one habit and write down everything it will cost you if you never change.
Then write how good life feels when you do.
