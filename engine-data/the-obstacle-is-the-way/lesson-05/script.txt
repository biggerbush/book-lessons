=== Title ===
[wall] You have been pushing on the same wall for weeks.
What if the smartest move is to stop pushing and walk around it?
=== The Wall Problem ===
[fist] When something blocks us, our instinct is to charge straight at it.
Harder, louder, again.
[cracks] Sometimes that works.
But often the wall barely moves, and we are the ones who end up bruised.
Holiday says there is another way.
=== The Indirect Approach ===
[historian] The military historian Basil Liddell Hart studied many battles across centuries.
[map] He found that decisive victories rarely came from head-on assaults.
[arrows] Most came from flanking moves, surprises, and approaches the enemy did not expect.
The direct route was usually the costliest.
=== Find the Side Door ===
[door] Holiday applies this to everyday life.
If the front entrance is locked, look for a window, a back path, a different person to ask.
[small_team] Smaller players especially cannot win by brute force.
They win by being clever about where they push.
=== Judo Thinking ===
[judo_mat] The second move is even more surprising.
[opponent] In judo, you do not block a charging opponent.
You step aside and let their own momentum carry them to the floor.
Holiday says obstacles can be handled the same way.
Their force can become your force.
=== Gandhi's Example ===
[gandhi] Gandhi faced an empire with soldiers, prisons and money.
He could never match that power directly.
[marchers] So he and his followers refused to fight back, even when beaten or arrested.
[newspaper] Every harsh crackdown made the empire look worse to the world.
Its strength became its weakness.
=== Turn It Around ===
[critic] You can do this too.
A harsh critic can sharpen your work.
[rival] A bigger rival's size can make them slow, giving you room to move fast.
[setback] Even a rejection can point you toward a better target.
Ask what this obstacle's energy could do for you.
=== Try It Today ===
[notebook] Pick one problem you keep attacking head-on.
[two_paths] Write down one way around it and one way to use its force.
Then try the gentler route first.
=== Your Challenge ===
Where are you pushing hardest right now?
What would happen if you stepped aside and let the obstacle do the work?
