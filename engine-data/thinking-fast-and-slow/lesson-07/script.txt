=== Catching Your Mind ===
[brain_alarm] What if you could not fix your mind, but could learn when not to trust it?
That is where Kahneman ends his book.
=== The Two Lines ===
[two_lines] Picture two horizontal lines with arrow tips on their ends.
[fins_out] One has fins pointing outward, the other has fins pointing inward.
The first line looks longer.
[ruler] Measure them with a ruler, and they turn out to be exactly equal.
=== Knowing Is Not Seeing ===
Here is the twist.
Even after measuring, you still see one line as longer.
[eye_open] System 1 keeps producing the illusion, no matter what you know.
[thought_note] What changes is that System 2 now knows not to believe it.
=== Kahneman Admits It ===
[author] Kahneman is honest about his own mind.
After decades of studying these errors, he says his intuitions are not much better than before.
[warning_sign] What improved was his ability to spot situations where mistakes are likely.
=== The Minefield ===
[minefield] His advice is to notice when you are in what he calls a cognitive minefield.
[stop_sign] Then you slow down and call on System 2 for backup.
[big_choice] Think of a big purchase, a major career move, or a snap judgment about a stranger.
Those are moments to pause.
=== Easier in Others ===
There is a catch.
[friend] It is far easier to spot these errors in other people than in ourselves.
[observer] An observer is less mentally busy, and more open to information, than the person caught up in deciding.
=== Better Together ===
[office] That is why Kahneman puts real hope in organizations.
They naturally think more slowly than individuals.
[checklist] They can also impose orderly procedures, like checklists, before a decision is final.
=== Words as Tools ===
[water_cooler] Kahneman wanted to give readers richer words for everyday gossip about judgment.
[label_tags] Naming an error makes it easier to notice the next time it happens.
[colleague] A colleague who knows the term can say, wait, that first number is anchoring us.
=== Your Challenge ===
[question] Before your next big decision, ask yourself one honest question.
Am I in a minefield right now, and should I slow down?
