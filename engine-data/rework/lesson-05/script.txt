=== Too Much Stuff ===
[cluttered_app] Picture a product crammed with fifty features, and none of them work quite right.
What if cutting half of them made it better?
=== Half, Not Half-Baked ===
[two_boxes] Fried and Hansson draw a sharp line between half a product and a bad product.
[half_box] Half a product does a few things, and does them beautifully.
[bad_box] A bad product tries to do everything, and does it all badly.
When time and money are limited, you choose which half to build.
=== Cut the Merely Good ===
[scissors] Their advice is to take your list of ideas and start cutting.
Keep cutting until only the truly essential things remain.
[good_pile] Much of what you remove will be good, and that's the hard part.
Great work comes from dropping things that are merely good.
=== The Hot Dog Comes First ===
[hot_dog_stand] The authors picture someone opening a hot dog stand.
[condiments] It's tempting to fuss over condiments, the cart, the sign, and the name.
[hot_dog] But the first thing to get right is the hot dog itself.
That's the epicenter, the part that can't be removed.
Everything else is a side dish.
=== Say No by Default ===
[no_sign] Then comes a simple rule.
In their words, "Say no by default."
[yes_pile] Every yes adds work, complexity, and something else to maintain.
A no keeps the product focused and simple.
=== Forget the Wish List ===
[request_list] So what about all those customer requests?
The authors suggest listening, then letting them go instead of keeping a giant list.
[repeat_voice] The requests that truly matter will keep coming up again and again.
=== Let Some People Go ===
[power_user] Some customers will want more and more advanced features.
Chasing them can bloat your product and scare away newcomers.
[newcomer] The authors would rather stay simple and let those customers move on.
New people with simpler needs keep arriving.
=== Your Own Epicenter ===
[your_list] Write down everything your project or role is trying to do.
[circle] Circle the one or two things that would make it pointless if removed.
Protect those, and treat everything else as optional.
=== Your Turn ===
[next_request] What is one request or feature you can say no to this week?
Say it kindly, and watch your real work get better.
