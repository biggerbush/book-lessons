=== Title ===
[hook_ear] You can listen perfectly and still leave someone feeling unheard.
Today's trick fixes that in three small steps.
=== Why Listening Fails ===
[two_people] In a disagreement, both people are usually busy preparing their next point.
[thought_bubble] Even when you are paying attention, the other person cannot see it.
[shield] So they assume you are not listening, and their defences go up.
=== The Gun Debate ===
[gun_debate] Duhigg describes efforts to get people on opposite sides of the gun debate to really talk.
[split_table] These are conversations where each side usually expects to be attacked.
What helped was not better arguments, but a simple listening habit called looping for understanding.
=== Step One: Ask ===
[question_mark] Looping starts with a question about what the other person thinks or feels.
[open_door] Ask it with curiosity, as if their answer might teach you something.
[ear] Then listen fully, without planning your reply.
=== Step Two: Repeat ===
[mirror] Next, repeat back what you heard, in your own words.
Not a parrot copy, but your best summary of their meaning.
[heart_icon] Include the feeling you noticed, not just the facts.
=== Step Three: Check ===
[checkmark] Then ask, did I get that right?
[loop_arrow] If they correct you, take it in and try again, until they say yes.
[gift_box] Each correction is a gift, because it shows you what really matters to them.
That circle is the loop.
=== Agree vs Understand ===
[handshake] Looping does not mean you agree.
[scale] It means you have proven you understand, and that is a different thing.
[eye_icon] The goal is to show, not just feel, that you are listening.
Once people feel understood, they relax, and they often start listening back.
=== Try It Out ===
[gun_owner] Imagine a gun owner says, I need to protect my family.
[speech_loop] You might reply, so safety for the people you love matters most to you, is that right?
[thermometer] You have argued nothing, yet the temperature in the room drops.
This is an illustration, but it shows the shape of a loop.
=== Your Challenge ===
[target] In your next disagreement, loop once before you make your own point.
Then notice how the other person's tone changes.
