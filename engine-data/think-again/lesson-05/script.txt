=== Title ===
[hook] What if winning an argument works less like a war and more like a dance?
The best persuaders know a secret most of us miss.
=== The War Mindset ===
[two_fighters] Most of us argue like soldiers on a battlefield.
[arrows] We fire every argument we have and refuse to give any ground.
[wall] But when people feel attacked, they simply dig in and build walls.
=== Human vs Machine ===
[stage] In 2019, a champion debater named Harish Natarajan faced a striking opponent on stage.
[robot] It was an IBM computer called Project Debater, packed with facts.
[topic_card] The question was whether governments should subsidize preschool.
The machine argued for it, and Harish argued against.
=== Start With Agreement ===
[harish] Harish could not match the machine's mountain of evidence.
[handshake] So he began by naming points where he and his opponent actually agreed.
That made the audience feel he was being fair, not just fighting.
[audience] By the end, more listeners had moved toward his side.
=== Experts at the Table ===
[table] Grant also describes research comparing skilled negotiators with average ones.
[magnifier] The researchers watched them at work and noted what the best ones did differently.
[venn] First, skilled negotiators spent far more time finding common ground.
Instead of opening with demands, they looked for shared goals.
=== Fewer, Stronger Points ===
[scale] Second, they used fewer arguments, not more.
[weak_reason] It sounds backwards, but each weak reason dilutes your strongest one.
Listeners latch onto your shakiest point and dismiss the whole case.
[gold_coin] A few solid reasons hit harder than a pile of mixed ones.
=== Ask, Don't Push ===
[question_mark] Third, skilled negotiators asked many more questions.
[ear] Questions show you care what the other person thinks.
Asking keeps the other person thinking, instead of bracing for impact.
[spiral] They also avoided attack and defend spirals, where each jab sparks a sharper one back.
=== The Dance ===
[dancers] Grant suggests treating a disagreement like a dance.
[footsteps] You do not need to lead every step or overpower your partner.
[rhythm] You move together, adjusting as you go, until you find a shared rhythm.
=== Your Next Debate ===
[your_turn] Next time you disagree, open by naming one thing you both agree on.
Then offer your single strongest reason, and ask one genuine question.
