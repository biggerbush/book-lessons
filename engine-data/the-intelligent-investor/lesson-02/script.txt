=== Mr. Market ===
[title_card] Imagine a business partner who shouts a new price at you every single morning.
Benjamin Graham says the smart move is to listen, smile, and decide for yourself.
=== The Partnership ===
[small_shop] Picture a small private business.
[stake_1000] You own a share of it, which cost you 1,000 dollars.
[mr_market] One of your partners is a fellow called Mr. Market.
He is very obliging, and he never misses a day.
=== The Daily Offer ===
[price_note] Every day, he tells you what he thinks your share is worth.
[buy_arrow] Then he offers to buy you out at that price.
[sell_arrow] Or he offers to sell you more of the business, at that same price.
=== His Calm Days ===
Sometimes his price makes perfect sense.
[sales_chart] It matches how the business is actually doing.
[nod_face] On those days, there is nothing to get excited about.
=== His Wild Days ===
But often his feelings run away with him.
[party_hat] When he is giddy, his price shoots far above what the business is worth.
[rain_cloud] When he is gloomy, his price sinks far below it.
Nothing about the business has changed.
Only his mood has.
=== You Can Say No ===
Here is the key point.
You are never forced to trade with him.
[closed_door] You can ignore his offer completely.
[report_book] Instead, judge your share's value from the company's own reports and results.
His daily quote is information, not an instruction.
=== Sell to Euphoria ===
[high_tag] When he offers a ridiculously high price, you can happily sell to him.
[coins_bag] You turn his excitement into cash.
Or you can simply hold on and let him rave.
=== Buy from Despair ===
[low_tag] When he panics and offers a silly low price, you can buy more from him.
[cart_shares] You get a bigger piece of the same business for less.
[stock_screen] Graham's point is that the stock market behaves exactly like this partner.
Every price on your screen is just his latest offer.
=== Your Move ===
Next time prices leap or crash, ask yourself one question.
Has the business really changed, or just Mr. Market's mood?
