=== Why We Say Yes ===
[friend_smile] Would you buy something you don't need, just because a friend asked?
Most of us would, and persuaders know it.
=== The Tupperware Party ===
[party_room] Picture a living room full of guests at a Tupperware party.
[hostess] A sales rep runs the demo, but the real pull comes from the friend who invited you.
[shopping_bag] Cialdini found that guests buy partly to please their hostess, not because they love the containers.
The rule is simple: we say yes to people we like.
=== Good Looks ===
[attractive_man] The first way to build liking is attractiveness.
[halo] Good-looking people get a halo, and we assume they are also kind, smart and honest.
[courtroom] Cialdini reports studies where attractive candidates won more votes and attractive defendants often received lighter sentences.
=== Just Like Me ===
[two_mirrors] Second comes similarity.
[matching_shirt] We warm to people who share our clothes, hometown, hobbies or background.
[camping_gear] Car salespeople are trained to spot camping gear in your trunk, then mention how much they love camping too.
=== I Like You ===
[joe_girard] Third come compliments.
[greeting_card] Joe Girard, called the world's greatest car salesman, mailed every past customer a card each month.
[like_you_text] Inside, Girard's card simply read, "I like you."
We know praise can be strategic, yet it still works on us.
=== Teams Together ===
[summer_camp] Fourth come contact and cooperation.
Simple contact can help, but working toward a shared goal helps far more.
[rival_groups] In a famous summer camp study, two hostile groups of boys made peace only after solving problems together.
[good_cop] Good cop, bad cop uses this too, with one officer acting like your ally.
=== Guilt by Weather ===
[weather_map] Fifth comes association.
[angry_viewer] TV weather forecasters get blamed, even threatened, for bad weather they merely report.
[celeb_product] That's why advertisers pair products with celebrities and sports stars we already adore.
=== Your Defense ===
[alarm_bell] Don't try to block every source of liking, because there are simply too many.
[clock] Instead, notice when you like someone more than makes sense, given how briefly you have known them.
[car_vs_seller] Then mentally separate the person from the deal, and judge the offer on its own.
=== Your Challenge ===
[thinking_face] Next time you feel unusually fond of a salesperson, pause for a moment.
Ask yourself: would I still want this if a stranger were selling it?
