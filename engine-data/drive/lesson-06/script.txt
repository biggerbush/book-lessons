=== Something Bigger ===
[hook] Imagine reaching every goal you set and feeling worse than before.
Daniel Pink says the missing ingredient is often purpose.
=== The Third Partner ===
[three_legs] Autonomy and mastery are powerful drives, but Pink says they work best with a third partner.
[compass] That partner is purpose, the sense that your effort serves a cause bigger than yourself.
[fast_car] Without it, freedom and skill can feel like a fast car with nowhere to go.
=== Asking What Matters ===
[clock] Pink notes that as large generations grow older, many people start asking what their work has really meant.
[question_mark] They want more than a paycheck and a title.
They want to know their time added up to something.
=== Two Kinds of Goals ===
[grads] Researchers at the University of Rochester followed a group of recent college graduates for their first years after school.
[money_path] Some graduates chased profit goals, like becoming wealthy or famous.
[heart_path] Others chased purpose goals, like helping others, learning, and growing as people.
=== A Surprising Result ===
[happy_face] Graduates who reached their purpose goals reported more satisfaction and well-being, with less anxiety and depression.
[trophy] Graduates who reached their profit goals felt no more satisfied than before.
[storm_cloud] Some even reported more anxiety and depression, despite getting exactly the money and status they wanted.
=== Alongside Profit ===
[scale] Pink is not saying profit is bad.
He argues profit should stand beside purpose, not in front of it.
[fuel_can] For organizations, that means treating money as fuel for the mission, not the mission itself.
=== Listen for We ===
[speech_bubble] Purpose shows up in everyday language.
[pronouns] Pink suggests listening for whether people talk about their organization using the word we or the word they.
[outsider] Lots of they is a sign people feel like outsiders, working for someone else's goal.
=== Money With Meaning ===
[cash] Purpose even changes how money feels.
[gift_box] In research by Elizabeth Dunn, people who spent money on others felt happier than those who spent on themselves.
[sticky_note] So this week, take one routine task and write down the person it actually helps.
=== Your Sentence ===
[sentence] If your life's work had to fit into one sentence, what would it say?
And what could you do tomorrow to live up to it?
