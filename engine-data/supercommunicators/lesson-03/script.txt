=== What's It About? ===
[rope] Ever argued with someone for an hour, only to discover you wanted different things all along?
That hour was wasted on a tug of war.
=== The Practical Layer ===
[two_people] Picture two people deciding something together, like where to live.
One wants the cheaper flat, the other wants the bigger one.
[thought_box] On the surface, they trade positions back and forth.
[hidden_goals] Underneath each position sits a goal they rarely say out loud.
=== Why We Get Stuck ===
[push_arrows] When goals stay hidden, each person just pushes their position harder.
[clock] The talk drags on, and nobody moves.
It feels like debate, but it is really a stalemate.
Duhigg says the question to ask is, what is this really about?
=== A Jury in a Room ===
[jury_table] Duhigg describes a real jury, filmed for television, deciding a gun possession case.
[law_book] Some jurors focused on what the law strictly said.
[scales] Others cared most about whether a guilty verdict would be fair.
The two groups kept talking past each other.
=== Goals on the Table ===
[speech_bubble] Progress came as people said plainly what mattered to them and why.
[open_hands] Once goals were visible, they could weigh them together instead of trading blows.
The argument turned into a shared puzzle.
=== Share, Then Ask ===
[my_goal] So start by naming your own goal, simply and honestly.
Keep it short, so it sounds like an offer, not a demand.
[question_mark] Then ask the other person what they hope to get out of this.
[venn] Look for where your goals overlap, and build from there.
=== Rope Becomes Bridge ===
[bridge] Notice what changes.
Instead of pulling against each other, you stand on the same side of the problem.
[map] Disagreements still exist, but now you can see them clearly and plan around them.
Duhigg's point is that practical talks go better when goals are out in the open.
=== Try It Today ===
[notepad] Before your next big decision, write one sentence: what do I actually want here?
[two_chairs] Then open with that sentence, and ask the other person for theirs.
Listen for the goal behind their first answer.
=== Your Challenge ===
[mirror] Think of a disagreement you keep having.
Do you truly know what the other person wants from it?
