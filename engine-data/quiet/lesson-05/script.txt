=== Chasing the Prize ===
[gold_trophy] Why do some people sprint toward a prize while others stop to read the fine print?
Susan Cain says the answer lies in how our brains respond to reward.
=== The Reward Buzz ===
[brain_glow] Cain describes a kind of excited buzz we feel when chasing something we want.
[dopamine_spark] It is linked to dopamine, a brain chemical tied to wanting and anticipation.
[party_crowd] Extroverts seem to feel this buzz more strongly, whether the prize is money, status or a lively party.
=== Gas and Brakes ===
[gas_pedal] Think of reward sensitivity as a gas pedal pushing you forward.
[brake_pedal] Introverts tend to tap the brakes more, pausing to notice warning signs.
Neither pedal is better. A car needs both.
=== The Card Game ===
[card_deck] Cain describes research by psychologist Joseph Newman using a simple card game.
[win_lose_sign] Players could win or lose points, and the odds slowly turned against them.
[running_man] Many extroverts kept playing, chasing the next win even as losses grew.
=== The Pause ===
[pause_button] Introverts were more likely to slow down after a loss.
[thought_cloud] They took a moment to reflect on what went wrong, and then adjusted.
That small pause helped them quit before the losses piled up.
=== Wall Street's Lesson ===
[stock_chart] Cain asks whether unchecked reward seeking played a part in the 2008 financial crash.
[bold_trader] She suggests bold, risk-hungry voices often won the room, while cautious voices were brushed aside.
=== Buffett's Calm ===
[buffett_desk] Investor Warren Buffett is Cain's model of the cautious mind.
[tech_bubble] In 1999, he warned a room of business leaders that soaring tech stocks were overpriced.
[falling_line] Many thought he was out of touch, until the bubble burst and proved him right.
=== Balance the Team ===
[team_table] The lesson is not that caution always wins.
[scale_balance] Groups make better decisions when the eager voices and the careful voices both get heard.
[raised_hand] If you are the quiet one, your doubts may be exactly what the team needs.
=== Your Turn ===
Next time you feel the rush of a big opportunity, pause and ask one question.
What would the most careful person in the room want to know?
