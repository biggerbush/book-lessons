=== Up and to the Right ===
[rising_chart] Your numbers are climbing every month, and everyone is celebrating.
But what if that chart is quietly lying to you?
=== The Flattering Total ===
[total_users] Eric Ries calls them vanity metrics, like total signups or total revenue.
[cheer_team] They almost always go up, which makes the team feel great.
[fog_cloud] But they cannot tell you whether your latest work actually made things better.
=== IMVU's Happy Chart ===
[imvu_office] Ries saw this firsthand at IMVU, the avatar chat company he cofounded.
[growth_graph] Their monthly charts of registered users and revenue rose steadily.
On paper, the business looked healthy.
[new_features] Meanwhile, the team kept shipping new features and improvements, week after week.
=== The Hidden Question ===
[question_mark] Yet nobody could say which of those changes, if any, were helping.
Growth might simply come from more ads bringing in more people.
[busy_hamster] The team was working hard, but they could not see if they were learning.
=== Slicing Into Cohorts ===
[cohort_groups] So they switched to cohort analysis, grouping customers by when they joined.
[funnel] For each group, they tracked what share took key steps, like chatting with someone or paying.
[side_by_side] Now every new group could be compared with the groups before it.
=== The Flat Truth ===
[flat_lines] The result was sobering.
Months of new features had barely changed how each new group behaved.
[mirror] The rising totals had hidden a product that was not really improving.
That hard truth forced them to rethink what customers truly wanted.
=== Three Tests ===
[three_a] Ries suggests good metrics share three qualities.
[cause_effect] Actionable means the number shows clear cause and effect, so you know what to do next.
Vanity metrics usually fail this first test.
[open_report] Accessible means everyone can understand it, and auditable means you can trust where it came from.
=== Your Next Move ===
[notebook] Pick one change you made recently.
[before_after] Compare people who arrived before it with people who arrived after it.
[split_test] Even better, show the change to only half your new customers and compare.
If their behavior did not shift, the change did not matter, however good the totals look.
=== Your Challenge ===
[mystery_dash] Look at your favorite number this week.
Does it tell you what to do next, or does it just make you feel good?
