=== Title ===
[two_faces] What if every conversation could leave you understanding someone better than before?
This lesson puts all the pieces together.
=== Beyond Winning ===
[scoreboard] Most of us treat conversations like a match we need to win.
[open_door] Duhigg suggests a different goal, which he calls a learning conversation.
The aim is not to persuade, but for both people to understand each other and feel understood.
=== A CIA Lesson ===
[cia_officer] In the book, Duhigg describes a CIA officer named Jim Lawler.
[stranger] His job was to build trust with people who had every reason to keep their guard up.
[key] What helped him most was not pressure, but paying close attention to what the other person needed.
=== Step One: Notice ===
[radar] The first step is simply to notice what kind of conversation is happening.
[three_bubbles] Is this person trying to solve a problem, share a feeling, or express who they are?
Pause and ask yourself that before you respond.
=== Step Two: Goals ===
[target] Next, share what you want from this conversation, and ask what they want.
[handshake] When goals are out in the open, you stop guessing and start working together.
=== Step Three: Feelings ===
[heart] Then ask about feelings, and be willing to share your own.
[question_mark] A simple question like, how did that feel for you, can open a door.
[mirror] When you offer something honest about yourself in return, the other person feels safer to go deeper.
=== Step Four: Identity ===
[id_badges] Finally, explore who the other person is.
Everyone carries many identities, like parent, neighbour, nurse, or football fan.
[bridge] Look for the ones you share, and you build a bridge instead of a wall.
This matters most when you disagree, because shared identities make hard messages easier to hear.
=== Putting It Together ===
[four_steps] These four steps do not have to happen in order.
[dial] Think of them as dials you adjust as the conversation moves.
[lightbulb] The skill is staying curious, so that both of you walk away knowing something new.
Duhigg's point is that this is not a talent. It is practice.
=== Your Challenge ===
[calendar] In your next real conversation, try one step on purpose, and notice what changes.
