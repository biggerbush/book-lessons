[full_desk] Picture a desk buried under papers, alerts and half-finished tasks.
[foggy_window] A crowded mind works like a foggy window, hiding what really matters.

=== Blank Time ===
[ceo_calendar] As reported in the book, Jeff Weiner, while running LinkedIn, blocked out blank time in his calendar.
[empty_blocks] He kept up to two hours a day free, in thirty minute chunks, with nothing scheduled.
[think_bubble] He came to see it as vital for thinking about the company's direction.
=== Think Week ===
[cabin] For years, Bill Gates took a Think Week twice a year, stepping away just to read and think.
[book_stack] He would read papers and ideas he never had time for during ordinary weeks.
It was distance, so the big picture could come back into focus.
=== Protect the Asset ===
[battery] Thinking clearly also depends on something simpler: sleep.
McKeown calls this "protect the asset," and the asset is you.
[late_night] Yet many of us wear lost sleep like a badge of commitment.
[sleepy_face] But a tired brain struggles to weigh options and spot what is trivial.
=== The Violinists ===
[violin] The book describes research on young violinists by the psychologist Anders Ericsson.
[top_players] The best players worked intensely, but they also slept more than the others.
[sleep_clock] They averaged about 8.6 hours of sleep in every 24, including naps.
Their rest was part of their excellence.
=== Room to Play ===
[kite] Play matters too.
[playful_brain] The book draws on play researcher Stuart Brown, who links play with creativity and flexible thinking.
[new_idea] When we play, we explore without pressure, and fresh possibilities surface.
