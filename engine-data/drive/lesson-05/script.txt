=== Getting Better ===
[climber] Think of the last time you lost track of time because a task was hard, but just hard enough.
That feeling is the doorway to mastery.
=== The Flow Zone ===
[researcher] Psychologist Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi studied the moments when people feel most alive.
[flow_wave] He called them flow, when the challenge fits our skill so well that we forget the clock.
Pink argues that flow is where mastery begins.
=== Goldilocks Tasks ===
[easy_task] If a task is too easy, we get bored.
[hard_task] If it is far too hard, we get anxious.
[just_right] The sweet spot is a Goldilocks task, just slightly beyond what we can already do.
=== Mastery Is a Mindset ===
[dweck] Pink draws on psychologist Carol Dweck to show that mastery starts in our heads.
[fixed_box] Some people believe ability is fixed, like a box you were handed at birth.
[growing_tree] Others believe ability grows with effort, like a tree you can keep tending.
Only the second belief makes practice feel worthwhile.
=== Mastery Is a Pain ===
[practice] Getting good also takes long, effortful practice, and much of it is not fun.
[scales] Think of a pianist repeating the same difficult passage again and again.
Flow comes in bright moments, but the road between them is often a grind.
=== The Grit Test ===
[west_point] Pink describes Angela Duckworth's research with new cadets at the West Point military academy.
[grit_gauge] She measured grit, meaning passion and perseverance toward long-term goals.
[finish_line] Grit predicted who would get through the brutal first summer better than other measures did.
=== Never Quite There ===
[curve] Picture a line rising toward a ceiling, getting closer and closer but never quite touching it.
[quote_card] Pink puts it simply: "Mastery is an asymptote."
[runner] We can never fully arrive, and that is exactly what keeps us chasing.
Pink says the frustration is part of the appeal, because the joy lives in the pursuit.
=== Your Next Step ===
[calendar] Pick one skill that truly matters to you.
[ladder] Set a task just one rung above your current level.
[notebook] Practice it on purpose, notice what goes wrong, and adjust next time.
=== Your Challenge ===
What skill would you keep practicing even if you knew you could never perfect it?
This week, give it 20 focused minutes.
