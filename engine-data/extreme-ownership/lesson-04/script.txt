=== Keep It Simple ===
[tangled_plan] If your team cannot explain your plan, you do not really have a plan.
You just have noise.
=== Why Simple Wins ===
[battlefield] Jocko and Leif learned this in combat, where plans meet chaos fast.
[radio] Gunfire, noise and fear make it hard to hear, think or remember.
[broken_gears] When something goes wrong, a complex plan has too many parts that can break.
Every member of the team must understand the plan, from the leader to the newest person.
A simple plan still works when everything around it falls apart.
=== When Things Go Wrong ===
[surprise] In the field, something always goes wrong.
Complexity makes each surprise bigger, because no one knows how to adjust.
[clear_path] Simple plans give people room to recover, because everyone knows the goal and their role.
=== The Bonus Nobody Got ===
[factory] Leif later worked with a manufacturing company that had a performance problem.
[bonus_chart] Management had built a bonus plan for frontline supervisors, packed with many metrics.
On paper, it looked thorough and smart.
It was meant to boost results, but it did not change much.
=== Ask the Front Line ===
[supervisor] Leif asked the supervisors a simple question.
How do you earn your bonus?
[shrug] They could not explain it.
If people do not understand the plan, it cannot shape what they do each day.
=== Cut It Down ===
[scissors] Leif suggested stripping the plan down to just a few clear measures.
[two_targets] Now every supervisor could see exactly what mattered and how to hit it.
The goal was not less ambition, just less confusion.
That clarity is what finally drives behavior.
=== The Leader's Job ===
[speech_bubble] The authors put the responsibility on the leader, not the listener.
If your team is confused, you have not communicated clearly enough.
[raised_hand] Good leaders invite questions and make it safe to say, I do not get it.
Asking for clarity is a strength, not a weakness.
=== Test Your Message ===
[mirror] Here is a practical test.
After you share a plan, ask someone to explain it back in their own words.
[checkmark] If they can, you are ready.
If they stumble, simplify and try again.
=== Your Challenge ===
[sticky_note] Take one plan you are running now and shrink it to a few plain sentences.
Then ask your team to repeat it back.
