=== Fail Forward ===
[broken_bar] What if your biggest failure was actually a set of instructions?
Today, we learn to read it.
=== The Record Attempt ===
[goggins_bar] David Goggins once set out to break the world record for most pull-ups in 24 hours.
It was a brutal test of strength and will.
[crowd] He tried in public, with people watching and cheering him on.
[clock] Hours into the attempt, his arms gave out, and he had to stop far short of the goal.
=== Failing Again ===
[second_try] He came back for a second attempt.
[empty_bar] It failed too.
Most of us would quit here and call it proof that we were never good enough.
[head_down] Goggins felt that pain too, but he refused to let it be the end.
=== The Military Habit ===
[soldiers] In the military, teams review every mission afterward, win or lose.
[report] This review is called an after-action report.
It asks simple questions: what happened, what worked, and what must change?
The goal is not blame.
The goal is learning.
=== Study the Wreckage ===
[notebook] Goggins applied that same habit to his failed record attempts.
[magnifier] He looked hard at every detail, from his setup and equipment to his training and plan.
He did not hide behind excuses or blame bad luck.
He wanted honest data.
=== Count the Wins Too ===
[thumbs_up] A good report also lists what went right.
[checklist] Even a failed attempt holds strengths worth keeping.
Those strengths become the foundation for the next try.
=== Fix and Return ===
[tools] Armed with his notes, Goggins changed what had gone wrong.
[third_try] Then he stepped up to the bar a third time.
Same man, same bar, but a smarter plan.
[trophy] This time he did more than 4,000 pull-ups and claimed the record.
=== Your Own Report ===
[pen_paper] You can do this with any failure, from a lost job to a broken habit.
[columns] Write down what went well, what went badly, and how you handled it emotionally.
[arrow_up] Then choose one fix and schedule your next attempt.
Failure only wins if you stop studying it.
=== Your Challenge ===
[old_goal] Think of one goal you gave up on.
What would your after-action report say, and when will you try again?
