=== Constraints ===
[hourglass] What if the things you think are holding you back are secretly your best tools?
That's the surprising claim at the heart of this lesson.
=== The Usual Wish ===
[wish_list] Most of us believe we could do great work if only we had more.
[money_pile] More money, more time, more people.
Fried and Hansson think that wish is often a trap.
[crowd] Big resources make it easy to throw money and bodies at a problem instead of thinking.
=== In Disguise ===
[quote_card] As they put it, "Constraints are advantages in disguise."
[toolbox] When you have little, you are forced to make do with what you've got.
There's no room for waste, so you have to get creative.
=== Building Basecamp ===
[laptop] When 37signals built Basecamp, they were far from a dream setup.
[client_folder] They were still running a web design business, with client work to finish.
[empty_wallet] They had no outside funding, and the team was small.
=== The Time Gap ===
[globe] David, the programmer, was in Denmark, while the others were in the United States.
[two_clocks] That meant a seven-hour time difference between the people building the product.
Long, constant back-and-forth discussions simply weren't possible.
=== Forced to Be Simple ===
[simple_app] Together, these limits pushed them toward a simple product.
They had to focus on what truly mattered and build only that.
[star_badge] That simplicity became a defining strength of Basecamp.
=== Small Beats Big ===
[big_company] A well-funded competitor can afford to be slow, bloated and wasteful.
[tiny_boat] A small team has to stay nimble, so it moves faster and changes direction easily.
Fewer people also means less coordination and fewer layers of approval.
=== Your Own Limits ===
[notebook] So look at your own situation with fresh eyes.
Maybe you only have weekends, a tiny budget, or just yourself.
[lightbulb] Instead of waiting for more, ask what that limit makes possible.
A short deadline forces decisions, and a small budget forces clever choices.
=== Your Challenge ===
[sticky_note] Pick one limit you've been complaining about this week.
Write down one way it could push you toward a simpler, smarter solution, then try it before asking for more.
