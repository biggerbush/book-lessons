=== Hours That Vanish ===
[clock_spin] Have you ever looked up from something and realised three hours had disappeared?
That feeling might be one of the best clues to your ikigai.
=== What Flow Feels Like ===
[painter] Picture a painter so absorbed that she forgets to eat.
[thought_cloud] Her worries fade, and even her sense of self goes quiet.
The authors call this state flow, and they treat it as a key to a happy life.
=== Naming the State ===
[psychologist] The idea comes from psychologist Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi, who studied when people feel most engaged.
[happy_face] He found that people are often happiest when completely absorbed in what they are doing.
=== The Sweet Spot ===
[seesaw] Flow lives in a balance between challenge and skill.
[yawn_face] If a task is too easy, we drift into boredom.
[sweat_face] If it is too hard, we tip into anxiety.
Aim for something just a little beyond what feels comfortable.
=== Know Your Target ===
[target] Flow also needs a clear goal.
[map_route] When you know what you are aiming for, your mind stops wandering and settles into the work.
Vague plans leave room for doubt, while a clear objective pulls you in.
=== One Thing at a Time ===
[juggler] The authors warn against multitasking, which scatters attention.
[phone_drawer] Put your phone out of sight and close extra tabs.
[single_candle] Give one task your full focus, and flow has room to begin.
=== The Sushi Master ===
[sushi_chef] The book points to Jiro Ono, a Tokyo sushi chef who has devoted most of his life to his craft.
[sushi_plate] Well into his nineties, he was still perfecting the same dishes, day after day.
[workshop] The authors see the same devotion in many Japanese artisans, who find deep joy in mastering a single skill.
=== Flow Points to Ikigai ===
[compass] The activities that put you in flow are strong hints about your ikigai.
[hobby_board] They can live in your job or in a hobby, like cooking, carpentry or music.
The more time you spend in flow, the more your days feel meaningful.
=== Your Flow Hunt ===
[notebook] This week, note the moments when time seems to vanish.
Then clear one distraction-free hour to do more of exactly that.
