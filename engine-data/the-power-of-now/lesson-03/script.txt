=== Two Kinds of Time ===
[two_clocks] Your mind runs on two kinds of time, and only one of them is truly useful.
Learning to tell them apart can quietly end a lot of worry.
=== Clock Time ===
[wall_clock] Eckhart Tolle calls the first kind clock time.
[calendar] It is the time you use to book a dentist appointment or catch a train.
[map_route] It helps you plan a trip, learn a skill and keep your promises.
Tolle has no problem with clock time at all.
=== Psychological Time ===
[thought_cloud] The second kind lives only in your head.
Tolle calls it psychological time.
[rewind_icon] It is the mind replaying old scenes over and over again.
[fast_forward] It is also the mind rushing ahead, rehearsing everything that might go wrong.
=== Learning From Mistakes ===
[broken_vase] Tolle gives a simple example: a mistake you once made.
[notebook] Using clock time, you notice the lesson and simply do better next time.
[heavy_sack] In psychological time, you carry that mistake around, blaming yourself and feeling guilty for years.
Same event, two very different burdens.
=== Chasing a Goal ===
[mountain_flag] His second example is a goal you want to reach.
[hiking_boots] Clock time sets the goal and takes the next practical step toward it.
[mirage] Psychological time believes you will only be happy once you finally arrive.
So the present becomes just a hallway to somewhere better.
=== Signs You Are Stuck ===
[storm_cloud] Tolle offers a useful clue for spotting the difference.
Worry, tension and anxiety usually mean too much future.
[old_photos] Regret, resentment and bitterness usually mean too much past.
[scale] Both pull your attention away from the only place life actually happens.
=== Use It, Then Return ===
[toolbox] Tolle's advice is not to throw away your clocks or your plans.
Treat clock time like a tool.
[hand_tool] Pick it up when you need it, then put it down again.
[open_door] Then come straight back to awareness of this moment.
=== A Daily Habit ===
[sticky_note] Try this whenever you notice your mind drifting.
Ask yourself one quiet question.
[question_mark] Is this thought helping me plan, or just replaying and worrying?
[deep_breath] If it is practical, act on it; if not, let it go and feel your breath.
=== Your Challenge ===
Today, catch one worry and ask which kind of time it belongs to.
Then take one practical step, and return to now.
