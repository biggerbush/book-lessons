=== The Loudest Voice ===
[meeting_room] In most meetings, the loudest or most senior voice wins.
Ray Dalio thinks that is a terrible way to decide anything.
=== Wired Differently ===
[brain_pair] Dalio argues that people are wired very differently.
[big_picture] Some people see the big picture clearly but miss the details.
[detail_lens] Others catch every detail but lose sight of the big picture.
So no single mind sees everything, and that includes yours.
=== Not All Votes Equal ===
[ballot_box] The obvious fix is to let everyone vote.
But one person, one vote ignores who actually knows what they are talking about.
[scale] Instead, Dalio weighs each opinion by how believable the person is.
=== Two Tests ===
[trophy_three] First test: has this person done the thing successfully, at least three times?
[chain_links] Second test: can they clearly explain the cause and effect behind their view?
A track record without an explanation might just be luck.
A great explanation without a track record is just a theory.
=== The Health Scare ===
[doctor_desk] Dalio describes a serious health scare that came with a frightening diagnosis.
[three_doctors] He sought out several top specialists, and their advice disagreed sharply.
[phone_call] So he got them talking to each other and probed their reasoning.
Hearing believable experts test each other showed him which arguments held up.
That gave him a far better decision than any single opinion could.
=== Earn Your Opinion ===
[mirror] The same test applies to you.
Dalio puts it bluntly: "Ask yourself whether you have earned the right to have an opinion."
[question_mark] If you have not, ask questions instead of arguing.
=== Ask, Then Listen ===
[student_desk] With someone more believable, your job is to understand their thinking, not defend yours.
[equal_chairs] With someone equally believable, debate openly as peers.
[small_voice] And a less experienced person can still be right, so check their reasoning before dismissing it.
=== Build Your Panel ===
[three_chairs] Before your next big decision, find three people who have done it successfully before.
Ask each one to explain why, not just what.
Then give the most weight to the clearest reasoning backed by the strongest record.
=== Your Turn ===
[open_door] Think of a decision you are stuck on right now.
Whose opinion are you trusting, and have they earned it?
