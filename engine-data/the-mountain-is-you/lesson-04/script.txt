=== Feelings as Messengers ===
[envelope] What if every strong feeling was a letter, not an order?
Today, we learn how to read the mail without obeying it.
=== Felt, Not Followed ===
[wave] Wiest describes emotional intelligence as letting feelings move through you.
You feel them fully, without pushing them away.
[steering_wheel] But you also do not hand them the steering wheel.
Feeling something and acting on it are two different steps.
=== Why We Get Stuck ===
[locked_box] When we suppress a feeling, it does not disappear.
It gets stored, and it leaks out later in ways we do not choose.
[storm_cloud] When we obey every feeling, we get pushed around by moods instead.
Both paths leave us out of control.
=== Anger Has a Message ===
[fence] In the book, anger often signals that a boundary has been crossed.
It points at something you care about protecting.
[open_hand] The message is useful, even if yelling is not.
=== Sadness and Guilt ===
[teardrop] Sadness can show you what you value, or what you need to grieve.
[compass] Guilt can show you where your actions drifted from who you want to be.
Each feeling carries directions, not verdicts.
=== Gut Versus Fear ===
[two_voices] Wiest also helps us tell intuition apart from fear.
[calm_pond] Intuition tends to be quiet, calm, and steady.
[alarm_bell] Fear tends to be loud, urgent, and frantic, demanding you act right now.
Panic is rarely the voice to follow.
=== A Simple Example ===
[phone] Imagine a friend cancels plans, and you feel a sudden sting.
[thought_bubble] The feeling might say, nobody cares about me.
That is a story, not a fact.
[notebook] Reading the message instead, you might notice you simply need more connection this week.
Then you can choose to ask for it.
=== Pause, Name, Choose ===
[pause_button] So when a big feeling arrives, pause before you react.
[name_tag] Name it plainly, like, this is anger, or this is fear.
[signpost] Ask what it is pointing toward, then decide what you actually want to do.
=== Your Challenge ===
Next time a strong feeling shows up, write down its message in one sentence.
Then ask yourself, what would I choose if I were calm?
