=== A Hidden Bet ===
[bet_chips] Every business plan looks like a map, but underneath it is a pile of bets.
Which one could quietly sink yours?
=== Bets Behind the Plan ===
[founder] Picture a founder with a bold new idea.
[spreadsheet] Her plan is full of confident numbers about customers and sales.
But Eric Ries says many of those numbers are really guesses.
[leap_arrow] He calls the riskiest guesses leap of faith assumptions, because the whole business jumps on them.
=== The Value Bet ===
[two_pillars] Ries says two assumptions matter most.
[value_heart] The first is the value hypothesis: do people actually get value from this once they use it?
Not what they say in a survey, but what they really do.
=== The Growth Bet ===
[growth_tree] The second is the growth hypothesis: how will new customers discover this?
[ripple] Will happy users bring friends, or will you pay for every single sign up?
A product people love can still stall if nobody hears about it.
=== Facebook's Value ===
[campus] Ries points to Facebook in its early days.
[laptop_user] It had little revenue at first, yet investors were excited.
Why?
Users weren't just signing up.
They kept coming back and spending lots of time there.
That was evidence for the value bet.
=== Facebook's Growth ===
[map_pins] When Facebook launched at a new college, it spread across that campus very quickly.
[no_ads] It did this without spending money on advertising.
That was strong evidence for the growth bet.
=== Spot the Riskiest ===
[walkman] To find your riskiest bets, Ries suggests looking at analogs and antilogs.
Apple could point to the Walkman, proving people would listen to music through earphones in public.
[napster_icon] Napster proved people would download music, but it gave songs away free.
[question_mark] So Apple's true leap of faith was whether people would pay for music.
=== Test It First ===
[magnifier] Once you spot the riskiest bet, test it before anything else.
[door_open] Ries urges founders to leave the office and see real customers firsthand.
[checklist] Write each assumption down, rank it by risk, and start at the top.
=== Your Leap ===
Look at your own idea today.
What is the one assumption that, if wrong, makes everything else you are building pointless?
