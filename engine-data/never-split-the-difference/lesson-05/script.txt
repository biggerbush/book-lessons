=== Who Is In Control? ===
[steering] What if the strongest move in a negotiation was handing the other side the steering wheel?
Or at least, letting them feel like they are holding it.
=== The Illusion of Control ===
[two_people] Voss noticed that people dig in when they feel cornered or pushed.
[crown] So he lets the other side feel they are in charge.
[question_mark] His tool is the open question, which he calls a calibrated question.
=== Start With How or What ===
[how_what] Calibrated questions begin with how or what.
[yes_no_box] They cannot be answered with a simple yes or no.
[thought_cloud] Instead, they make the other person stop, think, and explain.
And every explanation hands you new information.
=== Skip the Why ===
[why_finger] Voss steers away from questions that start with why.
To most people, why sounds like an accusation.
[defensive_man] It puts them on the defensive, just when you need them open.
=== The Ransom Demand ===
[kidnapper] Picture a kidnapper on the phone, demanding a huge ransom.
[angry_face] A flat no could anger him.
A quick yes would reward him and invite more demands.
[phone] So Voss's favorite reply is a question: how am I supposed to do that?
=== Their Problem Now ===
[puzzle] That question hands the kidnapper a puzzle to solve.
[gears] He now has to think about the obstacles on your side.
Voss describes this as forcing empathy.
[lightbulb] The other person starts working on your problem as if it were their own.
=== Everyday Versions ===
[boss] You can use the same move with a boss, a client, or a landlord.
[speech_bubble] Try asking, what about this is important to you?
Or ask, how can we solve this problem?
[compass] Each question invites them to help, while you quietly steer the conversation.
=== Stay Calm and Curious ===
[calm_face] These questions only work if your tone stays gentle and curious.
[clock] Ask, then pause, and let the silence do its work.
Give them room to answer fully.
Remember, the person asking the questions is the one really guiding the talk.
=== Your Turn ===
[your_turn] Next time someone makes a demand, resist the urge to say yes or no.
Instead ask, how am I supposed to do that, and listen closely to what comes back.
