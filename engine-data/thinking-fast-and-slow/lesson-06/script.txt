=== Two Ways to Live ===
[hook_clock] What if the person who lives your life is not the one who decides what comes next?
=== Two Selves ===
[self_now] Kahneman describes an experiencing self, the one living each moment as it happens.
[self_memory] He also describes a remembering self, the one who looks back and keeps score.
[split_line] These two selves often disagree about how good or bad something was.
=== The Colonoscopy Study ===
[patient_a] In one study Kahneman reports, patients rated their pain every minute during a colonoscopy.
[patient_b] One patient's procedure lasted 8 minutes, while another's lasted 24 minutes.
[pain_graph] The longer one clearly contained more total pain.
Yet the first patient remembered it as worse, because it ended at a painful moment.
=== The Peak and the End ===
[peak_spike] Memory did not add up every minute.
Instead, it averaged two moments: the worst point, and the very end.
[end_marker] Kahneman calls this the peak end rule.
A gentle ending softened the whole memory.
=== Duration Neglect ===
[stopwatch] The length of the experience barely mattered at all.
Many extra minutes of pain did little to change how bad it was remembered.
[faded_bar] Kahneman calls this duration neglect.
=== The Cold Water Test ===
[cold_bowl] In another experiment, people held one hand in painfully cold water for 60 seconds.
[longer_trial] In a second trial, they held it for 60 seconds, then 30 more while the water warmed slightly.
That second trial was strictly worse: the same pain, plus extra.
[choice_arrow] Yet when asked which to repeat, most people chose the longer one.
=== A Spoiled Ending ===
[concert_hall] Kahneman recalls a man enjoying a long symphony recording.
[scratch_mark] Near the end, a screeching sound ruined the whole experience, the man said.
But the music had been wonderful for most of the time.
Only the memory was spoiled, not the experience itself.
=== Who Makes Choices ===
[decision_desk] Here is the catch: the remembering self makes our decisions.
It picks the next holiday or doctor based on stories.
[quote_card] As Kahneman puts it, "I am my remembering self."
[stranger] The experiencing self, who actually lives through the hours, gets little say.
=== Your Challenge ===
Next time you plan something, ask: am I choosing for the memory, or for the moments?
And whenever you can, give good experiences a gentle ending.
