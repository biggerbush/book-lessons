=== Working Harder ===
[title_coins] What if working harder for your paycheck is exactly what keeps you stuck?
Robert Kiyosaki learned the answer at nine years old, dusting cans.
=== Ten Cents an Hour ===
[young_robert] At nine, Robert and his best friend Mike asked Mike's dad to teach them how to get rich.
[store] Rich dad put them to work in one of his stores.
[dusty_cans] They dusted cans for three hours every Saturday, for just 10 cents an hour.
=== Asking for a Raise ===
[angry_boy] After a few weeks, Robert felt cheated and marched in to complain.
[zero_pay] Rich dad's answer surprised him, because he cut their pay to nothing.
Now the boys were working for free.
=== Fear and Desire ===
[fear_cloud] Rich dad explained that most adults are ruled by two feelings, and the first is fear of not paying the bills.
[desire_cart] The second is desire for the nicer things that money could buy.
[alarm_clock] So people wake up, go to work, get paid, and repeat.
=== The Rat Race ===
[rat_wheel] Kiyosaki calls this endless loop the rat race.
[raise_arrow] A raise rarely frees anyone, because spending tends to rise right along with it.
[bills_pile] The fear simply returns, now with bigger bills.
=== Spotting Opportunity ===
Working without pay forced the boys to look at the store with fresh eyes.
[comic_stack] They noticed unsold comic books being thrown away.
[handshake] They asked if they could keep them, and the answer was yes.
=== Comic Book Library ===
[basement] The boys opened a little comic book library in a basement.
[coin_jar] Neighbourhood kids paid a small fee to sit and read.
[sister] They hired Mike's sister to look after it.
Now money came in even when the boys were somewhere else.
=== Money Working for You ===
[quote_card] As rich dad put it, "The poor and the middle class work for money."
[lightbulb] The rich learn to build things that make money work for them.
[notebook] So when fear or desire pushes you, pause, and look for the opportunity hiding in plain sight.
=== Your Challenge ===
[question_mark] This week, notice one moment when fear or desire drives a money choice.
Then ask yourself, what could I build that earns while I am not there?
