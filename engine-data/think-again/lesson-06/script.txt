=== Title ===
[title_card] What if the fastest way to change a mind is to stop talking?
Today we learn to open minds by listening first.
=== The Pushback Problem ===
[arguing_pair] When someone holds a view we think is wrong, our instinct is to argue.
[brick_wall] But pressure often makes people dig in and defend their belief even harder.
Adam Grant says there is a better path.
=== A Worried Mother ===
[mother_baby] In Quebec, a mother named Marie-Hélène had just given birth to a premature baby boy.
[vaccine_vial] She had read frightening claims online, and she did not want him vaccinated.
[nurse] Nurses tried to persuade her, but she held firm.
=== The Doctor Who Asked ===
[doctor] Then a neonatologist named Arnaud Gagneur came to talk with her.
[question_mark] He did not lecture her or pile on facts.
[two_chairs] He asked about her worries, listened closely, and treated her as a capable parent.
=== Her Own Decision ===
[checkmark] By the end of their conversation, Marie-Hélène decided to vaccinate her son.
Nobody cornered or shamed her.
[open_door] She had talked her way to her own reasons for saying yes.
The change came from inside her, not from outside pressure.
=== Motivational Interviewing ===
[book_icon] Gagneur was using motivational interviewing, a method developed by psychologists William Miller and Stephen Rollnick.
[key] Its core belief is simple.
You cannot force people to change.
You can only help them find their own motivation.
=== Three Simple Moves ===
[open_question] First, ask open questions that invite people to explore, not just answer yes or no.
[mirror] Second, listen reflectively by saying back what you heard, so they feel understood.
[thumbs_up] Third, affirm their ability to change, while leaving the choice in their hands.
=== Why It Works ===
[ear] When people feel truly heard, they relax and lower their defenses.
[scale] Talking through their own doubts lets them weigh both sides more honestly.
[lightbulb] And a conclusion they reach themselves feels like their own, so they are more likely to act on it.
=== Your Turn ===
Next time you want to change someone's mind, ask one curious question before making any point.
Then listen, and say back what you heard.
