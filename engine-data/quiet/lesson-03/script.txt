=== Born Sensitive ===
[title_baby] What if your quiet nature showed up before you could even talk?
Some scientists think they can spot it in babies just four months old.
=== The Lab Experiment ===
[kagan] Developmental psychologist Jerome Kagan set out to study where temperament comes from.
[babies] He brought about 500 four-month-old babies into his lab at Harvard.
[observers] His team watched each baby meet a series of new experiences.
=== A Burst of Newness ===
[balloon] The babies heard balloons pop and listened to recorded voices.
[mobile] They watched colorful mobiles dance in front of their eyes.
[cotton_swab] They even sniffed cotton swabs dipped in alcohol.
=== Two Kinds of Babies ===
[crying_baby] About 20 percent of the babies reacted strongly, pumping their arms and legs and crying.
Kagan called them high-reactive.
[calm_baby] About 40 percent stayed calm, barely moving or fussing.
These were the low-reactive babies.
[middle_baby] The rest fell somewhere in between.
=== The Bold Prediction ===
[crystal_ball] Kagan predicted the loud, wriggling babies would grow into quiet, careful kids.
[calm_arrow] And he guessed the calm babies would become relaxed, outgoing ones.
It sounds backwards, until you see what reactivity really means.
Their nervous systems are simply more sensitive to everything around them.
=== Growing Up ===
[calendar] Kagan's team checked in on the children at ages 2, 4, 7 and 11.
[shy_child] Many high-reactive babies grew into cautious children, who hung back with strangers and thought before acting.
[bold_child] Many low-reactive babies became bolder, chattier and more at ease in new situations.
=== Inside the Brain ===
[brain] Kagan suspected the difference lives in a small brain region called the amygdala.
[alarm_bell] It acts like an alarm system, reacting to anything new or possibly threatening.
In high-reactive people, that alarm seems to ring louder and sooner.
That can make a busy party feel overwhelming, even for someone who loves people.
=== Born, Not Bound ===
[twins] Cain notes that twin studies suggest introversion is roughly 40 to 50 percent heritable.
[seedling] That makes temperament a starting point, not a life sentence.
Not every high-reactive baby became an introvert.
[checkmark] So next time you need quiet after a crowded day, treat it as your wiring, not a weakness.
=== Your Turn ===
[mirror] Think back to how you reacted to new places as a child.
What might your own early sensitivity reveal about the way you work and rest today?
