=== Start Embarrassed ===
[hook_box] What if the smartest first version of your idea is one you would be embarrassed to show anyone?
Eric Ries says that discomfort is often a good sign.
=== The Hidden Workshop ===
[workshop] Picture a team building in secret for months, polishing every feature.
[launch_day] On launch day, they finally show it to customers.
[empty_store] And the customers simply walk past.
=== What an MVP Is ===
[mvp_label] Ries offers another path, the minimum viable product.
It is the simplest version that lets you learn from real customers.
[effort_dial] The goal is not to impress anyone, but to learn the most with the least effort.
[question] Think of it as a question you ask the market, not a finished answer.
=== Shoes on the Web ===
[nick] Ries tells the early story of Zappos and its founder, Nick Swinmurn.
[shoe_box] Swinmurn wondered if people would buy shoes online without trying them on.
[warehouse] Instead of building warehouses and supply deals, he did something much smaller.
=== Photos, Not Stock ===
[camera] He visited local shoe stores and took pictures of their shoes.
[website] He posted those photos on a simple website.
[receipt] When someone ordered a pair, he went back, bought it at full price, and shipped it himself.
=== Real Buyers, Real Data ===
This approach earned almost nothing per sale, and it was never meant to scale.
[customer] But it put real buyers in front of a real choice.
[checkmark] It tested demand, and also how people paid, asked for refunds, and returned shoes.
=== A Video Is Enough ===
[video_screen] Ries also describes Dropbox, whose file syncing was hard to build.
[demo_click] So the founder made a short video showing how the product would work.
[waitlist] Sign ups for the waiting list jumped overnight, proving people wanted it before the full product existed.
=== Rough Edges Are Fine ===
[blush_face] Early versions often feel unfinished, buggy, or plain embarrassing.
[scale] Ries argues that you cannot know what quality means until you know who your customer is.
[early_user] Early adopters forgive rough edges if you are solving a problem they care about.
=== Your Smallest Test ===
[pencil] Pick one idea you are sitting on and ask yourself a question.
What is the smallest thing you could put in front of a real customer this week?
