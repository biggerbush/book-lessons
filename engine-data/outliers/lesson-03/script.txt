=== The Magic Number ===
[magic_number] What if greatness had a number attached to it?
Malcolm Gladwell suggests it does, and that number is roughly 10,000.
=== Talent or Practice ===
[violin_school] Picture an elite music academy in Berlin, full of young violinists.
[researcher] Psychologist K. Anders Ericsson and his colleagues sorted these students into three groups.
[three_groups] There were future soloists, good players, and those likely to become music teachers.
Then they asked each student how many hours they had practiced since first picking up a violin.
=== The Hours Add Up ===
[start_age] Almost all of them had started playing at around age five.
[bar_chart] By age twenty, the future stars had practiced about 10,000 hours.
The good players had around 8,000.
The future teachers had just over 4,000.
=== No Shortcuts ===
[naturals] The researchers found no naturals who reached the top while barely practicing.
[grinders] And no hard workers who practiced endlessly yet never broke through.
[staircase] Practice is not what you do once you are good, it is what makes you good.
=== The Beatles in Hamburg ===
[hamburg_club] Now think of the Beatles before they were famous.
[night_clock] In Hamburg, Germany, they often played eight hours a night, seven days a week.
[concert_count] By 1964, they had performed live an estimated 1,200 times.
That marathon turned a scrappy band into a great one.
=== Bill Gates at School ===
[lakeside] As a teenager, Bill Gates attended a private school in Seattle.
[terminal] Its parents' club paid for a computer terminal, something almost no school had then.
[code_screen] Gates spent years programming, later finding ways onto other computers, often late at night.
=== The Hidden Doorway ===
[open_door] Here is Gladwell's twist.
[time_money] Ten thousand hours is a huge amount of time, and few people can find it alone.
[helping_hand] It usually takes a school, a family, or a rare lucky break.
The Beatles needed Hamburg, and Gates needed that terminal.
=== What This Means ===
[tally_marks] So mastery is less about magic and more about accumulated hours.
[calendar] Look at your own skills and roughly estimate the hours behind each one.
[gift_box] And remember that giving someone time and access may be the greatest gift of all.
=== Your Next Hour ===
[question_mark] Which skill would you master if you could find your own 10,000 hours?
And who could you help find theirs?
