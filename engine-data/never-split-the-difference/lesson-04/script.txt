=== Stop Chasing Yes ===
[hook_no] What if the word you fear most in a negotiation is actually the one you should be hunting for?
=== The Trouble With Yes ===
[salesman] Picture a pushy salesperson trying to drag a yes out of you.
[crossed_arms] You feel cornered, so you cross your arms and get defensive.
[fake_yes] Voss says a yes squeezed out like this is often counterfeit, just a way to make the pressure stop.
=== Why No Feels Safe ===
[shield] Saying no works differently, because it feels like a shield.
[steering] It tells the other person they are protected and still in control of the wheel.
[open_door] Once they feel safe, they relax, and real talk can begin.
For Voss, no is a starting point, not a dead end.
=== Ask for a No ===
[question_box] So shape your questions so the easy answer is no.
[two_cards] Instead of asking, is now a good time to talk, ask, is now a bad time to talk?
Instead of, does this work for you, try, is this a ridiculous idea?
=== The Ghosting Email ===
[silent_phone] Voss describes a common problem: someone simply stops replying.
[email] His fix is a one line email asking, have you given up on this project?
[reply_arrow] The question invites a quick no, and people often rush to answer and explain where things stand.
=== Listen for That's Right ===
[summary_card] The second goal is hearing two special words: that's right.
[speech_bubble] You earn them by summarizing the other person's view so well they feel completely understood.
[lightbulb] When they say that's right, they are agreeing with your summary, and that shifts how they see you.
=== The You're Right Trap ===
[thumbs_up] Watch out for a close cousin: you're right.
[exit_sign] It sounds like agreement, but people often say it just to make you stop talking.
Nothing has really changed.
=== A Hostage Standoff ===
[jungle_camp] In the Philippines, Voss helped coach a negotiation with Abu Sabaya, a rebel leader holding an American hostage.
[scroll] The negotiator summarized Sabaya's long list of grievances back to him, in detail.
[two_words] Sabaya answered, that's right, and after that he stopped pushing for ransom.
