=== The Question ===
[phone_girl] Why did teen girls' mental health fall so much faster than boys' after 2012?
The answer lies in what image-based social media does best.
=== A Wider Gap ===
[two_lines] Haidt shows that after 2012, depression and anxiety rose among both boys and girls.
But the line for girls climbed much more steeply.
[hospital] Visits for self-harm rose fastest among young adolescent girls.
=== Built to Belong ===
[two_circles] Haidt describes two deep motives: agency, the drive to stand out, and communion, the drive to belong.
Girls, on average, lean more toward communion.
[hook] Social media hooks right into that need, and then turns it against them.
=== The Perfect Feed ===
[insta_feed] Platforms like Instagram are built around photos of faces and bodies.
[filter_face] Filters and editing make everyone look flawless.
A girl scrolling at night compares her real self to hundreds of polished images.
[scoreboard] Likes and comments turn her appearance into a public score.
=== They Knew ===
[leaked_slide] Haidt reports that Facebook's own internal research, later leaked, studied teen Instagram users.
One slide admitted, "We make body image issues worse for one in three teen girls."
[company_hq] The harm was known inside the company.
=== Mean Without Fists ===
[schoolyard] Boys' aggression tends to be physical, while girls' aggression is more often relational.
[whisper] That means rumors, gossip, and leaving someone out.
[group_chat] Online, exclusion becomes visible and constant, a group chat you weren't added to, a party photo you weren't in.
And it follows her home, every hour of the day.
=== Feelings Spread ===
[ripples] Haidt also notes that girls share emotions more openly with each other.
[network_web] But online, anxiety and distress can spread like a contagion across huge networks.
[stranger_msg] Girls also face far more unwanted messages and harassment from strangers.
=== What Helps ===
[conversation] Ask a girl in your life how she feels after scrolling, and listen without judging.
[photo_reveal] Show her how filters and editing work, so the perfect images lose their power.
[park_bench] And help her find belonging offline, where friendships don't come with a scoreboard.
=== Your Turn ===
[question_mark] Think of one girl you care about.
What could you do this week to help her feel she belongs without a screen?
