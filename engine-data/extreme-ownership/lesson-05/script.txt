=== Too Much at Once ===
[juggler] Ten problems land on your desk at once, and every one screams urgent.
What do you do first?
=== The Chaos Drill ===
[instructor] In SEAL training, instructors deliberately pile problems onto young leaders during mock urban battles.
[gunfire] There is gunfire, a wounded teammate, and a radio crackling with questions, all at the same moment.
[frozen_leader] Leaders who try to fix everything at once freeze, and the whole team stalls with them.
=== One Simple Mantra ===
[mantra_card] Combat leaders lean on a saying the authors pass along: "Relax, look around, make a call."
[deep_breath] Staying calm is not a personality trait here.
It is a skill, and the team copies whatever the leader shows them.
=== Step Back to See ===
[high_ground] A good leader steps back from the details, even just one pace, to see the bigger picture.
[problem_map] From there, the problems stop looking like one blur and start looking like a list.
=== Pick the Top One ===
[target] Next, choose the single highest priority, the one that unlocks or protects everything else.
[team_huddle] Then explain it to the team in plain, simple words, so everyone knows what matters right now.
=== Solve and Execute ===
[whiteboard] Work out a solution, asking the team for input where time allows.
[arrow_forward] Then direct everyone's effort at that one problem until it is handled.
Momentum from one win builds confidence for the next.
=== Then the Next ===
[next_card] Only then move to the next priority, and repeat the cycle.
[tunnel] Watch out for tunnel vision, because fixating on one problem can blind you to a new one rising.
[ladder] When priorities shift, tell people above and below you, so the whole chain stays in sync.
=== Stay a Step Ahead ===
[calendar] The authors add that planning for likely problems in advance makes them easier to rank when they hit.
[office_desk] The same method works in business, where too many projects at once means none get done well.
[one_folder] Pick one, finish it, then pick again.
=== Your Turn ===
[sticky_note] Look at everything on your plate today.
Which single item, if handled first, would make the rest easier? Start there.
