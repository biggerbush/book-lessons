=== The Wall Ahead ===
[speeding_car] Imagine your car spinning out at full speed, with a concrete wall rushing toward you.
Where you look next might decide everything.
=== The Struggle ===
[lonely_leader] Ben Horowitz says every leader eventually meets something he calls the Struggle.
[dark_cloud] It is the moment you wonder why you ever started, and nobody can tell you.
Food loses its taste, and your own plan starts to feel like a lie.
=== Sleeping Like a Baby ===
[falling_chart] When the dot-com bubble burst, Loudcloud's future suddenly looked very uncertain.
[baby_crib] Horowitz later joked that he slept like a baby during those years.
[clock_face] He woke up every two hours and cried.
Every night, he was fighting fear.
=== The Hardest Skill ===
[brain_icon] Horowitz argues that managing your own mind is the toughest skill a leader needs.
No business school teaches it, yet every decision passes through it.
[fog_cloud] When fear takes over, you stop seeing options and start seeing only disaster.
=== Road, Not the Wall ===
[race_track] He borrows a lesson taught to race car drivers.
[skid_marks] When you skid, stare at the wall and you will steer straight into it.
[open_road] Look at the road instead, and your hands follow your eyes to safety.
For leaders, the wall is every way things could fail.
The road is the next good move.
=== Always a Move ===
[chess_board] Horowitz reminds leaders that business is more like chess than checkers.
Even when the position looks hopeless, there is usually a move you have not considered.
[light_bulb] Loudcloud found one, reinventing itself as the software company Opsware.
=== Stay in the Game ===
[hourglass] He also notes that if you keep playing long enough, luck sometimes turns your way.
[friend_pair] And you are not alone, because the leaders you admire went through these same dark nights.
Feeling the struggle does not mean you are failing.
It means you are leading.
=== Your Next Move ===
[notebook] So when panic rises, take a page and write down the worst outcome you fear.
Then cross it out and write the one move that pushes you forward today.
[arrow_forward] Put your attention there, and let the wall fade into the background.
=== Your Challenge ===
What wall are you staring at right now?
And what is one step down the open road you could take today?
