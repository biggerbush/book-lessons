=== Title ===
[title_card] Why do some messages wake us up while others drift right past?
The secret is a small, well-placed surprise.
=== Patterns Put Us to Sleep ===
[brain] Your brain is a pattern machine.
[routine] When things go as expected, it quietly tunes out, like hearing a safety talk on your hundredth flight.
[alarm_bell] Break the pattern, and attention snaps back in an instant.
=== Surprise Grabs ===
[question_mark] Surprise works like an alarm, the Heaths explain.
[eyebrows] Our eyebrows lift, our eyes widen, and we stop to figure out what we got wrong.
That pause is your chance to teach.
=== The Missing School Day ===
[notepad] Writer Nora Ephron remembered a high school journalism assignment.
[fact_list] Her teacher listed facts: the faculty would travel to a conference next Thursday to hear famous speakers.
[students] The students dutifully summarized who, what and where.
[headline] Then he revealed the real lead: "There will be no school next Thursday."
=== Surprise With a Point ===
[lightbulb] That surprise did not just entertain.
It showed the class that journalism means finding what matters to the reader.
[gimmick] The Heaths warn against gimmicks, because a surprise must point back to your core message.
Unexpected for its own sake just confuses people.
=== Curiosity Gaps ===
[gap] Surprise grabs attention, but curiosity holds it.
[scientist] Economist George Loewenstein argued that curiosity appears when we notice a gap in our knowledge.
[itch] That gap feels like an itch we must scratch.
=== Saturn's Rings ===
[saturn] Psychologist Robert Cialdini studied science writing that kept readers hooked.
[detective] The best pieces opened like mysteries, asking what Saturn's rings are made of.
Different scientific teams had offered conflicting answers, so readers followed the clues for pages.
[dust] The answer, ice-covered dust, felt like solving a case.
=== Open the Gap First ===
[map] Most of us give answers before anyone has asked the question.
[open_door] Instead, start by showing people what they do not know.
[puzzle] Ask a question, pose a puzzle, or admit what experts still argue about.
[key] Then fill the gap, one piece at a time.
=== Your Turn ===
[mirror] Before your next talk, ask yourself what is surprising about your message.
[hook_sign] Open with that gap, and let people lean in for the answer.
