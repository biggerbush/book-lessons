=== Title ===
[title_card] Is it possible to be too sure of yourself, and also not sure enough?
The answer might change how you learn everything.
=== Two Ditches ===
[road] Picture a road with a ditch on each side.
[armchair_qb] In one ditch sits the armchair quarterback, shouting advice with more confidence than skill.
[impostor] In the other ditch sits the impostor, skilled enough but convinced they are a fraud.
Adam Grant says both ditches keep us from rethinking.
=== The Overconfident ===
[test_paper] Psychologists David Dunning and Justin Kruger found a strange pattern on tests of skill.
[low_scorers] The people who scored lowest tended to overestimate their scores the most.
[blind_spot] When we know too little, we cannot even see what we are missing.
It is confidence without competence.
=== Mount Stupid ===
[mountain] Grant warns of a peak that beginners often climb, nicknamed Mount Stupid.
[climber] A little knowledge makes us feel like experts, so we stop asking questions.
That is exactly when we most need to keep learning.
=== The Impostor ===
[halla] Now meet Halla Tómasdóttir, a businesswoman from Iceland.
[ballot] In 2016 she ran for president, though she doubted she was qualified.
[doubt_cloud] Instead of quitting, she used that doubt as fuel to study, listen and prepare.
=== The Surprise ===
[poll_chart] She started out barely noticed in the polls.
[rising_line] By election day she had climbed to second place, ahead of far more established candidates.
She did not win, but she proved her doubters, and herself, wrong.
Her humility kept her curious, and her confidence kept her running.
=== The Sweet Spot ===
[two_dials] Grant calls this blend confident humility.
[arrow_future] Confidence means believing you can learn and reach your goal.
[question_mark] Humility means doubting that your current answer or method is the right one.
Together they keep you moving and keep you open.
=== Try It Today ===
[notebook] Before your next big decision, write down what you believe.
[two_columns] Beside it, write one thing you are sure you can learn, and one thing you might be wrong about.
[magnifier] Then go test the shakiest part first.
=== Your Turn ===
[steering] Where in your life are you sitting in a ditch?
Which way do you need to steer?
