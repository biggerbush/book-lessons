=== Wrong About Them ===
[marketer] You love your product, so surely everyone else will too.
That one assumption sinks more good ideas than bad luck.
=== The Empathy Gap ===
[mirror] Seth Godin says the first step is admitting a simple truth.
[other_person] Other people don't know what you know.
[thought_cloud] They don't want what you want, and they don't believe what you believe.
That isn't a flaw to fix.
It's the starting point.
=== The Drill Story ===
[drill] Godin retells a classic marketing lesson about a drill.
[hardware_shop] The seller in the shop gets excited about speed, power and the battery.
[hole] But the customer never wanted a drill.
They wanted a hole.
=== Dig Deeper ===
[shelf] Actually, they wanted the hole to hang a shelf.
[tidy_room] They wanted the shelf so the room would feel tidy.
[proud_face] And underneath, they wanted the feeling of having done it themselves.
=== Everyone Wears Glasses ===
[glasses] Godin calls this lens a worldview.
It's the mix of beliefs, biases and past experiences each person carries everywhere.
[two_people] Two people can hear the same pitch and hear completely different stories.
[filter] Whatever doesn't fit their lens simply bounces off.
=== Don't Argue, Match ===
[debate] Your instinct might be to argue people into seeing things your way.
Godin says that almost never works.
[puzzle_piece] Instead, find people whose worldview already leans toward what you offer.
Then tell a story that slots neatly into what they already believe.
=== Same Drill, New Story ===
[two_buyers] Picture the same drill offered to two different people.
[renter] One is a renter who worries about damaging the walls.
A story about small, clean holes that are easy to patch fits their worry.
[homeowner] The other just bought a first home and wants it to feel like theirs.
They need a story about finally putting their own stamp on the place.
=== Listen First ===
[notebook] So before you write any marketing, study the people you hope to serve.
[question_mark] Ask what they fear, what they dream about, and what they already assume is true.
Their answers become your story.
=== Your Turn ===
[pitch_card] Pick one customer you want to reach, and write down three things they believe that you don't.
Then rewrite your pitch so it starts from their beliefs, not yours.
