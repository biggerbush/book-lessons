=== Title ===
[smiling_mind] What if happiness were less like luck and more like a muscle you could train?
=== A Skill, Not a Mood ===
[naval] Naval Ravikant treats happiness the same way he treats getting rich.
[gym_weights] He sees it as a skill you practice, like fitness.
He says he made it a priority, worked on it over years, and became steadily happier.
[choice_sign] In his view, happiness is partly a choice you keep making each day.
=== Peace, Not Thrills ===
[fireworks] Most of us picture happiness as excitement, a big win or a thrilling moment.
Naval sees it differently.
[calm_lake] For him, happiness is mostly peace, a quiet state where nothing feels missing.
[empty_hole] When that nagging sense of lack goes away, happiness is what remains.
=== The Desire Contract ===
[contract] Here is his sharpest idea.
Every desire is like a contract you sign with yourself.
[signature] The terms say you agree to be unhappy until you get the thing you want.
You may not even notice you signed it.
=== A Simple Picture ===
[new_phone] Imagine you suddenly want the newest phone.
[storm_cloud] Before that moment, your old phone was fine and you felt calm.
Now a little cloud follows you, because something is missing.
[shopping_bag] You buy it, and the cloud lifts, briefly.
=== The Endless Line ===
[queue] The trouble is that a new desire quickly takes the place of the old one.
[treadmill] So you keep signing contracts, and the peace keeps slipping ahead of you.
Naval's point is not that wanting things is wrong.
It is that each want has a hidden cost in calm.
=== Pick One Desire ===
[single_target] So Naval suggests being very picky about your desires.
Choose one big thing you truly want and focus there.
[let_go_hands] Let the smaller cravings go, because each one would cost you peace.
[clear_path] You still pursue goals, but you sign far fewer contracts.
=== Catch the Contract ===
[magnifier] The practical move is to notice when you feel unsettled.
Ask what desire is sitting underneath that feeling.
[torn_paper] Then decide whether that want is worth the unhappiness it demands.
[balance_scale] Often it is not, and you can quietly tear up the contract.
=== Your Challenge ===
[notebook] Today, catch one desire that is quietly stealing your calm.
Ask yourself if it is worth the price, and if not, let it go.
