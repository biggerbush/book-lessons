=== A Simple Rule ===
[title_card] What if one simple rule could stop you from chasing the crowd?
Graham's answer is a steady split between stocks and bonds.
=== Two Buckets ===
[stock_bucket] Picture two buckets on a table.
The first bucket holds stocks, which are small pieces of companies.
[bond_bucket] The second holds high quality bonds, which are loans that pay you steady interest.
Stocks can grow more, but they swing.
Bonds grow slowly, but they hold steady.
=== The 25 to 75 Range ===
[range_bar] Graham told the defensive investor to keep somewhere between 25 and 75 percent in stocks.
Whatever is left goes into bonds.
[never_zero] So you never go all in on stocks, and you never abandon them completely.
=== Start at Fifty-Fifty ===
[even_scale] His standard suggestion was simple: put half in stocks and half in bonds.
[no_crystal] An even split means no guessing about where the market is heading.
It is a sensible default for most people.
=== When Stocks Run Up ===
[rising_arrow] Now imagine stocks have a great year, and your stock share grows to 55 percent.
[one_eleventh] Graham's example says sell about one eleventh of your stocks.
That brings stocks from 55 down to 50.
[move_money] Move that money into bonds, and you are back to fifty-fifty.
=== When Stocks Fall ===
[falling_arrow] The reverse works too.
If stocks drop and shrink below half, you shift some bond money back into stocks.
[cheap_tag] That means you are buying when prices are lower, almost automatically.
=== Leaning, Carefully ===
[tilt_dial] Graham allowed some flexibility within the range.
When stock prices look dangerously high, you might lean toward 25 percent stocks.
[bargain_sign] When genuine bargains appear, you might lean toward 75 percent.
But stay inside the limits, because nobody predicts the market reliably.
=== Why It Feels Good ===
[calm_face] This plan also protects your peace of mind.
When stocks soar, you are glad you own some.
[umbrella] When stocks crash, you are glad your bonds are still standing.
Either way, you have a clear job to do instead of panicking.
=== Your Turn ===
[your_pie] Look at your own savings today: what share is in stocks?
If it has drifted far from your target, what one move would bring it back?
