=== Opening ===
[mirror] What if the trait that annoys you most is actually someone's greatest strength?
=== Strength or Flaw ===
[two_lenses] Erikson points out something sneaky about the four colors.
[coin] Every strength has a flip side, depending on who is looking.
What one color admires, another color finds irritating.
That simple twist explains a lot of workplace friction.
=== Two Big Dividers ===
[axis_speed] The book places the colors along two lines.
One line runs from fast and outgoing to slower and more reserved.
[axis_focus] The other runs from focus on tasks to focus on people.
Reds and Yellows sit on the fast side, while Greens and Blues sit on the slower side.
[diagonal] The sharpest clashes come between colors sitting diagonally opposite each other.
=== Red Meets Green ===
[red_runner] A Red wants a decision now and moves straight to action.
[green_chair] A Green wants to check that everyone is on board first.
To the Red, the Green looks slow and passive.
To the Green, the Red looks pushy and careless about people.
=== Yellow Meets Blue ===
[yellow_bulb] A Yellow pitches a bold idea with lots of energy.
[blue_clip] A Blue immediately asks for numbers, sources and risks.
The Yellow hears negativity and feels the idea being crushed.
The Blue hears hot air and wonders where the facts went.
=== Same Trait, New Label ===
[word_flip] Notice that nobody here is describing a real defect.
Through a Green's eyes, decisive turns into reckless.
Through a Red's eyes, careful turns into stubborn.
Through a Blue's eyes, enthusiasm turns into sloppiness.
Through a Yellow's eyes, thoroughness turns into nitpicking.
=== Neighbors Clash Too ===
[red_blue] Neighboring colors can rub each other the wrong way too, just less sharply.
Reds and Blues both love tasks, yet the Red wants speed and the Blue wants accuracy.
[clock_check] One keeps checking the clock, while the other keeps checking the details.
=== Rename the Flaw ===
[pause_btn] So when someone drives you up the wall, pause before you judge.
Ask yourself which strength you might be reading as a weakness.
[new_label] Then give it a fairer name, like careful instead of slow.
=== Your Turn ===
[colleague] Think of the colleague who frustrates you most this week.
What strength of theirs have you been calling a flaw?
