=== Six Steps ===
[wish_star] What if the gap between a wish and a goal is one sheet of paper?
Napoleon Hill offers six steps to close it.
=== The Foggy Wish ===
[fog_cloud] Napoleon Hill says that simply wanting plenty of money gets you nowhere.
A wish stays foggy because it has no edges.
[carnegie] He credits Andrew Carnegie with passing him a formula to sharpen it.
[six_steps] It has six steps, and each one adds a hard edge.
=== Name the Number ===
[price_tag] Step one is to fix in your mind the exact amount you want.
[check] Not a lot, but a precise figure you could write on a check.
Vague amounts tend to produce vague effort.
[target] A specific number gives every action a target to aim at.
=== What Will You Give ===
[scale] Step two is to decide exactly what you will give in return.
Hill insists there is no such thing as something for nothing.
[toolbox] Your offer might be skills, service, time, or effort.
Think of it as the price you agree to pay for your goal.
=== Set the Date ===
[calendar] Step three is to set a definite date for having that money.
Pick an actual day you could circle on the calendar.
[stopwatch] A deadline turns someday into a countdown.
=== Plan and Start Now ===
[map] Step four is to create a definite plan for reaching your goal.
[runner] Then begin carrying it out at once, whether you feel ready or not.
Hill does not want you waiting for the perfect moment.
=== Write It Down ===
[notepad] Step five is to write a clear, short statement of your goal.
It holds four things: the amount, the deadline, what you will give, and your plan.
[one_page] Now your whole goal fits on one page you can hold.
=== Read It Aloud ===
[moon] Step six is to read that statement aloud every night before you sleep.
[sun] Then read it again each morning when you wake up.
[vision] As you read, Hill says, see and feel yourself already holding the money.
Morning and night, your goal stays right in front of you.
=== Your Turn ===
[pen_paper] Tonight, take one wish and run it through all six steps on a single page.
Then read it aloud before bed, and again tomorrow morning.
