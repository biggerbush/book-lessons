=== The Hardest Push ===
[stuck_cart] Why does getting started feel so heavy, while keeping going feels easy?
The answer is a famous law of physics.
=== Newton's Rule ===
[apple_newton] Isaac Newton noticed that objects at rest tend to stay at rest.
[rolling_ball] And objects in motion tend to stay in motion.
Darren Hardy says people work exactly the same way.
[sofa_person] When we are stuck, we stay stuck, and when we are moving, we keep moving.
=== The Merry-Go-Round ===
[merry_go] Hardy pictures a child pushing a playground merry-go-round full of friends.
[strain_face] The first push is brutal, and the wheel barely creeps forward.
The next few pushes are still hard work.
=== Then It Spins ===
[spin_lines] But with steady pushes, the wheel picks up speed.
[one_finger] Soon a light tap keeps it whirling, and the child can hop on and enjoy the ride.
That is momentum.
Early effort gets stored up, and then it pays you back.
=== Routines Do the Pushing ===
[sunrise] Hardy's tool for building momentum is routine.
He suggests bookending your day with a set morning ritual and a set evening ritual.
[routine_list] When a good behavior happens at the same time every day, you stop debating it.
It just runs, like brushing your teeth.
=== Like an Athlete ===
[swimmer] Hardy points to Olympic swimmer Michael Phelps, who follows the same routine before every race.
[stopwatch] Repeating the same steps lets his body and mind slip into peak performance automatically.
=== Don't Stop the Wheel ===
[water_pump] Hardy also warns about stopping, using an old hand water pump.
[water_gush] You pump and pump until water finally flows.
[empty_bucket] But if you quit, the water drains back down, and you must start priming all over again.
=== Keep the Rhythm ===
[metronome] So the real secret is not intensity, it is rhythm.
A modest action done every day beats a heroic burst followed by weeks off.
[calendar] Every day you keep the beat, the wheel spins a little faster and easier.
=== Your First Push ===
[small_step] Choose one small routine for tomorrow morning and do it at the same time every day this week.
Push through those first heavy days, and let the momentum carry you.
