=== Happily Ever After ===
[heart] What if believing that true love should feel easy is quietly harming your relationships?
=== The Fairy Tale Belief ===
[couple] Picture a couple who believe real soulmates never need to try.
[thought_cloud] In their minds, a good match means reading each other's thoughts and always agreeing.
Dweck says this is the fixed mindset showing up in love.
[castle] It treats a relationship like a finished castle, either perfect or doomed.
=== When Problems Feel Fatal ===
[crack] So when the first crack appears, it does not look like something to fix.
It looks like proof.
[verdict_stamp] The couple concludes they were never right for each other.
Even effort becomes a bad sign, because the right person supposedly should not need work.
=== From Fault to Flaw ===
[argument] Fixed-mindset partners also turn small disagreements into judgments about character.
[label_tag] A forgotten errand stops being a mistake and becomes proof that someone is selfish.
[blame_arrow] Blame lands on traits that cannot change, so there is nowhere to go but resentment.
=== The Rejection Study ===
[survey_paper] Dweck asked people to describe a time they had been badly rejected.
[sad_figure] Those with a fixed mindset felt judged and labeled, as if the rejection proved they were unlovable.
[revenge_fist] Many of them still wanted revenge.
[open_hands] Those with a growth mindset wanted to understand what happened, forgive, and move forward.
=== Conflict as a Skill ===
[two_chairs] In a growth mindset, conflict is a sign that partners have something to learn.
[toolbox] Communication, patience and understanding are skills, and skills can be built together.
No relationship is born finished.
[seedling] It grows through the work two people put into it.
=== Friends Who Grow You ===
[friend_pair] The same thinking applies to friendship.
In a growth mindset, a good friend helps you become the person you want to be.
[ladder] They encourage you to stretch, and you do the same for them.
=== Try This Next Time ===
[speech_bubble] In your next disagreement, describe the action, not the person.
Talk about the piled up dishes, not about someone being lazy.
[question_mark] Then ask what you could both try differently.
[practice_bar] Treat it as practice, not a final grade.
=== Your Challenge ===
[mirror] Think of one recent conflict you saw as a flaw.
What could it teach you both if you treated it as a skill to build?
