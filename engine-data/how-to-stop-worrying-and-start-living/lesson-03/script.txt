=== Paper Beats Panic ===
[spinning_head] Your mind can spin all night on one problem and still get nowhere.
[sheet_paper] Dale Carnegie says a pencil and a sheet of paper can stop the spin.
=== Worry Is Confusion ===
[dean_hawkes] Carnegie quotes Herbert Hawkes, a dean at Columbia College, who helped many students untangle their worries.
[fog_cloud] Hawkes believed that half the worry in the world comes from deciding before you know enough.
So he refused to worry about a problem until he had studied the facts.
=== Gather Facts Fairly ===
[magnifier] Getting the facts sounds easy, but fear bends what we notice.
[stranger] Carnegie suggests collecting facts as if the problem belonged to someone else.
[lawyer_scale] He also says to argue both sides, like a lawyer listing evidence for and against.
=== Danger in Shanghai ===
[galen] One example comes from Galen Litchfield, an American businessman in Shanghai during World War Two.
[officer] The city was under Japanese occupation, he faced a real threat from the officers, and he was terrified.
Instead of pacing in circles, he sat down and wrote.
=== Four Questions ===
[question_one] First he wrote, what am I worrying about?
[question_two] Then he wrote, what can I do about it?
[question_rest] Next came, here is what I will do, and finally, when do I start?
Seeing his choices in ink turned a frightening blur into a clear plan.
=== Decide, Then Stop ===
[gavel] Carnegie says the moment you make a firm decision, much of the worry drains away.
[william_james] He leans on psychologist William James, who advised dropping concern about the outcome once a choice is made.
Endless weighing is what keeps anxiety alive.
=== Act Right Away ===
[running_shoes] A decision only helps if you act on it quickly.
[calendar] That is why Litchfield's last question asked when to start, not just what to do.
Action leaves less room for second guessing.
=== Your Worry Sheet ===
[notebook] Try this tonight with your own biggest worry.
Write the facts as if for a friend, list your options, and circle one.
[clock] Then pick a time tomorrow to take the first step.
=== Your Challenge ===
What worry have you been carrying that you could put on paper today?
[pencil] Write it down, decide, and start before the day ends.
