=== A Mind Under Siege ===
[frankl_arrive] What does a mind do when the world it knows suddenly disappears?
Viktor Frankl watched it happen, to others and to himself.
=== Three Stages ===
Frankl was a psychiatrist, so even as a prisoner he quietly studied the people around him.
[three_doors] He saw that their inner reactions tended to move through three stages.
[stage_arrival] First came shock on arrival.
[stage_camp] Then numbness during camp life.
[stage_freedom] And finally, a strange confusion after liberation.
=== The First Shock ===
[train_station] When the trains reached Auschwitz, many prisoners clung to a desperate hope.
Frankl called it the delusion of reprieve.
[hope_bubble] People told themselves that somehow, they would be the ones spared.
[torn_bubble] Then reality broke through, and that hope collapsed.
=== Grim Humor ===
[grim_jokes] Frankl noticed odd reactions too, like grim jokes among the new arrivals.
[question_mark] There was also a cold, detached curiosity about what would happen next.
It was the mind stepping back, trying to cope from a distance.
=== Numbness Sets In ===
[grey_figures] After some days, a second stage began, and Frankl called it apathy.
Feelings dulled.
Prisoners stopped reacting to things that once would have horrified them.
[watching_eyes] They could witness suffering around them without looking away.
=== The Bowl of Soup ===
[soup_bowl] Frankl gives a striking example from his own experience.
He was eating soup in a hut while a dead fellow prisoner was dragged away.
[window_frame] The man's eyes seemed to stare in through the window.
Frankl kept eating.
He used this moment to show just how deep the numbness had gone.
=== A Protective Shell ===
[shell_armor] Frankl did not treat this numbness as a moral failure.
He described apathy as a necessary defense that shielded the mind.
By shutting down emotion, prisoners saved their strength for one task, staying alive.
=== Free but Not Free ===
[open_gate] Liberation brought the third stage, and it was not what anyone expected.
[meadow_flowers] Frankl recalls walking through meadows of flowers, yet feeling no joy at all.
The freed prisoners had to slowly relearn how to feel.
[bitter_cloud] Some also struggled with bitterness and disappointment once they returned home.
=== Your Turn ===
[mirror] Next time you feel numb after hard news, ask whether your mind is protecting you.
What small step could help you, gently, begin to feel again?
