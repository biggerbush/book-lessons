=== Title ===
[title_card] What if the kindest thing your boss could do was tell you something you did not want to hear?
=== Two Dimensions ===
[two_axes] Kim Scott says great bosses do two things at the same time.
[care_arrow] The first is caring personally about the people who work for them.
[chall_arrow] The second is challenging those people directly.
Put them together and you get what she calls Radical Candor.
=== Care Personally ===
[whole_person] Caring personally means seeing someone as a whole human, not just a job title.
[life_icons] It means knowing what they care about, what worries them, and where they hope to go.
Scott argues work is personal, so bosses should not leave their humanity at the door.
=== Challenge Directly ===
[red_pen] Challenging directly means saying clearly when work is not good enough.
[hard_call] It also means making hard calls, and being willing to disappoint people.
This is uncomfortable, and that discomfort is exactly why so many bosses avoid it.
=== Spinach in Teeth ===
[spinach_smile] Scott uses a simple everyday picture.
Imagine a colleague walking into a big meeting with spinach stuck in their teeth.
[whisper] Telling them feels awkward for a second, but it saves them real embarrassment.
Staying silent protects you, not them.
=== Why Both Matter ===
[balance_scale] Care without challenge lets people keep making the same mistakes.
[storm_cloud] Challenge without care feels like an attack, so people get defensive and stop listening.
When people know you are on their side, hard truths become something they can actually use.
=== Moments Not People ===
[speech_bubble] Scott stresses that Radical Candor describes what you say, not who you are.
[mirror] Anyone can slip, so judge each conversation rather than labeling yourself or others.
She also says candor is measured by how the listener hears it, not by what you meant.
=== Try It This Week ===
[calendar] This week, pick one person you work with.
[coffee_cup] Ask about something in their life beyond the current project.
[note_card] Then tell them one specific thing about their work that could be better, kindly and clearly.
=== Your Challenge ===
[question_mark] Who on your team has spinach in their teeth right now, and what is stopping you from telling them?
