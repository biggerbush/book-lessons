=== Title ===
[snail] What if the slowest part of any project is exactly the part that makes it yours?
=== The Hurry Habit ===
[stopwatch] Many of us want everything to happen faster than it actually can.
[growing_plant] But real work and real change grow at their own pace, like a plant in soil.
[tense_person] Burkeman argues that pushing against that pace mostly makes us anxious, not quicker.
=== Helsinki Bus Station ===
[photographer] Burkeman shares a theory from the Finnish American photographer Arno Rafael Minkkinen.
[bus_station] Picture the central bus station in Helsinki, with many bus lines leaving from it.
[shared_road] For the first stretch out of the city, every bus follows the same road and makes the same stops.
=== Getting Off Too Soon ===
[bus_stop] In this picture, each stop is like a year of an artist's career.
[portfolio] After a few stops, the artist shows their work and hears that it looks like someone else's.
[return_trip] Frustrated, they jump off, head back to the station, and start again on a different line.
[loop_arrow] Then the same thing happens, again and again.
=== Stay on the Bus ===
Minkkinen's advice fits in four words: "Stay on the bus."
[road_split] Further out, the routes finally split off in different directions.
[new_place] Only those who stayed aboard reach places nobody else has been.
=== Beyond Copying ===
[copy_stack] Burkeman draws the lesson that originality lies on the far side of unoriginality.
[learner] Early work resembles other people's because that is simply how anyone learns a craft.
[mirror] So sounding ordinary at first is not a sign you are failing.
=== Small Daily Steps ===
[researcher] Burkeman also points to the psychologist Robert Boice, who studied academics struggling to write.
[calendar] The most productive ones wrote in short, regular sessions, day after day.
[binge_writer] Those who waited for huge bursts of inspiration often stalled.
[stop_sign] Boice even advised stopping when your time is up, so you return eager tomorrow.
=== Slow Looking ===
[painting] Harvard art historian Jennifer Roberts asks her students to study a single painting for three hours.
[clock_face] At first, the long wait feels almost unbearable.
[hidden_detail] But slowly, details appear that a quick glance would never have revealed.
=== Your Challenge ===
[open_road] Which project have you been tempted to abandon at an early stop?
This week, give it a short daily session, and stay on the bus.
