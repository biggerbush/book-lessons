=== Zero to One ===
[title_card] What if copying a good idea is the surest way to stand still?
Peter Thiel thinks the future depends on doing the opposite.
=== Two Kinds of Progress ===
[two_arrows] Thiel says progress comes in two directions.
[flat_arrow] One arrow runs sideways, across the page.
[tall_arrow] The other arrow climbs straight up.
He calls these horizontal progress and vertical progress.
=== Going Sideways ===
[one_to_n] Horizontal progress means copying things that already work.
It takes the world from 1 to n.
[typewriter] Picture one typewriter.
[typewriter_row] Building 100 more of them is horizontal progress.
More of the same, spread wider.
=== Going Up ===
[zero_to_one] Vertical progress means doing something nobody has done before.
It takes the world from zero to one.
[word_proc] Now picture the first word processor appearing next to the typewriter.
That leap is vertical progress.
Something new exists that did not exist before.
=== Globalization vs Tech ===
[globe] Thiel gives each direction a big name.
Horizontal progress is globalization, spreading what works from place to place.
[lightbulb] Vertical progress is technology, which for him means any new and better way of doing things.
Not just computers.
=== The China Example ===
[china_map] Thiel points to China as a clear case of horizontal progress.
[us_skyline] Its long-range path, he says, is to become more like the United States today.
That means copying proven models at huge scale.
[smoke_cloud] But he warns that spreading old methods everywhere, without new technology, would strain resources and the environment.
=== Each Moment Once ===
[calendar_page] Thiel stresses that every moment in business happens only once.
[gates_portrait] "The next Bill Gates will not build an operating system," he writes.
[google_box] The next Larry Page will not make another search engine.
Copying their exact moves misses what made them great.
=== Why Vertical Matters ===
[easy_road] Copying is easier, because the path is already proven.
[hard_climb] Creating is harder, because nobody can hand you the map.
But only new things raise the ceiling on what is possible.
That is the climb Thiel urges founders to make.
=== Your Zero to One ===
[mirror] Look at your own work this week.
Where are you making 100 more typewriters, and where could you build the first word processor?
