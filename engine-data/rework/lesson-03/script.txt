=== Scratch Your Own Itch ===
[title_itch] What if your next great business idea is hiding in something that annoys you every single day?
=== Be Your Own Customer ===
[narrator_desk] Jason Fried and David Heinemeier Hansson say the simplest way to make something great is to make something you want to use.
[itch_bubble] Start with a problem you personally run into.
[mirror] When you are the customer, you already understand exactly what is missing.
=== How Basecamp Began ===
[design_studio] Before Basecamp, their company 37 Signals was a small web design firm.
[client_emails] Projects with clients were drowning in messy email threads and lost files.
[basecamp_app] So they built a simple tool to keep projects organised, just for themselves.
Other people had the same headache, and that tool became their main product.
=== Instant Feedback ===
[thumbs_up] Building for yourself has a hidden bonus.
You know right away whether the thing actually works.
[guess_cloud] You are not guessing what some imaginary customer might want.
[test_loop] You use it, you feel what is wrong, and you fix it.
=== The Dusty Vacuum ===
[vacuum] The book points to James Dyson, who was frustrated that his vacuum cleaner kept losing suction.
[workshop] Instead of just complaining, he set out to build a better one himself.
[lightbulb] Personal frustration gave him both the idea and the motivation to keep going.
=== Ideas Are Cheap ===
[idea_pile] Here is the second half of the lesson.
An idea on its own is worth very little.
[crowd_heads] Plenty of people have had the same brilliant thought you have.
[builder] What matters is who actually sits down and starts making it.
=== Stop Waiting ===
[calendar] The authors push back on the excuse of not having enough time or the right moment.
[clock_evening] Even a few hours a week, chipped away at night or on weekends, adds up.
[prototype] A rough first version teaches you more than a year of daydreaming.
=== Your First Step ===
[notebook] So try this.
For one week, write down every small problem that bugs you.
[circle_one] Circle the one you would happily pay to have solved.
[toolbox] Then make the smallest thing that solves it, even badly.
=== Your Challenge ===
[question_mark] What itch are you scratching every day, and what could you start building tonight to fix it?
