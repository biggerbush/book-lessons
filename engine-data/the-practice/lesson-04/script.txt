=== Who Is It For ===
[empty_room] If you make something for everyone, you might end up making it for no one.
[spotlight] Today, we find who your work is really for.
=== The Everyone Trap ===
[crowd] Picture a giant crowd, all waiting to see your work.
It feels safe to try to please every single face in it.
[blurry_work] But work aimed at everyone tends to come out bland and blurry.
=== Godin's First Question ===
[question_card] Seth Godin suggests starting every project with a simple question.
Who is it for?
[sketch_person] Not a vague market, but a real kind of person you can picture.
What do they care about, and what do they believe?
=== Small Is Enough ===
[tiny_circle] Godin calls this the smallest viable audience.
It is the smallest group that your work could truly matter to.
[few_fans] Serving a few people deeply beats reaching many people a little.
When those few care, they often tell others like them.
=== Godin's Second Question ===
[arrow_change] Then he asks a second question: what is it for?
Good work is meant to change something for the person receiving it.
[before_after] Maybe they feel less alone, learn a skill, or see a problem differently.
If you can name the change, you know what to aim at.
=== A Quick Picture ===
[songwriter] Imagine a songwriter writing for new parents who are exhausted at 3 in the morning.
[lullaby] Suddenly every choice gets easier: the tempo, the words, the length.
It is trying to help one tired parent get through the night.
=== Not For You ===
[critic] Now someone hears the song and says they don't like it.
[sign_not_you] Godin offers a calm answer: that's fine, it's not for you.
Criticism from outside your audience is not a verdict on your work.
It just tells you who the work was never meant to reach.
=== Why Specific Works ===
[split_path] Choosing an audience means choosing who you will leave out.
[compass] But it gives your work a direction, and gives you a way to judge it.
Instead of asking whether it's good, ask whether it works for them.
=== Your Turn ===
[notebook] Pick your current project and write one sentence naming who it is for and what change you want.
Then make the next piece only for them.
