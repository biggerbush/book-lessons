=== Title ===
[seed_pile] What if your best idea is hiding in the pile you almost threw away?
Today, we learn to gather before we choose.
=== The Seed Phase ===
[garden_bed] Rick Rubin describes the start of any project as a garden at planting time.
[seed_packet] Each rough idea is a seed, full of possibility but not yet a plant.
At this stage, your only job is to collect.
Not choosing, not polishing, just gathering.
=== What Counts as a Seed ===
[melody_note] A seed can be a scrap of melody, a phrase you overheard, or a shape you noticed.
[thought_cloud] It can be a feeling, a question, or a half-formed theme.
Seeds often look unimpressive when they first arrive.
=== Do Not Judge Yet ===
[red_pen] The danger is judging too early.
When you rank ideas the moment they appear, you squeeze the life out of them.
[tiny_sprout] A seed that seems small and plain may grow into the strongest plant in the garden.
Judgment has its place, but it belongs to a later phase.
=== You Cannot Know Yet ===
Rubin's point is humble and freeing.
[question_mark] You simply cannot tell which seeds will flourish just by staring at them.
[watering_can] The only way to find out is to give several of them time and attention later.
=== Gather Widely ===
[notebook] So keep a place to catch everything, like a notebook or voice memos on your phone.
[seed_jar] Drop ideas in without asking whether they are good.
Quantity matters more than quality here.
The more seeds you hold, the more chances something surprising will take root.
=== Some Seeds Wait ===
[calendar] Rubin also notes that not every seed is ready right away.
[shelf] An idea that goes nowhere today can be set aside and revisited months later.
Its season may simply not have come yet.
=== Try This Today ===
[timer] Set a timer for ten minutes and write down every idea that comes, however rough.
Include the silly ones and the strange ones.
[cross_out] Cross nothing out.
Let the list sit for a few days before you pick even one.
=== Your Challenge ===
[open_hand] Which idea have you dismissed too quickly?
Plant it back in the pile this week, and see what grows.
