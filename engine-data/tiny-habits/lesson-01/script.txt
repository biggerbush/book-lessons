=== It's Not You ===
[failed_list] Ever quit a habit and decided you just lack willpower?
BJ Fogg says you've been blaming the wrong thing.
=== The Blame Loop ===
[big_goal] Most of us start change with a big, bold goal.
[slump] A few days later, life gets busy and the goal slips.
[mirror] Then we look in the mirror and call ourselves lazy.
That loop makes the next attempt even harder.
=== The Willpower Myth ===
[scientist] BJ Fogg studies human behavior at Stanford University.
He argues that relying on willpower is a weak plan.
[wave_line] Motivation rises and falls like a wave, so it can't carry a habit day after day.
Some days you feel fired up, and some days you feel nothing at all.
=== Knowing Isn't Doing ===
[salad] Another myth is that information alone changes what we do.
Most people already know vegetables are healthy.
[fries] Yet knowing that rarely changes what lands on the plate.
Fogg says knowledge without good design usually fades.
=== Blame the Plan ===
[sticky_door] Think of a door that sticks every time you push it.
You wouldn't blame your arms.
[toolbox] You'd fix the door instead.
Fogg wants us to treat habits the same way.
=== Fogg's Push-Ups ===
[bathroom] Fogg wanted to get stronger, but big workout plans never stuck.
So he redesigned the habit instead of scolding himself.
[pushup] He did just two push-ups after using the bathroom at home.
[counter] Over time, that small start grew into dozens of push-ups a day.
The change came from design, not from a sudden burst of willpower.
=== Kindness Works ===
[heart] Fogg also argues that shame doesn't help people change.
Feeling good about small wins builds momentum.
[smile] When you stop judging yourself, you start experimenting instead.
Each small success makes you more confident trying the next change.
=== Become a Designer ===
[blueprint] So treat any stalled habit as a design problem to solve.
[question] Ask what made it hard, rather than what is wrong with you.
Maybe the goal was too big, or the timing was wrong.
[pencil] Then adjust one piece of the plan and try again.
=== Your Challenge ===
Think of one habit you gave up on recently.
[paper] Today, write down one design flaw, not one personal flaw.
