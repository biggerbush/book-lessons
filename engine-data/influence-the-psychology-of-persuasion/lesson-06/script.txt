=== The White Coat ===
[white_coat] Would you hurt a stranger just because someone in a lab coat told you to?
Most of us would say never.
=== The Shock Experiment ===
[shock_panel] Cialdini describes Stanley Milgram's famous experiments at Yale.
[teacher] Volunteers were told to give a learner electric shocks for every wrong answer.
The learner was an actor, and the shocks were fake, but his screams sounded real.
[two_thirds] About two thirds of volunteers kept going to the maximum, 450 volts.
Why? A researcher calmly kept telling them to continue.
=== Why We Obey ===
[family_tree] From childhood, we learn that parents, teachers and bosses usually know more than we do.
[shortcut] So obeying authority becomes a mental shortcut that mostly works.
The trouble is that we often respond to the symbols of authority, not the substance.
=== Nurses on the Phone ===
[telephone] In one hospital study Cialdini reports, a stranger phoned nurses claiming to be a doctor.
[pill_bottle] He ordered a drug dose twice the safe maximum printed on the label.
[nurse] Nearly every nurse went to give it before researchers stopped her.
=== The Power of Titles ===
[name_tag] Titles work the same way.
[professor] When one man was introduced to students as a professor rather than a student, they later remembered him as taller.
=== Uniforms and Suits ===
[guard] In street experiments, people obeyed odd orders from a stranger dressed like a security guard.
[suit] A man jaywalking in a business suit drew far more followers than in work clothes.
Clothing alone told people to trust him.
=== Is This an Expert? ===
[question_one] Cialdini's defense starts with a question: is this authority truly an expert on this matter?
[tv_doctor] He mentions an actor who played a doctor on television and then sold coffee in ads.
The costume carried trust that the man had not earned.
=== Are They Honest Here? ===
[question_two] Then ask a second question: how truthful is this expert likely to be right now?
[waiter] Watch for a small admitted drawback, like a waiter steering you to a cheaper dish.
It can be a tactic to earn your trust before a bigger recommendation.
