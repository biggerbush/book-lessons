=== Time Is the Real Superpower ===
[title_clock] What if the secret to great wealth is not being clever, but simply starting early and staying put?
=== The Famous Investor ===
[buffett] Warren Buffett is often called the greatest investor alive.
Most people assume his fortune comes from rare genius.
[question_mark] Morgan Housel says that misses the most important part of the story.
=== The Hidden Timeline ===
[timeline] Picture Buffett's life as one long line, from childhood to old age.
[age_ten] He started investing seriously around age 10.
[still_going] And he kept going for decades past normal retirement age.
=== Where the Money Came ===
[wealth_chart] According to the book, of his roughly 84 billion dollar fortune, over 81 billion arrived after his mid-sixties.
[steep_curve] On a chart, the line stays flat for decades, then shoots almost straight up.
That steep late climb is what compounding looks like.
=== Skill Versus Secret ===
[split_card] Housel puts it neatly: Buffett's "skill is investing, but his secret is time."
[short_career] Imagine Buffett had started at 30 and retired at 60, like many people do.
[tiny_pile] With the same strong returns, Housel estimates he would have had about 11.9 million dollars, not billions.
=== More Returns, Less Wealth ===
[simons] Now meet Jim Simons, whose hedge fund earned about 66 percent a year since 1988.
That is far better than Buffett's record.
[two_towers] Yet Simons ended up much less wealthy, because he did not find his investing stride until around age 50.
He had the returns, but not the years.
=== Why We Miss It ===
[straight_line] Our brains think in straight lines, so compounding feels unnatural.
[small_steps] For years, small gains look boring and easy to ignore.
[late_payoff] The big payoff only appears late, long after many people have lost patience and switched plans.
=== Patience Over Brilliance ===
[steady_saver] So the goal is not the highest possible return.
It is a decent return you can stick with for a very long time.
[calendar] Every extra year you stay invested can matter more than any clever move you make.
Patience, not brilliance, does the heavy lifting.
=== Your Challenge ===
[savings_jar] Look at one investment or savings habit today.
[mirror] Ask yourself honestly: could I happily keep this going for the next twenty years?
