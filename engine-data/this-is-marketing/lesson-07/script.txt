=== Earn Trust ===
[megaphone] Most marketers shout louder when nobody listens.
[open_door] Seth Godin says the better move is to earn the right to be invited in.
=== Who Is It For ===
[two_questions] Godin keeps returning to two simple questions before any work begins.
[target_person] The first is, who is it for?
If you cannot picture the person, you cannot make a promise to them.
[blank_face] Saying everyone means you have promised no one anything.
=== What Is It For ===
[compass] The second question is, what is it for?
[destination] It asks what change you hope to create for that person.
A clear answer keeps every message, product, and email pointed in one direction.
=== Permission ===
[mailbox] Once you know who and what, you can ask for permission.
[junk_pile] Interrupting strangers with ads they never requested burns trust fast.
[raised_hand] Permission means people have raised their hand and said, yes, keep talking to me.
=== The Three Tests ===
Godin describes permission as delivering messages that are "anticipated, personal, and relevant."
[calendar] Anticipated means people expect to hear from you.
[name_tag] Personal means it is clearly meant for them.
[puzzle_piece] Relevant means it helps with something they actually care about.
=== Would They Miss You ===
[empty_chair] Here is the test Godin offers.
If you stopped showing up tomorrow, would anyone miss you?
[inbox] A newsletter people look forward to passes.
A flood of promotions nobody opens does not.
[shrug] If no one would miss you, you have attention, but not permission.
=== Keep Your Promise ===
[handshake] Trust is simply a promise kept, again and again.
[brick_wall] Each consistent delivery adds one more brick.
Godin notes that a brand is really the expectation people have about what you will deliver.
[cracked_brick] Break that expectation, and the wall weakens quickly.
=== They Tell Others ===
[happy_customer] When people trust you, something shifts.
[speech_bubble] They start telling friends, because sharing something good makes them look good too.
[ripple_rings] Your customers become your marketers, and the work spreads without shouting.
That kind of word of mouth is earned, not bought.
=== Your Challenge ===
Write one sentence naming who your work is for and what it is for.
Then ask yourself honestly, would they miss you if you disappeared?
