=== Listen to Understand ===
[title_card] Most of us don't listen to understand.
We listen to reply.
=== The Borrowed Glasses ===
[eye_doctor] Covey tells of an eye doctor who barely hears your complaint.
[glasses] Then he pulls off his own glasses and hands them to you.
They've worked for him for years, so surely they'll work for you.
[blurry_view] You put them on, and everything looks worse.
=== Listening to Reply ===
[two_people] That doctor is all of us in conversation.
[speech_bubble] While the other person talks, we're quietly rehearsing our answer.
[own_story] We filter their words through our own story, then prescribe what worked for us.
=== The Listening Ladder ===
[ladder] Covey describes a ladder of listening.
[level_ignore] At the bottom, we simply ignore people.
One rung up, we pretend, nodding along without hearing.
Then selective listening, hearing only some parts, and attentive listening, focused on the words.
[level_empathy] At the top sits empathic listening, stepping inside the other person's frame of reference.
=== Four Bad Reflexes ===
[four_icons] When we listen from our own story, we usually respond in four ways.
We evaluate, agreeing or disagreeing, or we probe with questions that suit us.
[advice_arrow] We advise from our experience, or we interpret their motives using our own.
None of these make people feel understood.
=== Father and Son ===
[teen_son] Covey pictures a teenage son announcing that school is pointless.
[worried_dad] A typical father jumps in with advice and warnings, and the son shuts down.
[calm_dad] An empathic father simply reflects the feeling back, like, you sound really frustrated.
The son keeps talking, and the real worry underneath finally comes out.
=== Diagnose First ===
[doctor_desk] Think of a good doctor, who always diagnoses before prescribing.
[quote_card] Covey's principle puts it plainly: Seek first to understand, then to be understood.
[air_tank] He compares being understood to air; until people get it, they can't hear anything else.
=== Then Be Understood ===
[scales] The second half matters too, and it takes courage.
[three_pillars] Covey borrows three ancient Greek ideas: your character, your connection with the person, and your reasoning.
Once you truly understand them, share your view clearly, framed around their concerns.
=== Your Challenge ===
[mirror] In your next tough conversation, hold your view until you can restate theirs.
Then speak, once they say you got it.
