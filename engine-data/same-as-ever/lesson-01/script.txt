=== Bet on What Lasts ===
[crystal_ball] Everyone wants to know what happens next.
Morgan Housel says that is the wrong question to ask.
=== The Forecast Trap ===
[forecaster] Picture an expert at a desk, confidently predicting next year's markets and headlines.
[calendar] Then the year arrives, and something nobody mentioned changes everything.
Specific events are almost impossible to call, no matter how smart the expert is.
=== The Other Question ===
[question_mark] Housel suggests flipping the question around.
Instead of asking what will change, ask what will stay the same.
[timeline] That second question has far more reliable answers.
=== Bezos's Insight ===
[bezos] Housel points to a habit of Amazon founder Jeff Bezos.
Bezos noticed people constantly asked him what would change over the next ten years.
Almost nobody asked what would not change.
[shopping_cart] Yet he was sure customers would always want low prices and fast delivery.
So Amazon could pour energy into those things, knowing the effort would never be wasted.
=== Details Change ===
[old_market] Imagine a busy marketplace from centuries ago.
[modern_phone] Now picture someone trading stocks on a phone today.
The tools look nothing alike.
[two_faces] But the faces show the same excitement, the same envy, the same panic.
History is full of different costumes worn by the same people.
=== Human Constants ===
[greed_fear] Housel argues that greed and fear show up in every era.
[storyteller] People everywhere get swept up in stories, and they compare themselves with their neighbours.
These traits barely move, even as technology races ahead.
=== Why It Helps ===
[map] Knowing what stays constant gives you a sturdier map than any forecast.
You cannot say when the next boom or bust will come.
[thermometer] But you can bet that when it does, people will overreact, just as they always have.
[anchor] That lets you plan around behaviour, not headlines.
=== Use It Today ===
[notebook] Here is a simple practice.
Before a big decision, write down the forecast it depends on.
[two_columns] Then write what would still be true if that forecast turned out wrong.
Lean your choice toward the second column.
=== Your Turn ===
[mirror] What is one thing about people that you are sure will still be true in twenty years?
How could you build your next decision around it?
