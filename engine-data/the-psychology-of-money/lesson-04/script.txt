=== Two Different Skills ===
[two_doors] Getting rich and staying rich sound like the same thing.
Morgan Housel says they actually need almost opposite skills.
=== The Getting Part ===
[rocket] Getting money usually takes boldness.
[dice] You have to take risks, bet on yourself, and stay optimistic when others doubt you.
Without some daring, wealth rarely shows up at all.
=== The Keeping Part ===
[shield] Keeping money asks for something very different.
[piggy_bank] It takes caution, frugality, and a healthy dose of fear.
You have to accept that some of your success came from luck that may not repeat.
Humility becomes more useful than courage.
=== Jesse Livermore ===
[trader] Housel tells the story of Jesse Livermore, one of the most famous stock traders of his time.
[crash_chart] When the market crashed in 1929, Livermore had bet that prices would fall.
[money_pile] Almost overnight, he became one of the richest people in the world.
=== Losing It All ===
[bigger_bets] But success made him overconfident, so he kept making bigger and riskier bets.
[empty_pockets] Within a few years, he had lost everything.
He was brilliant at getting rich.
He simply never learned how to stay rich.
=== Survival First ===
[sequoia_tree] Housel contrasts this with Michael Moritz of Sequoia, a hugely successful venture capital firm.
[clock] Asked why the firm had lasted so long, Moritz talked about survival.
He said they had always been afraid of going out of business.
Staying in the game matters, because time can only work for you if you are still playing.
=== Room for Error ===
[cushion] The key habit for staying rich is leaving room for error.
[storm_cloud] That means keeping savings and slack for the moments when things go wrong.
They will go wrong, sooner or later.
So plan on your plan not going according to plan.
=== Optimist and Paranoid ===
[balance_scale] Housel suggests being two things at once.
[sunrise] Be optimistic that the long-term future will be good.
[umbrella] And be paranoid about the short-term shocks that could knock you out before you get there.
=== Your Turn ===
If something went badly wrong tomorrow, would your money survive it?
This month, build one cushion that would help it survive, like a few months of expenses set aside.
