=== The Flawless Trap ===
[title_card] What if trying to be perfect is the very thing holding you back?
Today, let's swap flawless for excellent.
=== Meet the Perfectionist ===
[perfectionist] Picture someone hunched over a desk, polishing the same page for hours.
[red_pen] Every tiny flaw gets circled in red.
Nothing ever feels ready to share.
From the outside, it looks like dedication.
But Adam Grant argues it often works against growth.
=== What Research Shows ===
[bar_chart] Grant points to research on perfectionism at work.
[equal_bars] On average, perfectionists do not perform better than their colleagues.
All that extra effort simply doesn't turn into better results.
The polishing doesn't buy what it promises.
=== Three Hidden Costs ===
[magnifier] First, perfectionists obsess over details that barely matter.
[closed_door] Second, they avoid hard challenges, because hard challenges risk failure.
[storm_cloud] Third, they punish themselves for mistakes instead of learning from them.
Each cost quietly shrinks how far they can travel.
=== Beauty in Flaws ===
[tea_bowl] Grant borrows a Japanese idea called wabi sabi.
It means finding beauty in imperfection.
[crack_line] Picture a handmade bowl with an uneven rim and a small crack.
The flaws are part of its character, not a reason to throw it away.
Growth works the same way, because progress never requires being flawless.
=== Excellence Instead ===
[high_bar] Excellence is different from perfection.
You still set a high bar.
[finish_flag] But you finish, share, and improve, rather than waiting until nothing could go wrong.
Mistakes become information, not verdicts.
A good draft that exists beats a perfect one that never leaves your desk.
=== Choose Your Battles ===
[checklist] Not every detail deserves the same care.
Decide which few things truly matter, and polish those carefully.
[scale_balance] Let the rest be good enough, so your energy goes where it counts.
=== Judge Your Progress ===
[past_self] Another shift is about who you compare yourself to.
Instead of measuring against an ideal, measure against who you were before.
[growth_path] Asking how far you have come keeps you moving instead of freezing.
Small steps forward count, even when the result is far from perfect.
=== Your Challenge ===
[challenge] This week, pick one project you have been polishing for too long.
Share it now, imperfections and all, and notice what you learn.
