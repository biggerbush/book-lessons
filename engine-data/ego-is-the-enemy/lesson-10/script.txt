=== Keep Your Own Scorecard ===
[scoreboard] Who decides whether you won today?
Holiday says it should be you, not the crowd.
=== The Ego Cycle ===
[cycle_wheel] Across the whole book, ego shows up in three seasons: aspiring, succeeding and failing.
[arrow_loop] But Holiday stresses that these seasons loop, again and again, all through our lives.
[climber] So beating ego once is never enough.
=== The Outside Score ===
[crowd] Most of us measure ourselves by what others can see.
[trophy] Applause, promotions, wins and followers become our score.
Those numbers depend on luck, timing and other people.
When they rise, ego swells, and when they fall, ego sulks.
=== Winning Is Not Enough ===
[coach] Holiday points to football coach Bill Belichick of the New England Patriots.
[game_film] Even after a win, Belichick would study what went wrong and push his team to fix it.
To him, the result on the board was not the real measure.
The real question was whether they played to their own standard.
=== Two Kinds of Score ===
[split_screen] This means you can win while playing badly, and lose while doing everything right.
[lucky_win] A lucky win teaches you nothing if you just celebrate it.
[honest_loss] An honest loss can still be a success by your own measure.
=== The Inner Scorecard ===
[investor] Holiday also borrows an idea from investor Warren Buffett, who talks about an inner scorecard and an outer scorecard.
[two_cards] The outer card records what the world thinks of you.
The inner card records whether you lived up to your own standards.
The inner card is the one Buffett says matters most.
=== What Goes on Your Card ===
[checklist] Your card should hold things you can actually control.
[sweat_drop] Did you give real effort today?
[handshake] Did you act with integrity, even when nobody was watching?
These marks cannot be taken by rivals or handed out by crowds.
=== Making It a Habit ===
[notebook] Holiday's cure is not one big victory over ego, but a steady practice.
[evening_lamp] At the end of each day, quietly review your own performance.
Then go back to work tomorrow, a little more honest.
=== Your Challenge ===
[pencil] Tonight, write down three standards that define a good day for you.
Then score yourself by those, and nothing else.
