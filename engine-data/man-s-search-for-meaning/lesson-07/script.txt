=== Life Is Asking You ===
[title_q] What if you have been asking life the wrong question all along?
Frankl believed the question points the other way.
=== The Usual Question ===
[person_wish] Most of us look at life and ask what we want from it.
It feels like the natural way to think.
[wish_list] We hope for comfort, success, and a little happiness.
[storm_cloud] When life fails to deliver, we feel cheated and lost.
=== Turning It Around ===
[arrow_flip] In the camps, Frankl came to see that the question had to be reversed.
What we expect from life matters less than what life expects from us.
[spotlight] We are not the ones asking.
We are the ones being asked.
=== Answer With Action ===
[open_hands] Frankl said we answer life not with clever words or deep thoughts.
[footsteps] We answer through what we actually do, and how we conduct ourselves.
To live, in his view, is to take responsibility for finding the right answer.
Not once in theory, but again and again, in each situation.
=== No Best Move ===
[chess_board] Frankl compared this to asking a chess champion for the best move in the world.
[champion] The champion would say there is no such thing.
The best move depends on this game, this board, and this opponent.
=== Your Unique Moment ===
[clock] Meaning works the same way, Frankl argued.
[two_people] It differs from person to person, and from one hour to the next.
[question_mark] So the real question is what this moment, right here, is asking of you.
=== The Grieving Doctor ===
[old_doctor] Frankl describes an elderly doctor who came to him, crushed by grief.
[empty_chair] His beloved wife had died two years earlier.
Frankl asked him what would have happened if he had died first.
[wife_photo] The doctor said she would have suffered terribly.
=== Pain With a Purpose ===
[shield] Frankl replied gently that he had spared her that suffering.
The price was that he now had to survive and mourn her.
[handshake] The doctor said nothing, shook Frankl's hand, and quietly left.
His loss had not changed, but now it had a meaning.
Frankl observed that suffering stops being mere suffering once it finds a meaning.
=== Your Turn ===
Tonight, swap one question for another.
Instead of asking what you want from today, ask what today is asking of you, and answer it.
