=== Title ===
[juggler] You have been told you can have it all.
Greg McKeown says that promise is quietly wearing you out.
=== Every Yes Is a No ===
[open_door] Every time you say yes to something, you are walking through one door.
[closed_doors] And behind you, other doors quietly close.
[clock] Your time and energy are limited, so every choice spends them somewhere.
McKeown calls these trade-offs, and he says we cannot escape them.
=== The Myth of All ===
[busy_person] Most of us respond by pretending trade-offs do not exist.
[todo_list] We keep adding to the list and ask, how can I fit it all in?
[thin_lines] The result is effort spread so thin that nothing gets our best.
=== A Bold Airline ===
[plane] McKeown points to Southwest Airlines and its founder, Herb Kelleher.
[crossed_meal] Southwest chose not to serve meals, assign seats, or offer first class.
[short_route] It flew simple point to point routes instead of trying to go everywhere.
Those deliberate trade-offs kept fares low and planes running on time.
=== Trying to Do Both ===
[rival_plane] Continental Airlines noticed and launched a budget service to compete.
[split_arrows] But it tried to keep its full service model running at the same time.
[money_drain] Straddling both strategies cost the company heavily, and the budget experiment was shut down.
=== Which Problem? ===
[question_mark] So McKeown suggests a different question.
Not, how can I do it all, but, which problem do I want?
[scales] A trade-off is a choice between two good things, not a failure.
Owning that choice turns guilt into clarity.
=== Go Big Somewhere ===
[spotlight] An Essentialist asks, what can I go big on?
[two_paths] Picture a free evening where you could take on an extra project or rest with your family.
Pretending you can do both usually means doing both badly.
Choosing one, on purpose, lets you give it everything.
=== Name the Trade ===
[notebook] Here is a simple habit to try.
Before saying yes to anything new, write down what it will cost.
[crossed_item] Name the specific thing you will give up to make room.
If the trade is not worth it, you have your answer.
=== Your Turn ===
[mirror] What trade-off are you avoiding right now?
This week, name it out loud, and choose on purpose.
