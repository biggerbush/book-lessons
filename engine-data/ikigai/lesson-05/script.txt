=== Why Get Up? ===
[alarm_clock] Some mornings it's hard to remember why you're getting out of bed.
Two very different therapies offer a surprisingly practical answer.
=== The Doctor in the Camps ===
[frankl] Viktor Frankl was a psychiatrist from Vienna who survived Nazi concentration camps.
[prisoners] He noticed that prisoners holding on to a reason to live, like a loved one or unfinished work, seemed to endure better.
=== A Why for Every How ===
[logotherapy] After the war, he built a therapy around this insight and called it logotherapy, from the Greek word for meaning.
[quote_card] Frankl loved a line from Nietzsche: "He who has a why to live for can bear almost any how."
[compass] The idea is simple: meaning is not handed to us, we have to find it ourselves.
=== The Unhappy Diplomat ===
[diplomat] The book retells one of Frankl's cases, an American diplomat who had spent years in therapy feeling stuck.
[old_couch] He had been told his unhappiness came from deep conflicts with his father.
[job_desk] Frankl saw a simpler problem: the diplomat's job had stopped feeling meaningful to him.
[new_door] Once he faced that, he chose a new career and his frustration eased.
=== Japan's Morita Way ===
[morita] In Japan, early in the twentieth century, a psychiatrist named Shoma Morita took a different route.
[weather] He believed feelings are like the weather: we cannot order them to change.
[hands] What we can control is what we do next.
=== Rest, Then Act ===
[bed] In Morita therapy, patients might begin with about a week of rest, simply noticing their thoughts and emotions come and go.
[garden_task] Then they move on to simple, quiet tasks with their hands.
[community] Step by step, they return to work and everyday life, focused on action rather than fixing feelings.
=== Feel It, Do It Anyway ===
[two_paths] Logotherapy helps you find your why.
[nervous_heart] Morita reminds you that you don't need to feel calm or confident before acting on it.
[first_step] You can feel anxious and still make the call, write the page, or visit the friend.
=== Your Challenge ===
[notebook] Write down one thing that gives your days meaning, and one feeling that keeps holding you back.
Tomorrow, let the feeling come along, and take one small step toward the meaning anyway.
