=== Autopilot ===
[brain_glow] What if most of your day is run by a part of you that you never consult?
Today, a man who forgot almost everything shows us why.
=== Meet Eugene ===
[eugene] Meet Eugene Pauly, a retired man living in California with his wife, Beverly.
[virus] In 1992, a viral infection attacked his brain and destroyed the parts that store new memories.
[blank_slate] Afterwards, he could not hold onto a new fact for more than a few moments.
=== The Lost Map ===
[house] Eugene and Beverly moved into a new house after his illness.
[map_blank] When scientists asked him to sketch its layout, he could not do it.
[kitchen] Yet when he felt hungry, he would stand up, walk to the kitchen, and find a snack.
=== The Daily Walk ===
[sneakers] Eugene also took a walk around his neighborhood every day.
[worried_wife] Beverly feared he would get lost, since he could not describe where he lived.
[front_door] But each time, he found his way home, with no idea how.
=== The Object Test ===
[scientist] A memory researcher named Larry Squire designed a simple experiment.
[object_pair] He showed Eugene pairs of small objects, one marked underneath as the correct choice.
[checkmark] Over weeks, Eugene picked the right ones more and more often, with no memory of ever seeing them.
=== Hidden Brain Part ===
[basal_ganglia] Squire concluded that habits live in an older brain region, the basal ganglia, which Eugene's illness had spared.
[two_paths] Memory and habit run on separate tracks.
[auto_pilot] So a habit can keep working even when we have no conscious idea why we are doing it.
=== Not Just Eugene ===
[pie_chart] Duhigg cites a 2006 Duke University study suggesting more than 40 percent of our daily actions are habits, not decisions.
[toothbrush] Brushing your teeth, the drive to work, reaching for your phone.
[you] You are doing them, but you are not really choosing them.
=== The Good News ===
[sun_rays] That is unsettling, but it is also good news.
[steering] If so much of life runs on autopilot, noticing those patterns is the first step to steering them.
=== Your Challenge ===
[clock] Tomorrow, pick one ordinary hour and watch yourself closely.
How many things happen before you decide to do them?
