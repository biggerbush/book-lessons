=== The Life of the Party ===
[party_room] You know the colleague who walks into a meeting, and suddenly the whole room feels louder and brighter?
In Thomas Erikson's color system, that person is probably a Yellow.
=== Meet the Yellow ===
[yellow_person] Yellows are sociable, optimistic and expressive.
[speech_bubble] They talk easily, laugh often, and think out loud.
[sun_icon] Their default setting is that things will probably work out fine.
[mixed_colors] Most people are a blend, but in Yellows this sunny, social side shines brightest.
=== Fueled by People ===
[crowd] Yellows get their energy from other people.
[spotlight] They enjoy attention, praise and being liked.
[empty_desk] Long stretches of quiet, solitary work can leave them restless and drained.
=== The Idea Machine ===
[lightbulb] Their great strength is creativity.
[whiteboard] In a brainstorm, a Yellow can produce idea after idea, building happily on whatever was said last.
[megaphone] They are also persuasive, and can get a whole team excited about a vision.
=== The Weak Spots ===
[ear_crossed] The flip side, Erikson notes, is that Yellows are often poor listeners.
[loose_papers] Details bore them, so facts get fuzzy and paperwork gets forgotten.
[broken_chain] They start projects with huge energy, then drift away before the finish line.
=== The Meeting Pattern ===
[meeting_table] Erikson describes a familiar pattern.
A Yellow leaves a meeting buzzing, having said yes to several tasks.
[calendar] A week later, many of those promises have quietly slipped away.
It is rarely bad intent.
Their attention has simply moved on to the next exciting thing.
=== How to Win Them Over ===
[handshake] Start with a little small talk and genuine warmth.
[big_picture] Talk about the vision and how it will feel, not just the numbers.
[thumbs_up] Show enthusiasm, and give them credit in front of others.
Avoid burying them in spreadsheets and fine print.
=== Then Help Them Finish ===
[checklist] Once they are excited, add the structure they tend to lack.
[email] Follow up in writing with clear tasks and deadlines.
[check_in] Schedule short check-ins, so their energy carries all the way to done.
Keep it light and friendly, so it feels like support, not control.
=== Your Turn ===
Think of the Yellow in your life.
Next time you need something from them, lead with excitement, then send a short written follow-up.
