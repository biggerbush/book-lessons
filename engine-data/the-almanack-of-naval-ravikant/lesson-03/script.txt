=== Your Effort, Multiplied ===
[sleeping] What if your work kept going while you slept?
Naval says that is exactly what the best kind of leverage does.
=== Fortunes Need Leverage ===
[lever] Leverage is anything that multiplies the effect of your effort.
[quote_card] As Naval puts it, "Fortunes require leverage."
[two_hands] Working harder with your own two hands only adds, but leverage multiplies.
=== Labor ===
[crowd] The oldest kind of leverage is labor, meaning other people working for you.
[manager] It is powerful, but you need people willing to follow you.
[messy_team] And managing people is messy, so Naval calls it the worst form of leverage.
=== Capital ===
[money_bag] The second kind is capital, meaning money that does the work.
[banker] Capital scales well, but someone has to trust you with it first.
Both labor and capital need another person's permission.
=== The New Leverage ===
[laptop] The third kind is new: code and media.
[copy_machine] These are products that cost almost nothing to copy again and again.
[open_door] And nobody has to say yes before you start.
Naval calls this permissionless leverage, and it sits behind many of today's new fortunes.
=== Code ===
[robot_army] Naval describes code as an army of robots working for you.
[night_moon] A programmer writes something once, and it keeps running at night while they sleep.
[users_map] One app can serve millions of people without a single new hire.
=== Media ===
[microphone] Media works the same way, through writing, podcasts and videos.
[podcast_ear] One good podcast episode can reach more listeners than a lifetime of speeches.
[phone_tweet] Naval's own example is a thread of tweets he wrote about how to get rich.
[world_globe] It spread to readers everywhere, and its ideas later helped shape this very book.
=== Start Small ===
[notebook] You do not need a team or investors to begin.
[first_post] Write one useful article, record one short video, or build one small tool.
[skill_fork] Pick whichever path matches your strengths, writing code or telling stories.
[snowball] Each piece you publish can keep working long after you stop typing.
=== Your Challenge ===
[question] This week, make one thing that can be copied for free.
Then ask yourself, what would it take for it to reach a million people?
