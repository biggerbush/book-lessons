=== The Paper Puzzle ===
[paper_note] Look at a banknote in your wallet.
It is just paper, so why would anyone trade a hot meal for it?
=== The Barter Headache ===
[apple_farmer] Picture an apple grower in the hills who needs new shoes.
[shoemaker] The shoemaker in town does not want apples, he wants a haircut.
Harari points out that barter gets messy very fast.
[rate_table] With just 100 different goods, traders would need to track 4,950 separate exchange rates.
=== Shells as Cash ===
[cowry_shell] Money fixes this by giving everyone one thing they all accept.
For about 4,000 years, people across Africa, South Asia, East Asia and Oceania used cowry shells as money.
[shell_string] A shell has little practical use, yet it could buy food, cloth or labour.
=== Barley and Silver ===
[barley_sack] In ancient Sumer, one of the earliest known currencies was barley, measured out in set amounts.
Barley could at least be eaten, so trusting it was easy.
[silver_bar] Later, people switched to silver, measured by a weight called the shekel.
[balance_scale] Silver was useless for eating or farming, but everyone believed others wanted it.
=== Cigarette Money ===
[prison_cell] Harari notes that in prisons and prisoner of war camps, cigarettes have often served as money.
[cigarette] Even prisoners who never smoked accepted them, because they could pass them on.
=== Money as Data ===
[computer] Today, most money is not even a physical object.
[pie_chart] Harari reports that of roughly 60 trillion dollars in the world, under 6 trillion exists as coins and notes.
The rest is simply numbers stored on computer servers.
=== Built on Trust ===
[trust_web] So what gives money its value? Not its material, but trust.
I accept your money only because I trust that strangers will accept it from me.
[quote_card] Harari calls money "the most universal and most efficient system of mutual trust ever devised."
=== Enemies Agree ===
[two_flags] That trust crosses almost every border.
People who disagreed bitterly about God still happily accepted the same gold and silver coins.
[gold_coin] Money lets total strangers cooperate without knowing or liking each other.
=== Your Turn ===
[pay_moment] Next time you pay for something, notice the invisible web of strangers trusting each other.
What else in your life works only because everyone agrees to believe in it?
