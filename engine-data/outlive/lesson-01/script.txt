=== Your Last Ten Years ===
[old_clock] Picture the final ten years of your life.
Will you be hiking and laughing, or stuck in a chair, waiting?
=== Two Kinds of Years ===
[timeline] Peter Attia says every life can be measured in two ways.
[lifespan_bar] The first is lifespan, simply how many years you live.
[healthspan_bar] The second is healthspan, the years you live with a body and mind that still work well.
=== The Marginal Decade ===
[last_segment] Attia gives the final stretch of life a name: the marginal decade.
For many people, those years are spent in slow decline.
[walker] Simple things, like carrying groceries or getting off the floor, become hard or impossible.
[question_mark] Attia wants you to ask what you want those years to look like.
=== The Body Declines ===
[muscle_icon] Healthspan has three parts, and the first is the body.
With age, we tend to lose muscle, strength, and balance.
[stairs] A flight of stairs that once felt like nothing can slowly become a wall.
=== The Mind Declines ===
[brain] The second part is the mind.
Memory, focus, and quick thinking can fade, sometimes years before anyone notices.
[puzzle] Losing them means losing independence, even if the body is still strong.
=== The Heart Matters Too ===
[smiling_face] The third part is emotional health.
Attia argues that feeling connected and having purpose are part of a good life, not extras.
[friends] A long life without joy or relationships is not the goal.
=== Two Curves ===
[graph_axes] Attia draws a simple graph, with age along the bottom and health up the side.
[gray_curve] On the usual path, health slides downward for years, then drops into a long, weak tail before death.
[green_curve] On his ideal path, health stays high much longer, and the decline at the end is short.
The goal is more good years, not just more years.
=== Start Decades Early ===
[young_adult] Here's the catch: the marginal decade is shaped long before it arrives.
[habit_blocks] The strength and habits you build in midlife are what you carry into old age.
[calendar] So the best time to plan for that decade is now.
=== Your Challenge ===
[notebook] Imagine an ordinary day in your marginal decade, and write down three things you want to do.
Keep that list somewhere you'll see it.
