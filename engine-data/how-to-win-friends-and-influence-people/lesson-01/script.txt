=== The Sharp Word ===
[title_card] Think of the last time someone criticized you.
Did it make you want to change, or just want to argue back?
=== The Urge to Scold ===
[coworker] Someone lets you down, and your first instinct is to point out exactly what they did wrong.
[pointing_hand] It feels fair, and it feels satisfying.
[question_mark] Dale Carnegie asks a simple question: does it ever actually work?
=== Nobody Feels Guilty ===
[gangster] Carnegie describes Two Gun Crowley, a killer cornered by police in a New York apartment.
[handwritten] During the siege, Crowley wrote a letter claiming he had a kind heart that would harm no one.
[prison_cell] Later, facing execution, he insisted he had only been defending himself.
=== Why It Backfires ===
[shield] Criticism puts people on the defensive.
[cracked_badge] It wounds their pride and their sense of being important.
[storm_cloud] And it stirs up resentment that can linger for years.
So instead of changing, people dig in and justify themselves.
=== Lincoln's Angry Letter ===
[battlefield] After the Battle of Gettysburg, Confederate general Robert E. Lee was retreating south.
[river] A flooded river trapped his army, and Lincoln ordered General Meade to attack at once.
[general] Meade hesitated, and Lee escaped.
[letter] Furious, Lincoln sat down and wrote Meade a bitter letter of disappointment.
=== The Letter Never Sent ===
[desk_drawer] But Lincoln never sent it.
It was found among his papers after his death.
[thought_cloud] Carnegie suggests Lincoln imagined himself in Meade's place, seeing the dead and wounded up close.
Perhaps, Lincoln may have thought, he would have hesitated too.
=== Understand Instead ===
[balance_scale] Carnegie's point is that understanding people does far more good than condemning them.
[open_hands] When you ask why someone acted as they did, you often find reasons that make sense to them.
[quote_card] As Carnegie put it, "Any fool can criticize, condemn and complain, and most fools do."
=== Your Unsent Letter ===
[pause_button] Next time you feel the urge to criticize, pause before you speak.
[notebook] If you must vent, write it down and leave it unsent, just as Lincoln did.
Then ask yourself what might have led them to act that way.
=== Your Challenge ===
[calendar] For one week, catch every criticism before it leaves your mouth.
Replace it with one honest question about the other person's side.
