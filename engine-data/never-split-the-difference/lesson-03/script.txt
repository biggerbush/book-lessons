=== Name the Feeling ===
[hook_door] What if the fastest way to calm an upset person was simply to say what they feel?
Not fix it, not argue, just name it.
=== What a Label Is ===
[label_tag] Chris Voss calls this a label.
You spot an emotion in the other person, and you say it out loud.
[speech_bubble] It sounds like you're frustrated.
It seems like this really matters to you.
=== The Magic Words ===
[three_phrases] Voss suggests starting with it sounds like, it seems like, or it looks like.
[no_i_sign] Avoid starting with the word I, because that turns the moment toward you.
[pause_clock] After the label, stop talking, and let the silence do its work.
=== The Harlem Door ===
[apartment] Voss once faced fugitives barricaded inside an apartment in Harlem.
[phone_line] For hours, he spoke calmly through the door and got no answer at all.
He kept labeling what they might be feeling.
It seems like you don't want to come out.
It seems like you worry we'll storm in.
[open_door] Eventually the door opened, and everyone inside came out without a shot fired.
=== Why It Works ===
[brain] Voss points to brain research showing that putting a fear into words weakens it.
[water_bucket] Naming a bad feeling is like pouring water on a small fire.
[warm_glow] Naming a good feeling, like hope or pride, helps it grow stronger.
=== Say the Worst First ===
[list_paper] Voss goes further with a tool he calls an accusation audit.
Before the talk, you list every unkind thing the other person might think about you.
[mouth_open] Then you say those things yourself, right at the start, before they can.
=== An Everyday Audit ===
[boss_desk] Picture asking your boss for more time on a project.
You might open with, you probably think I'm disorganized.
[shrug] You might even think I should have warned you sooner.
[nod_smile] Once the worst is said out loud, it loses its sting, and people often soften.
=== Your Turn ===
[mirror_you] In your next tense conversation, pause and name what the other person seems to feel.
Then stay quiet, and notice what they tell you next.
