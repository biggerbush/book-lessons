=== Almost Ready ===
[unfinished] Somewhere on your laptop is a project that is almost ready, and has been almost ready for a year.
What if almost is the problem?
=== Perfection Hides ===
[polish_cloth] Polishing feels like hard work, so it feels responsible.
But Seth Godin sees endless polishing as a place to hide.
[locked_door] As long as the work stays unfinished, nobody can judge it.
Perfectionism keeps you safe, and it keeps your work invisible.
=== Amateur or Pro ===
[amateur] An amateur works when inspiration strikes and waits until it feels right.
[professional] A professional, in Godin's view, does the work whether or not they are in the mood.
The difference is not talent or pay.
It is a commitment to deliver.
=== The Ship Date ===
[calendar] Professionals put a date on the calendar and treat it as real.
[ship_box] When the date arrives, the work goes out the door.
Godin calls this shipping.
A schedule turns a vague hope into a promise you keep.
=== A Daily Blog ===
[blog_post] Godin's own example is his blog, where he has published a new post every single day for many years.
Not every post is brilliant.
[stack_posts] Some land and some fall flat.
But the habit of shipping daily keeps the work flowing and the bar steady.
=== Good Enough ===
[ruler] Godin is careful about the phrase good enough.
It does not mean careless.
[checklist] It means the work meets its purpose for the people it is meant to serve.
[wedding_toast] A wedding toast does not need to be a novel.
=== Not Sloppy ===
[skilled_hands] Good enough still demands skill, care and honest effort.
What it rejects is the extra month of fiddling that no reader will ever notice.
[open_door] Once the work does its job, you let it go.
=== Pick Your Date ===
[project_card] Choose one stalled project today.
[red_circle] Circle a ship date on the calendar, and write down what good enough means for that piece.
Then honor the date, even if your nerves argue.
=== Your Challenge ===
[friday_flag] What will you ship by Friday, and who will you tell so you keep the promise?
