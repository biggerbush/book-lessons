=== The Paradox ===
[title_card] What if the thing that made you successful is the very thing that could pull you apart?
Greg McKeown calls this the paradox of success.
=== Clarity First ===
[focused_pro] Picture a young professional with one clear goal.
[single_arrow] All her energy points in a single direction, like one sharp arrow.
Because she is focused, she does excellent work and gets noticed.
[trophy] That clarity of purpose leads to success.
=== More Options ===
[inbox] Success brings attention, and attention brings requests.
[new_doors] Suddenly there are new projects, committees, meetings and favours, each one a door asking her to walk through.
She becomes known as the person who gets things done, so people ask for more.
=== Spread Thin ===
[scatter_arrow] Her single arrow splits into dozens of tiny arrows, all pointing different ways.
She says yes to most of it, because every request seems reasonable.
[tired_face] Now her effort is diffused, and she makes only a millimetre of progress in many directions.
=== Clarity Undermined ===
[foggy_lens] Here is the cruel twist.
The scattered effort clouds the very clarity that made her successful.
[falling_chart] Her best work slips, and the success starts to fade.
=== The Loop ===
[cycle_wheel] McKeown describes this as four phases in a loop.
Clarity leads to success, success leads to options, options lead to diffused effort, and diffused effort undermines clarity.
In other words, success can quietly become a catalyst for failure.
=== Companies Too ===
[collins_book] The same pattern shows up in organisations.
McKeown points to research by Jim Collins on why once great companies fall.
[pursuit_more] One key cause Collins names is "the undisciplined pursuit of more."
[company_tower] Firms grow, chase every opportunity, and lose sight of what made them great.
=== Break the Cycle ===
[magnifier] The way out is not to stop succeeding.
It is to notice when new options start crowding your original purpose.
[back_arrow] When success arrives, pause and ask what made you good in the first place.
[shield] Then protect that focus before you say yes to anything new.
=== Your Turn ===
[mirror] Think back to what first made you successful.
Is it still getting your best energy, or has success buried it?
