=== Calm Plants Crazy ===
[calm_sea] What if the calmest moments are secretly the most dangerous?
Morgan Housel says that is exactly how people work.
=== The Big Idea ===
[economist] Housel leans on the economist Hyman Minsky.
He studied why financial crises keep happening, even in modern economies.
[quote_card] Minsky's core insight, as Housel tells it: "Stability is destabilizing."
That sounds backwards, but it describes something every era repeats.
=== How It Works ===
[good_years] Picture a long run of good years.
Jobs are steady, prices rise, and nothing breaks.
[confidence] People start to believe the calm is permanent.
Each year without trouble makes the next risk feel smaller.
[borrowing] So they borrow more and take bigger bets.
=== The Turn ===
[stack] Each bet makes sense on its own.
Together, they stack up into a tower of risk.
[crack] Then a small shock arrives, and the tower falls hard.
The calm did not just come before the crisis. It helped cause it.
=== Great Moderation ===
[timeline] Housel points to the years before 2008.
Economists called that stretch the Great Moderation.
[flat_line] Growth was smooth, and many believed big recessions were a thing of the past.
[house] That confidence fed easy lending and risky home loans.
=== The Crash ===
[falling_bar] Then came the financial crisis and a deep recession.
The very belief that things were safe had made them unsafe.
[soil] Calm was not the opposite of the crash. It was the soil it grew in.
=== It Is Not a Flaw ===
[human_head] Housel stresses this is not stupidity. It is human nature.
[pendulum] When risk feels gone, taking more risk feels rational.
Every generation thinks it has learned the lesson, then a long calm teaches it to forget.
So booms and busts keep returning, no matter how smart we get.
=== In Your Life ===
[wallet] The same pattern shows up in your own money and habits.
[checklist] After a long good stretch, you may stop saving, skip checkups, or stretch your budget.
[alarm] Treat that comfortable feeling as a warning light, not a green light.
The longer things go well, the more deliberately you should check your margins.
=== Your Challenge ===
Think of one area where life has felt calm for years.
What risk have you quietly added because nothing has gone wrong?
