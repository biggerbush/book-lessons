=== Don't Believe Your Story ===
[trophy_shelf] You've just had a big win, and a voice inside says you were always meant for this.
That voice is ego, and it is lying to you.
=== The Neat Story ===
[storybook] After success, we love to tidy the past into a neat story.
[arrow_path] Every step suddenly looks planned, like a straight line to greatness.
Holiday warns that this story feels good but quietly makes us arrogant.
=== Why It's Dangerous ===
[crown] If we believe we were destined to win, we start thinking we deserve to keep winning.
Success starts to feel like a birthright instead of a result.
[skipped_steps] Then we skip the boring habits that got us there.
The story replaces the work.
=== Meet Bill Walsh ===
[walsh] Holiday's example is Bill Walsh, who became head coach of the San Francisco Forty-Niners in 1979.
[losing_team] The team was one of the worst in professional football.
[super_bowl] Within three seasons, they won the Super Bowl.
=== No Grand Vision ===
[thought_cloud] It is tempting to say Walsh had a master plan for a championship.
Holiday says that was not really how he worked.
[rulebook] Walsh focused instead on what he called a Standard of Performance.
=== Standards Everywhere ===
[locker_room] He set high expectations for small things, like how the locker room looked and how players dressed.
[stopwatch] Practices were planned carefully, down to the details.
[staff] Even staff away from the field were expected to do their jobs with care.
Walsh believed these small details shaped how the whole team thought about excellence.
=== Score Takes Care ===
[scoreboard] Walsh's belief, quoted in the book, was simple: The score takes care of itself.
Wins were the result, not the goal he stared at.
[checklist] He kept the team's eyes on doing each day well.
=== Your Daily Standard ===
[mirror] So when you succeed, resist the urge to write your legend.
[notebook] Instead, write down the daily habits that actually produced the result.
[calendar] Then keep doing them, especially when it feels like you no longer need to.
=== Your Challenge ===
Think of your latest win.
[question_mark] What small standard got you there, and are you still keeping it tomorrow?
