=== You Make Things ===
[hook_person] Maybe you think you are not a creative person.
Rick Rubin would gently disagree, and he has a surprisingly simple reason why.
=== The Old Myth ===
[spotlight] Many of us picture creativity as a spotlight that shines on a lucky few.
[painter] Painters, musicians and poets stand inside it.
[crowd] Everyone else watches from the dark, convinced they lack the gift.
=== Rubin's Claim ===
[rubin] Rubin has spent decades in studios with famous artists, yet he rejects that picture completely.
[quote_card] As he puts it, "Creativity is not a rare ability."
[human_icon] He sees it as a fundamental part of being human.
=== Everyday Making ===
To create, in Rubin's view, simply means bringing something into being that was not there before.
[chat_bubbles] That could be a conversation with a friend.
[sofa] It could be rearranging the furniture in your living room.
[new_route] It could be finding a fresh way home to dodge a traffic jam.
=== Seeing Is Making ===
[eye] Rubin goes further and says even perceiving is creative.
[brain] Your senses take in the world, and your mind assembles its own version of it.
[two_heads] No one else builds exactly the same picture, so your view of reality is already an original work.
=== Respond, Then Add ===
[input_arrow] Then you respond to what you notice.
[choice_paths] Every choice you make shapes something, however small, and leaves your mark on the day.
So you are creating constantly, whether or not you call it art.
=== No Audience Needed ===
[gallery_frame] Rubin says a work does not need to be sold, recorded or hung in a gallery to count.
[note_card] A note to a friend counts just as much as a hit record.
The value lies in the making itself.
=== A Way of Being ===
[artist_badge] For Rubin, being an artist is less a job title and more a way of moving through the world.
[open_door] Once you accept that you already create, the door stops looking locked.
You are not starting from zero, you are simply continuing.
=== Your Challenge ===
[checklist] Tonight, list three things you made today, from a conversation to a quick fix.
Then ask yourself, what might you make if you did it on purpose?
