=== You Are Not Luck ===
[title_card] Is your future a lottery ticket, or a blueprint?
Peter Thiel says how you answer changes everything.
=== The Lottery Mindset ===
[lottery_ticket] Many people treat the future like a lottery ticket.
They believe success mostly comes from luck, so planning seems pointless.
[option_doors] Instead, they keep every option open and wait to see what happens.
=== Two Big Questions ===
[quad_grid] Thiel sorts attitudes about the future using two simple questions.
[sun_cloud] First, do you expect the future to be better or worse?
[blueprint] Second, is it definite, something you can plan, or indefinite, something blurry and unknowable?
=== Four Mindsets ===
[china_flag] Thiel describes China as definitely pessimistic, planning hard while fearing the future.
[europe_map] He sees much of Europe as indefinitely pessimistic, drifting and expecting decline.
[usa_flag] And he sees today's America as indefinitely optimistic, hopeful but without a plan.
The fourth mindset, definite optimism, is the one he wants back.
=== America Used to Plan ===
In the mid twentieth century, America was definitely optimistic.
[rocket] It planned bold projects like the Apollo program.
[highway] It built the interstate highway system on purpose, over decades.
[bank_tower] Later, Thiel argues, energy shifted toward finance, where money moves around because nobody knows what to build.
=== The Well Rounded Trap ===
[resume] Thiel sees the same pattern in ambitious students.
They stack up activities to look well rounded, collecting options instead of mastering anything.
[scattered] The result is many shallow skills and no clear direction.
[deep_well] He argues it's better to choose one path and go deep.
=== Planners Who Won ===
[apple_logo] Thiel points to Steve Jobs, who planned new products years ahead.
[offer_check] He also recalls when Yahoo offered to buy Facebook for 1 billion dollars.
[zuckerberg] Mark Zuckerberg turned it down, because he had a definite vision for what Facebook could become.
=== Make Your Plan ===
[quote_card] One chapter title says it plainly: "You are not a lottery ticket."
[plan_map] So write down a concrete, long-term plan for what you want to build.
[hammer] Then work every week to make it real, and adjust as you learn.
Thiel argues even a bad plan beats having no plan at all.
=== Your Challenge ===
[first_step] What would you build over ten years if luck played no part?
Write down the first step today.
