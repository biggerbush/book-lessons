=== Spinach in Your Teeth ===
[spinach] Imagine a colleague walks into a big meeting with spinach stuck in their teeth.
Would you tell them, and how?
=== The Simplest Candor ===
[colleague] Kim Scott uses this tiny moment to show what good feedback looks like.
[whisper] You quietly pull them aside, tell them right away, and help them fix it.
That small act holds four qualities she says all guidance needs.
=== Be Humble ===
[magnifier] First, be humble.
You might be wrong about what you saw.
[speech_bubble] So share your view as your perspective, and invite the other person to correct you.
=== Be Helpful ===
[toolbox] Second, be helpful.
Feedback is not a chance to vent or prove you are smart.
[target] Aim it at something the person can actually change, so they leave better off.
=== Be Immediate ===
[clock] Third, be immediate.
[calendar] Do not save criticism for an annual performance review, when the moment is long gone.
[stopwatch] Scott favours quick two or three minute conversations right after something happens.
=== Be In Person ===
[laptop] Fourth, give it in person whenever you can.
Email strips out tone, so a small note can land like a blow.
[faces] Face to face, you can see how your words are received and adjust.
If you cannot meet, a video or phone call beats a typed message.
=== Private and Public ===
[closed_door] Criticism usually belongs in private, so people can listen without feeling shamed.
[spotlight] Praise often belongs in public, so the whole team learns what great work looks like.
Make praise specific too, because vague praise teaches nothing.
[label] And describe the work, not the person, so it never feels like an attack on who they are.
=== Small and Often ===
[small_notes] Put together, these habits turn feedback into something small, frequent and normal.
Feedback stops being a dramatic event and becomes part of everyday work.
[trust_meter] When guidance arrives quickly and kindly, people stop dreading it and start trusting it.
=== Your Next Move ===
Today, notice one thing a teammate did well or could improve.
Tell them within the hour, face to face, in under three minutes.
Then ask them how it landed.
