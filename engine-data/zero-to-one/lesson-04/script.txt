=== Competition Is for Losers ===
[hook_founder] Most of us were taught that competition is healthy.
Peter Thiel argues it might be the fastest way to watch your profits disappear.
=== Two Kinds of Business ===
[two_doors] Thiel sorts businesses into two kinds.
[crowd_market] Behind the first door, many rivals sell nearly identical products.
[single_shop] Behind the second door stands one company offering something nobody else can match.
He calls the first perfect competition, and the second monopoly.
=== Airlines Versus Google ===
[airplane] Picture American airlines in 2012, carrying millions of passengers and creating enormous value.
[coin_37] Yet the book reports they kept only about 37 cents of profit per passenger trip.
[google_logo] Google brought in less revenue than the whole airline industry.
But it kept about 21 percent of that revenue as profit.
=== Why Profits Vanish ===
[price_arrow] When rivals sell the same thing, each one cuts prices to win customers.
[shrinking_pie] The profit pie shrinks until there is barely a slice left for anyone.
Everyone is busy surviving this quarter, with no room to plan for the next decade.
=== What Monopoly Buys ===
[treasure_chest] Thiel argues lasting profits are not just about greed.
[lab_bench] They give a company the freedom to fund ambitious, long term projects.
[happy_staff] They also let it treat employees well and care about its wider impact.
In Thiel's view, these are luxuries a struggling competitor simply cannot afford.
=== The Stories Companies Tell ===
[tiny_circle] Companies in brutal markets often describe their market as tiny, so they look unique.
[giant_circle] Dominant companies do the opposite, describing their market as huge to look small.
Google, Thiel notes, prefers to call itself a technology company rather than the king of search.
=== Rivalry Blinds You ===
[boxing_ring] Competition also warps how we think.
When you obsess over a rival, you start copying them instead of creating.
[two_giants] The book describes Microsoft and Google fixating on each other.
[apple_rising] Meanwhile, Apple quietly rose past both of them.
=== Know When to Stop ===
[two_rockets] In 1999, PayPal was locked in a costly battle with X dot com, Elon Musk's company.
[handshake] Both were burning cash, so in 2000 they chose to merge instead.
As Thiel puts it, "Competition is for losers."
=== Your Turn ===
[mirror] Look at your own work and ask whether you are fighting rivals for crumbs.
Then ask what you could offer that would make you the only real choice.
