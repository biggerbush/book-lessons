=== The Perfect Failure ===
[perfect_ship] Imagine building your product exactly as planned, on time and on budget.
[empty_store] And then discovering that nobody wants it.
=== What Is a Startup ===
[eric_ries] Eric Ries argues this is how many startups really fail.
[team_huddle] To him, a startup is any team making something new, where the outcome is deeply unclear.
[uncertainty] He describes it as working under "conditions of extreme uncertainty."
=== Not Just Garages ===
[garage] That team might be two founders in a garage.
[office_tower] It might also be a small group inside a giant company.
What makes it a startup is not size, but how little anyone knows about what customers want.
=== Why Plans Feel Safe ===
[business_plan] Traditional management loves detailed plans and forecasts.
[factory] Those tools work well for a steady business with years of history to predict from.
[crystal_ball] A startup has no such history, so its forecasts are mostly guesses dressed up as facts.
=== Achieving Failure ===
[checklist] Here is the trap.
A team can hit every milestone and follow the plan flawlessly.
[bullseye_miss] Ries calls this achieving failure, which means executing perfectly on a plan that was wrong from the start.
=== The IMVU Add-On ===
[imvu_logo] Ries saw this firsthand at IMVU, a company where people chatted using 3D avatars.
[im_networks] The team's plan was to plug into existing instant messaging programs, so people could chat with friends they already had.
[code_hours] They worked hard to make the add-on connect with many different messaging networks.
=== Customers Said No ===
[user_test] Then real customers tried it.
[friends_no] Many did not want to invite their existing friends into something they weren't sure was cool.
[trash_bin] Ries ended up throwing away much of the code he had worked so hard to write.
The plan had been followed carefully.
It was simply aimed at the wrong target.
=== Treat Plans as Guesses ===
[lightbulb] The lesson is not to stop planning.
[question_mark] It is to treat your plan as a list of guesses about customers, not a list of orders.
[real_people] And to check those guesses with real people before you invest months building.
=== Your Turn ===
[sticky_note] Look at your current project and write down one thing you are assuming customers want.
How could you check it with a real person this week?
