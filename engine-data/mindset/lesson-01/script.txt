=== Two Worlds ===
[two_doors] Two people face the exact same bad day.
One feels crushed, the other feels ready, and the difference comes down to a single belief.
=== The Puzzle Room ===
[puzzle_table] Early in her career, Carol Dweck gave children a series of puzzles that grew harder and harder.
She expected them to struggle and get discouraged.
[smiling_boy] Instead, one ten year old boy rubbed his hands together and cried out, "I love a challenge!"
[lightbulb] To him, difficulty was not failure, it was a chance to learn.
=== The Fixed Mindset ===
[stone_block] Dweck calls one belief the fixed mindset.
Here, intelligence and talent are like a block of stone, you get a set amount, and that is that.
[test_scale] So every task becomes a test, weighing how much you really have.
[spotlight] You feel you must prove yourself, again and again.
=== The Growth Mindset ===
[seedling] The other belief is the growth mindset.
[helping_hands] Here, abilities are like a seed that grows with effort, good strategies and help from others.
[open_path] A challenge becomes a path to walk, not a judgment to fear.
=== Two Lenses ===
[two_glasses] Think of each mindset as a pair of glasses.
[street_scene] The world outside is the same, but each pair changes what you see.
[crossroads] And what you see shapes what you do next, step forward or step back.
=== A Rotten Day ===
[calendar_day] Dweck asked people to picture a rotten day.
[grade_paper] You get a C plus in a class that really matters to you.
[parking_ticket] Then you find a parking ticket, and your best friend brushes you off.
=== The Fixed Reading ===
[slumped_person] People with a fixed mindset heard a verdict on who they are.
[reject_stamp] They said things like, I'm a failure, or I feel like a reject.
=== The Growth Reading ===
[study_desk] People with a growth mindset saw problems they could handle.
They planned to work out what went wrong and study harder.
[ticket_paid] They would pay the ticket and park more carefully.
[phone_friend] They wondered if their friend was just having a bad day.
Same events, two completely different worlds.
=== Your Turn ===
[mirror] Next time something goes wrong, notice the story you tell yourself.
Is it a verdict on you, or a problem you can work on?
