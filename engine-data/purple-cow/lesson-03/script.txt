=== Worth a Remark ===
[paint_can] What if the smartest marketing move a paint company ever made was simply changing the can?
That is the heart of this lesson.
=== The Word Itself ===
[dictionary] Godin takes the word remarkable very literally.
It means worth making a remark about.
[two_friends] Picture two friends at a cafe, and one says, you have to see this.
[speech_bubble] That little sentence is what every product is really competing for.
If nobody feels the urge to mention it, it barely exists.
=== The Old Paint Can ===
[metal_can] For decades, house paint came in the same round metal can.
[screwdriver] You pried the lid off with a screwdriver, and paint dripped down the sides.
[wire_handle] The thin wire handle dug into your fingers as you carried it.
=== Twist and Pour ===
[dutch_boy] Then Dutch Boy redesigned the container itself.
[plastic_jug] Its Twist and Pour paint came in a plastic jug with a real handle.
[screw_lid] The lid screwed off by hand, and a spout let you pour without a mess.
Same paint inside, a completely different experience outside.
=== People Noticed ===
[customer] Painters and homeowners noticed the difference the first time they picked one up.
[store_shelf] Godin reports the new design helped Dutch Boy win more shelf space and more sales.
The can did the explaining that an ad would usually try to do.
=== Product as Marketing ===
[old_timeline] Most companies build a product first, then hand it to marketing to promote.
[new_timeline] Godin says marketing belongs inside the product, from the very first design meeting.
When people use it, show it, and talk about it, the product spreads itself.
=== The Remark Test ===
[magnifier] So how do you know if something is remarkable?
Imagine a customer describing it to a friend in one sentence.
[sentence_card] If the sentence is boring, like it is fine paint, nothing will travel.
[spark] If the sentence makes the listener lean in, you have something worth spreading.
=== Try It Today ===
[your_product] Take one thing you make, sell, or offer at work.
[notepad] Write down the exact sentence a delighted customer would say about it.
[pencil_edit] Then change the product, not the slogan, until that sentence is worth repeating.
=== Your Challenge ===
[question_mark] What would people say about your work if they had to describe it in one line?
