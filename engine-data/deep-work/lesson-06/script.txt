=== Drain the Shallows ===
[inbox_flood] What if doing more meaningful work means doing far less of everything else?
=== The Shallow Flood ===
[pond] Picture your workday as a pond with a few deep spots and wide shallow edges.
[email_icons] Emails, meetings, and quick requests spread across those edges.
Left alone, the shallows creep inward until there's no deep water left.
Cal Newport's rule is simple: shrink the shallow work on purpose.
=== Block Every Minute ===
[notebook] Start each workday by drawing your hours down the left side of a page.
[time_blocks] Then give every minute a job, grouping tasks into blocks.
[eraser] When something interrupts, just redraw the rest of the day.
The point is to stop letting the inbox decide your day.
=== Measure the Depth ===
[question_mark] Not sure if a task is shallow?
Newport offers a test.
[graduate] Ask how many months it would take to train a smart recent graduate to do it.
If the answer is short, the task is shallow.
If it's long, the task is deep.
=== Set a Budget ===
[boss] Next, ask your manager a direct question.
[pie_chart] What share of my time should go to shallow work?
Newport suggests most people land somewhere between 30 and 50 percent.
A clear number lets you protect the rest.
=== A Fixed End Time ===
[clock] Newport sets a firm end to his workday, around 5:30 in the evening.
He calls this fixed-schedule productivity.
[deadline] That hard stop forces hard choices about what deserves his limited hours.
[trash_can] Low-value tasks get declined or dropped, because there simply isn't room.
=== Make Senders Work ===
[website] On his website, Newport doesn't offer a simple open inbox.
[filter] Instead, he explains what kinds of messages he welcomes, and says he'll reply only if interested.
[envelope] This sets expectations, so ignoring a message no longer feels rude.
=== Close the Loop ===
[coffee_cup] Imagine someone emails: should we grab coffee sometime?
Replying sure, when works, starts a long back and forth.
[calendar] Instead, propose a specific time and place, and say it's confirmed if they agree.
[check_mark] One thoughtful reply ends the conversation instead of extending it.
=== Your Challenge ===
Tomorrow morning, block out your whole day on paper before opening email.
Then pick a time to stop, and keep it.
