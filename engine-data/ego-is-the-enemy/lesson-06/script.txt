=== Restraint Under Pressure ===
[fist] What if the strongest move in a fight is the punch you never throw?
=== The 1947 Season ===
[ballpark] In 1947, Jackie Robinson walked onto a major league baseball field for the Brooklyn Dodgers.
He was the first Black player in the modern major leagues.
[crowd] Many fans, opponents and even some teammates wanted him to fail.
=== A Strange Deal ===
[rickey] Before he signed, Dodgers executive Branch Rickey made an unusual request.
He wanted Robinson to take the abuse without fighting back.
[handshake] Rickey said he needed a player "with guts enough not to fight back."
Robinson agreed, and kept that promise.
=== The Abuse ===
[insults] Opposing players and managers shouted vicious insults from the dugout.
[baseball] Pitchers threw at his head.
[spikes] Runners slid into him with their metal spikes raised.
Every instinct must have told him to strike back.
=== Why He Held Back ===
[scale] Robinson understood that one angry reaction could hand his critics the excuse they wanted.
[open_door] His pride mattered less than the door he was opening for others.
So he swallowed the insults and let his play answer instead.
=== Ego Wants Revenge ===
[mirror] Holiday's point is that ego treats every slight as an attack demanding a response.
It insists we prove we cannot be pushed around.
[puppet] But reacting puts the other person in control of what we do.
Restraint keeps the choice in our hands.
=== Strength, Not Weakness ===
[bat] Robinson poured his fury into hitting, fielding and stealing bases.
[legacy] He became one of the game's great players, and the barrier he broke stayed broken.
Holding back was not weakness.
It took far more strength than throwing a punch.
=== Your Turn to Pause ===
[inbox] You will face smaller tests, like a rude email, an unfair comment, or a colleague taking credit.
[balance] Before you respond, ask whether reacting serves your goal or only your pride.
Then let your work do the answering.
=== Your Challenge ===
[calendar] This week, when someone provokes you, wait one full day before replying.
What happens when your ego does not get the last word?
