=== The Four Horsemen ===
[horsemen] Four riders are coming for most of us, and they are already on the road.
The good news is they move very slowly.
=== Who They Are ===
[heart] Peter Attia names them the Four Horsemen, starting with heart disease.
[cancer_cell] Then comes cancer.
[brain] Third is neurodegenerative disease, like Alzheimer's.
[sugar_cube] Fourth is metabolic dysfunction, including type 2 diabetes.
Together, they cause most deaths among people who do not smoke.
=== Silent for Decades ===
[timeline] Each horseman builds quietly for twenty, thirty, even forty years.
[iceberg] Symptoms are only the tip that finally breaks the surface.
By the time a diagnosis arrives, the disease has had a long head start.
=== Plaque Starts Young ===
[artery] Take heart disease.
As the book reports, autopsies of young people often reveal early fatty plaque in their arteries.
[young_man] That damage can begin in your teens or twenties.
[blood_tube] Attia urges tracking a blood marker called apo B, which counts the particles that drive this plaque.
Lowering it early, he argues, slows the whole process.
=== The Root Horseman ===
[liver] Metabolic dysfunction often shows up first, sometimes as fat building up in the liver.
[insulin_key] Your cells start ignoring insulin long before blood sugar looks abnormal.
[arrows] Attia sees this as fuel for the others, raising the risk of heart disease, cancer, and dementia.
=== Catch Cancer Early ===
Cancer is hardest to beat once it has spread.
[magnifier] So Attia favors earlier and broader screening, finding tumors while they are small.
[small_tumor] A tiny cancer caught early is far easier to treat than a large one found late.
=== Guard the Brain ===
[gene] Alzheimer's also begins long before memory slips, and some people carry genes that raise their risk.
[shield] Because treatments remain weak, Attia argues prevention is our best defense.
Many habits that protect the heart and metabolism protect the brain too.
=== Start Before Symptoms ===
[checklist] So the strategy is to act decades before anything hurts.
[doctor] Ask your doctor about your family history, apo B, and how well your body handles insulin.
[calendar] Your forties matter more than your seventies here.
=== Your Move ===
Which horseman runs in your family?
[phone] This week, book a checkup and ask what it would take to spot it early.
