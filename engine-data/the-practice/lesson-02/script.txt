=== Trust the Process ===
[writer] Imagine pouring your heart into something, sharing it, and hearing nothing back.
What if that silence was never the point?
=== Two Things You Hold ===
[two_boxes] Every piece of creative work comes with two parts.
[process_box] One is the process, the daily act of making the thing.
[outcome_box] The other is the outcome, how people react once it is out there.
Most of us mix them up.
=== What You Control ===
[control_dial] Godin points out that only one of these is truly yours.
You decide when you sit down, what you try, and how hard you work.
[crowd] But you cannot decide whether a crowd claps, buys, or shares.
That part belongs to them.
=== The Outcome Trap ===
[scoreboard] When we tie our worth to results, every launch becomes a verdict on us.
[stress_cloud] That pressure makes us anxious, cautious, and tempted to copy whatever already worked.
The work gets smaller and safer.
=== It Might Not Work ===
[question_mark] Godin argues that real creative work always carries the chance it might not work.
[safe_path] If success were guaranteed, it would just be following instructions.
So the uncertainty is not a flaw in your plan.
It is part of the deal.
=== Godin's Daily Blog ===
[laptop] Godin points to his own habit of writing and posting on his blog every single day.
[calendar] Not every post is a hit, and he does not need it to be.
He keeps going because his commitment is to the writing, not to any one post's popularity.
=== Results Follow Practice ===
[seedlings] This does not mean outcomes are irrelevant.
Godin's point is that a steady practice gives good results more chances to show up.
[garden] You tend the soil daily, without demanding that today's seed bloom by tonight.
=== Shift Your Question ===
[old_question] Instead of asking whether this will succeed, ask a different question.
[new_question] Did I show up and do the work as well as I could today?
[checkmark] That is a question you can answer yes to, no matter what anyone else does.
=== Your Challenge ===
Pick one small project and decide what your process will be this week.
Judge yourself only on whether you followed it.
