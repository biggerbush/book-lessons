=== The Pull of a Gift ===
[free_gift] Why does a tiny free gift make it so hard to say no?
The answer is an ancient rule.
=== The Rule ===
[two_people] Every human society teaches the same lesson: if someone gives you something, you should give something back.
[web_arrows] This rule built trust, trade and cooperation between strangers.
But it also creates a feeling of debt that persuaders can trigger.
=== The Soft Drink Study ===
[lab_room] Cialdini describes a study by psychologist Dennis Regan.
[cola_bottle] During a break, an assistant named Joe bought some participants a soft drink, unasked.
[raffle_ticket] Later, Joe asked them to buy raffle tickets.
Those who got the drink bought about twice as many tickets.
Even people who disliked Joe felt the pull to repay him.
=== Uninvited Gifts ===
[airport] Cialdini also watched the Hare Krishna movement fundraise in airports.
[flower] Members pressed a flower into a traveler's hand and refused to take it back.
[donation_cup] Then came the request for a donation.
Many travelers paid, even while visibly annoyed.
The gift was unwanted, yet the debt felt real.
=== The Boy Scout ===
[boy_scout] One day a boy asked Cialdini to buy circus tickets for 5 dollars each.
[circus_ticket] Cialdini politely declined.
[chocolate_bar] So the boy asked instead: would he buy some chocolate bars for just 1 dollar?
Cialdini bought two, even though he does not like chocolate.
=== Rejection Then Retreat ===
The boy's retreat felt like a concession, so Cialdini made one back.
[students] Cialdini's team asked students to chaperone young offenders on a day trip to the zoo.
[bar_17] Only 17 percent agreed.
Others were first asked to mentor them for two hours a week, for two years.
Everyone refused.
[bar_50] Then the zoo trip was offered, and 50 percent said yes.
=== Your Defense ===
[open_hands] Cialdini does not say refuse every gift.
[magnifier] Instead, accept what you are given, but look honestly at what it is.
If it turns out to be a sales tactic, call it that.
[broken_chain] The rule asks us to repay favors, not tricks, so you owe nothing back.
=== Your Challenge ===
[question_mark] Think of the last time a free sample or smaller second request won your yes.
Next time, pause and ask: is this a gift, or a tactic?
