=== Why Is It So Pricey ===
[price_tag] Why does something cost what it costs?
Most of us never ask, and that is exactly where good ideas hide.
=== Copying By Default ===
[two_people] Usually we think by analogy.
[old_map] We copy how things have always been done, or how others do them.
It is fast, and it often works.
[backpack] But we also inherit every assumption that came along with it.
=== The Other Way ===
[blocks] First principles thinking works differently.
You break a problem down to what you know is definitely true.
[ground_floor] Then you build your answer up from there.
[aristotle] The idea goes back to Aristotle, who described a first principle as the first basis from which a thing is known.
=== The Battery Problem ===
[battery_pack] In a 2012 interview, Elon Musk talked about battery packs.
[price_600] They cost around 600 dollars per kilowatt hour.
Many people took that as a fixed fact.
[shrug] Batteries are expensive, everyone said, and they will always be that way.
=== Strip It Back ===
[magnifier] Musk asked a different question.
What is a battery pack actually made of?
[raw_metals] He listed the raw materials, like cobalt, nickel, aluminium and carbon.
[price_80] Bought on the open market, they came to around 80 dollars per kilowatt hour.
=== Where The Gap Lives ===
[gap_arrow] So the materials were not the real problem.
The real cost was in how the packs were made.
[factory] That gap is a design challenge, not a law of nature.
Once you can see it, you can start working on it.
=== Rebuild From Truth ===
[two_columns] Here is the core move.
Sort what you know for certain from what you are only assuming.
[why_ladder] Ask why at each step, until you reach something you know is true.
[cross_out] Cross out the assumptions.
[rebuild] Then rebuild your answer using only the solid pieces.
=== Try It This Week ===
[calendar] Pick one cost or rule at work that seems fixed.
[question] Ask what it is truly made of, and what really must be true.
[notepad] Write down every assumption you find, and test just one of them.
You may find the rule rests on nothing at all.
=== Your Challenge ===
[mirror] What is one thing you accept only because everyone else does?
Break it down this week, and see what is really there.
