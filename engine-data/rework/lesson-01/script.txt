=== The Warning ===
[hook_phrase] "That would never work in the real world."
If someone has said that about your idea, this lesson is for you.
=== A Familiar Scene ===
[coffee_table] Picture sharing a fresh idea with a friend over coffee.
[skeptic] They lean back, cross their arms, and slowly shake their head.
Then comes the verdict: nice dream, but that's not how things really work.
It sounds wise and experienced, so you nod along and quietly shelve your idea.
=== The Old Rulebook ===
[rule_book] The so-called real world comes with a thick, dusty rulebook.
[rule_money] It says you must raise money from investors before you begin.
[rule_team] It says you need a big team and a fancy office to be taken seriously.
Break those rules, it warns, and you are doomed to fail.
=== An Excuse, Not a Place ===
Jason Fried and David Heinemeier Hansson push back hard on all of this.
[quote_card] In their words, "The real world isn't a place, it's an excuse."
[shield] It is a shield people hold up so they never have to try something new.
=== It's Not About You ===
[thought_cloud] Often the warning reveals the speaker's own fears and habits.
It describes the limits they have accepted, not the limits you must accept.
[open_door] Their pessimism is not a fact about your idea or your abilities.
=== The Authors' Proof ===
[small_office] The authors' own company, 37signals, is their living counterexample.
[piggy_bank] They grew without taking money from outside investors.
[tiny_team] They stayed deliberately small instead of hiring as fast as possible.
[basecamp_logo] And their product, Basecamp, still won over a great many paying customers.
=== Your Own Terms ===
[blank_page] So stop asking whether your idea fits the old rulebook.
[pencil] Ask instead what a saner, simpler version of it could look like.
[signpost] You are allowed to build something on terms that actually suit your life.
=== Test the Warning ===
[notebook] Write down one "you can't do that" you have heard recently.
[question_mark] Next to it, write the concrete reason it supposedly could not work.
If the only reason you find is fear or habit, cross the warning out.
If there is a real obstacle, you now know exactly what to solve.
=== Your Turn ===
[mirror] Which real world rule have you been obeying without ever questioning it?
[calendar] This week, pick one and run a small, low-risk test against it.
