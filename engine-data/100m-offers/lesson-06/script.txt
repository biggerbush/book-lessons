=== The Final Wrapping ===
[gift_box] You have built a strong offer.
Now let's wrap it so tightly that walking away feels like a real loss.
=== Scarcity ===
[few_seats] Scarcity means limited supply.
Hormozi suggests stating real limits, like how many clients you can properly serve each week.
[full_sign] When people see spots filling up, they stop delaying and start deciding.
=== Urgency ===
[calendar] Urgency is about time, not quantity.
Think of a group start date, a seasonal promotion, or a bonus that disappears on a set day.
[clock] A real deadline turns someday into today.
=== Bonuses ===
[bonus_stack] Rather than cutting your price, add bonuses.
Each bonus should solve one specific worry the buyer still has.
[price_tag] Give every bonus a name and a stated value, so the pile of extras feels concrete.
=== Guarantees ===
[shield] A guarantee shifts risk off the buyer and onto you.
Hormozi describes unconditional, conditional, and implied guarantees, plus the anti guarantee, where all sales are final.
[handshake] A conditional one might say: do the work, and if you miss the result, we keep helping you free.
He argues the extra sales usually outweigh the refunds.
=== The Name ===
[name_card] Finally, give the offer a name that sells it in seconds.
[magic_wand] His MAGIC formula covers five parts: a magnetic reason, the avatar, a goal, a time frame, and a container word.
=== One Gym, Two Offers ===
[gym] Picture a local gym selling plain monthly memberships, just like every rival.
[new_banner] Instead, it launches a Six Week Busy Moms Body Challenge, with only 20 spots and a firm start date.
[gift_tags] Bonuses include meal plans and weekly check ins.
A guarantee promises free extra coaching to anyone who follows the plan and falls short.
Same service, completely different pull.
=== Keep It Honest ===
[broken_trust] Fake deadlines and invented limits might win one sale, but they wreck trust.
[scale] Hormozi's enhancers work because they are true, so only promise limits and guarantees you will honor.
