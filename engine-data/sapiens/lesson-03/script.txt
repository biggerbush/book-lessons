=== Life Before Farms ===
[forager] Picture the life of a Stone Age forager.
Was it short and miserable, or surprisingly good?
=== The Old Story ===
[cave_family] We often imagine hunter-gatherers as hungry, dirty and desperate.
[farm_arrow] In that story, farming rescued them and life got steadily better.
Harari argues the real picture is more surprising.
=== A Walking Library ===
[mental_map] To survive, a forager carried a detailed mental map of her territory.
[mushrooms] She knew which mushrooms were food and which were poison.
[animal_track] She could read animal tracks, follow the seasons, and make a stone knife.
Her life depended on this knowledge every single day.
=== Broad Skills ===
[toolkit] Each forager had to master almost everything needed to stay alive.
[specialist] Today, most of us know a narrow slice and rely on experts for the rest.
Harari suggests the average forager had wider knowledge of her surroundings than most modern people.
=== A Varied Menu ===
[berry_basket] A forager's meal might include berries, nuts, snails, turtles, rabbits and wild greens.
[food_circle] Dozens of different foods meant fewer gaps in nutrition.
If one food failed, there were plenty of others to fall back on.
[rice_bowl] Later farmers often depended on one or two staple crops, like wheat or rice.
=== Shorter Workdays ===
[clock] Foragers often needed fewer hours to feed themselves than later farmers spent in the fields.
[kalahari] Harari points to foragers living in the Kalahari Desert today, a harsh environment.
[week_chart] By his account, they work about 35 to 45 hours a week, and hunt only one day in three.
=== Fewer Plagues ===
[small_band] Foragers lived in small bands that moved often.
[sick_village] Many infectious diseases came later, from domesticated animals and crowded villages.
So foragers suffered far fewer epidemics.
=== Not Paradise ===
[storm] This was no paradise.
[baby] Many children died young, and life could be violent and harsh.
[scale] But on diet, skills and leisure, the average forager often did better than the farmer who followed.
