=== The Smile Trap ===
[ceo_smile] Your company is in trouble, and your instinct is to smile and say everything is fine.
Ben Horowitz says that smile might be the most dangerous thing in the building.
=== Loudcloud Under Fire ===
[cloud_logo] When the dot-com bubble burst, Loudcloud's world began to crumble.
[falling_dots] Many of its internet customers were going out of business.
[fear_cloud] Some days it looked like the company itself might not survive.
[worried_team] Horowitz felt the pull every leader feels, to protect the team by keeping the bad news to himself.
=== Why Leaders Hide It ===
[heavy_rock] The urge comes from a kind instinct.
You want to carry the weight alone so nobody else panics.
[pep_talk] So you give upbeat speeches and hope things turn around quietly.
=== People Already Know ===
[magnifier] But your team is not blind.
They see customers leaving and deals falling through.
[trust_gauge] When the boss sounds cheerful anyway, they stop believing what the boss says.
Every sunny speech quietly drains trust.
=== More Brains ===
[light_bulbs] Horowitz's second point is practical.
The hardest problems need the most minds working on them.
If only you know the problem, only one brain is trying to solve it.
[many_hands] Share it, and dozens of smart people can start looking for answers.
=== Bad News Travels Fast ===
[speed_arrow] Horowitz wants a culture where bad news moves fast and good news moves slowly.
[open_door] When leaders talk openly about problems, employees feel safe raising problems too.
Small issues surface early, while they are still cheap to fix.
=== How to Say It ===
[clear_words] Telling the truth does not mean spreading panic.
Explain plainly what is wrong and why.
[plan_map] Then explain what the company is doing about it, and invite ideas.
People can handle hard news, but they cannot handle being kept in the dark.
=== Start This Week ===
[calendar] This week, pick one problem you have been keeping to yourself.
[team_table] Bring it to your team, state it plainly, and ask for their help.
You may be surprised how much lighter the problem feels once it is shared.
=== Your Challenge ===
[question_mark] What bad news are you protecting your team from right now?
And what would they figure out if you trusted them with it?
