=== Jumping to Conclusions ===
[mindik_card] Will Mindik be a good leader? She is intelligent and strong.
If you already said yes, your fast mind answered without asking for more.
=== The Missing Words ===
[question_mark] Notice what you never asked.
What came next in her description?
[adjectives] The next two words could have been corrupt and cruel.
[thumbs_down] Suddenly, a strong and clever leader sounds frightening, not inspiring.
=== The Story Machine ===
[story_engine] Kahneman says System 1 is a machine for jumping to conclusions.
It takes whatever scraps are in front of it and stitches them into a story.
[puzzle_pieces] It does not check how many pieces are missing.
It only asks whether the pieces it has fit together.
=== Naming the Habit ===
[speech_bubble] Kahneman gives this habit a name: what you see is all there is.
[spotlight] Information under the spotlight shapes the judgment.
[dark_stage] Everything left in the dark simply does not count, because it never enters the story.
=== Smooth Feels True ===
[smooth_road] A story that flows smoothly feels true.
[few_facts] And a few facts often make a smoother story than many facts.
[bumpy_road] More facts bring contradictions, and contradictions make the story bumpy.
=== One Side of the Case ===
[courtroom] The book reports a study where people read about a legal dispute.
[one_lawyer] Some heard only one side's argument.
[two_lawyers] Others heard both sides.
[gavel] Those who heard just one side felt more confident in their judgment.
They knew a second side existed, yet the half story still won them over.
=== Useful but Risky ===
[runner] This shortcut is often useful.
It lets us act quickly in a busy world.
[stop_sign] But it means we rarely stop to ask what we have not been told.
[headline] One headline, one review, or one first impression can become a whole verdict.
=== Ask What Is Missing ===
[notepad] The fix is a simple question before any important judgment.
[blank_page] What would I need to know that I do not know yet?
[second_view] Then go looking for the other side, the way a fair judge would.

[mirror] Think of a recent snap judgment about a person or a decision.
What was missing from the story you told yourself?
