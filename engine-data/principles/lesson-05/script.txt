=== Title ===
[brain_fork] What if the biggest obstacle to your best decisions is sitting right inside your own head?
=== Two Barriers ===
[two_walls] Ray Dalio says two barriers block good decisions more than anything else.
[ego_wall] The first is your ego.
[blind_wall] The second is your blind spots.
Both are invisible to the person who has them.
=== The Ego Barrier ===
[brain_halves] Dalio describes two parts of you that compete when someone disagrees.
[shield] A fast, emotional part treats the challenge like an attack and rushes to defend.
[thinker] A slower, thoughtful part wants to know what is actually true.
When the emotional part wins, you protect your pride instead of finding the best answer.
=== The Blind Spot Barrier ===
[eye_cone] Your blind spots are the things your mind simply cannot see.
Everyone is wired differently, so each of us notices some patterns and misses others.
[color_wheel] Dalio compares it to asking someone who is color blind to describe colors.
You cannot fix what you do not even know is missing.
=== Dalio's Own Gaps ===
[schoolbook] Dalio admits his own weaknesses, like poor rote memory, which made school a struggle.
[big_picture] He was strong at seeing big connections but weak at details.
[team_puzzle] So he learned to lean on people who were strong exactly where he was weak.
=== Radical Open Mind ===
[open_door] The cure for both barriers is what Dalio calls radical open-mindedness.
It means sincerely believing you might not know the best answer.
[question_mark] Dealing well with not knowing, he argues, matters more than whatever you already know.
=== Open or Closed ===
[closed_box] Closed-minded people make statements and get angry when their ideas are challenged.
[magnifier] Open-minded people ask questions and get curious about why someone sees things differently.
Notice the shift from proving yourself right to finding out what is true.
=== Seek Smart Critics ===
[critic_chair] Dalio's practical fix is to seek out the smartest people who disagree with you.
[flip_view] Then try to see the issue through their eyes, before you defend your own view.
[spark] Treat the sting of disagreement as a cue to stay curious, not to fight.
=== Your Challenge ===
This week, when someone disagrees with you, pause and ask one honest question before you reply.
Could they be seeing something you cannot?
