=== Two Paths ===
[two_paths] Two of the greatest athletes ever took completely different roads to the top.
Only one of those roads gets most of the attention.
=== The Tiger Story ===
[baby_tiger] Picture a baby in a walker, holding a tiny golf club.
That baby is Tiger Woods, whose father handed him a putter before he was one.
[tv_screen] At two, Tiger showed off his swing on national television.
[trophy] He grew into one of the most dominant golfers in history.
=== The Head Start Myth ===
[book_stack] His story became a model for parents, coaches and business books.
[early_arrow] The lesson seemed simple: start early, pick one thing, and practice hard.
Anyone who waits, the thinking goes, gets left behind.
=== Meet Roger ===
[young_roger] Now picture a different boy in Switzerland, Roger Federer.
[sport_balls] He played soccer, basketball, handball, badminton, table tennis, skiing, swimming, wrestling and skateboarding.
If anything, his parents urged him to take tennis less seriously.
=== A Late Focus ===
[calendar] Roger focused on tennis later than many of his rivals.
[tennis_court] Yet he became one of the greatest tennis players of all time.
His wide early play built all-round athletic skill and let him find the sport that truly suited him.
=== The Research ===
[chart] Epstein reports that studies of elite athletes find Roger's path is the common one.
[sample_zone] Many future stars went through a sampling period, trying varied sports with little structured training.
[narrow_zone] They narrowed down to one sport only later, then trained intensely.
=== The Exception ===
[spotlight] So Tiger's head start is real, but it is the exception, not the rule.
[crowd] Early specializers often look ahead at first, but later starters frequently catch up.
=== Range in Practice ===
[sampler] A wide start is not wasted time; it is often how people find their best fit.
[open_door] If you or your child are still exploring, that may be a strength.
[notebook] This week, try one new skill just to sample it, with no pressure to be great.
=== Your Turn ===
Think of one area where you feel behind for not starting early.
What did your detours teach you that a straight path never could?
