=== Your Worst Enemy ===
[mirror] Picture the most dangerous person in your investing life.
Now look in the mirror, because it is probably you.
=== Brains Are Not Enough ===
[brain] You might think great investors win because they are brilliant.
[ordinary_folk] Graham saw something different over decades on Wall Street.
He watched ordinary people with calm temperaments build and keep more wealth than many experts.
[expert] Those experts often knew finance, accounting and market lore inside out.
Yet their knowledge did not protect them from their own impulses.
=== Graham's Warning ===
[quote_card] Graham put it bluntly in one famous line.
"The investor's chief problem, and even his worst enemy, is likely to be himself."
[scale] In other words, temperament outweighs intelligence.
The market is not your main threat.
Your reactions to it are.
=== Greed at the Top ===
[rising_chart] In the late 1920s, American stock prices climbed year after year.
[crowd] Excited crowds rushed in, many buying with borrowed money.
[new_era] People told themselves a new era had arrived, and old rules no longer applied.
Greed felt like confidence, and rising prices felt like proof.
=== Fear at the Bottom ===
[crash] Then came the crash of 1929 and years of falling prices.
[sell_button] Frightened investors sold at the worst moments, locking in painful losses.
[swear_off] Many swore off stocks entirely, just when bargains were everywhere.
=== The Backwards Loop ===
[loop_arrow] Notice the pattern Graham kept warning about.
[buy_high] Greed pushes people to buy after prices have soared.
[sell_low] Fear pushes them to sell after prices have collapsed.
That is buying high and selling low, exactly backwards.
And it happens to clever people just as often as anyone else.
=== What Intelligent Means ===
[patience] For Graham, an intelligent investor is not a genius.
It is someone patient, disciplined and willing to keep learning.
[own_compass] It is someone who thinks for themselves instead of following the crowd.
[calm_face] Above all, it is someone who can stay steady when prices swing.
=== Rules Before Moods ===
[notebook] So decide your plan while you are calm, before the market moves.
[pen] Write down what you will buy, how much, and why.
[rule_list] When prices soar or sink, check your written rules, not your feelings.
=== Your Challenge ===
[question] Think back to your last big money decision.
Was it driven by careful thought, or by fear and greed?
