=== Finding Joy in Wrong ===
[wrong_face] What if realizing you were wrong could feel like winning?
Today, let's learn how to actually enjoy our mistakes.
=== Why It Stings ===
[shield] Most of us carry our opinions like a shield.
When someone proves us wrong, it feels like an attack on who we are.
[welded_heart] Adam Grant says that sting comes from welding our beliefs to our identity.
If the belief breaks, it feels like we break too.
=== The Delighted Expert ===
[kahneman] Grant once told Nobel prize winner Daniel Kahneman about findings that contradicted Kahneman's own work.
[bright_eyes] Instead of getting defensive, Kahneman's face lit up with genuine delight.
[quote_card] As Kahneman put it, "Being wrong is the only way I feel sure I've learned anything."
=== Detach From the Past ===
[two_selves] Grant describes two kinds of detachment that make this joy possible.
The first is separating your present self from your past self.
[old_photo] If you look back and cringe at what you believed a year ago, that's a good sign.
It means you've grown since then.
=== Detach From Opinions ===
[values_badge] The second is separating your opinions from your identity.
Grant suggests defining yourself by your values, like curiosity or honesty, rather than your beliefs.
[opinion_tag] Then an opinion becomes something you hold, not something you are.
Letting it go no longer feels like losing a piece of yourself.
=== The Astronomer ===
[telescope] Astronomer Andrew Lyne announced that he had discovered a planet circling a distant star.
It was big, exciting news in his field.
[math_error] Then he found a mistake in his calculations.
The planet wasn't there at all.
=== A Standing Ovation ===
[stage] At a major astronomy conference, Lyne stood before his colleagues and admitted he had gotten it wrong.
[applause] The room rose and gave him a standing ovation.
His honesty earned him respect instead of shame.
=== Make It a Habit ===
[lab_notebook] So the next time you're proven wrong, try saying to yourself, interesting, what did I just learn?
[deposit_jar] Picture each mistake as a deposit in your bank of knowledge, not a dent in your pride.
Over time, being wrong starts to feel like progress.
=== Your Challenge ===
[mirror] What's one belief you held five years ago that you've since changed?
This week, admit one thing you were wrong about out loud, and notice how freeing it feels.
