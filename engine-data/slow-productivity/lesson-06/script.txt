=== A Year of Harvest ===
[farm_field] Imagine a farmer trying to harvest every single day of the year.
It sounds absurd, yet that is how most of us work.
=== Always On ===
[flat_line] Picture your workload as a line drawn across the calendar.
For many knowledge workers, that line stays flat and high, from January to December.
[tired_worker] Every week asks for the same full intensity.
Newport argues this constant pace is not natural, and it slowly wears people down.
=== The Farmer's Rhythm ===
[farmer] For most of human history, work followed the seasons.
[planting] Farmers pushed hard at planting time and again at harvest.
[winter_hut] In winter, the work slowed, leaving time to rest and repair tools.
Effort rose and fell, but over a full year, the work got done.
=== A Recent Habit ===
[timeline] Newport suggests that flat, year-round intensity is the odd one out in human history.
[factory_clock] Factory work brought fixed hours, and office work inherited that same steady drumbeat.
But our minds were never built to sprint every single week.
=== The Academic Year ===
[academic_year] Newport, a professor himself, points to the academic calendar as a modern example.
[semester] Semesters are busy, packed with teaching and deadlines.
[summer_sun] Summers are lighter, leaving room for reflection and deeper projects.
The year as a whole is productive precisely because the pace varies.
=== Plan a Slow Season ===
[calendar] You can borrow this rhythm, even in a regular job.
[slow_month] Pick a lighter stretch, like a quieter summer month, and protect it.
During that season, take on fewer new commitments and schedule fewer meetings.
=== Smaller Seasons ===
Seasons can be small, too.
[after_launch] After a big deadline, plan a lighter week to recover, instead of jumping straight into the next push.
[easy_day] Within a month, you might set aside an easier day for thinking and catching up.
=== Peaks and Valleys ===
[wave_line] Now redraw your workload line as a gentle wave.
[peak_valley] High peaks for intense pushes, and real valleys for recovery.
The valleys are not wasted time.
They are what make the peaks possible.
=== Your Turn ===
Look at your calendar for the next 12 months.
[circle_season] Where could you circle one lighter season, and what would you need to do to protect it?
