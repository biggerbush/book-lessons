=== The Puzzle ===
[apple_logo] Why do people line up for hours to buy a gadget from one company, but shrug at a similar one from another?
=== Same Resources ===
[two_companies] Picture two tech companies standing side by side.
[talent_pool] Both can hire the same engineers, the same agencies, the same consultants.
Yet one inspires fierce loyalty, and the other just makes sales.
Sinek says the difference is not in the product at all.
=== The Usual Pitch ===
[computer] Most companies start with what they make, like a fast, well designed computer.
[spec_sheet] Then they list features and hope you buy.
It sounds reasonable, but it gives you nothing to believe in.
=== Apple's Pitch ===
[apple_store] Sinek imagines Apple speaking in its own voice.
[lightbulb] It starts with a belief: challenging the way things have always been done, and thinking differently.
Then it explains how: products that are beautiful and simple to use.
[laptop] Only at the end does it mention what: it happens to make great computers.
=== Beyond Computers ===
[mp3_player] That belief is why Apple could sell music players and phones, and people trusted it.
[dell_box] Sinek points out that Dell tried selling music players and handheld devices too.
They were decent products, but buyers did not take them seriously.
People saw Dell as a computer company, nothing more.
=== The Core Idea ===
[quote_card] Sinek sums it up simply: "People don't buy what you do; they buy why you do it."
[heart] When you share your purpose first, you attract people who believe what you believe.
[customer] They are not just buying a product; they are joining a cause.
=== Not Just Business ===
[podium] This pattern goes far beyond tech companies.
[crowd] Sinek argues that leaders like Martin Luther King Junior drew people by sharing a belief, not a plan.
Followers showed up for themselves, because the message spoke to what they already felt.
=== Your Own Why ===
[notebook] So what does this mean for you?
[pen] Before your next pitch, presentation or job interview, write down why you do your work.
[arrow] Lead with that belief, then share the details.
Watch how differently people lean in.
=== Your Challenge ===
Can you explain why you do what you do, in one sentence, without mentioning what you make?
