=== Title ===
[cockpit] Imagine a pilot who sees danger coming, but cannot bring himself to say so plainly.
That silence can bring down a plane.
=== Small Errors Add Up ===
[plane] Gladwell explains that plane crashes are rarely caused by one dramatic failure.
[error_chain] Usually, several small errors pile up, one after another.
[tired_crew] Bad weather, a tired crew and a slip in communication combine into disaster.
=== The Guam Crash ===
[guam_island] In 1997, a Korean Air jet crashed while approaching the island of Guam at night.
[rain_cloud] The weather was poor, and the captain was exhausted.
[first_officer] His first officer and flight engineer sensed trouble, but only dropped gentle hints about the rain.
Neither one said the danger out loud.
=== Mitigated Speech ===
[speech_bubble] Gladwell calls this mitigated speech, which means softening a message to sound polite or respectful.
[ladder] Picture a ladder, with a blunt command at the top and a vague hint at the bottom.
A hint is easy to miss when a busy captain is struggling.
=== Power Distance ===
[scale] Gladwell draws on researcher Geert Hofstede, who measured power distance across cultures.
That means how much people defer to those above them.
[boss_desk] In high power distance cultures, challenging a superior feels deeply wrong, even in an emergency.
=== Not Just One Country ===
[fuel_gauge] Gladwell also describes a Colombian crew in 1990 that ran out of fuel near New York.
[radio] The co-pilot never clearly told air traffic control it was an emergency.
Here too, deference to authority muffled the warning.
=== The Fix ===
[airline_logo] Korean Air brought in outside help to rebuild its training.
[english_book] Crews learned to work in English, a language without the same built-in levels of respect.
English gave everyone in the cockpit a more equal footing.
[raised_hand] Junior pilots were taught to speak up clearly and directly.
=== The Turnaround ===
[safety_badge] Korean Air went on to become a safe, respected airline.
[team_circle] Gladwell's point is hopeful: culture shapes us, but once we see it, we can change it.
[clear_voice] When something matters, say it plainly, not as a hint.
That habit can make your own team safer too.
