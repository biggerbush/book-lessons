=== The Corner Office ===
[boss_throne] Some bosses walk into a room needing to be the smartest person there.
Others walk in hoping to learn something.
=== Proving Versus Growing ===
[two_leaders] Dweck sees two kinds of leaders, shaped by the two mindsets.
[mirror] A fixed-mindset leader treats the company as a stage for proving their own brilliance.
[toolbox] A growth-mindset leader treats it as a place where everyone, including the boss, keeps getting better.
=== The Rescuer ===
[car_factory] One example from the book is Lee Iacocca, the celebrated boss of the carmaker Chrysler.
[newspaper] He helped rescue the company and became a national hero.
[spotlight] But Dweck argues he then grew focused on his image and on staying the star.
=== Silencing Bad News ===
[exit_door] He pushed out people who challenged him.
[falling_line] Meanwhile, the company slipped, and fewer voices were left to warn him.
[locked_mouth] This is the trap: when the leader must look superior, honest feedback starts to feel dangerous, so people stop giving it.
=== The Experiment ===
[students] Dweck also reports a study by Robert Wood and Albert Bandura with business school students.
[computer] Each student ran a simulated company, assigning workers to jobs and guiding them.
[two_labels] Half were told management skill is fixed.
The other half were told it grows with practice.
=== What Happened ===
[rising_line] The growth group kept learning from their mistakes, and their results got better over time.
[flat_line] The fixed group did not improve, even though the task was the same.
[magnifier] The difference was whether people used errors as clues or saw them as threats.
=== Learning Leaders ===
[round_table] Growth-minded leaders ask questions, admit what they don't know, and face hard facts.
[blue_logo] Dweck points to Lou Gerstner at IBM, who broke down a culture of status and rewarded teamwork.
[open_door] Teams like that adapt, because problems reach the top while there's still time to fix them.
=== Try This ===
[calendar] This week, notice how you react when someone brings you bad news.
[thank_you] Thank them first, before you defend or explain.
[question] Then ask what you could learn together.
