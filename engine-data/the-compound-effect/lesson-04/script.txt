=== Why Over Willpower ===
[title_card] You already know what to do, so why don't you actually do it?
Hardy says the missing piece is not willpower, it is a reason.
=== Willpower Runs Out ===
[tired_man] Picture someone vowing to skip dessert, gritting his teeth every single night.
[fuel_gauge] Willpower alone is like a fuel tank that drains with each temptation.
Sooner or later, the tank hits empty and the old habit wins.
=== The Plank Test ===
[plank] Hardy asks you to imagine a narrow plank laid between two very tall buildings.
[cash] Someone offers you twenty dollars to walk across it.
Most of us would refuse, because the reward is too small for the risk.
=== A Reason to Cross ===
[fire] Now imagine the far building is on fire.
[child] Your child is trapped there, calling for you.
Suddenly you would cross without a second thought.
The plank did not change, only your reason did.
Hardy's point is that a strong enough why makes the hard thing feel obvious.
=== Find Your Core Why ===
[notebook] Hardy suggests writing down your core values, the things that matter most to you.
[heart_icon] Then link each habit to one of them, for example eating well so you have energy for your family.
When a habit serves something you deeply care about, saying no gets easier.
=== Goals Give Direction ===
[target] Next, set clear goals that grow out of those values.
[arrow] A goal is the destination, and your why is the engine that gets you there.
Without both, small choices drift wherever temptation pulls.
=== Spot the Triggers ===
[trigger_list] To remove a bad habit, first notice what sets it off.
[clock] Maybe it is a certain time of day, a mood, or a particular place.
[pantry] Then clear out what tempts you, like junk food in the cupboard.
=== Swap, Don't Just Stop ===
[swap_arrows] Simply stopping leaves a gap that the old habit rushes to fill.
[fruit_bowl] So replace it with something healthier, like fruit instead of chips.
[buddy] Building a good habit is also easier with a partner who keeps you honest.
=== Your Turn ===
[question] Pick one habit you want to change, and write down why it truly matters to you.
If the reason does not move you, keep digging until it does.
