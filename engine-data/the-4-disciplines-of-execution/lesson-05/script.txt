=== The Scale Problem ===
[bathroom_scale] Stepping on the scale every morning will never make you lighter.
Yet that is exactly how most teams try to hit their goals.
=== Two Kinds of Measure ===
[lag_label] The authors call the result you want a lag measure.
[rear_mirror] It is like a rear view mirror, showing what already happened.
[lead_label] A lead measure is different, because it tracks the actions that cause the result.
=== The Weight Loss Example ===
[person_goal] Imagine you want to lose 10 pounds.
Your weight is the lag measure.
[food_plate] The calories you eat each day are a lead measure.
[running_shoes] So are the minutes you spend exercising.
Change those two, and the scale will follow.
=== Two Tests ===
[check_one] A good lead measure passes two tests.
First, it is predictive, meaning when it moves, the result is likely to move too.
[check_two] Second, it is influenceable, meaning your team can directly make it happen.
You cannot will the scale down, but you can skip dessert tonight.
=== Why Lags Tempt Us ===
[boss_desk] Leaders naturally stare at lag measures, because those are what they get judged on.
[late_clock] The trouble is, by the time a lag shows up, it is too late to change it.
So the team ends up worrying about numbers it cannot touch.
=== Data Takes Effort ===
[clipboard] Lead measures are often harder to track than results.
Nobody hands you a report of daily calories.
[tally_marks] You may need to build a simple way to count them yourself.
That extra effort is part of what makes the discipline work.
=== From Hope to Action ===
[team_huddle] When a team tracks leads, the conversation changes.
Instead of hoping the quarter goes well, people ask what they did today.
[gear_turn] Small daily actions become the gears that turn the big result.
=== Find Your Lead ===
[goal_flag] Start with one goal you care about.
[question_mark] Ask what single action, done more often, would most likely move it.
That action is your lead measure.
=== Your Challenge ===
[stopwatch] Pick one result you keep checking but cannot control.
This week, name the daily action behind it and track that instead.
