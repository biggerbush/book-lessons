=== Title ===
[wheat_stalk] Ten thousand years ago, a humble grass struck a deal with humans.
Harari asks who really got the better end of it.
=== A Grass Goes Global ===
[wild_grass] Wheat began as a wild grass growing in one small corner of the Middle East.
[world_map] Within a few thousand years, it covered fields across the world.
By evolution's measure, few plants have ever succeeded so spectacularly.
=== Wheat's Demands ===
[farmer] Wheat was fussy, and Sapiens became its servant.
[rocks] It disliked rocks, so farmers cleared stones from their fields by hand.
[weeds] It hated sharing soil and water, so they pulled weeds under the hot sun.
They also hauled water and fought off hungry pests.
=== Bodies Not Built for It ===
[skeleton] Our bodies had evolved for climbing trees and chasing animals, not bending over fields all day.
Ancient skeletons, Harari notes, show slipped discs, arthritis and hernias.
[grain_bowl] Diets narrowed to a few grains, poor in vitamins and minerals, and hard on teeth.
=== New Dangers ===
[village] Farmers crowded into permanent villages beside their animals and their waste.
[germs] Infectious diseases spread through these settlements as never before.
[dry_field] And when rains failed or locusts came, a village relying on one crop faced starvation.
=== Who Tamed Whom ===
[house] The word domesticate comes from the Latin word for house.
[farmer_hut] Wheat stayed out in the field, while Sapiens settled into houses beside it.
Harari puts it bluntly: "We did not domesticate wheat. It domesticated us."
=== The Luxury Trap ===
[family] Why did anyone accept this? Because each step seemed sensible at the time.
More wheat meant more food, so families had more children.
[crowd] More mouths needed more fields, and soon nobody could go back to foraging.
Harari calls this a luxury trap, where small improvements quietly become necessities.
=== Success Isn't Happiness ===
[headcount] In evolution's eyes, the revolution was a triumph, because there were far more humans.
[scales] But more people living harder lives is not the same as better lives.
A species can thrive while its individuals suffer.
=== Your Wheat ===
[your_chores] What convenience in your life began as a luxury and now demands your time?
Notice one this week, and ask honestly who is serving whom.
