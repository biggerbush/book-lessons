=== Last One Left ===
[last_item] The website says only 2 left in stock, and suddenly your finger is already on the buy button.
Why does almost gone feel so precious?
=== The Scarcity Rule ===
[scarce_gem] Cialdini's final principle is scarcity.
[value_gauge] We judge things as more valuable when they are rare or becoming harder to get.
This shortcut usually works, because good things often are hard to find.
[lost_freedom] And when a choice is slipping away, we hate losing the freedom to have it.
=== The Cookie Jars ===
[two_jars] In a study described in the book, people tasted cookies from a jar.
[full_jar] Some jars held 10 cookies.
[near_empty] Others held only 2.
The very same cookies were rated more desirable when they came from the nearly empty jar.
=== Shrinking Supply ===
[shrink_arrow] The effect grew stronger when a jar of 10 was suddenly swapped for a jar of 2.
Newly scarce felt even better than always scarce.
[crowd_hands] It peaked when people were told the cookies were running out because others wanted them.
Competition turns a mild want into a hungry grab.
=== How It Is Used ===
[sign_limited] Sellers know this.
They say limited number, only while supplies last, or this price ends at midnight.
[clock_ticking] Deadlines and dwindling stock squeeze the time you have to think.
=== Feel the Rush ===
[heart_racing] Cialdini's defense starts with your body.
When you feel a sudden rush of urgency, treat that heat as a warning light.
[pause_hand] Stop, calm down, and let your thinking catch up.
=== Use or Own ===
[question_mark] Then ask why you want the thing.
If you want to own something rare, scarcity is a fair reason.
[cookie_bite] But if you want to use it, remember that a rare cookie is still just the same cookie.
=== Your Autopilot ===
[six_switches] This same move works for all six triggers in the book.
Gifts, commitments, crowds, liking, authority and scarcity are usually helpful shortcuts.
[honest_check] The question is simple: is this cue genuine, or was it planted to rush me?
If it is honest, follow it gladly.
If it is faked, walk away.
