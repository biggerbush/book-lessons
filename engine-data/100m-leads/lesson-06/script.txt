=== Title ===
[title_coin] What if every new customer paid for the next one?
Paid ads can do exactly that, if you set them up right.
=== Money for Speed ===
[hourglass] Free methods like posting and outreach mostly cost you time.
[coin_stack] Paid ads flip that trade, so you spend money instead.
[crowd] In return, you can reach thousands of strangers this week, not next year.
=== The Real Risk ===
[faucet] Think of an ad as a faucet you pay to keep running.
[leaky_bucket] If customers cost more than they bring in, every click drains your bank account.
So Hormozi asks one key question.
How fast does each customer pay you back?
=== Know Your Numbers ===
[ad_receipt] Imagine you spend 100 dollars on ads to win one customer.
That is your cost to acquire them.
[profit_bar] Next, track the profit that customer brings in after you pay to serve them.
=== The 30 Day Goal ===
[calendar] Hormozi's target is to earn back that acquisition cost within the first 30 days.
[second_buyer] Even better, earn enough to pay for winning another customer too.
[chain_link] Then each buyer funds the next, and growth no longer depends on your savings.
=== Speed Up Payback ===
[upsell_box] If you fall short, don't just slash your ad budget.
Offer customers more value early, like a bigger package or a useful add-on.
[cash_arrow] More money arrives sooner, and your ads start paying for themselves faster.
=== Anatomy of an Ad ===
[megaphone] A strong ad first calls out the exact people it is for.
[gift_tag] Then it shows the value they will get.
[click_button] Finally, it tells them clearly what to do next.
=== Test, Then Scale ===
[split_screen] Run small tests and keep only the ads that bring in customers cheaply.
[volume_dial] When an ad pays you back fast, turn up the spending with confidence.
Give it time, because good ads usually take many attempts to find.
=== Your Challenge ===
[calculator] This week, work out what one customer costs you and what they earn you in 30 days.
Close that gap, and your ads become a money machine instead of a money pit.
