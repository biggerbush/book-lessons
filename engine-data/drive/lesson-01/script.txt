=== Title ===
[monkey_puzzle] Why would a monkey solve a puzzle with no treat on offer?
The answer reveals a hidden engine inside you too.
=== Two Known Drives ===
[scientists] Scientists long believed behavior had two main drivers.
[food_water] The first was biological, like hunger and thirst.
[carrot_stick] The second was outside rewards and punishments, the carrot and the stick.
=== Harlow's Monkeys ===
[harry_harlow] In 1949, psychologist Harry Harlow placed a mechanical puzzle in the cages of rhesus monkeys.
[pin_hook_lid] To solve it, a monkey had to pull a pin, undo a hook, and lift a cover.
Nobody offered food or praise.
[happy_monkeys] Yet the monkeys dove in, seemed to enjoy it, and quickly got good at it.
=== Raisins Backfire ===
[raisins] Then Harlow offered raisins for solving the puzzle.
[error_marks] Instead of improving, the monkeys made more mistakes and solved it less often.
The treat got in the way.
=== A Strange New Idea ===
[lightbulb] Harlow suggested a new force he called intrinsic motivation.
[gift_box] Doing the task was its own reward.
But the idea was so unusual that it was largely ignored.
=== Deci's Cubes ===
[ed_deci] Two decades later, Edward Deci picked up the thread with university students.
[soma_cubes] He gave them Soma cubes, odd shaped blocks that fit together into many designs.
[timer] In each session, he left the room for eight minutes of free time.
[magazines] Students could read magazines or keep playing with the puzzle.
=== Paying Changes Play ===
[dollar_coin] One group was paid one dollar per solved design, but only in the second session.
That day, they played more during the break.
[falling_line] When the pay stopped, their free play dropped below where it began.
[rising_line] The unpaid group kept playing a little more each time.
=== The Third Drive ===
[broken_toy] Money had turned play into a job.
[three_engines] Together, these studies point to a third drive beside hunger and rewards.
We do some things simply because they are satisfying.
Ignore it, and we waste one of our strongest motivators.
=== Your Turn ===
[notebook] Think of one task you would happily do for free.
This week, notice what makes it satisfying, and borrow that feeling for your work.
