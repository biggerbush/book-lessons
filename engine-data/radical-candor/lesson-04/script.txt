=== Turn the Tables ===
[boss_mirror] Before you tell anyone what they are doing wrong, ask them what you are doing wrong.
That small reversal is where candor begins.
=== Why Start With You ===
[new_manager] Imagine a new boss who starts handing out criticism on day one.
[wary_team] The team hears judgment from someone they do not yet trust.
Kim Scott, who managed teams at Google and Apple, argues it works far better the other way around.
[trust_bridge] When you ask for criticism first, you show you can take what you plan to dish out.
=== Feel It Yourself ===
[sting_heart] Hearing criticism stings, even when you asked for it.
Feeling that sting yourself teaches you how your words will land on others.
[two_way_road] It also makes feedback a two way street, not a one way order.
=== A Go-To Question ===
[speech_bubble] Scott suggests having one go to question, phrased in your own words.
Something like, what could I do, or stop doing, that would make working with me easier?
[closed_door] Vague questions like, any feedback for me, invite a polite nothing.
[open_door] A specific question makes it easier to answer honestly.
=== Count to Six ===
[silent_room] Expect silence, because people fear that criticizing the boss is risky.
[counting_hand] Scott's advice is to sit with the quiet and count slowly to six before speaking.
Often someone fills the silence with something real and useful.
=== Listen, Don't Defend ===
[open_ears] When criticism comes, listen to understand, not to argue.
[locked_lips] Resist the urge to explain yourself or criticize the criticism.
A defensive reply teaches everyone to stay quiet next time.
=== Reward the Candor ===
[thank_you] Thank the person, and mean it.
[fixed_gear] Then act on what you heard, and let people see the change.
[reason_card] If you cannot fix it, explain why, so they know you took it seriously.
=== Then Give It ===
[growing_plant] Repeat this until asking and answering feel normal on your team.
[handshake] Only then does your own criticism arrive with trust already built in.
Your team has watched you take it, so they believe you mean well.
=== Your Challenge ===
[calendar_page] This week, ask one person your go to question.
Then count to six, listen, and change one thing because of what you hear.
