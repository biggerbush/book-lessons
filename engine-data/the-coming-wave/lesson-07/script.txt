=== The Narrow Path ===
[tightrope] Picture a tightrope stretched between catastrophe on one side and dystopia on the other.
How do we walk it without falling?
=== No Silver Bullet ===
[magic_wand] Suleyman says there is no single law, invention or treaty that will contain the coming wave.
[circles] Instead, he pictures containment as a set of rings, spreading out from the technology itself.
Each ring is imperfect, but together they hold.
=== Start in the Lab ===
[engineer] The innermost ring is technical safety, built right into the code and the lab.
He calls for a huge, Apollo-style push on safety research, far bigger than today's.
Safety should be a core part of the work, not an afterthought.
[magnifier] Next come audits, so outsiders can check what powerful systems can actually do.
=== Squeeze Choke Points ===
[microchip] Some technologies depend on a few hard-to-make parts, like the most advanced computer chips.
[factory] Only a handful of companies in the world can produce them.
[hourglass] Controlling those choke points, as the United States did with chip export limits on China, buys precious time.
=== Makers and Companies ===
[builder] Suleyman argues that people worried about technology should help build it, not just criticise from outside.
[balance] Companies, too, must be designed to balance profit with purpose, not chase growth alone.
=== Laws and Treaties ===
[capitol] Governments need to understand these tools deeply, and build the skills to regulate them.
[handshake] Because the wave crosses borders, nations also need alliances and treaties.
[warhead] He points to nuclear arms control as proof rivals can agree to limit dangerous weapons.
=== Learn From Failure ===
[airplane] Culture matters too, and aviation shows why.
[black_box] When planes crash or nearly crash, the industry studies what went wrong and shares the lessons widely.
That openness about mistakes helped make flying remarkably safe.
Suleyman wants technology builders to admit failures the same way.
=== People Power ===
[crowd] The outermost ring is public pressure, ordinary citizens demanding safer technology.
[stack] Stack all these rings together, and the wave becomes something we can steer.
Each layer catches what the others miss.
=== Your Next Step ===
[mirror] Which ring could you strengthen, at work, in your vote, or in a conversation?
Pick one, and start this week.
