=== Hook ===
[hero_tag] What if your low price is the very thing hurting your customers?
Today, we flip the pricing instinct upside down.
=== The cheap trap ===
[thin_coin] Most owners think a lower price wins more buyers.
Hormozi warns that cheap leaves you with thin margins and almost nothing to spend.
[tired_owner] So you cut corners, rush the work, and results suffer.
Being the cheapest is a race, and nobody wins it.
=== The vicious cycle ===
[down_spiral] Picture a spiral pointing downward.
Low price means low margin, which means weaker service and unhappy customers.
[bad_review] Unhappy customers leave, so you discount again to replace them.
Round and round it goes, each loop a little worse.
=== Flip the cycle ===
[up_spiral] Now picture the same spiral pointing up.
A higher price gives you healthy margin.
[toolbox] That extra money buys better staff, better tools, and more attention for each client.
Better delivery creates better results.
Results bring referrals, and the cycle keeps lifting.
=== Price buys commitment ===
[wallet_open] Something else happens when people pay more.
They take it seriously, show up, and follow through.
[effort_bar] Hormozi argues that committed buyers get better outcomes, which makes your service look even better.
=== Better customers ===
[filter_funnel] High prices also filter who walks through your door.
In his experience, the lowest paying clients often complain the most and value the work the least.
[happy_client] Premium buyers tend to be respectful, motivated, and easier to serve.
=== A trainer example ===
[trainer] Imagine a personal trainer charging a small monthly fee to crowds of clients.
[clock_strain] She is stretched thin and can barely remember names.
[meal_plan] Charging far more, she takes fewer clients and adds custom meal plans and daily check ins.
Her clients transform, and referrals roll in.
=== Ask the big question ===
[ten_x] Hormozi suggests a powerful thought experiment.
Ask what you would need to deliver to justify charging ten times more.
[idea_list] The answers become your roadmap for a better offer.
Then raise your price and actually deliver that value.
=== Your challenge ===
[mirror] What would make your offer worth ten times its price?
Write three answers today, and pick one to add this week.
