=== A Perfect Book ===
[perfect_book] Imagine a book so perfect that it could never be wrong.
Now ask yourself a harder question: who gets to read it out loud?
=== The Human Problem ===
[crowd] People make mistakes all the time.
Leaders lie, judges take bribes, and experts get things badly wrong.
[scales] So for thousands of years, people dreamed of a source that could never err.
They wanted something above human weakness, something that could settle every argument for good.
=== Enter the Holy Book ===
[scroll] Many religions offered an answer: a sacred text, believed to come from beyond humanity.
Unlike a priest, a book cannot be bribed, and it cannot change its mind.
[copies] And identical copies could carry the same words to every town.
It looked like the perfect shield against human error.
=== Who Chose the Pages ===
[pile_texts] But first, someone had to decide which writings counted as holy.
In ancient Judaism, many texts circulated, and scholars debated over generations which ones belonged in the Bible.
[church_elders] Christians did the same, with church leaders arguing over which books made the New Testament.
So fallible people made the very choice that was meant to remove fallible people.
=== Words Need Readers ===
[question_mark] Then a deeper problem appeared.
A text cannot explain itself.
What does an ancient rule mean in a new city, a new century, a new situation?
[rabbi] Someone had to interpret it, and in Judaism, that role fell to the rabbis.
=== Books About Books ===
[mishnah] The rabbis' interpretations were gathered into new texts, first the Mishnah, and later the Talmud.
[talmud_stack] Over time, these commentaries became sacred themselves, and many Jews studied them more closely than the Bible.
And of course, the Talmud soon needed its own interpreters too.
=== Power Shifts ===
[crown] Harari's point is simple.
The dream of an infallible source never removed human power.
It quietly handed that power to whoever controlled the interpretation.
[hierarchy] In Christianity, that meant the church hierarchy, which claimed the final word on scripture.
Behind every perfect source, there is always a human voice.
=== Spot It Today ===
[podium] This pattern did not end with ancient religion.
Any authority that claims to be beyond question still has people reading and explaining it.
[magnifier] So the useful question is not whether a source is perfect.
It is who interprets it, and whether anyone is allowed to challenge them.
=== Your Challenge ===
[notebook] This week, notice one claim backed by an authority that supposedly cannot be questioned.
Then ask yourself who is actually doing the interpreting.
