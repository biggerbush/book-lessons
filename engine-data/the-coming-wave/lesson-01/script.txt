=== The Wave ===
[title_wave] Every few generations, a new technology washes over the world and changes everything in its path.
Once it starts, almost nothing can hold it back.
=== What a Wave Is ===
[gpt_icon] Mustafa Suleyman calls the biggest inventions general-purpose technologies.
[branches] These tools are so useful they spread into almost every part of life, like fire, writing or electricity.
[wave_cluster] They rarely arrive alone, but in clusters that ride together like a wave.
=== The First Car ===
[benz_car] In 1886, Carl Benz built a three-wheeled motor car in Germany.
[crowd_doubt] At first it was a noisy curiosity that few people wanted or could afford.
[horse_cart] Horses still ruled the roads.
=== Cheaper Every Year ===
[ford_line] Then Henry Ford's assembly line turned out the Model T in huge numbers.
[price_drop] Each improvement made cars cheaper, and each drop in price brought in more buyers.
[cycle_arrow] More buyers paid for more improvements, and the cycle kept spinning.
=== Everywhere at Once ===
[city_traffic] Within a few decades, cars and engines filled streets across the world.
[engine_parts] The engine itself spread far beyond cars, into ships, tractors, planes and generators.
[world_map] A rare luxury became an ordinary part of daily life on every continent.
=== The Printing Press ===
[gutenberg] The same story played out with Gutenberg's printing press in the fifteenth century.
[press_spread] Presses multiplied rapidly across Europe, and the price of books fell sharply.
[book_stack] Ideas that once lived in a few handwritten copies now reached ordinary readers.
=== The Default Setting ===
[quote_card] Suleyman sums up the pattern simply: "proliferation is the default."
[ban_sign] Rulers sometimes tried to block new tools, but bans mostly delayed them or left the banners behind.
[demand_arrow] When something is useful and cheap, people find a way to get it.
=== Reading the Signs ===
[eye_glass] So the real question about any new technology is not whether it will spread.
[clock_face] It is how fast, how cheaply, and who will hold it when it does.
[checklist] Make that your habit: when a tool clearly works, assume it will soon be everywhere.
