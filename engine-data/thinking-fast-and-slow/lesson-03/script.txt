=== The Pull of Numbers ===
[title_wheel] A number you know is meaningless can still bend your judgment.
You probably will not even notice.
=== A Rigged Wheel ===
[wheel] Kahneman and Amos Tversky built a wheel of fortune, numbered from 0 to 100.
[secret_stops] Secretly, it was rigged to stop only at 10 or at 65.
[students] Students spun it, wrote down their number, then answered a question.
[un_question] What percentage of African nations belong to the United Nations?
=== The Number Leaks In ===
Everyone knew the wheel had nothing to do with Africa.
[group_ten] Yet people who saw 10 guessed about 25 percent, on average.
[group_65] People who saw 65 guessed about 45 percent.
The random number had pulled their answers toward it.
=== Stopping Too Soon ===
Kahneman describes two ways anchors work.
[ladder] The first is slow and deliberate: you start at the anchor and adjust away from it.
[stop_sign] But System 2 tires quickly and stops adjusting while still too close to the starting point.
=== A Quiet Suggestion ===
[lightbulb] The second way is automatic.
[thought_cloud] The anchor makes System 1 call up ideas that fit it, so a high number brings high-sounding answers to mind.
You never feel the nudge.
=== Even Experts ===
[judge] Professionals are not immune.
[dice] In one study, experienced German judges rolled dice loaded to land on 3 or 9.
[shoplifter] Then they read about a woman caught shoplifting and proposed her sentence.
[jail_bars] Judges who rolled 9 suggested about 8 months.
Those who rolled 3 suggested about 5.
=== Anchors Everywhere ===
[price_tag] Anchors show up wherever numbers do.
[house] A home's asking price, a first salary offer, a suggested donation.
[sign_limit] Even a supermarket sign saying limit 12 per person can lead shoppers to buy more.
Whoever names the first number has a head start.
=== Fight Back ===
[shield] Kahneman suggests assuming that any number on the table has already influenced you.
[flip_arrows] Then deliberately argue the opposite: list reasons the true value might be far from the anchor.
[walk_away] If a first offer is outrageous, refuse to counter it, and say plainly you will not negotiate from there.
=== Your Challenge ===
[question_mark] Next time you see a price, a deadline or an estimate, ask yourself: who chose this number first?
Then build your own estimate from scratch.
