=== Struggle Now ===
[easy_road] What if the study method that feels best is the one failing you?
In Range, David Epstein argues that good learning should feel a little hard.
=== The Hint Trap ===
[classroom] Picture a middle school math class, filmed for research.
[student] A student gets stuck on a problem.
[teacher] The teacher jumps in with hints until the answer pops out.
Everyone feels better, but the student never wrestled with the actual concept.
=== Good Difficulty ===
[bjork] Psychologist Robert Bjork calls this kind of productive struggle a desirable difficulty.
[mountain] Learning that feels slow and effortful often sticks far longer.
[fast_lane] Learning that feels smooth and quick often fades fast.
=== Test Yourself ===
[quiz_card] One difficulty is testing yourself before you feel ready.
[wrong_guess] Even guessing wrong first helps, because your brain is searching hard for the answer.
That search makes the right answer stick when you finally see it.
=== Mix It Up ===
[blocked_set] Most practice comes in blocks, with ten problems of the same type in a row.
[mixed_set] Epstein describes the alternative, called interleaving, where problem types are shuffled together.
Mixed practice feels clumsier and scores worse in the moment.
But on later tests, interleavers tend to outperform, because they learned to pick the right strategy.
=== The Cadet Study ===
[academy] At the United States Air Force Academy, cadets were randomly assigned to calculus professors.
[prof_a] Some professors produced great scores in the first course and glowing student reviews.
[prof_b] Others got lower scores and harsher reviews.
[next_course] Yet in the next calculus course, students of the tougher professors did better.
=== Built to Last ===
[deep_roots] The book suggests the tougher professors taught deeper concepts, not just what the next exam needed.
Their students struggled more, but their knowledge grew roots.
[bridge] Deep knowledge transfers, so it helps with new problems you have never seen.
=== Try This ===
[notebook] Before you reread your notes, close them and write what you remember.
[shuffle] Mix different types of problems in one practice session.
[stopwatch] When you get stuck, wait a minute before reaching for the hint.
=== Your Challenge ===
[question] What are you learning right now that feels too easy?
This week, make it harder on purpose, and watch what stays with you.
