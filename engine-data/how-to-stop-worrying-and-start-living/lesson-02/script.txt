=== The Magic Formula ===
[worried_man] What if the fastest way out of worry is to stare straight at the worst that could happen?
That sounds backwards, but one young engineer proved it works.
=== A Machine That Failed ===
[carrier] Willis Carrier was a young engineer, years before he became famous for air conditioning.
[glass_plant] His company sent him to install a new gas cleaning device at a glass plant in Missouri.
[broken_device] But the machine did not work as well as promised.
He felt crushed and could not stop worrying.
=== Worry Blocks Thinking ===
[tangled_head] Carrier realized that worrying was getting him nowhere.
Carnegie explains why: worry scatters the mind, so we cannot focus on fixing anything.
[three_steps] So Carrier worked out a simple three step formula.
=== Step One: The Worst ===
[step_one] First, he asked honestly what was the worst that could happen.
[pink_slip] He might lose his job.
[money_loss] His employer might lose the 20,000 dollars it had invested.
=== Step Two: Accept It ===
[step_two] Second, he made himself accept that worst outcome in his mind.
[new_job] He could always find another job.
His bosses would understand that testing a new method sometimes fails.
[calm_face] Once he accepted it, he felt a wave of relief and calm.
=== Step Three: Improve ===
[step_three] Third, with a clear head, he set out to make things better than the worst.
[test_bench] He ran tests and found that 5,000 dollars of extra equipment would fix the problem.
[profit_chart] Instead of losing 20,000 dollars, the company earned 15,000.
=== Why Acceptance Works ===
[lin_yutang] Carnegie quotes the writer Lin Yutang: "True peace of mind comes from accepting the worst."
[energy_burst] Once you accept the worst, you stop fighting shadows.
All that nervous energy becomes energy for solving the problem.
[floor_bottom] There is nowhere further to fall, so you can only climb.
=== Try It Today ===
[notebook] Pick one worry and ask yourself the three questions.
What is the worst that could realistically happen?
Can I make peace with it?
[ladder] Then, what one step can I take right now to improve on it?
=== Your Challenge ===
[question_mark] What worry has been chasing you this week?
Face its worst case tonight, accept it, and take one small step tomorrow.
