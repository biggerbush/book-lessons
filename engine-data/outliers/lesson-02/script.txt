=== The Hockey Mystery ===
[hockey_rink] What if the month you were born decided whether you could become a professional athlete?
In Canadian hockey, it often does.
=== A Strange Roster ===
[team_roster] A psychologist named Roger Barnsley was watching a junior hockey game when his wife noticed something odd about the program.
[calendar] An unusual number of the players had birthdays in January, February and March.
[magnifier] Barnsley dug into the records and found the same pattern across elite hockey.
=== The Cutoff Date ===
[jan_first] In Canada, youth hockey groups children by calendar year, with a cutoff of January 1.
[two_kids] So a boy born in early January plays alongside a boy born in late December.
[height_bars] At age nine, those extra months mean a bigger, stronger, more coordinated body.
=== Picked Early ===
[coach] Coaches pick the best nine and ten year olds for special all star teams.
[clipboard] They think they are spotting talent, but often they are spotting maturity.
[badge] The older kids get chosen far more often.
=== The Snowball ===
[snowball] Once chosen, those players get better coaching, better teammates, and many more games.
[practice_net] They practice far more than the kids who were left behind.
[growing_ball] By thirteen or fourteen, they really are better, and the gap keeps growing.
=== The Matthew Effect ===
[open_book] Gladwell borrows a name for this from sociology: the Matthew Effect.
[bible_verse] It comes from a line in the Gospel of Matthew: "unto every one that hath shall be given."
[stairs] Success brings more opportunity, which brings more success.
Gladwell calls this accumulative advantage.
=== Beyond the Rink ===
[soccer_ball] Gladwell notes similar birth month patterns in other sports with age cutoffs, like soccer.
[classroom] In schools too, the oldest children in a class tend to score higher on tests.
[school_path] Those early gaps can steer kids toward advanced tracks, and the advantage carries on for years.
=== Fixing the System ===
[lost_players] The real loss is all the talented younger kids who never got the chance.
[split_leagues] Gladwell suggests a simple fix: separate leagues or classes by birth months, so kids compete with true peers.
[check_mark] The lesson is that a small, arbitrary head start can be mistaken for natural talent.
