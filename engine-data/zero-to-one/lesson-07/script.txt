=== Seven Questions ===
[checklist] What if seven questions could tell you whether a business will thrive or crash?
Peter Thiel says they can, and he has a cautionary tale to prove it.
=== The Clean Energy Rush ===
[solar_panel] In the mid 2000s, clean energy looked like the next big thing.
[money_pile] Investors and governments poured huge sums into solar, wind and batteries.
[crash_chart] Then, around 2011, many of these companies collapsed.
Thiel argues they failed because they ignored basic business questions.
=== Engineering and Timing ===
[gear] Question one is engineering: is your technology a real breakthrough, not just a small improvement?
Thiel suggests aiming for something about ten times better than what exists.
[clock] Question two is timing: is this truly the right moment to start?
Many clean energy firms rushed in simply because everyone else was rushing in.
=== Monopoly and People ===
[small_circle] Question three is monopoly: are you starting with a big share of a small market?
[team] Question four is people: do you have the right team?
Thiel noticed many clean energy leaders looked like polished salespeople in suits, not technical builders.
=== Distribution ===
[delivery_van] Question five is distribution: can you actually get your product to customers?
Thiel warns that great products do not sell themselves.
[warehouse] Without a real way to reach buyers, even a clever invention just sits on a shelf.
=== Durability and Secrets ===
[calendar] Question six is durability: will your position still be defensible in 10 and 20 years?
[keyhole] Question seven is the secret: have you spotted a unique opportunity others do not see?
Most clean energy companies just pitched the obvious idea that the world needs green power.
=== Solyndra's Fall ===
[solyndra] Thiel highlights Solyndra, a solar company with unusual tube shaped panels.
[gov_loan] It won a large government loan guarantee and plenty of public praise.
But its panels were not dramatically better or cheaper than rivals.
[bankruptcy] In 2011, Solyndra went bankrupt.
In Thiel's view, it failed nearly every one of the seven questions.
=== Tesla's Answer ===
[tesla_car] One green company stood out: Tesla.
[seven_ticks] Thiel argues Tesla could answer all seven questions well.
It started small, with a pricey electric sports car for a narrow group of buyers.
[showroom] It also sold cars through its own stores, controlling its own distribution.
=== Your Turn ===
Pick one project you care about and test it against all seven questions.
Which question can you not answer yet, and what will you do about it?
