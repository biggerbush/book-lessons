=== Borrow Ideas ===
[lightbulb] What if the answer to your hardest problem is hiding in a totally different field?
Let's see how borrowing ideas from far away can crack new puzzles.
=== The Tumor Puzzle ===
[patient] Picture a patient with a tumor deep inside the body.
[ray_machine] A doctor has rays that can destroy it.
[burst] But one strong ray would also burn the healthy tissue it passes through.
Most people given this classic puzzle cannot solve it.
=== The Fortress Story ===
[fortress] Now picture a general attacking a fortress.
[roads] Every road is mined, so a big army crossing at once would set off explosions.
[small_groups] He splits his troops into small groups that arrive together from all sides.
=== Deep Structure ===
[arrows] Read that story first, and many more people solve the tumor puzzle.
Aim many weak rays from different angles, meeting at the tumor.
[iceberg] On the surface, armies and medicine look nothing alike.
Underneath, they share the same structure.
=== Many Distant Examples ===
[scientist] Psychologist Dedre Gentner studies how we spot those hidden structures.
[cards] Her work suggests comparing several different examples helps us see the deep pattern.
[web] Comparing cases from varied domains makes the shared idea stand out from surface details.
=== Kepler's Wandering ===
[kepler] Astronomer Johannes Kepler faced a problem nobody had a field for.
[planets] Why do planets move the way they do?
[magnet] He reached for analogies to light, magnetism, even smell, to imagine a force coming from the sun.
Each comparison pushed his thinking somewhere new.
=== Inside Versus Outside ===
[inside_view] When facing a problem, we usually zoom in on its own details and a few similar cases.
Psychologists call this the inside view.
[outside_view] Range means zooming out to many, unlike cases that share a deeper structure.
That wider view often reveals options a narrow view hides.
=== Try It Yourself ===
[question] Next time you are stuck, strip the problem down to its core.
[map] Ask where else that core shows up, in nature, sports, cooking, or history.
[toolbox] Collect a few far-flung examples before choosing a solution.
=== Your Challenge ===
[challenge] Pick one problem you face this week.
Can you find three distant fields that have already solved something like it?
