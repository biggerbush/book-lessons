=== The Hidden Cost ===
[phone_glow] A smartphone in a teenager's pocket costs more than its price tag.
It quietly takes away what a growing brain needs most.
=== Opportunity Cost ===
[clock] Haidt starts with a simple economics idea called opportunity cost.
Every hour on one thing is an hour lost to another.
[scale] When a phone fills many hours a day, something else must shrink.
[four_boxes] Haidt calls the four biggest losses the foundational harms.
=== Social Deprivation ===
[friends_bench] The first harm is losing face-to-face time with friends.
[time_chart] Time-use surveys reported in the book show teens' time hanging out in person fell sharply after the early 2010s.
[two_phones] Screen chats miss the eye contact, laughter, and shared rhythm that truly bond people.
=== Sleep Deprivation ===
[bed] The second harm happens at night.
[phone_in_bed] Phones come into the bedroom, and scrolling pushes bedtime later and later.
Teenagers need more sleep than adults, yet many now get far less than they need.
[tired_kid] Short sleep hurts memory, mood, and learning the next day.
=== Fragmented Attention ===
[notification] The third harm is broken focus.
Alerts ping all day, each one pulling a teen away from what they were doing.
[puzzle] Deep thinking, like reading a book or solving a hard problem, needs long stretches without interruption.
[shards] Constant alerts shatter attention while the brain's ability to focus is still developing.
=== Addiction ===
[slot_machine] The fourth harm is addiction.
Haidt explains that many apps borrow tricks from slot machines.
[reward_hand] Sometimes you get a like, sometimes nothing, and that unpredictable reward keeps you pulling.
[endless_feed] Infinite scrolling and autoplay remove natural stopping points, so a quick look becomes an hour.
=== How They Stack Up ===
[stack] These four harms feed each other.
A teen who scrolls late is tired, struggles to focus, then reaches for the phone again.
[empty_park] Meanwhile, the friends and outdoor time that might break the cycle slowly disappear.
=== Take Back the Night ===
[charger] You can start with one small change tonight.
[kitchen] Charge every phone, including your own, outside the bedrooms overnight.
[notif_off] Then turn off all nonessential notifications, so attention belongs to the person again.
=== Your Turn ===
Look at your family's screen time and ask one question.
What is all that scrolling quietly replacing?
