=== Drop Your Tools ===
[firefighter] Picture a firefighter running from a wall of flame, still gripping a heavy saw.
Letting go could save her life, so why won't she?
=== The Heavy Load ===
[forest_fire] Epstein retells how wildland firefighters have died fleeing fires they could have outrun.
[backpack] They were still carrying packs and tools that slowed them down.
[radio_order] In some cases, they had even been told to drop them.
=== Tools Become Identity ===
[karl_weick] Organizational psychologist Karl Weick studied these tragedies.
He saw that for firefighters, tools are not just equipment.
[id_badge] They are part of who they are, so dropping them feels like giving up.
[mind_toolbox] Epstein argues that experts carry mental tools the same way.
=== Hooked on Numbers ===
[rocket] Before the Challenger shuttle launch, NASA had a deeply ingrained habit: arguments needed hard numbers.
[cold_photo] An engineer worried about cold damage, but his evidence came from photos and experience, not data charts.
[dismissed] Without numbers, his warning was set aside, and the familiar tool stayed in hand.
=== One Big Idea ===
[forecasts] Psychologist Philip Tetlock tracked thousands of predictions made by experts over many years.
[hedgehog] Some experts, he called them hedgehogs, viewed every problem through one big idea.
[bad_scores] Their forecasts were poor, and deep knowledge of a topic did not rescue them.
=== Many Small Ideas ===
[fox] Other experts, the foxes, drew on many ideas and many sources.
[scales] They weighed conflicting views and changed their minds when evidence shifted.
[trophy] The foxes made notably better predictions.
=== Deliberate Amateurs ===
[scientist] Epstein also highlights scientists who stay playful outside their specialty.
[paintbrush] Nobel-winning scientists, he reports, are far more likely than typical scientists to have serious artistic hobbies.
[playground] Dabbling as a deliberate amateur keeps the mind loose and curious.
=== Your Range Habits ===
[question_mark] So treat your favorite method as a tool, not an identity.
[open_door] Ask what would make you wrong, and seek views from outside your field.
[new_hobby] And keep learning something where you are a happy beginner.
=== Your Challenge ===
What is one familiar tool you carry everywhere, even when it slows you down?
This week, try setting it down once and see what you notice.
