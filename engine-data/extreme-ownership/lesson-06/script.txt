=== Decentralized Command ===
[boss_phone] Picture a leader whose phone never stops ringing, because nobody acts without asking first.
What if the fix is to explain more and decide less?
=== One Leader, Too Many ===
[crowd] Jocko and Leif argue that no single person can directly lead a large crowd.
[small_circle] They suggest most leaders can handle only about 6 to 10 people well.
Beyond that, things start slipping through the cracks.
Lead too many directly, and you become a bottleneck.
=== Small Teams ===
[platoon] So SEAL units are broken down into small groups.
[fire_teams] A platoon splits into squads, and squads split into fire teams of 4 or 5.
[team_leader] Each little team has its own leader, often quite junior.
[tree_chart] Each leader reports up to the next level, like a branching tree.
=== Ramadi Streets ===
[city_map] In Ramadi, Task Unit Bruiser fought in chaotic city streets.
[radio_static] Junior leaders could not wait for orders from the top while bullets flew.
[arrow_fast] They had to see, decide and act on the spot.
[eye] The leaders closest to the action knew things their bosses could never see.
=== Know the Why ===
[question_mark] That only works if every leader understands why the mission matters.
[target] The authors call this the commander's intent, the purpose behind the plan.
When people know the goal, they can adapt when the plan breaks.
=== Clear Boundaries ===
[fence] Freedom still has edges.
Senior leaders set clear limits on what frontline leaders can decide alone.
Those limits keep small teams from wandering off mission.
[open_field] Inside those limits, juniors are trusted to make the call.
=== Trust Both Ways ===
[handshake] Senior leaders must stop hovering and let their people lead.
[hand_raised] Junior leaders must ask questions until they truly understand the intent.
Without that trust, decentralized command falls apart.
[binoculars] Freed from small details, the top leader can watch the bigger picture.
=== Your Version ===
[office] The same rule applies in any company.
[briefing] Before handing off a task, explain the purpose and the limits.
[green_light] Then let the person closest to the problem decide.
[front_desk] Frontline staff often spot problems long before the boss does.
=== Your Challenge ===
[calendar] This week, pick one decision you usually make yourself.
Explain the why to your team, and let them make it.
