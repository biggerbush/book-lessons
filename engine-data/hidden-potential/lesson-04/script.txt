=== Be a Sponge ===
[sponge_rock] Picture a sponge and a rock dropped into the same bucket of water.
One soaks it all up, and one comes out exactly the same.
=== Rocks and Sponges ===
[rock_person] Many of us move through life like rocks.
[splashes] New ideas splash around us, but very little sinks in.
Adam Grant argues that people who grow the most act more like sponges.
[growth_arrow] They take in knowledge, ideas and input, and they turn it into improvement.
=== Soak It Up Actively ===
A sponge does not wait for water to find it.
[reaching_hand] Grant stresses that sponges go looking for knowledge on purpose.
[question_mark] They ask questions, seek out people who know more, and treat every conversation as a chance to learn.
That is proactivity in action.
=== Filter What You Absorb ===
But a good sponge does not keep everything.
[filter] It also squeezes out what is useless.
[two_voices] Not all input deserves equal weight.
Grant suggests trusting people who know the subject and genuinely care about your growth.
=== The Feedback Problem ===
Now here is the surprise.
[feedback_form] Most of us ask for feedback when we want to improve.
[scorecard] But feedback invites people to judge what you already did.
[thumbs] They become critics, and they often give vague praise or polite criticism.
=== Ask for Advice ===
[speech_bubble] Grant shares research comparing two simple requests.
Some people were asked for feedback, and others were asked for advice on the same work.
[checklist] Those asked for advice gave more specific, more useful suggestions.
[arrow_forward] Advice turns their eyes toward the future, toward what you could do next time.
=== From Critic to Coach ===
[critic] When you ask for feedback, people play critic.
[coach] When you ask for advice, they become your coach.
[side_by_side] And a coach is on your side, focused on what comes next.
That small change in wording can unlock far better help.
=== Try It This Week ===
[notebook] Pick one thing you are working on right now.
[mentor] Find someone credible who wants you to succeed.
Instead of asking what they thought, ask one question.
[next_time] What is one thing I could do better next time?
=== Your Challenge ===
Who will you ask for advice before this week is over?
And what will you squeeze out, and what will you soak up?
