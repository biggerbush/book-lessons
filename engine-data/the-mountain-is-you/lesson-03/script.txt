=== Your Triggers ===
[flare] Some moments set you off faster than they should.
What if that spark is actually pointing somewhere useful?
=== The Overreaction ===
[friend_text] Picture a friend's short text, or a passing comment at work.
[thermometer] Your reaction shoots far higher than the moment deserves.
Wiest says that gap is the clue.
The size of the reaction is about something older than this moment.
=== An Old Wound ===
[bruise] Think of a bruise on your arm.
[light_tap] A light tap barely hurts healthy skin.
On the bruise, the same tap makes you wince.
The tap is not the real problem.
Triggers work the same way, pressing on pain that never fully healed.
=== Why It Stings ===
[critic] Wiest notes that criticism stings most when part of us already believes it.
[mirror] If you felt secure about it, the comment would slide right off.
So the sting shows you a fear you are still carrying.
That is not a weakness, it is useful information.
=== Jealousy Points ===
[two_people] Envy is another signpost she highlights.
[open_door] When someone's life makes you jealous, it often reveals something you want but have not let yourself pursue.
[spotlight] The feeling is shining a light on a desire you keep hiding from yourself.
Instead of shame, you get directions.
=== Don't Bury It ===
[lid] Our instinct is to push the reaction down and move on.
[pot] But a pressed lid only makes the pot boil over later.
Suppressing a trigger keeps the wound hidden, so it keeps getting hit.
=== Ask, Don't Blame ===
[signpost] Wiest suggests treating the trigger like a signpost, not an enemy.
[question] Ask what this moment reminds you of.
[notebook] Then ask what you needed back then, and what you need now.
=== Tend the Wound ===
[bandage] Once you see the wound, you can care for it directly.
That might mean reassurance, a boundary, or finally going after a goal.
Healing the wound is what quiets the alarm.
[calm_face] Over time, the same moments lose their grip on you.
=== Your Next Trigger ===
[map] Next time something sets you off, pause and get curious.
Ask yourself what this feeling is pointing you toward.
