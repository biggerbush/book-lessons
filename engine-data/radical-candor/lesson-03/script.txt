=== Four Ways to Fail ===
[open_fly] Imagine your colleague's fly is down, minutes before a big presentation.
What you do next reveals what kind of boss you are.
=== The Grid ===
[grid] Kim Scott draws two lines, one for caring personally and one for challenging directly.
[top_right] Only the top right box, where both are high, is Radical Candor.
[three_boxes] The other three boxes are the ways most feedback goes wrong.
=== The Loud Mocker ===
[crowd] Suppose you point at the zipper and laugh in front of everyone.
That is Obnoxious Aggression, challenging someone without caring about them.
[jerk] It is the boss who tears your work apart, and calls it just being honest.
=== The Whisperer ===
[whisper] Now suppose you say nothing to your colleague, but snicker about it to others later.
That is Manipulative Insincerity, neither caring nor challenging.
[backstab] It shows up as fake praise to your face and criticism behind your back.
Scott says people slide here when they care more about being liked, or are too worn out to argue.
=== The Silent Friend ===
[silent] Or suppose you say nothing at all, to spare your colleague the awkward moment.
That is Ruinous Empathy, caring but not challenging.
[stage] Your colleague walks on stage with the zipper still down.
Scott says this is the most common mistake bosses make, by far.
=== The Story of Bob ===
[bob] Scott learned this the hard way with an employee she calls Bob.
Bob was charming and likeable, but his work was not good enough.
[pat_back] Instead of saying so, she kept reassuring him and hoping things would improve.
[team] Meanwhile, the rest of her team grew frustrated covering for him.
[exit_door] When she finally had to let him go, Bob asked why she had never told him.
=== Not Personality Types ===
[labels] Scott stresses that these boxes describe moments of behavior, not types of people.
[mirror] Everyone lands in each box sometimes, including Scott herself.
[arrow] The goal is to notice which box you are in, and move toward the top right.
=== Your Move ===
Think of one piece of feedback you have been holding back to stay nice.
This week, say it kindly and clearly, before it costs someone more.
