=== Title ===
[brain_glow] Your brain has a clever trick for saving energy.
It turns repeated actions into a simple three-step loop.
=== The Rat in the Maze ===
[maze] Picture a small maze shaped like the letter T.
[rat] Researchers at MIT placed a rat at one end, behind a partition.
[chocolate] At the other end sat a piece of chocolate.
Sensors tracked what was happening inside the rat's brain.
=== The First Runs ===
[click] A click sounded, and the partition slid open.
[sniffing] At first, the rat wandered slowly, sniffing corners and scratching at walls.
[busy_brain] Its brain was working hard the whole time, processing every smell and turn.
Eventually, it found the chocolate.
=== Going Automatic ===
[fast_rat] After many runs, the rat stopped exploring and raced straight to the treat.
[quiet_brain] Something surprising happened inside its head.
Its brain activity dropped as the route became automatic.
[spikes] Activity now spiked only at the start, with the click, and at the end, with the chocolate.
=== Chunking ===
[chunk_block] Scientists call this process chunking.
The brain bundles a sequence of actions into one automatic routine.
[basal_ganglia] A small, ancient region deep in the brain, the basal ganglia, stores these chunks.
[battery] That frees up mental energy for other things.
=== The Three Steps ===
[loop] Duhigg says every habit follows this same loop.
[cue_icon] First comes a cue, a trigger telling your brain which routine to use.
[routine_icon] Next comes the routine, the behaviour itself, physical, mental or emotional.
[reward_icon] Finally comes the reward, which tells your brain this loop is worth remembering.
=== Why It Matters ===
[cycle_arrows] Each time the loop repeats, it becomes more automatic.
[sleep_bulb] Once a habit forms, the brain stops fully joining in decision making.
That helps when backing out of your driveway, but hurts with habits you would rather drop.
[magnifier] Once you can see the loop, you can start working with it.
=== Spot Your Own Loop ===
[phone] Think of a habit like checking your phone.
[notification] The cue might be boredom, or a buzz in your pocket.
The routine is scrolling.
[smile_face] The reward might be a quick hit of novelty or connection.
=== Your Turn ===
[question_mark] Pick one habit you did today without thinking.
Can you name its cue, its routine and its reward?
