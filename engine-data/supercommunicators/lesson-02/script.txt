=== Brains in Sync ===
[brain_scan] Duhigg describes research by neuroscientist Thalia Wheatley on what happens when people truly connect.
[sync_waves] Their brain activity starts to line up, almost like two instruments playing the same tune.
Scientists call this neural entrainment.
[broken_wave] When people are having different kinds of conversation, that syncing is much harder to reach.

=== Listen for Clues ===
[ear_icon] So the first step is to notice which conversation the other person is in.
[speech_bubble] Are they asking for decisions, sharing feelings, or talking about who they are and where they belong?
[clue_glass] Their words, tone and body language often tell you, if you pay attention.
=== Meet Them There ===
[hand_shoulder] If someone is sharing a feeling, respond to the feeling first.
[toolbox] If they want a solution, skip the long sympathy and help solve it.
[group_circle] If they are talking about identity, acknowledge it before arguing any facts.
Matching does not mean agreeing.
It means joining their conversation before steering anywhere.
=== When You Are Unsure ===
[question_mark] Sometimes the clues are fuzzy.
Then simply ask.
[two_options] Do you want me to help solve this, or would you rather I just listen?
That one question can stop a conversation from drifting apart.
=== Your Challenge ===
In your next tough chat, pause before replying and ask yourself which conversation they are having.
Then meet them there first.
