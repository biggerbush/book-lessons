=== Two Little Words ===
[kid_smiling] One kind compliment can quietly make a child afraid of hard work.
Here is how it happens, and what to say instead.
=== The Experiment ===
[researchers] Carol Dweck and her colleagues gave hundreds of young adolescents a set of fairly hard puzzles from a nonverbal I Q test.
[two_groups] Then each child got one sentence of praise.
[smart_badge] Some were praised for ability, told they must be smart at this.
[effort_badge] Others were praised for effort, told they must have worked really hard.
=== The Choice ===
[two_doors] Next, the children could pick an easy task or a challenging one they could learn from.
Most of the kids praised for being smart chose the easy task.
They had a label to protect.
[ninety_pct] About 90 percent of the effort-praised kids chose the challenge.
=== When It Got Hard ===
[hard_puzzles] Then everyone got harder puzzles, which nobody solved well.
[sad_face] The smart-praised kids decided they were not smart after all, and their enjoyment dropped.
[curious_face] The effort-praised kids saw difficulty as a sign to try harder, and many enjoyed the hard problems most.
=== The Aftermath ===
[easy_round] Finally, the puzzles became as easy as the first set.
[score_chart] The smart-praised group now scored worse than at the start.
The effort-praised group scored better.
[letter] Then the children wrote to students at another school, describing the test and their scores.
[forty_pct] Almost 40 percent of the smart-praised kids inflated their scores.
=== Why It Backfires ===
[label_tag] Praising intelligence tells a child that their worth is a fixed trait to protect.
[road] Praising the process says success comes from things they did and can do again.
=== What to Praise ===
[strategy_map] Dweck suggests praising the process, meaning effort, strategies, focus, persistence and improvement.
[speech_bubble] You might say, you tried several approaches until you found the one that worked.
[new_path] If effort is not working, help them find a new strategy.
=== Easy Wins ===
[quick_finish] What about when a child finishes something quickly and perfectly?
[raised_bar] Dweck suggests skipping the gushing, apologizing for wasting their time, and offering something they can learn from.
The fun lives in the stretch.
=== Your Turn ===
[mirror] Today, catch yourself before saying someone is so smart.
Instead, name one thing they actually did that made the difference.
