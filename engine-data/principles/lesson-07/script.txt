=== Title ===
[title_card] What if the best idea in the room won, no matter who said it?
=== Bosses Usually Win ===
[boss_desk] In most companies, the boss's opinion wins simply because they are the boss.
[quiet_team] Everyone else nods, even when they see a problem.
Dalio wanted a place where ideas competed on merit instead.
=== Radical Truth ===
[email_screen] After one client meeting, an employee emailed Dalio a blunt review of his performance.
[grade_d] He gave his boss a D minus for rambling and being unprepared.
Dalio did not punish him.
[thumbs_up] He praised the honesty and wanted more of it.
=== Radical Transparency ===
[tape_recorder] At Bridgewater, almost every meeting is recorded.
[library_shelf] Employees can review those recordings to see how decisions really get made.
Nothing important happens behind closed doors.
[open_door] That openness keeps people honest, and it lets everyone learn from real examples.
=== Weighing the Votes ===
[dot_app] In meetings, people rate each other's thinking in real time on an app called the Dot Collector.
[score_grid] Over time, these ratings show who is reliably strong at what.
[scale] When decisions are made, views from proven people count for more.
=== The Machine ===
[gears] Dalio pictures the company as a machine made of people and culture.
[issue_log] When something goes wrong, it gets written into a shared issue log.
[wrench] Then the team diagnoses the root cause and fixes the part that failed.
=== Why It Gets Better ===
[loop_arrow] Truth surfaces the problems.
Transparency lets everyone see them.
[fix_choice] Believability decides which fix to try.
[rising_line] Each turn of the loop leaves the machine a little smarter than before.
=== Start Small ===
[team_table] You do not need a hedge fund to try this.
[feedback_card] After your next meeting, ask one colleague for an honest grade on how you did.
[notebook] Write down what went wrong, and look for the cause, not the culprit.
[light_bulb] Then let the best answer win, even when it is not yours.
=== Your Challenge ===
Who on your team sees a problem right now but is not saying it?
[open_mic] What would it take for them to tell you this week?
