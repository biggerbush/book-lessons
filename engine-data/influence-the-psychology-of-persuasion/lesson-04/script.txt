=== Follow the Crowd ===
[crowd_look] Have you ever chosen a restaurant just because it was packed?
Then you have felt the pull of social proof.
=== The Shortcut ===
[question_mark] When we are not sure what is right, we look around.
[people_row] We copy what other people are doing, especially people who seem like us.
[thumbs_up] If many people do something, it is usually a sensible thing to do.
=== Canned Laughter ===
[tv_set] Cialdini starts with something odd: laugh tracks on television comedies.
[viewers] Many viewers say they find recorded laughter annoying and fake.
[laugh_meter] Yet research shows it makes audiences laugh longer and rate the jokes as funnier.
Even an obviously fake crowd steers us.
=== Salting the Jar ===
[tip_jar] Persuaders know this, so they manufacture the crowd.
[dollar_bills] Bartenders often put a few of their own bills in the tip jar at the start of a shift.
Customers see the money and assume tipping is normal.
[ad_crowd] Ads do the same by calling a product the most popular.
=== Uncertainty Rules ===
[fog] Social proof is strongest when a situation is unclear.
[mirror_people] And it is strongest when the crowd looks like us.
Then we simply follow.
=== Frozen Bystanders ===
[street_scene] This can turn dangerous in an emergency.
[bystanders] Imagine someone collapses on a busy sidewalk while many people pass.
Each person glances around, sees others staying calm, and concludes nothing is wrong.
[fewer_helpers] Researchers found that a victim is often less likely to get help when many people are watching.
Everyone waits for someone else.
=== Breaking the Freeze ===
[pointing_hand] If you ever need help, Cialdini says, remove the uncertainty.
[blue_jacket] Pick out one person and speak directly to them.
Say something like, you in the blue jacket, I need help, call an ambulance.
[phone_call] Now that person knows it is an emergency, and that it is their job.
=== Your Defense ===
[fake_crowd] Watch for faked evidence, like salted jars, staged testimonials, or recorded laughter.
[look_up] Look up from the crowd now and then and check the facts yourself.
[compass] A crowd is a useful guide, but it is not always right.
=== Your Challenge ===
This week, notice one choice you made because everyone else was doing it.
Ask yourself, would I still choose it if I were alone?
