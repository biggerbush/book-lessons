=== Title ===
[worried_face] What if most of your worries were bets you would almost never lose?
Today, we let the numbers calm you down.
=== A Boy Full of Fears ===
[farm_boy] As a boy on a Missouri farm, Carnegie was full of fears.
[lightning] He was afraid of being killed by lightning.
[grave] He even feared being buried alive.
Years later, he realized almost none of those things ever happened.
=== The Fruit Shipper ===
[jim_grant] Carnegie tells the story of Jim Grant, who ran a fruit business.
[train] He shipped oranges and grapefruit across the country by the trainload.
[wreck] He kept picturing his freight cars wrecked and his fruit ruined.
The worry was wearing him down.
=== Counting the Cars ===
[notepad] So Grant asked himself a simple question.
[count_25000] How many cars of fruit had he shipped over the years? About 25,000.
[count_5] And how many had been wrecked? Maybe five.
=== One in Five Thousand ===
[ratio] That works out to roughly one wreck in every 5,000 cars.
[scale] Seen that way, his fear looked tiny next to the facts.
Once he saw that, the worry began to lose its grip.
=== Bet Like an Insurer ===
[lloyds] Carnegie points to Lloyd's of London, the famous insurance company.
[coins] It made a fortune betting that the disasters people fear will rarely happen.
[dice] It never called this gambling.
It called it insurance, built on the law of averages.
=== Why This Works ===
[mind_movie] Worry plays the worst case like a movie on repeat.
[odds_meter] Checking the odds swaps that movie for a real number.
Your feelings shout, but the record of what usually happens speaks calmly.
That small shift is often enough to steady you.
=== Run Your Own Odds ===
[worry_card] Pick one worry and write it down.
[tally] Then count how often this thing has actually happened to you, or to people like you.
Be honest, and use real numbers wherever you can.
[check_mark] If the answer is almost never, tell yourself so, and get on with your day.
=== Your Challenge ===
[question] What are you dreading right now?
Ask yourself what the odds really are, and let the answer set you free.
