=== Born Brave? ===
[hero_cape] What if courage is not something you are born with, but something you practice like a sport?
That is the big idea Brené Brown starts with.
=== The Big Question ===
[researcher] Brené Brown has spent years researching courage, shame and vulnerability.
[question_card] For this book, she and her team asked senior leaders a simple question.
[globe] What about the way people lead needs to change, to succeed in a complex and fast changing world?
=== One Clear Answer ===
[speech_bubble] One answer kept coming back, in many different words.
As Brown reports it, we need braver leaders and more courageous cultures.
[gap_sign] But wanting more courage is not the same as knowing how to build it.
=== Skills, Not Charisma ===
[charisma_star] Many of us picture brave leaders as naturally bold, loud or charming.
Brown's research points somewhere else.
[toolbox] Courage turned out to be a set of skills.
[checklist] Her team found those skills can be taught, observed and measured.
That means nobody is locked out of bravery.
=== Four Skill Sets ===
[four_blocks] The book organises daring leadership into four skill sets.
They cover hard conversations, clear values, trust, and getting back up after failure.
We will explore each one in later lessons.
For now, notice that every one is something you can learn and practice.
=== Who Is a Leader? ===
[name_badge] Here is the twist.
Brown's definition of a leader has nothing to do with job titles.
[seedling] A leader is anyone who takes responsibility for finding the potential in people and processes.
[watering_can] And who has the courage to develop that potential.
=== That Includes You ===
[parent] That could be a parent helping a child try again after a setback.
[team_member] It could be a new hire fixing a clumsy process nobody else wants to touch.
[big_office] A manager with a big title is not automatically leading.
Responsibility and courage are what count.
=== Start Practicing ===
[gym_weights] If courage is a skill, it grows with practice, like a muscle.
Expect to feel awkward at first, just like learning any new skill.
[calendar] So choose one small moment this week to lead on purpose.
=== Your Challenge ===
[mirror] Whose potential, or which process, will you take responsibility for developing this week?
What is one brave step you could take tomorrow?
