=== Hidden Potential ===
[climber] Who would you bet on, the fast starter or the steady climber?
Most of us pick the wrong one.
=== The Snapshot Trap ===
[report_card] We often judge potential the way we glance at a report card.
[star_kid] We notice who shines early and quickly label them gifted.
[slow_kid] Then we quietly write off the kid who struggles at first.
Adam Grant says this early snapshot fools us again and again.
=== Start Versus Distance ===
[mountain] Picture potential as a long mountain trail.
[base_camp] Where you begin is only base camp.
[summit_flag] What really counts is how high you can climb from there.
Grant calls this the distance traveled, not the starting point.
=== Two Climbers ===
[climber_a] Imagine one climber who starts halfway up the mountain.
[climber_b] Another climber starts at the very bottom.
[trail_line] If the second climbs farther, she has shown more potential, even if she ends a little lower.
Grant's point is that the climb, not the altitude, reveals what someone can do.
=== The Bloom Study ===
[bloom] Grant points to research led by the psychologist Benjamin Bloom.
[performers] Bloom studied people who reached world-class level, like pianists, swimmers and mathematicians.
[child_photo] Most of them did not look like prodigies when they were young.
They became great by growing, not by starting ahead of everyone else.
=== Why We Miss It ===
[stopwatch] Early talent is fast, flashy and easy to see.
[seedling] Growth is slow, so it hides from a quick glance.
[magnifier] To spot it, we need to watch the rate of progress, not just the current level.
=== What Drives the Climb ===
[toolbox] So what carries people that whole distance?
[habits] Grant argues it is less about inborn gifts and more about learnable habits.
[support] It also depends on the people and structures around you.
Later lessons unpack exactly how that works.
=== See Yourself Fairly ===
[mirror] This idea applies to you too.
[old_photo] Comparing yourself to someone else's starting point is a rigged game.
[growth_chart] Instead, compare yourself to where you were a year ago.
Your past self is the only fair benchmark.
=== Your Challenge ===
Think of one skill you gave up on because you started slow.
This week, take one step and measure it by distance traveled, not by where you began.
